The grids next door, same policy

PRF sign-up and billing dates for 2027

PRF has one deadline a year. For the 2027 crop year it is Dec 1, 2026. Miss Dec 1 and the next chance is a year later.

  • Dec 1, 2026Sign-up closes. New policies, and changes to coverage level, months or acres on an existing one, are due at your agent.
  • Dec 1, 2026Acreage report due. Without one there is no coverage for the year.
  • Jan 1 to Dec 31The coverage year. Each two-month interval you insure stands on its own.
  • Sep 1, 2027Premium billing date. The policy says you are billed no earlier than this, and that premium you owe is taken out of any payment due you.

A payment, when one is owed, is issued “not later than 60 days following the date RMA publishes the final grid index” for that interval (Rainfall Index Basic Provisions, section 8(d)). For 2026, RMA published the March to April index on July 7. The dates above are read from USDA’s 2027 actuarial files.

What PRF costs, and USDA’s share

USDA pays part of every PRF premium: 59% at the 70% and 75% coverage levels, 55% at 80% and 85%, and 51% at 90%. You pay the rest.

The premium rate is different for every grid, every interval and every coverage level. The calculator above uses the rate for yours. It leaves out the policy’s administrative fee, which is charged per crop and county.

New for 2027

  • A new rain record. The index now comes from NOAA’s National Centers for Environmental Information, in place of the Climate Prediction Center data used through 2026 (USDA RMA, April 30, 2026). The record starts in 1951, not 1948, and each interval is measured against its 1951 to 2025 average (RMA’s processing note). The grids did not move. Every past year on this page is the figure from that new record.
  • January to February is capped at 25%. In USDA’s 2027 files, 3,067 of the 3,070 counties with PRF limit that interval to 25% of a policy’s value. The other intervals allow 50%, 60% or 70%, by county. The calculator applies your county’s limits.
  • Grazing land needs livestock on it. The 2027 Crop Provisions, section 4(j): “For the intended use of grazing, you must have an insurable interest in enough livestock grazing the acres that would equate to at least 25% of the stocking rate for the insured acres.” RMA’s question-and-answer sheet on the rule says: “Failure means no indemnity is due and full premium is owed.” Records are asked for on request, not filed. Hay ground is not under this rule. This page does not work out head counts; your agent does, from the FSA National Crop Table or your state or federal lease (RMA stocking rate questions).

What PRF does not do

“This policy does not directly measure, capture, or utilize any actual crop production, including any of your actual crop production. For example, it is possible for you to receive an indemnity payment without suffering a loss of actual crop production. However, you may experience a loss of crop production and not receive an indemnity payment.”USDA RMA, Rainfall Index Basic Provisions (27.1-RI), section 13
  • It pays on one thing: rain in your grid over two months, against that grid’s long-run average. Not on hay yield, cattle weights, heat, hail or a drought declaration.
  • The grid is a quarter of a degree on each side. The rain figure is worked out for the whole grid from weather stations. Your own gauge does not count, and a neighbor a mile away can be in a different grid.
  • Rain and grass do not move together as closely as the policy assumes. On three university ranches in Kansas and Nebraska, swapping the grid figure for rain measured at the site would have cut the mismatch by only 5% to 9% (Yu and others, 2019). On California rangeland, the chance of a forage loss in an insured interval with no payment was put at 31% to 46% (Keller and Saitone, 2022).
  • Six states have a native sod rule this page does not apply: in Iowa, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota and South Dakota, recently broken native sod gets less protection and less subsidy (Crop Provisions, section 4(g)).

How this page figures it

Nothing here is modelled or estimated. The inputs are USDA’s own, copied unchanged: premium rates, county base values, interval limits, subsidy rates and dates from the 2027 Actuarial Data Master, and each grid’s index history from the service behind RMA’s PRF tool. The arithmetic is the policy’s.

protection per acre  = county base value x coverage level x productivity factor
interval protection  = protection per acre x acres x your share x share of value in the interval
interval premium     = interval protection x premium rate
you pay              = interval premium - USDA's share
payment              = interval protection x (coverage level x 100 - index) / (coverage level x 100)
                       when the index is below the coverage level, else nothing

Dollars are rounded the way RMA’s tool rounds them: protection per acre to the cent, everything else to the dollar. Last checked against RMA’s own tool on Oct 12, 2026: 30 of 30 policies priced here matched it to the dollar.

What the history is: this year’s rates, this year’s county values and this year’s rain record, run over each past year. RMA’s tool does the same, one year at a time. It is not a record of what PRF paid. Rates, values and the data source were different then, and the averages each year is measured against move every crop year.

What it leaves out: Hawaii, which has its own program and grids. The administrative fee. The native sod rule. Any year USDA has not finished publishing is listed and kept out of every total.

Questions ranchers and hay growers ask

What is PRF insurance?

Pasture, Rangeland, Forage is a federal crop insurance policy for grazing land and hay ground. It pays when rain in your grid, over a two-month interval you chose, comes in below a share of that grid’s long-run average. You pick the coverage level, from 70% to 90%, and how to spread the policy across the intervals. USDA pays part of the premium.

When is the PRF deadline for 2027?

Sign-up closes Dec 1, 2026. The acreage report is due Dec 1, 2026. The premium is billed Sep 1, 2027.

How much does PRF cost per acre?

It depends on the grid, the months, the coverage level and the county’s value for grazing or hay, so there is no one number. USDA pays 59% at the 70% and 75% coverage levels, 55% at 80% and 85%, and 51% at 90%. Enter your grid and acres above to see your share of the premium in dollars and per acre, billed Sep 1 of the crop year.

Which months should I insure?

The policy needs at least two intervals, no month used twice, each between your county’s minimum and maximum. Past that it is your call, and no record can make it for you. The calculator shows how each interval did over 75 years at your grid. Months that paid back the most are often winter months when little grows. RMA’s handbook puts it this way: choosing intervals “when precipitation is not needed for the insured crop or when precipitation does not normally occur is not an effective use” of the policy.

Is PRF drought insurance?

No. It pays on a rain index for a grid, two months at a time. A dry summer after a wet spring, a drought declaration, heat or a short hay crop do not trigger it. Only the index does, and only for the months you insured.

How big is a PRF grid, and which one am I in?

A grid is a quarter of a degree of latitude by a quarter of a degree of longitude: about 17 miles north to south, and 11 to 16 miles east to west, narrower the farther north you are. There are 13,414 of them with PRF offers in 48 states. Type a ZIP or coordinates above to see yours. Your agent sets the official grid from a point on your land.

Why are these numbers different from what PRF paid in past years?

Because they are not a payment history. Every year is run at 2027 rates and county values, on the rain record USDA publishes for 2027, which starts in 1951. Policies through 2026 were paid on a different data source, with the rates of their own year. RMA’s own tool works the same way.

Do I need livestock to insure grazing land?

Yes. Starting with the 2027 crop year, land insured for grazing needs livestock you have an interest in, grazing those acres, equal to at least 25% of the stocking rate. Fall short and no payment is due while the full premium is still owed. Hay ground is not under the rule. Your agent works out the number with you.

Why did my neighbor get a PRF payment when I did not?

Three usual reasons: they are in a different grid, they insured different months, or they chose a higher coverage level. Each interval pays on its own, so one dry pair of months can pay a neighbor who insured it and miss you if you did not.

When would I be paid?

Not later than 60 days after RMA publishes the final index for the interval. For 2026, RMA published the March to April index on July 7. Premium you owe is taken out of a payment first.
