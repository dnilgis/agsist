Insect thresholds for corn and soybean: what extension says, and when it said it

A threshold is the count where treating pays. I list the ones I have read in university extension sources, word for word where it matters, with the date of each source beside it. Where sources disagree I show both. Four rows come from a UW sheet dated February 2000; they are in grey, marked old, and sit next to the newer Wisconsin numbers.

Silk feeders at pollination

Silk clipping only costs yield while the silks are fresh and pollen is shedding. Once the silks are brown it no longer matters. The sources below count beetles per plant or per ear, so read the unit before you compare.

About the old sheet

The UW corn insect sheet (L023) prints 2/00, February 2000, at the bottom. I kept its rows, in grey with that date, because they show how the numbers moved: black cutworm went from 5% damaged to 2-5% cut, and the silk-clipping count is now stated per ear for Japanese beetle. It also covers European corn borer and stalk borer, but those are worksheets where the answer depends on your yield, your price and what control costs, so I do not copy a single number from them. UW's current guide, A3646, has a stalk borer table (Table 2-8) built the same way; read it there.

Questions

What is the economic threshold for soybean aphid?

Iowa State and Minnesota give the same one: through R5, an average of 250 aphids per plant, more than 80 percent of plants with aphids, and numbers still increasing. All three must be true. UW A3646 words it as about 80% of the field at an average of 250 per plant and actively increasing, with R1 to R5 the best time to treat. Minnesota says no threshold has been developed for R6; A3646 says treating at R6 is not recommended. Sources: Iowa State ICM News, Aug 28, 2017; UMN Extension, reviewed 2023; UW A3646, 2023 edition.

How many rootworm beetles does it take to treat for silk clipping?

The sources do not agree on the unit, so I print each as written. UW Extension (Jul 2020) says about 5 to 6 beetles per plant. Iowa State (Jul 9, 2024) says 5 or more per ear plus silks clipped under an inch and pollination not done. SDSU says an average of 5 or more per plant in the first week of pollen shed. Silk clipping does not matter once the silks are brown.

Is the 0.75 beetles per plant number a spray threshold?

No. The old UW sheet (Feb 2000) uses 0.75 or more beetles per plant to decide whether to treat for the following year, when corn will follow corn in the same field. It is not a trigger to spray this year's corn.

Why is the UW corn insect sheet marked old?

Its own print date is 2/00, February 2000. UW has since published A3646, Pest Management in Wisconsin Field Crops (2023 edition), and Crops and Soils articles. For black cutworm, armyworm and silk feeders they give numbers that differ from the old sheet, so the old rows stay for comparison and the newer ones sit beside them. Where the old sheet is the only source I have, as for the next-year rootworm count, I say so.

What is the black cutworm threshold in Wisconsin?

A3646 (2023 edition) says treat when 2-5% of plants show cutting activity. UW Crops and Soils says the same, and works it out for you: 5 to 12 cut plants out of 250 scouted. The February 2000 UW sheet said 5% or more damaged with larvae 6th instar or smaller. Moth trap counts are not a reason to treat.

Why not print the European corn borer and stalk borer thresholds?

On the UW sheet they are worksheets, not single numbers: the answer depends on your yield, the price you expect, the cost of control and how many borers you count. I will not print a number that only holds for one set of inputs.
