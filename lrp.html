What the numbers would pick

History only, not advice

Nothing. No coverage choice beat 95 to 100% on the record.

A choice is kept only if, after allowing for all 36 tests (Holm), the odds of luck are under 0.05, it came out ahead, and it came out ahead at every length. None did, so the numbers pick nothing over 95 to 100%.

Closest call, not proven: fed cattle at 85 to 90%, instead of 95 to 100%. Since July 2013 it came out ahead in 10 of 13 reinsurance years, about $1.06 per $100 of coverage. 90 to 95% did about the same (ahead in 10 of 13 years). Since July 2020 it was ahead in 6 of 6 years. With 6 years, the smallest odds the test can give are 1 in 64, which cannot clear the bar across 36 tests. After allowing for all 36 tests, the odds it was luck are 0.33, far from the 0.05 bar.

I am not recommending it. Lower coverage pays less often and costs less; on this record that trade did not clearly win.

How the record is worked

  • The data. 6,922 of RMA’s daily LRP files (ADM livestock), every sales day from Jul 1, 2013 to Oct 14, 2026: the coverage prices, rates and cost per cwt RMA posted that day, and the actual ending values it posted later. Nothing is estimated or modeled. 65,350 offers, 204,111 entries once each coverage band is counted.
  • What is tested. feeder cattle (steers, weight 2) (type 810), fed cattle (type 820), swine (type 997). Other feeder types settle on the same index times their factor, so per $100 of coverage they come out the same. Not tested: unborn hogs, and the types RMA added from RY2026 on (unborn calves, cull cows, weight-2 unborn feeders), which are too new.
  • One entry. One animal, one sales day, one length, one coverage band. Per $100 of coverage: paid = coverage price minus actual ending value, when that is above zero; your premium = RMA’s cost per cwt less the subsidy of that policy’s year; net = paid minus your premium. In each band the entry takes the highest level offered that day, the most protection at that band’s subsidy.
  • Walk-forward “best so far.” On each sales day it picks the band with the best record among endorsements of the same length that had already ended at least 14 days before, so the ending value was out, and only once a band had 60 such entries. Nothing a buyer could not have known that day.
  • The odds. Daily entries overlap, so each reinsurance year counts once: the test flips the sign of each year’s average every possible way (exact sign flip) and asks how often that does as well as the real record. It runs only with 5 or more years of 20+ sales days. All 36 tests are adjusted together (Holm); kept means adjusted odds under 0.05, ahead on average, and ahead at every length with enough data.
  • Gaps. 174 sales-day offers whose end date had passed with no ending value posted by RMA are left out (hogs 61, fed 83, feeder 30). Endorsements still running are left out until they end. RMA’s file for Mar 25, 2026 (2025_ADMLivestockLrp_Daily_20260325.zip) would not open (BadZipFile); that day’s offers are not in the record, and the reader tries it again on later runs.
  • Basis is not covered. LRP pays on the index, so every number here is at the index, not at your sale barn or packer.

Questions

What is LRP insurance?

Livestock Risk Protection (LRP) is federal insurance from USDA’s Risk Management Agency (RMA) that pays when a price index for feeder cattle, fed cattle or hogs ends under the coverage price you bought. You buy it one endorsement at a time: a type of animal, a number of head, a target weight, a length and a coverage level. On Oct 14, 2026 RMA offered lengths of 13 to 52 weeks and coverage levels of 75% to 100% of its expected ending value. It covers the price, not basis (RMA LRP fact sheet).

How does an LRP payment work?

At the end date RMA compares the actual ending value with your coverage price. If the ending value is lower, it pays the difference per cwt, times the target weight per head, the number of head and your share. Feeder cattle settle on the CME Feeder Cattle Index, times the price adjustment factor for the type and weight; fed cattle on USDA’s 5-area weekly weighted average price for slaughter steers; hogs on the CME Lean Hog Index (RMA fed cattle endorsement; RMA LRP fact sheet).

How much of the LRP premium does USDA pay?

Since July 1, 2020, USDA pays 35% of the premium at 95 to 100% coverage, 40% at 90 to 94.99%, 45% at 85 to 89.99%, 50% at 80 to 84.99% and 55% at 70 to 79.99% (RMA PM-20-064). RMA’s own Summary of Business rows from 2021 to 2027 show exactly that subsidy on 97% of 272,841 rows. Beginning and veteran farmers and ranchers get more.

When can I buy LRP, and when do sales close?

RMA posts new coverage prices and rates each sales day, and sales close at 8:25 a.m. Central the next day. A Friday’s sales close Saturday at 8:25 a.m. There are no sales on federal or CME holidays, none for cattle on USDA Cattle on Feed report days and none for hogs on Hogs and Pigs report days (RMA Livestock Insurance Offer Calendar). A price you see is good only for that day’s window.

When is the LRP premium due?

Not when you buy. Since the 2021 changes the premium is billed after the endorsement ends (RMA PM-20-064), and RMA’s 2026 LRP handbook puts the billing date at the first day of the second month after the end date (RMA LRP Handbook 2026).

How many head can I cover with LRP?

Feeder cattle and fed cattle: up to 12,000 head on one endorsement and 25,000 head a year, July 1 to June 30. Hogs: up to 70,000 head on one endorsement and 750,000 a year (RMA PM-22-029; RMA LRP fact sheet).

Does LRP cover basis?

No. It pays on the index, not on what you sell for. If your local cash price falls further than the index, LRP does not cover the gap, and if the index falls while your cash price holds, it still pays. Nothing on this page includes basis (RMA LRP fact sheet).

How does LRP compare with a put option?

Both set a price floor for a premium. LRP is sold by the head instead of by the futures contract, needs no brokerage account and no margin, carries a 35% to 55% subsidy, and the premium is billed after the end date (RMA PM-20-064). It settles on the cash index on the end date, not on a futures price.
