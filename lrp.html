What the numbers would pick

History only, not advice

No coverage level beat 95 to 100%.

This compares coverage levels. It does not say whether to insure. A level is kept only if, adjusted for running all 36 tests (Holm), the odds of luck are under 0.05, it came out ahead, and it came out ahead at every length. None did.

Closest call, not proven: fed cattle at 85 to 90%, instead of 95 to 100%. Since July 2013 it came out ahead in 10 of 13 insurance years, about $1.38 per $100 of expected value, averaged over the years. Ahead means it lost less on average because it covered less; in a crash it pays less. 90 to 95% did about the same (ahead in 10 of 13 years). Since July 2020 it was ahead in 6 of 6 years. With 6 years, the smallest odds the test can give are 1 in 32, which cannot clear the bar across 36 tests. Adjusted for running all 36 tests, the odds it was luck are 0.24, far from the 0.05 bar. Even counting only the 12 tests that ask whether a lower level beat 95 to 100% since 2013, the smallest adjusted odds are 0.082, still not under 0.05.

Fed cattle ending values (USDA’s 5-area weekly steer price) came in above RMA’s expected ending value in 10 of 13 insurance years; the ending value is a weekly cash average, the expected value comes from futures. On what producers actually bought, RMA’s Summary of Business shows fed cattle LRP paid back $0.20 per $1 producers paid, insurance years 2014 to 2025 (RMA Summary of Business).

I am not recommending it.

Why high coverage is the usual default: the subsidy is a share of the premium, and the premium per head is largest at the top level, so if RMA’s premiums are fair the subsidy dollars per head are largest there too. Published work from Utah State University (Haviland and Feuz, the USU LRP tool) also found 95 to 100% coverage did best on past contracts. That is their finding, not a test on this page.

How the record is worked

  • The data. 6,922 of RMA’s daily LRP files (ADM livestock), every sales day from Jul 1, 2013 to Oct 15, 2026: the coverage prices, rates and cost per cwt RMA posted that day, and the actual ending values it posted later. Nothing is estimated or modeled. 65,375 offers, 204,111 entries once each coverage band is counted.
  • What is tested. Feeder cattle steers, weight 2 (RMA type 810), fed cattle steers and heifers (type 820) and hogs (type 997). Other feeder types settle on the same index times their factor, so per $100 of coverage they come out the same. Not tested: unborn hogs, and the types RMA added from insurance year 2026 on (unborn calves, cull cows, weight-2 unborn feeders), which are too new.
  • One entry. One animal, one sales day, one length, one coverage band. Per $100 of coverage: paid = coverage price minus actual ending value, when that is above zero; your premium = RMA’s cost per cwt less the subsidy; net = paid minus your premium. In each band the entry takes the highest level offered that day, so the 95 to 100% record is about 99.1% coverage on average. The record above is the plain average over every sales day.
  • Walk-forward “best so far.” On each sales day it ranks the bands by their record among policies of the same length that had already ended at least 14 days before, so the ending value was out, once a band had 60 such entries, and takes the best-ranked band RMA offered that day. It uses RMA’s latest posted ending values; RMA restated 399 of them after first posting them.
  • The odds. Daily entries overlap, so each insurance year counts once: the test flips the sign of each year’s average every possible way (exact sign flip) and asks how often that does as well as the real record. It runs only with 5 or more years of 20+ sales days. All 36 tests are adjusted together (Holm); kept means adjusted odds under 0.05, ahead on average, and ahead at every length with enough years.
  • Gaps. 47 sales-day offers whose end date had passed with no ending value posted by RMA are left out (hogs 15, fed 20, feeder 12). Policies still running are left out until they end. RMA’s file for Mar 25, 2026 (2025_ADMLivestockLrp_Daily_20260325.zip) would not open; 19 of the missing values (fed 16, feeder 3) would have come from it, and the reader tries it again on every run until it opens or has failed on three days.
  • Basis is not covered. LRP pays on the index, so every number here is at the index, not at your sale barn or packer.

Questions

Is LRP worth it?

It depends on what a price drop would cost you, because that is what it covers. On RMA’s own record, buying the top coverage offered every sales day since July 2013 for 13 weeks, at today’s subsidy, each $1 of premium paid back $1.05 on feeder cattle, $0.59 on fed cattle and $1.32 on hogs. Since July 2020 it was $0.91, $0.36 and $0.84. It paid something on 27 to 49 of every 100 sales days bought. These are index figures, before basis. They compare coverage levels; they do not say whether you should insure.

What is LRP insurance?

Livestock Risk Protection (LRP) is federal insurance from USDA’s Risk Management Agency (RMA) that protects against a decline in prices below the coverage price you buy (RMA LRP Handbook 2027 (FCIC-20010), section 22B). You buy it one policy at a time (RMA calls each one an endorsement): a type of animal, a number of head, a target weight, a length and a coverage level. Lengths run 13 to 52 weeks, depending on the animal, and coverage levels 75% to 100% of RMA’s expected ending value (sections 22B, 23A, 24A, 25A). On Oct 15, 2026 RMA offered 13 to 52 weeks and 75% to 100%. It covers the price, not basis.

How does an LRP payment work?

If the actual ending value on the end date is under your coverage price, it pays the difference per cwt, times the head, the target weight and your share (RMA LRP Handbook 2027 (FCIC-20010), section 22D). Feeder cattle settle on the CME Feeder Cattle Index, times the price adjustment factor for the type and weight (section 23C); fed cattle on USDA’s 5-area weekly weighted average price for slaughter steers (RMA fed cattle endorsement); hogs on the CME Lean Hog Index (RMA LRP fact sheet). To be paid you must own the animals: selling or giving them up more than 60 days before the end date ends coverage on them, and the premium is still owed (section 21C(3)). Fed cattle and hogs, and feeder cattle you sold, need sale records from the insurance period or no later than 60 days after the end date (30 days for unborn calves); feeder cattle you still own need ownership records and your signed statement that they were marketable on the end date (section 21C(2)). Fed cattle are covered to be slaughtered on or near the end date (section 24A). When the ending value is under the coverage price, the claim form goes back within 60 days of getting it, and payment comes within 30 days of the form (section 21G).

How much of the LRP premium does USDA pay?

Since July 1, 2020, USDA pays 35% of the premium at 95 to 100% coverage, 40% at 90 to 94.99%, 45% at 85 to 89.99%, 50% at 80 to 84.99% and 55% below 80% (RMA PM-20-064; RMA now offers 75% and up). RMA’s own Summary of Business rows from 2021 to 2027 show exactly that subsidy on 97% of 272,841 rows. Beginning and veteran farmers and ranchers get more.

When can I buy LRP, and when do sales close?

Sales open when RMA posts the day’s coverage prices and rates and close at 8:25 a.m. Central the following calendar day, so a Friday’s close Saturday (RMA LRP Handbook 2027 (FCIC-20010), Exhibit 2 and section 22B). There are no sales for cattle on the days USDA releases the Cattle and Cattle on Feed reports, none for hogs on Hogs and Pigs report days, none on days the CME livestock markets are closed, none after a limit move in the futures, and none if RMA has not posted the offers by 4:30 p.m. Central (section 22B). RMA’s files have landed between about 2:15 and 4:30 p.m. Central; this page picks them up within about an hour.

When is the LRP premium due?

Not when you buy. The premium billing date is the first day of the second month after the end date (RMA LRP Handbook 2027 (FCIC-20010), section 21E). Premium can also be taken out of an indemnity (section 22E).

How many head can I cover with LRP?

Feeder cattle and fed cattle: up to 12,000 head on one endorsement and 25,000 head a crop year, July 1 to June 30. Hogs: up to 70,000 head on one endorsement and 750,000 a crop year (RMA LRP Handbook 2027 (FCIC-20010), sections 23B, 24B and 25B).

Does LRP cover basis?

No. It pays on the index, not on what you sell for (RMA LRP Handbook 2027 (FCIC-20010), sections 22B and 22D). If your local cash price falls further than the index, LRP does not cover the gap, and if the index falls while your cash price holds, it still pays. Nothing on this page includes basis.

How does LRP compare with a put option?

Both set a price floor for a premium. With LRP there is no futures account, the premium carries a 35% to 55% subsidy (RMA PM-20-064) and is billed after the end date (RMA LRP Handbook 2027 (FCIC-20010), section 21E), and it is sold by the head in any number up to the limit instead of by the futures contract. RMA sets the coverage prices and rates each day (section 22A), you must own the livestock and show records to be paid (section 21C), and the endorsement settles on the index on its end date, not on a futures price.
