PRF insurance in Virginia: 2027 county values and rates

Pasture, Rangeland, Forage (PRF) is USDA’s rainfall-index insurance for grazing land and hay ground. USDA’s 2027 files offer it in 98 Virginia counties, on 219 grids. The policy pays when precipitation in the grid over two months you choose comes in under a set share of normal.

PRF dates for 2027

  • Dec 1, 2026: sales closing. New policies and changes are due at your agent.
  • Dec 1, 2026: acreage report, with any lease signed and in hand.
  • Sep 1, 2027: premium billed, no earlier than this. Premium owed comes out of any payment first.

From 2027, grazing acres need livestock you have an interest in, equal to at least 25% of the stocking rate, or no payment is due and the premium is still owed (2027 Crop Provisions, section 4(j)). USDA’s share of the premium needs form AD-1026 on file with the Farm Service Agency. PRF pays on a precipitation index for the grid, not on your production. PRF questions in Virginia

When is the PRF deadline in Virginia for 2027?

The sales closing date is Dec 1, 2026, the same across the country. The acreage report is due Dec 1, 2026, and the premium is billed no earlier than Sep 1, 2027.

How much does PRF cost per acre in Virginia?

It depends on the grid, the months, the coverage level and the county’s value. USDA’s county base value for grazing land in Virginia runs from $44.00 to $81.00 an acre, and for non-irrigated hay from $204.00 to $375.00. USDA pays 51% to 59% of the premium. The calculator gives the figure for your grid, acres and months.

Is PRF offered in every Virginia county?

USDA’s 2027 files list PRF in 98 Virginia counties, across 219 grids. Each county row below opens the calculator on that county.

Has PRF paid more than it cost in Virginia?

On the full premium, about the same as everywhere: across Virginia’s grids at 90% coverage, $1 of premium would have paid back a middle $0.86 over 1951 to 2025 at 2027 rates. USDA pays half or more of the premium, so the producer’s share has done better on average. Most of that comes in a few dry years, and none of it says what next year will do.
