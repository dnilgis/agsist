PRF insurance in Warren County, Iowa: 2027 grids, values and record

Pasture, Rangeland, Forage (PRF) is USDA’s rainfall-index insurance for grazing land and hay ground. USDA’s 2027 files offer it in Warren County on 9 grids: 25345, 25346, 25347, 25645, 25646, 25647, 25945, 25946, 25947. It pays when precipitation in the grid over two months you choose comes in under a set share of normal.

As of Oct 12, the record here runs 75 years, from 1951 to 2025. On the middle of the county’s grids, August to September came in under 90% of normal in 38 of those years. In 2025 that same pair sat at 63% of normal on the middle, while June to July ran 168%. Grid 25345 had its driest year in 1988, at 50% of normal.

USDA’s 2027 county base values, the same on every grid in the county. Protection per acre is the value times your coverage level times a productivity factor of 60 to 150: grazing at 90% and 100 is $60.03 an acre.

Example, not a recommendation: 100 acres of grazing at 90% coverage, productivity factor 100, half in April to May and half in June to July, on grid 25645, the middle of the county’s 9 grids by premium. The full premium is $730: USDA pays $372 and you pay $358, or $3.58 an acre. Run over 1951 to 2025 at 2027 rates, it would have paid in 50 of 75 years, an average of $620 a year, or $6.20 an acre. That average is under the full premium of $730; it comes out above your share only because USDA pays the rest. That is the past record, not a forecast, and it leaves out the administrative fee.

Iowa has the native sod rule: native sod broken in recent years gets 65% of the protection and 50 points less of USDA’s share. The values here are for land the rule does not touch.

What each pair of months costs in Warren County

At 90% coverage, across the 9 grids that touch the county: first the middle of the 9 for that pair of months (it can be a different grid in each row), then the lowest and highest. The premium is the full rate per $100 of protection; USDA pays 59% at 70%, 59% at 75%, 55% at 80%, 55% at 85%, 51% at 90%. Normal is the grid’s average precipitation for those months. A year counts, of the 75 from 1951 to 2025, when the grid’s index for those months came in under 90% of normal, which is when a 90% policy pays.

PRF dates for 2027

  • Dec 1, 2026: sales closing. New policies and changes are due at your agent.
  • Dec 1, 2026: acreage report.
  • Sep 1, 2027: premium billed, no earlier than this.

From 2027, grazing acres need livestock you have an interest in, equal to at least 25% of the stocking rate, or no payment is due and the premium is still owed. PRF pays on the grid’s precipitation index, not on your production. How PRF works · Before you sign · PRF in Iowa

PRF questions in Warren County

When is the PRF deadline in Warren County for 2027?

The sales closing date is Dec 1, 2026, the same across the country. The acreage report is due Dec 1, 2026, and the premium is billed no earlier than Sep 1, 2027.

Which PRF grid is Warren County in?

Grids 25345, 25346, 25347, 25645, 25646, 25647, 25945, 25946, 25947 touch Warren County. A grid is a quarter degree of latitude by a quarter degree of longitude, about 17 miles north to south. Your grid follows from a point inside the acres you insure; type a ZIP or coordinates in the calculator to find it.

How much does PRF cost per acre in Warren County?

It depends on the grid, the months, the coverage level and your productivity factor. USDA’s 2027 county base value is $66.70 an acre for grazing, $331.00 for non-irrigated hay. At 90% coverage and a productivity factor of 100, grazing land is protected for $60.03 an acre, and the full premium runs $11.69 to $21.74 per $100 of protection depending on the months, on the middle of the 9 grids. USDA pays 51% to 59% of the premium. In the example above, 100 acres of grazing at 90% with half in April to May and half in June to July, you pay $3.58 an acre on grid 25645. The calculator gives the figure for your acres and months.

How often has PRF paid in Warren County?

At 90% coverage a pair of months pays when the index comes in under 90% of normal. Over 1951 to 2025, on the middle of the 9 grids, that happened in 28 to 38 of 75 years, depending on the months. Run at 2027 rates, $1 of full premium at 90% would have paid back a middle $0.87 across the county’s grids and months. That is the past record, not a forecast.
