PRF insurance in Cochise County, Arizona: 2027 grids, values and record

Pasture, Rangeland, Forage (PRF) is USDA’s rainfall-index insurance for grazing land and hay ground. USDA’s 2027 files offer it in Cochise County on 30 grids: 13579, 13580, 13581, 13582, 13583, 13584, 13879, 13880, 13881, 13882, 13883, 13884, 14179, 14180, 14181, 14182, 14183, 14184, 14479, 14480, 14481, 14482, 14483, 14484, 14779, 14780, 14781, 14782, 14783, 14784. It pays when precipitation in the grid over two months you choose comes in under a set share of normal.

As of Oct 12, the record here runs 75 years, 1951 to 2025, and on the middle value of the county’s 30 grids November to December came in under 90% of normal in 47 of them. In 2025 the middle of the grids got 10% of normal in January to February and 17% in April to May. April to May is the driest pair at 0.46 inches of normal, and July to August the wettest at 6.46. Grid 13583 had its driest year in 1956 at 41%.

USDA’s 2027 county base values, the same on every grid in the county. Protection per acre is the value times your coverage level times a productivity factor of 60 to 150: grazing at 90% and 100 is $7.02 an acre.

Example, not a recommendation: 100 acres of grazing at 90% coverage, productivity factor 100, half in April to May and half in June to July, on grid 13581, the middle of the county’s 30 grids by premium. The full premium is $182: USDA pays $93 and you pay $89, or $0.89 an acre. Run over 1951 to 2025 at 2027 rates, it would have paid in 58 of 75 years, an average of $160 a year, or $1.60 an acre. That average is under the full premium of $182; it comes out above your share only because USDA pays the rest. That is the past record, not a forecast, and it leaves out the administrative fee.

What each pair of months costs in Cochise County

At 90% coverage, across the 30 grids that touch the county: first the middle of the 30 for that pair of months (it can be a different grid in each row), then the lowest and highest. The premium is the full rate per $100 of protection; USDA pays 59% at 70%, 59% at 75%, 55% at 80%, 55% at 85%, 51% at 90%. Normal is the grid’s average precipitation for those months. A year counts, of the 75 from 1951 to 2025, when the grid’s index for those months came in under 90% of normal, which is when a 90% policy pays.

PRF dates for 2027

  • Dec 1, 2026: sales closing. New policies and changes are due at your agent.

PRF questions in Cochise County

When is the PRF deadline in Cochise County for 2027?

The sales closing date is Dec 1, 2026, the same across the country. The acreage report is due Dec 1, 2026, and the premium is billed no earlier than Sep 1, 2027.

Which PRF grid is Cochise County in?

Grids 13579, 13580, 13581, 13582, 13583, 13584, 13879, 13880, 13881, 13882, 13883, 13884, 14179, 14180, 14181, 14182, 14183, 14184, 14479, 14480, 14481, 14482, 14483, 14484, 14779, 14780, 14781, 14782, 14783, 14784 touch Cochise County. A grid is a quarter degree of latitude by a quarter degree of longitude, about 17 miles north to south. Your grid follows from a point inside the acres you insure; type a ZIP or coordinates in the calculator to find it.

How much does PRF cost per acre in Cochise County?

It depends on the grid, the months, the coverage level and your productivity factor. USDA’s 2027 county base value is $7.80 an acre for grazing. At 90% coverage and a productivity factor of 100, grazing land is protected for $7.02 an acre, and the full premium runs $9.38 to $39.23 per $100 of protection depending on the months, on the middle of the 30 grids. USDA pays 51% to 59% of the premium. In the example above, 100 acres of grazing at 90% with half in April to May and half in June to July, you pay $0.89 an acre on grid 13581. The calculator gives the figure for your acres and months.

How often has PRF paid in Cochise County?

At 90% coverage a pair of months pays when the index comes in under 90% of normal. Over 1951 to 2025, on the middle of the 30 grids, that happened in 30 to 47 of 75 years, depending on the months. Run at 2027 rates, $1 of full premium at 90% would have paid back a middle $0.88 across the county’s grids and months. That is the past record, not a forecast.
