Crop Insurance & Drought

Different USDA programs respond to drought in different ways. LFP (Livestock Forage Disaster Program) is tied directly to the USDM: sustained D2 or worse on grazing land can make you eligible. Check the current FSA schedule. PRF (Pasture/Rangeland/Forage) is a rainfall-index product and does not use USDM categories. It pays when grid precipitation falls below the historical norm. WFRP (Whole-Farm Revenue Protection) pays when year-end revenue falls below your insured revenue from any covered cause, not on USDM status. Check the FSA LFP eligibility tool for your county, and ask your crop insurance agent for specifics on your coverage.

Regional Drought Patterns

The western Corn Belt (Iowa, Nebraska, Kansas, South Dakota) and Southern Plains see drought most frequently. Sandy soils drain fast and show stress first in any region. Once D1+ conditions set in during late June growth, they rarely self-correct without meaningful rain within 10 days. Check your county on the state view and watch the CPC outlook for 1–3 month trend.

Basis Impact

Regional D2+ drought tightens local basis as elevators anticipate reduced receipts. Watch basis move in real time on the Cash Bids page. A drought rally in futures often means basis simultaneously weakens, so track both.

Common questions

How does drought affect corn and soybean yields?
Corn is most vulnerable to drought during pollination (late July) and grain fill (August). A D2 drought during those weeks is commonly cited to cut yields substantially. Soybeans are most sensitive during pod set and fill, roughly late July through early September. Even D1 moderate drought during peak demand can reduce APH yields meaningfully.
What is D2 drought and what does it mean for farmers?
D2 is Severe Drought on the US Drought Monitor scale. At D2 and above, crop and pasture losses become likely and water restrictions may begin. Sustained D2 or worse on grazing land can make you eligible for the Livestock Forage Program. Check the current FSA schedule. D3 (Extreme) and D4 (Exceptional) mean widespread losses and major economic impacts for agriculture. Revenue and rainfall-index insurance products use different mechanisms and are not triggered directly by USDM categories.
When is the US Drought Monitor updated?
The US Drought Monitor is published every Thursday by 8:30 AM ET by the National Drought Mitigation Center, USDA, and NOAA. The map reflects conditions through the previous Tuesday. AGSIST links directly to the official NDMC map with no delay.
Does drought monitor status affect crop insurance?
Different USDA programs work differently. The Livestock Forage Disaster Program (LFP) is tied directly to USDM status: sustained D2 or worse on grazing land can make you eligible. Check the current FSA schedule. The Pasture, Rangeland, and Forage (PRF) rainfall-index program does NOT use USDM categories. It pays on grid precipitation deviation from the historical norm. Whole-Farm Revenue Protection (WFRP) pays on year-end revenue shortfall from any covered cause, not USDM status. Contact your crop insurance agent for program-specific details.
Which states are most affected by drought in the Corn Belt?
Drought impacts in the Corn Belt vary year to year. The western Corn Belt (Iowa, Nebraska, Kansas, South Dakota) tends to see more drought frequency than the eastern Corn Belt.
