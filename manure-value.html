What manure is worth at fertilizer prices posted Sep 24 to Oct 12, 2026

Sigurd Lindquist, CCA, Chetek, Wisconsin

All the nutrients$30.71 per 1,000 gallons
What the crop can use the first year$18.16 worked in within 3 days

That is every pound of N, P₂O₅ and K₂O in liquid dairy manure (4 to 11% dry matter), priced at Kansas and Wisconsin co-op fertilizer prices: about $7.36 a ton, or $184.23 an acre at 6,000 gallons.

Pounds are UW Extension A2809’s typical totals for Wisconsin manure (24 lb N, 8 lb P₂O₅ and 21 lb K₂O per 1,000 gallons, Table 9.2). The crop gets 80% of the P and K the first year and 30 to 50% of the N, depending on how fast it is worked in (Table 9.1). A test of your own manure beats the book. N is priced at urea, $0.67 a lb. At anhydrous ammonia, $832 a ton (Kansas co-ops, middle of 4, posted Sep 24 to Oct 6, 2026), N is $0.51 a lb, 24% less.

Where these prices come from and the math

Each price is the middle cash price posted by co-ops on their own websites, each with the dates its co-ops posted it. A product the co-ops do not post has no price here. The same list is on fertilizer prices. Nothing here is typed in by hand.

  • Urea 46-0-0 at $615.95 a ton (Kansas and Wisconsin co-ops, middle of 5, posted Sep 24 to Oct 6, 2026) / (2,000 lb x 46% N) = $0.67 a lb of N.
  • MAP 11-52-0 at $980.00 a ton (Kansas co-ops, average of 2, posted Oct 6 to Oct 12, 2026), less its 220 lb of N at the urea price ($147), / 1040 lb of P2O5 = $0.80 a lb of P2O5.
  • Potash 0-0-60 at $470.40 a ton (Kansas co-ops, middle of 4, posted Sep 24 to Oct 6, 2026) / (2,000 lb x 60% K2O) = $0.39 a lb of K2O.

MAP 11-52-0 carries 220 lb of N a ton, worth $147 at the urea price, so that much comes off before the rest is charged to phosphate. Charging it all to P would make P₂O₅ $0.94 a lb.

Worked example: liquid dairy manure on 80 acres

Liquid dairy manure, 4 to 11% dry matter, 6,000 gallons an acre on 80 acres. Prices are the posted prices above.

  1. Per 1,000 gallons: $16.07 of N, $6.41 of P₂O₅ and $8.23 of K₂O. That is $30.71 per 1,000 gallons, about $7.36 a ton.
  2. Pounds an acre at 6,000 gallons: 144 lb N, 48 lb P₂O₅, 126 lb K₂O.
  3. An acre: $96.41 of N, $38.43 of P₂O₅, $49.39 of K₂O. That is $184.23 an acre.
  4. The 80 acres: $7,713 of N, $3,075 of P₂O₅, $3,951 of K₂O.
All the nutrients: $14,739 for the 80 acres.

What the crop can use the first year, worked in within 3 days: 60 lb N, 36 lb P₂O₅ and 102 lb K₂O an acre, worth $18.16 per 1,000 gallons, $108.98 an acre and $8,718 for the 80 acres. Getting manure tested

Book values are averages. Real manure varies with ration, bedding, how much water gets into the pit, and how the pit was agitated. Two dairies down the road from each other can differ by half. A test turns a guess into a number.

  • What to ask for: total N, ammonium N, P₂O₅, K₂O and dry matter, from a lab certified for manure in your state.
  • Liquid: agitate the pit well, then sample while hauling. Take a cup from several loads through the season, mix them in a clean pail, fill a plastic jar about three quarters full, keep it cold and ship it early in the week so it does not sit over a weekend.
  • Solid: take 10 to 20 handfuls from different spots in the pile or from different loads, mix, and send a gallon bag’s worth.
  • Cost: commonly $25 to $60 a sample. Test every year for a few years and you learn your own manure.

Rules when manure leaves the farm

If you sell or give manure away, it is worth what all the nutrients in it would cost as fertilizer. A test of the manure that is going gives those pounds. Solid usually goes per ton at the pile, liquid per 1,000 gallons applied.

This is not legal advice, and the rules change by state. A few that come up often:

  • Large CAFOs. Under the federal CAFO permit rule, a permitted large CAFO that gives manure to someone else has to give the receiver its most recent manure analysis and keep a record of the date, the receiver’s name and address, and about how much went, for five years.
  • Nutrient management plans. Many states require a nutrient management plan for permitted livestock farms, and some require the fields that receive the manure to be in the plan. A buyer’s fields may need to be added before the first load.
  • Setbacks and timing. States set distances from wells, streams, lakes and sinkholes, and limits on spreading on frozen or snow-covered ground.
  • Commercial applicators. Some states require a license or certification to haul or spread manure for someone else.

Your county land conservation office or state agriculture department can tell you what applies to your farm.

Questions

How much is dairy manure worth per 1,000 gallons?

At Kansas and Wisconsin co-op fertilizer prices posted Sep 24 to Oct 12, 2026, all the N, P2O5 and K2O in 1,000 gallons of liquid dairy manure at 4 to 11% dry matter is worth about $30.71. That is 24 lb of N, 8 lb of P2O5 and 21 lb of K2O, the typical totals in UW Extension A2809 (Table 9.2). The part a crop can use the first year is worth about $18.16 if it is worked into the soil within 3 days, $19.50 injected or worked in within an hour, and $16.15 left on top longer. A lab test of your own manure beats the book.

How much is manure worth per ton?

At Kansas and Wisconsin co-op fertilizer prices posted Sep 24 to Oct 12, 2026, all the N, P2O5 and K2O in a ton of solid dairy manure over 20% dry matter is worth about $11.97, beef manure about $19.81, hog manure about $26.38, and chicken litter about $80.97, by UW Extension A2809's typical totals. Liquid dairy manure at 4 to 11% dry matter works out to about $7.36 a ton, since 1,000 gallons weighs a little over 4 tons.

Is this the value of all the nutrients in the manure?

Yes. The headline numbers price every pound of N, P2O5 and K2O in the manure, using UW Extension A2809's typical totals for Wisconsin manure tested from 1998 to 2012 (Table 9.2). The crop does not get it all the first year. UW counts 80% of the P2O5 and K2O and 25 to 65% of the N, depending on the manure and how fast it is worked in (Table 9.1), so the first-year value is shown beside each total. Your own manure test beats the book: type it into the calculator under Use my manure test.

What is the manure on a whole field worth?

Liquid dairy manure at 4 to 11% dry matter, 6,000 gallons an acre, puts on 144 lb of N, 48 lb of P2O5 and 126 lb of K2O an acre by UW's typical totals. At Kansas and Wisconsin co-op prices posted Sep 24 to Oct 12, 2026 that is $96.41 of N, $38.43 of P2O5 and $49.39 of K2O, $184.23 an acre, or $14,739 for 80 acres. Worked in within 3 days, the part a crop can use the first year is worth $108.98 an acre, $8,718 for the 80 acres.

Why is phosphorus priced with the nitrogen taken out?

Phosphate here is priced from MAP 11-52-0, which carries 220 lb of N in every ton along with its P2O5. Charging the whole price to phosphorus makes P2O5 look about $0.94 a pound. Crediting the N at the urea price, about $147 a ton, puts P2O5 at about $0.80 a pound. MAP is used when co-ops or USDA price it, DAP when they do not.

How do I get manure tested and what does it cost?

Use a lab certified for manure analysis in your state and ask for total N, ammonium N, P2O5, K2O and dry matter. Sample during hauling, after the pit is well agitated: take a cup from several loads, mix them in a clean pail, fill a plastic jar about three quarters full, keep it cold and ship it early in the week. For solid manure, take 10 to 20 handfuls from different spots or loads and mix. Tests commonly run $25 to $60 a sample. Test every year for a few years to learn your own manure.
