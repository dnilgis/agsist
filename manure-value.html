What manure is worth at today’s fertilizer prices

Sigurd Lindquist, Certified Crop Adviser (CCA), Chetek, Wisconsin

$18.75 per 1,000 gallons

That is the fertilizer in liquid dairy manure (4 to 11% dry matter, worked in within 3 days), priced at Midwest dealer quotes from Sep 1, 2026. It comes to about $4.50 a ton, or $112.50 an acre at 6,000 gallons.

This is the book value: what the same N, P and K would cost as urea, MAP and potash. If a neighbor wants to buy some, he should pay less than this. Selling to a neighbor explains why and how much.

Selling manure to a neighbor

A neighbor asks what you want for some manure. The book value above is a ceiling, not a price. Here is what brings it down.

He only saves on what his soil test calls for

Manure replaces fertilizer he would otherwise buy. If his soil tests high in P and K, the P and K in your manure save him nothing this year. They build soil test levels, which is worth something later but not cash now. The N is a credit against the N he would have bought for corn. Ask to see his soil test before you settle on a number.

Somebody has to haul and spread it

Liquid manure is heavy and the nutrients in it are thin. Iowa State University custom rate surveys from 2022 to 2024 put custom injection of liquid manure at roughly $13 to $17 per 1,000 gallons, close to the whole book value of dairy manure. Who pays for hauling and spreading moves the price more than anything else. If you have to empty the pit anyway, you would pay to spread it on your own ground too, so the only extra cost is the distance.

What your own fields give up

Every load you sell is fertilizer your own fields do not get. If your ground could use the N, P or K, that value is the least you should take. If your fields already test high and you have more manure than acres, the floor is close to zero, and getting it hauled off is worth something to you.

How deals are usually priced

  • Per ton at the pile. Common for solid manure and poultry litter. The buyer loads, hauls and spreads.
  • Per 1,000 gallons applied. Common for liquid. The seller or his custom hauler injects it, and the price covers the nutrients, or the nutrients plus the application.
  • A share of the nutrient value. Both sides agree on a manure test and today’s fertilizer prices, figure the value of what the buyer needs, and split it. Writing that down ahead of time saves an argument later.
  • Free for the hauling. When the seller has too much manure for his acres, he often gives it away to anyone who will take it, or pays to have it spread.

Rules that can apply

This is not legal advice, and the rules change by state. A few that come up often:

  • Large CAFOs. Under the federal CAFO permit rule, a permitted large CAFO that gives manure to someone else has to give the receiver its most recent manure analysis and keep a record of the date, the receiver’s name and address, and about how much went, for five years.
  • Nutrient management plans. Many states require a nutrient management plan for permitted livestock farms, and some require the fields that receive the manure to be in the plan. A buyer’s fields may need to be added before the first load.
  • Setbacks and timing. States set distances from wells, streams, lakes and sinkholes, and limits on spreading on frozen or snow-covered ground.
  • Commercial applicators. Some states require a license or certification to haul or spread manure for someone else.

Your county land conservation office or state agriculture department can tell you what applies to your farm.

Worked example: a dairy neighbor sale

Liquid dairy manure, 4 to 11% dry matter, worked in within 3 days, 6,000 gallons an acre on 80 acres. The buyer’s soil test calls for N and P but his K tests high. The seller’s own ground tests high in P and K, so at home the manure would only have saved him N. Prices are the dealer quotes above.

  1. Pounds an acre: 60 lb N, 36 lb P₂O₅, 102 lb K₂O.
  2. Book value: $38.48 of N, $34.92 of P₂O₅, $39.10 of K₂O. That is $112.50 an acre, or $18.75 per 1,000 gallons.
  3. Worth to this buyer: he needs no K, so it saves him $73.40 an acre, $12.23 per 1,000 gallons.
  4. If the buyer pays to haul it: at $15 per 1,000 gallons, application costs $90.00 an acre. That leaves -$2.77 per 1,000 gallons for the manure, so he cannot pay anything for it and come out ahead.
  5. If the seller hauls and spreads, as he would to empty his pit anyway: the most the buyer can pay is $12.23 per 1,000 gallons. The least the seller should take is the N his own fields would have used, $6.41.
Fair range: $6.41 to $12.23 per 1,000 gallons applied, seller spreads.

That is $38 to $73 an acre, or $3,078 to $5,872 for the 80 acres. Splitting the difference is a common place to land. With a manure test in hand, redo it with the real pounds.

Getting manure tested

Book values are averages. Real manure varies with ration, bedding, how much water gets into the pit, and how the pit was agitated. Two dairies down the road from each other can differ by half. A test turns a guess into a number both sides can agree on.

  • What to ask for: total N, ammonium N, P₂O₅, K₂O and dry matter, from a lab certified for manure in your state.
  • Liquid: agitate the pit well, then sample while hauling. Take a cup from several loads through the season, mix them in a clean pail, fill a plastic jar about three quarters full, keep it cold and ship it early in the week so it does not sit over a weekend.
  • Solid: take 10 to 20 handfuls from different spots in the pile or from different loads, mix, and send a gallon bag’s worth.
  • Cost: commonly $25 to $60 a sample. Test every year for a few years and you learn your own manure.

Questions

How much is dairy manure worth per 1,000 gallons?

At Midwest dealer prices from Sep 1, 2026, liquid dairy manure at 4 to 11% dry matter, worked into the soil within 3 days, carries about $18.75 of fertilizer per 1,000 gallons: 10 lb of first-year N, 6 lb of P2O5 and 17 lb of K2O. Injected or worked in within an hour it is about $20.03. Left on top for more than 3 days it is about $16.83. These are book values from UW Extension A2809. A lab test of your own manure is better.

How much is manure worth per ton?

At Sep 1, 2026 dealer prices, solid dairy manure over 20% dry matter is worth about $7.13 a ton in fertilizer, beef manure about $12.22, hog manure about $18.54, and chicken litter about $61.24, worked in within 3 days. Liquid dairy manure works out to about $4.50 a ton, since 1,000 gallons weighs a little over 4 tons.

What should I charge a neighbor for manure?

Less than the book value. The book value is what the N, P and K would cost to buy, so it is the most a buyer could pay, and only if he needs all three. He saves nothing on P or K his soil test says he does not need, and someone has to pay to haul and spread it. A fair price sits between what the manure would have saved on your own fields and what it saves him after hauling. Liquid manure is usually priced per 1,000 gallons applied, solid manure per ton at the pile or spread.

Who pays for hauling and spreading manure?

Either side can, and it changes the price more than anything else. Custom injection of liquid manure has run roughly $13 to $17 per 1,000 gallons in Iowa State University custom rate surveys, close to the whole book value of dairy manure. When the seller has to empty the pit anyway, he often hauls and spreads and the buyer pays for the nutrients. When the buyer pays to haul, there is often little left to pay for the manure itself.

Why is phosphorus priced from MAP with the nitrogen taken out?

MAP 12-40-0-10S carries 240 lb of N in every ton along with 800 lb of P2O5. Charging the whole price to phosphorus makes P look about $1.16 a pound. Crediting the N at the urea price, about $154 a ton, puts P2O5 at about $0.97 a pound. The sulfur is not credited, so this still runs a little high.

How do I get manure tested and what does it cost?

Use a lab certified for manure analysis in your state and ask for total N, ammonium N, P2O5, K2O and dry matter. Sample during hauling, after the pit is well agitated: take a cup from several loads, mix them in a clean pail, fill a plastic jar about three quarters full, keep it cold and ship it early in the week. For solid manure, take 10 to 20 handfuls from different spots or loads and mix. Tests commonly run $25 to $60 a sample. Test every year for a few years to learn your own manure.
