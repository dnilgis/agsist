Sigurd Lindquist, CCA, Chetek, Wisconsin · Checked Oct 16, 2026

When to plant

Corn
About Apr 25 to May 10 in southern and central WI and MN, once 2-inch soil holds 50°F. After May 20, about 1 point of yield a day, more in June (UMN trials).
Soybeans
As soon as the field is fit and soil is 50°F. In southern Wisconsin each week past about Apr 10 costs about 2.1 bu/a (UW-Madison).
Deadline
Insurance final planting dates in every WI and MN county: corn May 25 or 31, soybeans Jun 10 or 15 (USDA RMA, 2026).

How the numbers are made, and sources
  • Soil temperature: Open-Meteo forecast-model soil temperature at 6 cm (~2 in), hourly. The reading is the mean of the last 72 hours; the forecast line is each coming day’s mean. A model, not a probe. Wisconsin growers can compare with the measured soil temperatures on UW’s Wisconet stations.
  • Station frost dates and heat units: NOAA NCEI U.S. Climate Normals, 1991-2020: freeze probability dates (32°F and 28°F at the 10, 50 and 90% levels) and daily growing degree days (base 50°F, cap 86°F), nearest station within 75 miles.
  • Final planting dates: USDA RMA Revenue Protection final planting dates, 2026 crop year.
  • Corn yield by planting date: UMN Extension’s table of two planting date studies, Lamberton 1988-2003 and Lamberton, Morris and Waseca 2009-2011, % of maximum yield. Each row shows both as a range.
  • Soybean yield by planting date: UW-Madison’s southern Wisconsin figure, about 2.1 bu/a lost per week of delay past roughly Apr 10 (Conley, 2026), drawn as a straight line; the June rows extend it. Not a rule for every field or for the north.
  • GDU ratings: corn uses a reference line of 22 GDUs per day of RM, set on K-State’s 110-day figure (about 2,670; Purdue’s 112-day figure, about 2,700, sits 14 under it); soybeans use NDSU’s soybean GDD model for MG 1.1 and earlier only. Your seed company’s rating beats both.
  • Zone averages: approximate freeze dates by zone that run early (Madison median last spring freeze is about May 5, UW-Madison SSEC, 1940-2024) and a Wisconsin monthly GDU table with no April rate. Shown only as a fallback.

Common questions

When should I plant corn in Wisconsin?
In southern and central Wisconsin and Minnesota, about April 25 to May 10, once soil at 2 inches holds 50°F. University of Minnesota planting date studies found April 21 to May 6 within 1% of the maximum yield at Lamberton (1988-2003) and April 25 to May 10 best on average at Lamberton, Morris and Waseca (2009-2011). Northern areas plant later as soils allow. Enter your ZIP on this page for soil temperature now and your nearest station's frost odds.
When should I plant soybeans in Wisconsin?
As soon as the field is fit and soil is near 50°F, often before corn. UW-Madison soybean specialist Shawn Conley puts the loss in southern Wisconsin at about 2.1 bushels per acre per week of delay past roughly April 10, about 0.3 a day. Farther north the losses start later. Early planting can raise SDS pressure; manage it with variety choice and seed treatment.
What soil temperature do I need to plant corn?
Corn should be planted when soil temperature at 2" depth reaches a consistent 50°F or above for at least 3 consecutive days with a warming trend. Planting into cold (below 50°F) soils risks imbibitional chilling injury, uneven emergence, and increased seedling disease pressure.
How much yield do I lose for late corn planting?
In the University of Minnesota studies, corn planted May 20 made 92 to 95% of the maximum yield, May 30 made 82 to 89% and June 9 made 69 to 79% (Lamberton 1988-2003; Lamberton, Morris and Waseca 2009-2011). That is roughly 1 point a day after May 20 and faster into June. On a 200 bu field a May 30 planting gives up about 22 to 36 bu. Final planting dates for crop insurance are May 25 or May 31 in every Wisconsin and Minnesota county.
What is the last frost date in Wisconsin?
At Madison the median last spring freeze is about May 5 (UW-Madison SSEC, 1940-2024 records). The zone table on this page runs earlier than that: Southern WI 50% April 22, 90% May 5; Central WI 50% May 2, 90% May 15; Northern WI 50% May 15, 90% May 28. Treat those dates as approximate and check your nearest station in the NOAA NCEI 1991-2020 climate normals before planting. Actual dates vary year to year.
Does planting corn too early mess up my crop insurance?
It can. Minnesota Extension says if you plant before RMA's earliest allowable planting date you lose replant coverage, even when the replant is for something other than frost or poor emergence. For Minnesota that date is April 11. Ask your agent for your county's date.
Should I switch to an earlier corn hybrid if I'm planting late?
Minnesota Extension says once corn planting slips past the third week of May, going from a late hybrid to an earlier one cuts the risk of a fall freeze before the crop matures. Leave your seeding rate alone: Illinois and Minnesota research found the best final stand does not change with planting date.
If my soybeans go in mid-May, do I change maturity group or seeding rate?
UW-Madison's Shawn Conley says no. Keep your maturity group, variety and seeding rate. The one change he suggests is narrowing rows to 15 or 20 inches if you can.
