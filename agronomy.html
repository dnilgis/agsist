Fertility: what to put on, and what it costs

  • Corn nitrogen rate

    How much N for corn at posted fertilizer and corn prices, by soil and last crop, with legume credits.

    Source: UW Extension A2809 (Wisconsin), 2012 printing, Table 6.1

  • Nitrogen credits

    What manure, alfalfa and other legumes give back, so you do not pay for the same pound twice. Soybeans before corn add nothing here, because the rate table already counts them.

    Source: UW Extension A2809 (Wisconsin), Chapter 9, 2012 printing

  • P and K by soil test

    Enter your soil test, get the phosphate and potash rate, for corn, soybeans, silage and alfalfa.

    Source: UW Extension A2809 (Wisconsin), Tables 7.1, 7.2 and 7.4

  • Lime and micronutrients

    Soil pH targets, a lime requirement calculator, and which crops need zinc, manganese, boron or sulfur.

    Source: UW Extension A2809 (Wisconsin), Chapter 5 (lime) and Chapter 8 (micronutrients)

  • Fertilizer prices

    Posted co-op cash prices per ton and per pound of nutrient, and a fertility bill for your own program.

    Source: Co-op prices, each dated on its row

  • Nutrient removal

    P and K hauled off in grain, stalks, straw, silage and hay, and what it costs to put back.

    Source: UW Extension A2809, Table 4.2; Iowa State; Purdue

  • Manure value

    What the N, P and K in your manure are worth, by type, with a calculator.

    Source: UW Extension A2809, Tables 9.1 to 9.3

  • Urea nitrogen loss

    How much surface urea can be lost to the air today, and what NBPT does and does not do.

    Source: Montana State Extension, EB0209

  • Fall anhydrous timing

    Whether the soil is cold enough yet, by county, and what each state's extension says.

    Source: Iowa State, Illinois Agronomy Handbook and other state guides, named on the page

In season and harvest

  • Field Scout

    Draw a field and see its soils, ten years of crops, and satellite greenness. It points, it does not diagnose.

    Source: USDA soil survey and Cropland Data Layer; Sentinel-2 imagery

  • Yield estimator

    Pod counts for soybeans and ear counts for corn, turned into bushels an acre.

    Source: Purdue, Iowa State, Ohio State and Penn State Extension methods

  • Combine loss

    Count beans or kernels in a frame behind the combine and get bushels an acre lost.

    Source: Nebraska CropWatch; Iowa State; NDSU

  • Harvest now or wait

    The drying bill today against the bill after a week or two of drydown.

    Source: NDSU (Hellevang); Iowa State; Purdue

  • Aeration fans

    When to run fans to dry, cool or hold grain, hour by hour at your ZIP code.

    Source: Minnesota, Iowa State and NDSU Extension

  • Shrink and moisture discount

    What wet grain nets at the elevator after shrink and drying charges.

    Source: Iowa State; Nebraska CropWatch; Extension FSA1078

How we choose sources

What counts: university extension guides and replicated university trials, each with its date. A product label counts for what the label says, such as a guaranteed analysis.

What does not: company trials, testimonials, dealer claims, and tests that were not replicated.

Products: none is sold here. A product name shows up only where a university table uses it to find a group or a rating, never as a recommendation. We make no claim for biologicals or yield boosters without independent replicated trials.

Your state: the fertility tables are Wisconsin's (UW Extension A2809) unless a table names another source. Other states set their own rates; use your state's guide outside Wisconsin.

Questions

Where do the numbers on these pages come from?

Every rate and threshold comes from university extension guides and replicated university trials. Each page names its source where the number shows, with a date. Prices, forecasts and satellite data are named on their cards. Where trials disagree or do not exist, the page says so.

Does AGSIST sell or recommend any product?

No. Nothing here is for sale. A product name appears only when a university table uses it as a lookup key, such as a fungicide rating or a herbicide group. It is never a recommendation.

Are the rates for my state?

Not always. The fertility tables are Wisconsin's (UW Extension A2809) unless a table names another source. Other states set their own rates, so use your state's guide outside Wisconsin.
