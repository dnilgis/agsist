Show the math

2026. The 2026 crop is mostly harvested, so each scenario uses a normal Clearwater County crop (FSA’s 2026 benchmark yield) and only the price moves. Prices start from USDA’s projected 2026/27 season-average prices (USDA WASDE, Oct 9, 2026: wheat $6.30, barley $5.70, oats $3.35; FSA table of Sep 11, 2026: large chickpeas $0.2100, lentils $0.1700, small chickpeas $0.1670, canola $0.2450, dry peas $0.1120). Around that price we ran each past year with a final USDA price: how far that year’s final season-average price ended from October futures, for corn and soybeans. Wheat, barley, large chickpeas, lentils, oats, small chickpeas, canola, dry peas have no October futures history of their own, so they borrow corn’s October swings (wheat its own price changes, scaled to corn’s size) and stop at Leans.

2027. Wheat, barley, lentils, oats, canola, dry peas: no 2027 price outlook we can check against past years, so no 2027 answer. Large chickpeas, small chickpeas wait on USDA’s closing 2025 price, which sets the 2027 reference price.

Typical farm. FSA’s county average PLC yield (wheat 52.8 bu; barley 47.1 bu; large chickpeas 875.8 lb; lentils 676.6 lb; oats 51.6 bu; small chickpeas 1,087.7 lb; canola 936.0 lb; dry peas 1,393.0 lb) and FSA’s official benchmark. “Lower” and “Higher” mean 15% under or over the county average. Payments are per base acre and count FSA’s 85% payment acres, before sequestration and payment limits.

How we call it. Clear pick when one program averages at least $2.00 more per base acre, the gap is more than t times its standard error (2.26 for 10 years, 2.23 for 11), and it paid more in at least 3 of the years. Leans when the gap is at least $3.00 and one standard error, and it paid more in at least 3 years. Close otherwise. When both round to $0: neither expected to pay.
