Wheat 2026 in Latah County: leans PLC

All practices, 2026: Leans PLC: expected about $4.56 more per base acre, but the gap is within the normal swing; either is a defensible choice (expected $35.89 PLC vs $31.33 ARC-CO per base acre; PLC paid more in 4 of 10 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 2).

PLC pays on any season-average price below $6.35, up to $156.26 per base acre at the $3.72 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. ARC-CO is capped at $57.95 per base acre but also pays when the county’s yield is short; it paid something in 6 of the 10 past-year scenarios. With prices centered at $6.30, PLC is expected to pay $35.89 and ARC-CO $31.33 per base acre.

2027: Leans PLC: expected about $4.56 more per base acre, but the gap is within the normal swing; either is a defensible choice (expected $35.89 PLC vs $31.33 ARC-CO per base acre; PLC paid more in 4 of 10 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 2). Should I pick ARC or PLC for wheat in Latah County for 2026?

For a typical farm (all practices): Leans PLC: expected about $4.56 more per base acre, but the gap is within the normal swing; either is a defensible choice (expected $35.89 PLC vs $31.33 ARC-CO per base acre; PLC paid more in 4 of 10 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 2). PLC pays on any season-average price below $6.35, up to $156.26 per base acre at the $3.72 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. ARC-CO is capped at $57.95 per base acre but also pays when the county’s yield is short; it paid something in 6 of the 10 past-year scenarios. With prices centered at $6.30, PLC is expected to pay $35.89 and ARC-CO $31.33 per base acre. Run your own PLC yield and base acres; the answer can change.
