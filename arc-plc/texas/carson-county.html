Wheat · irrigated

ARC-COLeans

ARC-CO leads across the 11 price years we ran, but that swings from year to year. PLC did not come out ahead in any of them. Wheat has no October futures history of its own on file, so its price swings are borrowed from corn’s, and the expected dollars would rest on them. We do not print them. So it stops at Leans.

PLC only pays if wheat averages under $6.35 a bushel for 2026.

Figured with the county’s all-practice PLC yield; an irrigated farm’s own is often higher.

Non-irrigated ground: either one.

2027: no 2027 price yet

ARC-CO pays here for 2026 if the county yield comes in under 52 bu at USDA’s price outlook, $6.30.

90% of the 52.3 bu benchmark × the $6.98 benchmark price, divided by $6.30.

At $5.80, 50 cents under USDA’s $6.30 for 2026: PLC $14.82, ARC-CO $21.43 per base acre (typical farm).

2025, our estimate, not a USDA figure: higher of PLC $34.79 or ARC-CO $0 per base acre, typical farm, before 5.7% sequestration.

Questions about Carson County

Should I pick ARC or PLC for wheat in Carson County for 2026?

For a typical farm (irrigated): Leans ARC-CO: PLC did not pay more in any of the 11 years, but the gap is within the normal swing (PLC paid more in 0 of 11 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 6; no dollar figure, because the price swings are borrowed from corn’s). Capped at Leans: this crop borrows corn’s price swings. PLC pays on any season-average price below $6.35, up to $70.87 per base acre at the $3.72 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. ARC-CO is capped at $37.24 per base acre but also pays when the county’s yield is short; it paid something in 6 of the 11 past-year scenarios. With prices centered at $6.30, the expected payments are not printed. Wheat has no October futures history of its own on file, so its price swings are borrowed from corn’s, and the expected dollars would rest on them. We do not print them. Run your own PLC yield and base acres; the answer can change.
