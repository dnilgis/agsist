Show the math

2026. The 2026 crop is mostly harvested, so each scenario uses a normal Jones County crop (FSA’s 2026 benchmark yield) and only the price moves. Prices start from USDA’s projected 2026/27 season-average prices (FSA table of Sep 11, 2026: seed cotton $0.4114, peanuts $0.2400, sunflower seed $0.2470, sesame seed $0.3800, safflower $0.2150; USDA WASDE, Oct 9, 2026: wheat $6.30, sorghum $4.50, oats $3.35, barley $5.70, corn $4.70, soybeans $12.00). Around that price we ran each past year with a final USDA price: how far that year’s final season-average price ended from October futures, for corn and soybeans. Seed cotton, wheat, sorghum, oats, barley, peanuts, sunflower seed, sesame seed, safflower have no October futures history of their own, so they borrow corn’s October swings (wheat its own price changes, scaled to corn’s size) and stop at Leans.

Two price starts. Corn: USDA $4.70; futures-based $4.97 (Dec '26 October average so far, $5.01 over 5 of 22 trading days, × 0.991, the 2015 to 2025 average of final price / October futures). Soybeans: USDA $12.00; futures-based $12.84 (Nov '26 October average so far, $12.89 over 5 of 22 trading days, × 0.996, the 2015 to 2025 average of final price / October futures). Here, the typical farm’s answer at that start: neither expected to pay (the same). Where the two disagree on which program leads, or on a clear pick, a clear pick drops to Leans.

2027. Corn and soybeans start from futures: corn $4.91 (Dec 2027 corn futures $5.12 (Oct 9, 2026) × 0.959, the 2011 to 2025 average of final MYA / February futures); soybeans $12.17 (Nov 2027 soybean futures $12.45 (Oct 9, 2026) × 0.978, the 2011 to 2025 average of final MYA / February futures). Each scenario year also moves the county yield the way it moved that year. Wheat, oats, barley, peanuts: no 2027 price outlook we can check against past years, so no 2027 answer. Seed cotton, sorghum, sunflower seed, sesame seed, safflower wait on USDA’s closing 2025 price, which sets the 2027 reference price.

Typical farm. FSA’s county average PLC yield (seed cotton 1,088.0 lb; wheat 22.5 bu; sorghum 34.8 bu; oats 28.1 bu; barley 26.8 bu; peanuts 2,865.8 lb; corn 121.0 bu; sunflower seed 300.0 lb; sesame seed 234.0 lb; safflower 520.0 lb; soybeans 18.0 bu) and FSA’s official benchmark. “Lower” and “Higher” mean 15% under or over the county average. Payments are per base acre and count FSA’s 85% payment acres, before sequestration and payment limits.

How we call it. Clear pick when one program averages at least $2.00 more per base acre, the gap is more than t times its standard error (2.26 for 10 years, 2.23 for 11), and it paid more in at least 3 of the years. Leans when the gap is at least $3.00 and one standard error, and it paid more in at least 3 years. Close otherwise. When both round to $0: neither expected to pay.

Seed cotton 2026 in Jones County: Leans PLC

FSA’s seed cotton price is its own weighted average of the upland cotton lint price and the cottonseed price. We use FSA’s seed cotton series as published and never build it from the lint price. All seed cotton counties.

Non-irrigated: no typical-farm verdict. FSA’s average PLC yield for the county (1,088.0 lb) covers all practices and is above this benchmark (535.66 lb), so it does not describe a typical farm here. Run your own PLC yield below.

Irrigated, 2026: Leans PLC: expected about $14.86 more per base acre; ARC-CO did not pay more in any of the 11 years, but the gap is within the normal swing (expected $16.17 PLC vs $1.32 ARC-CO per base acre; PLC paid more in 8 of 11 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 0). Capped at Leans: this crop borrows corn’s price swings.

PLC pays on any season-average price below $0.4200, up to $110.98 per base acre at the $0.3000 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. ARC-CO is capped at $73.14 per base acre but also pays when the county’s yield is short; it paid something in 2 of the 11 past-year scenarios. With prices centered at $0.4114, PLC is expected to pay $16.17 and ARC-CO $1.32 per base acre.
