North Carolina ARC-CO benchmark yields for 2026, by county

Data updated Nov 24, 2026

Find your county’s short answer

FSA’s official program year 2026 ARC-CO benchmark yields for 100 North Carolina counties, by crop and by practice where FSA splits irrigated and non-irrigated. FSA has not posted 2027 benchmarks; the 2027 benchmark shifts the window one year.

  • Dec 112026 signup ends Dec 11, 2026.
  • Nov 2 to Mar 152027 signup is open through Mar 15, 2027.
  • Skip itDo nothing for 2026 and that farm gets no 2026 payment.
  • New baseA 2025 law added 30 million base acres nationwide. More acres qualified than that cap, so FSA cut every new allocation by 3.69%.
  • SignProducers with a share in the crop sign. FSA says a landowner with no share interest, such as a cash-rent landlord, cannot enroll.
  • BringBring the farm’s FSA-156EZ. It lists the base acres and PLC yields.

Dates: USDA Farm Service Agency ARC/PLC page (read Nov 12, 2026) and its Sep 15, 2026 release.

Prices that apply in every county. 2026 effective reference price: corn $4.42, soybeans $10.71, wheat $6.35. 2026 ARC-CO benchmark price: corn $5.03, soybeans $12.17, wheat $6.98. When the county yield comes in at its benchmark, ARC-CO pays when the season-average price ends at $4.52 or lower for corn, $10.95 for soybeans and $6.28 for wheat.

Other crops with FSA benchmarks here: Sorghum · Barley · Oats · Peanuts · Seed cotton · Dry peas · Canola · Flaxseed · Sunflower seed · Rapeseed · Safflower · Sesame seed.

What the check is, by county

Per base acre for a typical farm (FSA’s county average PLC yield), the 85% factor in, before sequestration, the same figures as each county page. 2025 (paid on or after Oct 1, 2026, as FSA processes them): ARC-CO is the FSA posted rate, PLC is from FSA’s posted prices; the law pays the higher one. 2026: our estimate of the expected payment under each program; only the program the farm enrolls in pays. n/a: the county page has no figure. 5.7% is the sequestration rate reported for these payments; the rate is set each fiscal year and can change; source: Farm CPA Report, sequestration and the 2025 limit.

PLC vs ARC-CO break-even at the benchmark yield

When the county yield comes in at its benchmark, which program pays more depends on the season-average price and on your PLC yield as a share of the county’s official benchmark. Expressed that way, the break-even is the same in every North Carolina county. 2026 prices, corn:

PLC yield, % of your official benchmarkWhich pays more, by season-average price
60%PLC pays more at $3.41 or lower. ARC-CO pays more from $3.42 to $4.52. Neither pays at $4.53 or higher.
70%PLC pays more at $3.55 or lower. ARC-CO pays more from $3.56 to $4.52. Neither pays at $4.53 or higher.
80%PLC pays more at $3.66 or lower. ARC-CO pays more from $3.67 to $4.52. Neither pays at $4.53 or higher.
90%PLC pays more at $3.74 or lower. ARC-CO pays more from $3.75 to $4.52. Neither pays at $4.53 or higher.
100%PLC pays more at $3.81 or lower. ARC-CO pays more from $3.82 to $4.52. Neither pays at $4.53 or higher.
