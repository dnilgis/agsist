2026. The 2026 crop is mostly harvested, so each scenario uses a normal Bowman County crop (FSA’s 2026 benchmark yield) and only the price moves. Prices start from USDA’s projected 2026/27 season-average prices (USDA WASDE, Oct 9, 2026: wheat $6.30, barley $5.70, corn $4.70, oats $3.35, sorghum $4.50, soybeans $12.00; FSA table of Sep 11, 2026: sunflower seed $0.2470, dry peas $0.1120, canola $0.2450, safflower $0.2150, flaxseed $13.00, lentils $0.1700, small chickpeas $0.1670, mustard seed $0.3170). Around that price we ran each past year with a final USDA price: how far that year’s final season-average price ended from October futures, for corn and soybeans. Wheat, barley, oats, sunflower seed, dry peas, canola, safflower, flaxseed, lentils, sorghum, small chickpeas, mustard seed have no October futures history of their own, so they borrow corn’s October swings (wheat its own price changes, scaled to corn’s size) and stop at Leans.

Two price starts. Corn: USDA $4.70; futures-based $4.97 (Dec '26 October average so far, $5.01 over 5 of 22 trading days, × 0.991, the 2015 to 2025 average of final price / October futures). Soybeans: USDA $12.00; futures-based $12.84 (Nov '26 October average so far, $12.89 over 5 of 22 trading days, × 0.996, the 2015 to 2025 average of final price / October futures). Where the two disagree on which program leads, or on a clear pick, a clear pick drops to Leans.

How we call it. Clear pick when one program averages at least $2.00 more per base acre, the gap is more than t times its standard error (2.26 for 10 years, 2.23 for 11), and it paid more in at least 3 of the years. Leans when the gap is at least $3.00 and one standard error, and it paid more in at least 3 years. Close otherwise. When both round to $0: neither expected to pay.
