2026. The 2026 crop is mostly harvested, so each scenario uses a normal Stanley County crop (FSA’s 2026 benchmark yield) and only the price moves. Prices start from USDA’s projected 2026/27 season-average prices (USDA WASDE, Oct 9, 2026: wheat $6.30, sorghum $4.50, corn $4.70, oats $3.35, barley $5.70, soybeans $12.00; FSA table of Sep 11, 2026: sunflower seed $0.2470, safflower $0.2150, dry peas $0.1120, small chickpeas $0.1670, flaxseed $13.00). Around that price we ran each past year with a final USDA price: how far that year’s final season-average price ended from October futures, for corn and soybeans. Wheat, sorghum, sunflower seed, oats, safflower, barley, dry peas, small chickpeas, flaxseed have no October futures history of their own, so they borrow corn’s October swings (wheat its own price changes, scaled to corn’s size) and stop at Leans.

2027. Corn and soybeans start from futures: corn $4.91 (Dec 2027 corn futures $5.12 (Oct 9, 2026) × 0.959, the 2011 to 2025 average of final MYA / February futures); soybeans $12.17 (Nov 2027 soybean futures $12.45 (Oct 9, 2026) × 0.978, the 2011 to 2025 average of final MYA / February futures). Typical farm. FSA’s county average PLC yield (wheat 34.1 bu; sorghum 50.7 bu; sunflower seed 1,208.7 lb; corn 63.2 bu; oats 43.8 bu; safflower 832.6 lb; barley 29.1 bu; soybeans 14.7 bu; dry peas 1,790.1 lb; small chickpeas 1,531.0 lb; flaxseed 12.0 bu) and FSA’s official benchmark. “Lower” and “Higher” mean 15% under or over the county average. Payments are per base acre and count FSA’s 85% payment acres, before sequestration and payment limits.

How we call it. Clear pick when one program averages at least $2.00 more per base acre, the gap is more than t times its standard error (2.26 for 10 years, 2.23 for 11), and it paid more in at least 3 of the years. Leans when the gap is at least $3.00 and one standard error, and it paid more in at least 3 years. Close otherwise. When both round to $0: neither expected to pay.
