2026. The 2026 crop is mostly harvested, so each scenario uses a normal Lee County crop (FSA’s 2026 benchmark yield) and only the price moves. Prices start from USDA’s projected 2026/27 season-average prices (USDA WASDE, Oct 9, 2026: soybeans $12.00, wheat $6.30, long grain rice $0.1400, corn $4.70, sorghum $4.50, oats $3.35; FSA table of Sep 11, 2026: seed cotton $0.4114, medium and short grain rice $0.1500, peanuts $0.2400, sunflower seed $0.2470). Around that price we ran each past year with a final USDA price: how far that year’s final season-average price ended from October futures, for corn and soybeans. Seed cotton, wheat, long grain rice, sorghum, medium and short grain rice, peanuts, sunflower seed, oats have no October futures history of their own, so they borrow corn’s October swings (wheat its own price changes, scaled to corn’s size) and stop at Leans.

FSA’s county average PLC yield (soybeans 36.3 bu; seed cotton 2,107.4 lb; wheat 52.5 bu; long grain rice 5,602.7 lb; corn 138.4 bu; sorghum 81.5 bu; medium and short grain rice 5,283.4 lb; peanuts 4,992.4 lb; sunflower seed 666.9 lb; oats 57.1 bu) and FSA’s official benchmark. “Lower” and “Higher” mean 15% under or over the county average. How we call it. Clear pick when one program averages at least $2.00 more per base acre, the gap is more than t times its standard error (2.26 for 10 years, 2.23 for 11), and it paid more in at least 3 of the years. Leans when the gap is at least $3.00 and one standard error, and it paid more in at least 3 years. Close otherwise. When both round to $0: neither expected to pay.
