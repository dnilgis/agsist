Seed cotton · non-irrigated

PLCLeans

PLC leads across the 11 price years we ran, but that swings from year to year. ARC-CO did not come out ahead in any of them. Seed cotton has too few years of its own prices in FSA’s tables, so its price swings are corn’s, and the expected dollars would rest on them. We do not print them. So it stops at Leans.

PLC only pays if seed cotton averages under 42 cents a pound for 2026.

Irrigated ground: leans PLC. Figured with the county’s all-practice PLC yield; an irrigated farm’s own is often higher.

2027: no 2027 price yet

ARC-CO pays here for 2026 if the county yield comes in under 2,271 lb at USDA’s price outlook, $0.4147.

90% of the 2,428 lb benchmark × the $0.4311 benchmark price, divided by $0.4147.

At $0.3732, 10% under USDA’s $0.4147 for 2026: PLC $87.03, ARC-CO $30.52 per base acre (typical farm).

2025, our estimate, not a USDA figure: higher of PLC $147.28 or ARC-CO $60.41 per base acre, typical farm, before 5.7% sequestration.

Questions about Carroll County

Should I pick ARC or PLC for seed cotton in Carroll County for 2026?

For a typical farm (non-irrigated): Leans PLC: ARC-CO did not pay more in any of the 11 years, but the gap is within the normal swing (PLC paid more in 8 of 11 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 0; no dollar figure, because the price swings are borrowed from corn’s). Capped at Leans: this crop borrows corn’s price swings. PLC pays on any season-average price below $0.4200, up to $223.15 per base acre at the $0.3000 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. ARC-CO is capped at $106.76 per base acre but also pays when the county’s yield is short; it paid something in 1 of the 11 past-year scenarios. With prices centered at $0.4147, the expected payments are not printed. Seed cotton has too few years of its own prices in FSA’s tables, so its price swings are corn’s, and the expected dollars would rest on them. We do not print them. Run your own PLC yield and base acres; the answer can change.
