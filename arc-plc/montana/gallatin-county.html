Wheat 2026 in Gallatin County: leans ARC-CO

Non-irrigated: no typical-farm verdict. FSA’s average PLC yield for the county (53.4 bu) covers all practices and is above this benchmark (52.80 bu), so it does not describe a typical farm here. Run your own PLC yield below.

Irrigated, 2026: Leans ARC-CO: expected about $5.21 more per base acre, but the gap is within the normal swing; either is a defensible choice (expected $27.42 PLC vs $32.63 ARC-CO per base acre; PLC paid more in 1 of 10 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 4).

PLC pays on any season-average price below $6.35, up to $119.38 per base acre at the $3.72 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. Should I pick ARC or PLC for wheat in Gallatin County for 2026?

For a typical farm (irrigated): Leans ARC-CO: expected about $5.21 more per base acre, but the gap is within the normal swing; either is a defensible choice (expected $27.42 PLC vs $32.63 ARC-CO per base acre; PLC paid more in 1 of 10 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 4). PLC pays on any season-average price below $6.35, up to $119.38 per base acre at the $3.72 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. PLC pays on any season-average price below $4.42, up to $190.23 per base acre at the $2.42 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. ARC-CO is capped at $84.94 per base acre but also pays when the county’s yield is short; it paid something in 4 of the 10 past-year scenarios. With prices centered at $4.70, PLC is expected to pay $0.93 and ARC-CO $9.32 per base acre. Run your own PLC yield and base acres; the answer can change.
