Wheat · irrigated

ARC-COLeans

ARC-CO leads across the 11 price years we ran, but that swings from year to year. PLC did not come out ahead in any of them. Wheat has no October futures history of its own on file, so its price swings are borrowed from corn’s, and the expected dollars would rest on them. We do not print them. So it stops at Leans.

Questions about Liberty County

Should I pick ARC or PLC for wheat in Liberty County for 2026?

For a typical farm (irrigated): Leans ARC-CO: PLC did not pay more in any of the 11 years, but the gap is within the normal swing (PLC paid more in 0 of 11 past-year scenarios, ARC-CO in 6; no dollar figure, because the price swings are borrowed from corn’s). Capped at Leans: this crop borrows corn’s price swings. PLC pays on any season-average price below $6.35, up to $79.36 per base acre at the $3.72 loan rate, so it protects against a deep price drop. ARC-CO is capped at $42.69 per base acre but also pays when the county’s yield is short; it paid something in 6 of the 11 past-year scenarios. With prices centered at $6.30, the expected payments are not printed. Wheat has no October futures history of its own on file, so its price swings are borrowed from corn’s, and the expected dollars would rest on them. We do not print them.
