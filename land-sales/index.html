    How the index is built, and its limits
    • Which sales. Every Illinois transfer declaration for farm parcels that passes the same market-sale rule the Farmland Atlas uses: a deed type that normally carries a market price (no quit claims, gifts, court or sheriff deeds, trades), a whole interest, not between related parties, not exempt. Then: one parcel on the return, known acres, 20 acres or more, no "with buildings" flag, and $1,000 to $40,000 an acre. 13,503 sales from 2018 through Oct 31, 2026.
    • Why one parcel. Since about October 2025 the state's open data stopped giving acres for returns that cover several parcels, so including them would change what the index measures partway through. Every quarter uses one-parcel sales alike.
    • County mix held fixed. The index comes from a model of each sale's log price an acre with a county effect, a quarter effect and a tract-size band (20 to 40, 40 to 80, 80 to 160, 160+ acres). The quarter effects are the index. A county that sells more often in one quarter does not move it.
    • Ranges. The index is re-run 400 times on counties drawn at random with replacement; the 90% range is the middle 90% of those runs. Quarters with fewer than 100 sales are withheld.
    • Filing lag. Deeds are filed after the sale. Market sales on file for the last four quarters against the same quarter a year earlier: Jan to Mar 2026: 1,153 on file vs 1,056 a year earlier (109%); Apr to Jun 2026: 864 on file vs 868 a year earlier (100%); Jul to Sep 2026: 694 on file vs 628 a year earlier (111%); Oct to Dec 2026: 176 on file vs 1,004 a year earlier (18%). The latest quarter is always provisional, and so is any recent quarter under 85% of its year-earlier count.
    • What it is not. Not an appraisal of any farm. Quality of the ground (soil productivity, drainage, tillable share) is not in the record, so a quarter when better ground sold reads higher. Auctions, listed and private sales all count alike.
    • Other states.
      • Indiana (sales from 2022, 4,803 sales in 92 counties): held back because the typical 90% range is 15.2 points wide, more than 12; the latest settled quarter is 2026Q1; later quarters are still filling in.
      • Wisconsin (sales from 2021, 3,838 sales in 71 counties): held back because the typical 90% range is 21.1 points wide, more than 12.
    Questions about the index
    What does the farmland sale index measure?
    What Illinois farm ground has sold for, quarter by quarter, from the state's own record of every sale (the PTAX-203 transfer declarations on Illinois' open data portal). It counts arm's-length sales of bare land, 20 acres or more, one parcel per sale, and compares each county with itself, so a quarter when more of the sales came from high-priced counties does not read as a price rise. The four quarters of 2018 average 100.
    Why not just use the median sale price?
    Because the median moves with which counties happened to sell. From 2018 to 2025 the plain median of these sales went from $7,360 to $10,932 an acre (+48.5%), while the index, which holds the county mix and tract size fixed, went from 100.0 to 154.6 (+54.6%).
    How is this different from the USDA number?
    USDA NASS publishes one survey value a year for all Illinois cropland ($10,200 an acre in 2026), from what farmers and landowners report their ground is worth, sold or not. This index comes from sales that actually closed. The two are shown on the same base, side by side, and never mixed. Illinois' farm managers and appraisers (ISPFMRA) run a third, survey-based report; its numbers are not on this page.
    Why is the latest quarter marked provisional?
    Deeds are filed after the sale, so the newest months fill in late. For October to December 2026, 176 market sales are on file so far, against 1,004 for the same months a year earlier (18%). Until a quarter's count of sales catches up to the same quarter a year earlier (85% or more), and always for the latest quarter, the number is marked provisional and can change.
    How sure is the number?
    Each quarter carries a 90% range from re-running the index on counties drawn at random, with replacement, 400 times. The typical quarter's range is 10.9 points wide, so a move of a few points from one quarter to the next is mostly noise. The headline uses four quarters together for that reason; its range for the latest 12 months is 4.7% lower to 1.4% higher.
    Why are Indiana and Wisconsin not shown?
    Each state has to hold up on its own before it gets an index: enough sales every quarter, a range no wider than 12 points in the typical quarter, and recent quarters on file. Held back this run: Indiana: the typical 90% range is 15.2 points wide, more than 12; the latest settled quarter is 2026Q1; later quarters are still filling in; Wisconsin: the typical 90% range is 21.1 points wide, more than 12.
    Are buyer or seller names on this page?
    No. The public record carries names, but this page and its data file hold only counts, prices and acres. Buyer type (individual, trust or estate, company, public or other) comes from a word pattern on the buyer field, read in memory; no name is written anywhere.

    Sources and terms

    Every state

    A sale is listed when it carries a price and acres and a whole interest changed hands in a normal sale: gifts, family and related-party sales, quit claims, foreclosures and the like are left out, the same rule as Field Scout and the Farmland Atlas. Township and town are where the state places the sale; no parcel is pinned. None of this is an appraisal.

    Illinois

    A farm sale is a farm parcel on a MyDec transfer declaration. Left out: gifts, family and related-party sales and anything less than the whole interest.

    Wisconsin

    A farm sale is a transfer return for land in the farm or forest classes. Left out: gifts, family and related-party sales and anything less than the whole interest.

    Indiana

    A farm sale is a sale of land the county classes as farm. Left out: gifts, family and related-party sales and anything less than the whole interest. The date is the sale date on the disclosure form, and the town is the parcel's mailing town, placed at the middle of its ZIP code.

    Vermont

    A farm sale is a return where the land's use before or after the sale is an operating farm or timberland, or open land enrolled in current use. Left out: a buyer related to the seller, a return that claims a transfer tax exemption, transfers without pay, by divorce or by foreclosure, and part interests such as a share, a life estate, a lease or an easement.

    New Jersey

    A farm sale is a deed whose lot is classed farm, either the farmhouse lot or farmland-assessed land; acres are today's lot on the 2026 assessment list, which may not be the lot as it sold, and a deed whose lot is not on the list has no acres and is kept out of the middle. Left out: gifts and family sales, sales between related companies, part interests, tax liens, sheriff, bankruptcy and short sales, quit claims, government and charity transfers, deeds tied to a foreclosure, exchanges, token-price sales and sales that were not between a willing buyer and seller. The state's file names no buyer or seller.

    Florida

    A farm sale is land the assessment roll codes agricultural, timberland included. Left out: any deed the county property appraiser did not mark as a qualified sale. The files give the month of each sale, not the day, and name no buyer or seller.
