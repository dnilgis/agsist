Acres not stated: a return covering several parcels that does not give them.

A sale marked unusual is bare farmland whose price per acre sits well outside the market middle half; possible reasons above it: a house or buildings, timber or hunting ground, a building site or development land, or a neighbor paying more for the piece next door; below: a family or neighbor sale, a part interest, wooded, wet or poorer ground, no road access, or a land contract.

Sources and terms

Every state

A sale is listed when it carries a price and acres and a whole interest changed hands in a normal sale: gifts, family and related-party sales, quit claims, foreclosures and the like are left out, the same rule as Field Scout and the Farmland Atlas. Township and town are where the state places the sale; no parcel is pinned. None of this is an appraisal.

Names

New Jersey sales carry no buyer or seller names in the state's file; the deed at the county has them.

New Jersey

New Jersey Division of Taxation, SR-1A deed abstracts with acres from the MOD-IV 2026 assessment list, farm lots (classes 3A and 3B), read Oct 29, 2026. A farm sale is a deed whose lot is classed farm, either the farmhouse lot or farmland-assessed land; acres are today's lot on the 2026 assessment list, which may not be the lot as it sold, and a deed whose lot is not on the list has no acres and is kept out of the middle. Left out: gifts and family sales, sales between related companies, part interests, tax liens, sheriff, bankruptcy and short sales, quit claims, government and charity transfers, deeds tied to a foreclosure, exchanges, token-price sales and sales that were not between a willing buyer and seller. The state's file names no buyer or seller.
