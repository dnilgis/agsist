Farmland sales, September 2026

450 farm sales of 10 acres or more were recorded in September 2026: 131 in Illinois (by deed date), 183 in Wisconsin, 122 in Indiana (by deed date), 14 in Vermont. The largest by acres: 2,280 acres in Sawyer County, WI, for $4,141,800.

* Illinois and Indiana sales are dated by the deed for now, so a sale signed late in the month may still be filed after this was read and show up next month.

Indiana's sales disclosure forms carry the sale date, not a recording date, and the town is the parcel's mailing town, placed at the middle of its ZIP code rather than a township. Our Indiana copy starts with 2021 forms.

Vermont sales come from the property transfer tax return. A return is a farm sale here when the land's use before or after the sale is an operating farm or timberland, or open land enrolled in current use. Left out of the market rule: a buyer the return marks as related to the seller (family, the seller's own trust or another relation), a return that claims a transfer tax exemption, a transfer the land gains tax return says was without pay, by divorce or by foreclosure, and anything less than the whole interest (a share, a life estate, a lease or an easement). Buildings come from the return's buildings boxes; timberland with no operating farm and no buildings is forest. Current use enrollment is shown on each sale; Vermont enrolls both farm and forest land, so open land in current use is counted as farmland and may include some woods. Our Vermont copy starts with the returns posted in 2019.

Above the usual, possible reasons: a house or buildings, timber or hunting ground, a building site or development land, or a neighbor paying more for the piece next door. Below, possible reasons: a family or neighbor sale, a part interest, wooded, wet or poorer ground, no road access, or a land contract. The return does not say which, and a price is not wrong because it is unusual.

A sale is listed when it carries a price and acres and a whole interest changed hands in a normal sale: gifts, family and related-party sales, quit claims, foreclosures and the like are left out, the same rule as Field Scout and the Farmland Atlas.
