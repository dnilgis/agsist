Prediction Bot Scorecard

A simple statistical bot calls whether corn, soybeans, Chicago wheat and KC wheat will settle higher or lower about four weeks from now. It makes one call per crop for each weekly CFTC report, at the first close after the report comes out, and grades it at the same point four reports later. Code grades every call against settlement prices. Nobody edits the calls or picks which ones count.

The rule

Written down on 2026-10-03, and backtested once that day, before the first live call, as rule set bot-v1.1. The backtest came out no better than a coin flip and is published as such. The same day, before any live call, the timing was corrected so live calls match what was backtested (one call per report, the release date for holiday weeks, shutdown weeks skipped); the direction rule did not change. Any later change gets a new version number and a new record of its own.

  1. Crowding first. Take managed money's net position (CFTC) as a share of open interest, from that week's report. Rank it against the 156 reports before it, about three years. This is the bot's own threshold; the COT page's “crowded” label uses the 85th/15th percentile of the full record and makes no forecast. At or above the 90th percentile, the funds are crowded long: call down. At or below the 10th, crowded short: call up.
  2. Otherwise, trend. If the front-month price is higher than 13 weeks ago, call up; lower, call down; exactly flat, no call.
  3. Grade. An up call is right when the settlement at the exit (the entry close of the report 4 weeks later) is above the entry settlement. A down call is right when it is below. A settlement equal to the entry is a miss.

There is no seasonal signal. The seasonal figures the site has are built from all years at once, so using them would grade 2013 with knowledge from 2026.

How the arithmetic works

Sources, dates, units and conventions
Live prices
Settled daily closes of the named contract (for example Dec ’26 corn), from Yahoo Finance daily bars. The call is made after the close, before the 7 PM Central evening reopen, from that day’s reading; its entry price is then replaced once, on the next run, by the day’s bar as Yahoo restates it, and the log notes any change. A call is graded on the run after its exit day, from the restated exit bar. Yahoo’s bars are not the exchange’s settlement file. The Oct 5, 2026 calls were first read at 8:34 PM, after the reopen; each note says how the entry was set. Grains are quoted in cents per bushel; the page shows them in dollars. The contract is the first listed month still trading on the grading day, so no live call spans a contract roll.
Entry and exit days
Entry: the first session close after CFTC published the report. Exit: the entry close of the report four weeks later. Both are fixed when the call is made. Release dates: scripts/cot_calendar.py. CFTC publishes on the Friday of the report week at 3:30 PM Eastern, after grains settle; a federal holiday delays it. Reports held back by a government shutdown (2013, 2018–19, 2025) are skipped, because their real release dates are not all known.
Positioning
CFTC Disaggregated Futures-Only Commitments of Traders, managed money long minus short, divided by open interest. A report counts from the first session after CFTC published it (it lands at 3:30 PM Eastern, after grains settle; a federal holiday delays it).
Backtest prices
The roll-repaired front-month index in data/cot-deep.json: Yahoo's continuous series with the return across each contract change dropped. It is an index, not a price. One call per weekly report, same entry and exit days as live. Decisions whose grading day falls after 2026-10-02 are left out, so the backtest never overlaps the live record. The continuous series follows Yahoo's own roll, which can differ by a few days from the exchange calendar the repair uses.
Overlap, counted every way
Weekly calls on a four-week window overlap, and the four crops on one report are one bet on one month of markets. So the record is counted every way: all calls, tested with a Newey–West test that allows for the overlap; and four disjoint groups, by report week counted mod 4, in which no two calls overlap. The page prints the range across the four groups. Picking one group would let the luckiest stand in for all.
Coin-flip odds
The chance that pure guessing does at least as well, from the Newey–West test on all calls. Five records are tested at once (all crops together and each crop), so the odds are adjusted with the Benjamini–Hochberg method before any record is called a finding.
Baselines
How often the price fell, and rose, over the same windows: what a bot that always said down, or always said up, would have scored.
Minimum before a rate is shown
At least 40 graded calls and 30 non-overlapping four-week windows in time (crops on the same report share one window). Below that the page says “Not enough calls yet”.
Raw files
/data/predictions.json (calls, record, rule) and /data/predictions-backtest.json (every backtest call). Code: scripts/prediction_bot.py; its selftests carry hand-worked answers.
