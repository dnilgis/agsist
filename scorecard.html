Prediction Bot Scorecard

A simple statistical bot calls whether corn, soybeans, Chicago wheat and KC wheat will settle higher or lower 20 trading sessions from now. It makes one call per crop every trading day after the close. Code grades every call against the settlement price. Nobody edits the calls or picks which ones count.

The rule

Written down on 2026-10-03, before the first live call, as rule set bot-v1. It is the only rule that was tested. It was not picked from a set of candidates. Any change gets a new version number and a new record of its own.

  1. Crowding first. Take the funds' (CFTC managed money) net position as a share of open interest, from the newest report a reader could have traded on by that close. Rank it against the 156 reports before it, about three years. At or above the 90th percentile, the funds are crowded long: call down. At or below the 10th, crowded short: call up.
  2. Otherwise, trend. If the front-month price is higher than 13 weeks ago, call up; lower, call down; exactly flat, no call.
  3. Grade. An up call is right when the settlement 20 trading sessions later is above the entry settlement. A down call is right when it is below. A settlement equal to the entry is a miss.

There is no seasonal signal. The seasonal figures the site has are built from all years at once, so using them would grade 2013 with knowledge from 2026.

How the arithmetic works

Sources, dates, units and conventions
Live prices
Settlement closes of the named contract (for example ZCZ26), from Yahoo Finance daily bars, read by the bot after the close. Grains are quoted in cents per bushel; the page shows them in dollars. The contract is the first listed month still trading on the grading day, so no live call spans a contract roll.
Trading sessions
Counted with the exchange holiday calendar in scripts/contract_calendar.py. The grading day is fixed when the call is made.
Positioning
CFTC Disaggregated Futures-Only Commitments of Traders, managed money long minus short, divided by open interest. A report counts from the first session after CFTC published it (it lands at 3:30 PM Eastern, after grains settle; a federal holiday delays it).
Backtest prices
The roll-repaired front-month index in data/cot-deep.json: Yahoo's continuous series with the return across each contract change dropped. It is an index, not a price. One call per weekly report, made at the first close after publication, graded at the same point four reports later (about 20 sessions). Decisions whose grading day falls after 2026-10-02 are left out, so the backtest never overlaps the live record. The continuous series follows Yahoo's own roll, which can differ by a few days from the exchange calendar the repair uses.
Separate spells
Calls made a day or a week apart share most of their 20 sessions, so they are not independent. For each crop the bot takes the first call, then the first call made on or after that call's grading day, and so on. Those calls do not overlap. The coin-flip odds are computed on them only.
Coin-flip odds
The chance that pure guessing (a fair coin, one flip per separate spell) gets at least as many right. With n spells and k right, it is the sum of C(n,j) / 2n for j from k to n. Five records are tested at once (all crops together and each crop), so the odds are adjusted for that with the Benjamini–Hochberg method before any record is called a finding. Crops move together, so the five are not independent tests.
Minimum before a rate is shown
At least 40 graded calls and 8 separate spells. Below that the page says “Not enough calls yet”.
Raw files
/data/predictions.json (calls, record, rule) and /data/predictions-backtest.json (every backtest call). Code: scripts/prediction_bot.py; its selftests carry hand-worked answers.
