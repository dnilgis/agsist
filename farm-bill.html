The 2026 Farm Bill

What it changes for a corn and soybean farm this year: not much.

Your ARC and PLC numbers are already law: reference prices of $4.10 for corn and $10.00 for soybeans through 2030, and crop insurance runs on permanent law. What is stuck: new CRP enrollment and new aid money.

House passed 224–200 · Apr 30, 2026 Senate Ag Committee passed S. 5526, 12–11 · Sep 16, 2026 Extension lapsed Sep 30, 2026 · Senate back Nov 9

A bill passes: Five more years of rules. CRP reopens and the Jan 1 problem goes away (both bills extend the permanent-law suspension through 2031).

A short extension: Depends on what it carries. It fixes Jan 1 only if it extends the permanent-law suspension.

Nothing: Insurance, ARC and PLC keep running. CRP stays shut to new acres. Dairy hits permanent law Jan 1, wheat Jun 1, corn Oct 1.

If you farm for a living
  • Your program numbers aren't in play. The 2025 reconciliation law (P.L. 119–21) runs ARC and PLC through the 2031 crop year and set the reference prices through 2030. Its crop-insurance premium-support changes are permanent. Neither bill reopens them.
  • Little here needs action from you. Sep 30 was Congress's deadline, not yours. No insurance date or ARC/PLC signup changes while the bill is pending. The exception: new CRP enrollment is paused until Congress acts, and crop insurance runs on permanent law either way.
  • The aid money isn't real yet. The $15–17B economic-aid push (proposed in January; $17.3B by June) sits outside both bills: no rules, no signup, no timeline. Anyone quoting your payment is guessing. Know your own number instead: break-even per acre · what you can safely pre-sell.
What this means for my operation
What actually happened October 1

The extension ran out Sep 30 and was not renewed. Most of what you depend on kept running.

Crop insurance, ARC and PLC and SNAP kept running. New CRP enrollment, six programs with no budget baseline and two small dairy programs stopped. The date that matters is January 1, 2027, when permanent law starts with dairy. The extension (P.L. 119–37, Div. E §5002) ran through FY2026 and crop year 2026; the stopgap funding law to December 11 did not extend it.

What kept running, what stopped, and the January 1 cliff Program by program, with the CRS sources.

Did not change on October 1

  • Crop insurance. It runs on permanent statutory authority. It does not lapse, it has never lapsed, and no farm bill deadline touches your policy, your premium support or your guarantee.
  • Reference prices, ARC and PLC, base acres. Set by the 2025 reconciliation law (P.L. 119–21) through the 2031 crop year.
  • SNAP. Continues as long as Congress appropriates for it, which is a separate fight on a separate calendar.

Actually stopped

  • The programs reconciliation left without a budget baseline: nothing to spend, so nothing to continue. Named by CRS: Biobased Markets, Specialty Crop Block Grants, the Local Agriculture Market Program, Organic Agriculture Research and Extension, Animal Disease Prevention and Management, and Farming Opportunities Training and Outreach.
  • New CRP enrollment. FSA set Sep 30 as the last day to approve CRP contracts; after it the agency has no statutory authority to approve new ones, even with money left. FSA says a new notice will follow if Congress re-authorizes CRP (DTN, Oct 1, 2026).
  • Some small dairy programs, including Dairy Forward Pricing and the Dairy Indemnity Payment Program (Farm Policy News, Oct 2026).
  • They resume when a farm bill or another extension restores them. If you are not in one of those programs and not signing up new CRP acres, October 1 was a headline and not an event.

The date that actually matters is January 1, 2027

The suspension of permanent law (the 1938 and 1949 Acts that still sit underneath all of this) runs through the 2026 crop year. The 2025 reconciliation law did not extend it. CRS is blunt about why: those extensions “have not scored as having a budgetary effect,” so there was no reason to carry one in a reconciliation bill.

Without congressional action, permanent law begins to take effect January 1, 2027. Dairy goes first, then wheat on June 1 and corn on October 1. In July 2025 the permanent-law parity support price for milk was 252% of the market price of milk: USDA would be required to buy milk until the market got there.

That is the cliff. It is three months past the deadline everyone was writing about. Both bills would fix it by extending the suspension through crop year 2031, but neither is law, and the Senate committee's September 16 vote does not change that. Congress has from Nov 9 to the end of the year to pass a bill or an extension, and a short-term extension would only fix it if it carries the suspension with it.

05 Where it stands

The path to the President's desk

What’s actually in the way · as of Oct 16

  • Committee is done. The floor is not. On Aug 6 Sens. McConnell and Tuberville were both absent, and a committee proxy vote cannot be the deciding vote, so the bill failed 10–11 (NSAC, sources below). McConnell came back for the 12–11 vote on Sep 16.
  • The 60-vote math still applies. A farm bill needs 60 votes to pass the full Senate, and Republicans hold nowhere near that on their own. The committee vote was party-line. Democrats are still holding out for a two-year delay in the SNAP state cost-share; the bill has one year.
  • No floor time until Nov 9. The lame-duck session also has to fund the government past Dec 11. Boozman says the committees will keep working on a deal with the House until then.
  • Conference is still ahead. Even a Senate floor win only produces a Senate bill. It would still need to be reconciled with the House-passed H.R. 7567 before either version reaches the President. Republican staff from both committees started informal talks in late September; no formal conference has been named.

06 Questions, answered

Farm bill FAQ

Short answers, with sources.

What is the 2026 Farm Bill?
The 2026 Farm Bill is an omnibus bill that would reauthorize USDA farm, food, conservation, and rural programs for five years, fiscal 2027 through fiscal 2031. Two versions now exist: the House passed H.R. 7567 (the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026) on April 30, 2026, and the Senate Agriculture Committee passed its own version, the Agricultural Act of 2026 (branded Farm Bill 2.0), on September 16, 2026. It was reported to the Senate as S. 5526 on September 24.
Is the 2026 Farm Bill law?
No. The House passed its version in April and the Senate Agriculture Committee passed its version on September 16, but neither has been enacted. The Senate bill still needs a floor vote, then the two chambers need one bill. The extension of the 2018 farm bill expired September 30, 2026.
How much does the 2026 Farm Bill cost?
Programs carry a roughly $1.374 trillion ten-year baseline (FY2027–FY2036) under current law. Each bill's own effect on that baseline is small: the House bill scores close to budget-neutral, and the Senate bill, S. 5526, is also described as budget-neutral; we have not found a CBO score for it as of October 15.
Where does most farm bill money go?
Nutrition, mainly SNAP, is about 71.7% of the baseline. Four titles account for roughly 99% of the money: Nutrition, Crop Insurance, Commodities, and Conservation.
What does the Senate's Agricultural Act of 2026 change?
It raises USDA farm loan limits (guaranteed operating loans to $3 million, ownership to $3.5 million), raises conservation easement cost-share and adds new easement and state conservation programs, more than doubles Market Access Program and Foreign Market Development money, and writes rural broadband into law. The committee version also allows year-round E15 sales, delays the SNAP state cost-share by a year, and carries Sen. Thune’s beef country-of-origin labeling amendment, adopted 17-6 on August 6.
When did the current farm bill expire?
The extension of the 2018 farm bill expired September 30, 2026, and Congress has not passed another. The stopgap funding law through December 11 keeps the government funded but does not extend the farm bill. New CRP enrollment is paused, and without action permanent law begins to take effect January 1, 2027.
What happened when the farm bill lapsed on September 30, 2026?
Less than the headlines suggested, and the real deadline is later. Crop insurance runs on permanent statutory authority and does not lapse. ARC, PLC and reference prices were set by the 2025 reconciliation law (P.L. 119–21) through the 2031 crop year. SNAP continues as long as Congress appropriates for it. What actually stops are the programs reconciliation left without a budget baseline: Biobased Markets, Specialty Crop Block Grants, the Local Agriculture Market Program, Organic Agriculture Research and Extension, Animal Disease Prevention and Management, and Farming Opportunities Training and Outreach, plus new CRP enrollment, which FSA cannot approve without authority, and some small dairy programs. They resume when a bill or extension funds them. The date that matters more is January 1, 2027: the suspension of permanent law runs only through the 2026 crop year, reconciliation did not extend it, and without action the 1938 and 1949 Acts begin to take effect: dairy first, then wheat on June 1 and corn on October 1. Source: CRS R48775.
What's the difference between the House and Senate bills?
Both use the same twelve titles and build on the 2025 reconciliation law. The House bill cuts EQIP by $786 million and moves it to other conservation programs; the Senate bill cuts EQIP and CSP to pay for easements and two new conservation programs, and adds year-round E15. The House bill has a CBO score; we have not found one for S. 5526.
What is holding the farm bill up right now?
Sixty votes on the Senate floor, which Republicans cannot reach alone, and then a deal with the House. The main fight is SNAP: Democrats want a two-year delay in the state cost-share; the bill has one. The Senate returns November 9.
Does the Senate bill change late planting rules?
Only in a pilot: S. 5526 would drop the late planting guarantee cut in at least 4 Texas Panhandle counties. Everywhere else stays the same, and the bill is not law.
