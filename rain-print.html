Rain Print

A year of rain on the farm, or back home, printed for the wall

Every farmer has a year they still talk about. Type the town and the year, and this draws every rain the nearest NOAA weather station caught that year. Put a name on it and mark up to three days that mattered. Drawing it is free. A print file is $19; a 12 x 18 print is $45, or $89 framed, shipping included.

  • The year the farm changed hands
  • A mom’s or dad’s last crop
  • The year you got married

Questions

Where does the rain data come from?

From the nearest NOAA weather station with a good record for that year. The print names the station and how far it is from town, in a small line at the bottom. It’s that station’s gauge, so a storm that hit the farm and missed the station won’t show. If there’s a station closer to the farm, pick it under Weather station.

What does “a usual year” mean?

When you compare with NOAA’s 30-year normal, a usual year is that normal for the station. When you compare with the station’s full record, it’s the station’s own average over every year it has kept. Either way, a day the station didn’t report is left out of both totals. The print says which one it used.

What if the station missed days?

Missing days show as gaps, never as dry days, and a small line on the print says what a gap is. The page reads the three nearest stations and picks the first with 10 or fewer missing days. A station missing 11 to 31 days can still be drawn, and the line under the print says how many. A station missing more than 31 days in the year gets no print, because the gaps would look like dry weeks that may not have been dry. A morning reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show on the next day.
