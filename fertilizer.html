How this is calculated

Cost per pound of nutrient = price ÷ (2000 lb/ton × grade %). Each nutrient is sourced from the cheapest product carrying it; nitrogen already supplied by your phosphate source (e.g. MAP) is credited before pricing urea.

Crop removal (lb/bu): Corn P₂O₅ 0.38, K₂O 0.29 · Soybeans 0.80 / 1.40 · Wheat 0.50 / 0.35: grain removal from UW Extension A2809 Table 4.2, the same numbers the nutrient removal calculator uses. Nitrogen uses a yield-goal rate (default 0.9 lb N/bu corn, adjustable; soybeans fix their own N), so the N line is a budget estimate, not a rate recommendation. Most Corn Belt extension services set corn N rates with the Corn N Rate Calculator (MRTN). These are benchmarks, not a soil-test recommendation. Your soil tests, credits, and agronomist override them.

Why prices move

Why fertilizer prices move

Natural gas drives nitrogen costs (urea, ammonium sulfate). Phosphate (MAP) prices follow global mining supply, with Morocco and China as dominant producers. Potash is dominated by Canada, Russia and Belarus; K-Mag comes from a single U.S. mine in Carlsbad, New Mexico, so any export disruption moves prices fast. Pelletized lime is more local, set by quarry output and freight. Seasonal demand spikes before spring and fall applications regardless of global fundamentals.

What the NOLA barge price means

NOLA barge prices reflect fertilizer trading at New Orleans barge terminals and are a benchmark for the wholesale market. The prices on this page are retail cash prices co-ops post for farmers. What you pay is your own dealer's delivered price.

Timing fertilizer purchases

Historically, fall and early winter offer softer urea and phosphate prices before spring demand builds. But natural gas direction, global supply, and tariffs can override seasonal patterns. A common approach: lock in 50–60% of needs in fall at a price you can live with, leave the rest flexible for spring.

How these prices affect break-evens

For corn at 180 bu/acre applying 160 lbs actual N as urea: a $100/ton price increase adds roughly $17/acre to input costs, or about $0.10/bu to your break-even. That dime comes straight off your margin.

How does natural gas price affect fertilizer costs?
Natural gas is the primary feedstock for nitrogen fertilizers including urea and ammonium sulfate. Higher Henry Hub natural gas prices commonly flow through to urea prices over the following weeks to months as production costs pass through the supply chain.
What is the difference between NOLA barge prices and the prices on this page?
NOLA barge prices reflect fertilizer trading at New Orleans barge terminals and are a benchmark for the wholesale market. The prices on this page are retail cash prices co-ops post for farmers. What you pay is your own dealer's delivered price.
When is the best time to buy fertilizer for corn?
Historically, fall and early winter (October-December) have offered lower urea and phosphate prices ahead of spring planting demand. However, price timing is difficult: natural gas price direction, global supply disruptions, and tariff policy can override seasonal patterns. Locking in a portion of needs in fall while leaving flexibility for spring is a common approach.
