Online survey of Kansas custom operators; the report covers custom work during 2025-2026. The prices include charges for machinery, power, fuel, and the operator. They do not include chemicals, seeds, and other materials unless specified; hay baling materials are the one exception. KDA and K-State say the prices should not be regarded as official or established rates. Rows are the report's STATE lines (district tables are in the report). The range is the lowest and highest rate reported. The report's table for hauling round bales by the ton disagrees with its own text (which calls it per hour), so that line is left out.

Source: Kansas Department of Agriculture and the Kansas State University Land Use Survey Office, the survey. AGSIST did not set these rates and does not adjust them. Bordering states with a survey here: Missouri (2025 rates), Nebraska (2026), Oklahoma (2021).

Questions

What does custom combining cost in Kansas?

In the Kansas Custom Rates 2026 (Rates Paid by Kansas Farmers for Custom Work), combining corn averaged $41.87 per acre ($18.00 to $58.00, 82 answers) and combining soybeans averaged $38.67 per acre ($18.00 to $55.00, 79 answers). Rates are survey averages; your local custom operator may charge more or less.

What is the custom planting rate in Kansas?

Corn, row planting: $22.64 per acre in the 2026 survey ($10.00 to $35.00, 48 answers). The survey's other planting lines are in the table above.

What does custom spraying cost in Kansas?

Herbicide, ground rig: $8.57 per acre in the 2026 survey ($5.00 to $18.00, 196 answers). Chemical is not included.

Who set these rates?

Nobody set them. They are averages of what farmers and custom operators told Kansas Department of Agriculture and the Kansas State University Land Use Survey Office they charged or paid. AGSIST copied them from the survey and links it. Your local custom operator may charge more or less.

What is included in a custom rate?

The prices include charges for machinery, power, fuel, and the operator. They do not include chemicals, seeds, and other materials unless specified; hay baling materials are the one exception. KDA and K-State say the prices should not be regarded as official or established rates.
