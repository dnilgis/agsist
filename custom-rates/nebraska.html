108 responses; rates reflect late winter 2025 and early 2026. Reported rates include charges for equipment, fuel, labor, and supplies such as baling wire or twine. Seed, fertilizer, and chemical costs are not included unless otherwise specified. Rows are from the report's State Summary (operations with six or more answers). The range is the lowest and highest answer.

The State Summary's dryland corn contract farming row repeats the CRP mowing row's numbers exactly and has no matching detail table, so it is left out. The summary labels small grain hauling "cents per bushel"; the detail table says "flat rate per bushel" and shows dollars, so it is listed per bushel.

Questions

What does custom combining cost in Nebraska?

In the 2026 Nebraska Agricultural Custom Rates (Nebraska Extension EC823, April 24, 2026), combining corn averaged $49.29 per acre ($30.00 to $82.00, 42 answers) and combining soybeans averaged $50.87 per acre ($28.00 to $85.00, 38 answers). Rates are survey averages; your local custom operator may charge more or less.

What is the custom planting rate in Nebraska?

Planting row crops, no coulters, without band applicator: $29.19 per acre in the 2026 survey ($20.00 to $44.00, 8 answers). The survey's other planting lines are in the table above.

What does custom spraying cost in Nebraska?

Spraying weed control, self-propelled sprayer: $9.85 per acre in the 2026 survey ($7.00 to $12.50, 29 answers). Chemical is not included.

Who set these rates?

Nobody set them. They are averages of what farmers and custom operators told University of Nebraska-Lincoln Extension, Center for Agricultural Profitability they charged or paid. AGSIST copied them from the survey and links it. Your local custom operator may charge more or less.

What is included in a custom rate?

Reported rates include charges for equipment, fuel, labor, and supplies such as baling wire or twine. Seed, fertilizer, and chemical costs are not included unless otherwise specified.
