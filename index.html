Corn drops nearly 4% Friday on WASDE; cattle hold the gains

Corn settled at $4.80 Friday, down almost 20 cents, after WASDE numbers came in heavier than the trade wanted. Cattle didn't blink, closing at $224.60, up 1.2% on the week. Every bushel still in the bin is worth less than it was Thursday morning.

THE ACTION

Every Briefing
Corn takes the WASDE hit
DRIVER
  • Corn fell 19¾ cents to $4.80, down 3.9% on the day, after Friday's WASDE report leaned heavier than expected.
  • Oats dropped with it, off 3.1% to $4.20, and Chicago wheat lost 1.8% to $6.71, so the cut wasn't corn alone.
Cattle and feeders close the week strong
DRIVER
  • Live cattle closed at $224.60, up 1.2%, and feeders gained 1.7% to $343.90.
  • The Cargill lockout, running since May 19, still has roughly 6,000 head a day of processing capacity off the table, and packer margins rather than cattle supply remain the squeeze.

     CFTC COT: Managed Money
    Loading…
    
        
    Rank: share of open interest, every week since 2010. Bars: net within its past 52 weeks, in contracts. COT is the CFTC’s weekly Commitments of Traders report, positions as of Tuesday. Managed money means hedge funds and commodity trading advisers. Net futures contracts held long vs short by that group in CFTC’s disaggregated report. The rank beside each market is the COT page’s one measure: the net as a share of open interest, ranked against every week since 2010; crowded means the top or bottom tenth of that record on the side the position is on. The bar is a second, shorter yardstick: where the net sits between its 52-week low and high in contracts, not adjusted for the size of the market. The badges name any market that is crowded on the rank, grains and live cattle separately. Neither is a headcount of who’s long or short, and one grain can run the other way. “Since” compares the front-month close on the report’s Tuesday with today’s, so a contract roll in between is inside the number. Interpretation of public CFTC data, not a trading signal.

    Frequently Asked Questions
    Cash Bids Near You
    
    
    
      
    What are corn futures prices today?
    AGSIST displays CBOT corn futures prices from Yahoo Finance, delayed about 15 minutes and updated every 15 minutes. The front-month contract and the next December contract are both shown, each labeled with its contract month (for example Dec ’26), with net change, percent change, and 52-week range. Prices are in dollars per bushel. See Full Corn Futures
    
      
    How do I find cash grain bids near me?
    Enter any US ZIP code on AGSIST’s Cash Bids page at agsist.com/cash-bids to see posted corn, soybean, and wheat elevator prices from grain elevators within 50 miles of that ZIP by default. You can widen the search up to 250 miles. Basis is shown alongside cash price for each location. Find Cash Bids
    
      
    Is AGSIST free?
    Yes. AGSIST is available at no charge with no account or subscription required. Tools on the site (spray advisory, urea volatilization risk monitor, break-even calculator, grain bin calculator, cash bids, and the AGSIST Daily briefing) are available at no charge for non-commercial use.
    
      
    How often are grain prices updated on AGSIST?
    Grain futures prices on AGSIST are updated about every 15 minutes while futures markets trade, from Sunday evening to Friday afternoon, Central time. The AGSIST Daily briefing is published every morning, weekends included, with overnight market analysis.
    
      
    What is urea volatilization risk?
    Urea volatilization is the loss of nitrogen fertilizer as ammonia gas when urea is surface-applied without incorporation. Risk is highest when soil temperatures exceed 50°F, humidity is high, and no rain is forecast within 2–4 days. AGSIST’s free urea risk monitor scores your real-time local conditions from 0–100. Check Urea Volatilization Risk
    
      
    What is grain basis and why does it matter?
    Grain basis is the difference between your local cash elevator price and the futures contract it is quoted against. A basis of −30 cents means the elevator pays 30 cents below futures. Stronger (less negative) basis signals higher local demand. AGSIST displays each elevator’s posted basis alongside its cash price in the cash bids tool.
    
      
    When is the WASDE report released?
    The USDA WASDE (World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates) report is released monthly, typically around the 10th of each month at 12:00 PM Eastern Time. AGSIST’s USDA Calendar tracks all report dates with market impact ratings. USDA Report Calendar
    
      
    What is the corn-to-soybean price ratio and how do farmers use it?
    The corn-to-soybean price ratio is November soybean futures divided by December corn futures. A higher ratio leans toward soybeans and a lower one toward corn, but where the line falls depends on your own yields, input costs, and basis, so run break-even math on your own numbers. Both contract prices update every 15 minutes on AGSIST’s corn and soybean futures pages. Break-Even Price Calculator
