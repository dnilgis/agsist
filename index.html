    Frequently Asked Questions
  
    
        
    Frequently Asked Questions
    Cash bids near you
    
    
    
      
    What are corn futures prices today?
    AGSIST shows CBOT corn futures from Yahoo Finance, at a 10-minute exchange delay and refreshed about every 15 minutes, so a price is about 10 to 25 minutes old while markets trade. You get the front month and the next December, each labeled with its contract month (for example Dec ’26), with net change, percent change, and 52-week range. Prices are in dollars per bushel. Corn futures
    
      
    How do I find cash grain bids near me?
    Enter any US ZIP code on AGSIST’s Cash Bids page at agsist.com/cash-bids to see posted corn, soybean, and wheat elevator prices from grain elevators within 50 miles of that ZIP by default. You can widen the search up to 250 miles. Basis is shown alongside cash price for each location. Cash bids near you
    
      
    Is AGSIST free?
    Yes. You don't need an account or a subscription. The spray advisory, urea risk monitor, break-even and grain bin calculators, cash bids and the AGSIST Daily briefing are free for non-commercial use.
    
      
    How often are grain prices updated on AGSIST?
    Grain futures prices on AGSIST are updated about every 15 minutes while futures markets trade, from Sunday evening to Friday afternoon, Central time. The AGSIST Daily briefing is published every morning, weekends included, with overnight market analysis.
    
      
    What is urea volatilization risk?
    Urea volatilization is the loss of nitrogen fertilizer as ammonia gas when urea is surface-applied without incorporation. Risk is highest when soil temperatures exceed 50°F, humidity is high, and no rain is forecast within 2–4 days. The AGSIST urea risk monitor scores your local conditions right now from 0–100. Urea loss risk
    
      
    What is grain basis and why does it matter?
    Grain basis is the difference between your local cash elevator price and the futures contract it is quoted against. A basis of −30 cents means the elevator pays 30 cents below futures. A stronger (less negative) basis means local buyers want grain. The cash bids page shows each elevator’s posted basis next to its cash price.
    
      
    When is the WASDE report released?
    The USDA WASDE (World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates) report is released monthly, typically around the 10th of each month at 12:00 PM Eastern Time. The AGSIST USDA calendar lists every report date and how much each one tends to move the market. USDA report calendar
    
      
    What is the soybean-to-corn price ratio and how do farmers use it?
    The soybean-to-corn price ratio is November soybean futures divided by December corn futures. A higher ratio leans toward soybeans and a lower one toward corn, but where the line falls depends on your own yields, input costs, and basis, so run break-even math on your own numbers. Both contract prices update every 15 minutes on AGSIST’s corn and soybean futures pages. Break-even calculator

    Sources and terms
      
    Prices from Yahoo Finance, delayed about 10 to 25 minutes · Cash bids via the AGSIST elevator network · Weather: National Weather Service forecast grid, PRISM daily history (RCC-ACIS), soil at the nearest USCRN, SCAN, Iowa State or Wisconet station · USDA NASS (This product uses the NASS API but is not endorsed or certified by NASS.) · NOAA CPC
    
      
    Farmland sale at the top: the highest price per acre among bare farmland sales recorded in the state in its newest 30 days on file, inside the county’s usual range; no buyer or seller names. Export sales: USDA reports metric tons; shown in bushels (39.368 a ton for corn, 36.744 for soybeans and wheat); commitments are shipments so far plus sales not yet shipped. Funds: the CFTC Commitments of Traders report, managed money (hedge funds and commodity trading advisers), net futures positions as of Tuesday, in contracts; not a headcount of who is long or short, and not a trading signal. The “Since” price line compares the front-month price now with its close on the report’s Tuesday, so a contract roll in between is inside the number. Yield nowcast: built from the weekly USDA crop ratings, state by state, weighted by acres; the 80% band means that in 8 of every 10 of 12 past years, each estimated from earlier years only, the estimate at the same week missed USDA’s final yield by no more than this. The band gets narrower as the season goes on. It is withheld once the ratings are two weeks old. WASDE grades compare each USDA figure with the average of a published trade survey.
