Corn steadies near $4.81 after Friday's WASDE hit

Corn is at $4.81 overnight, up a quarter cent from Friday's $4.79¾ settle, a small bounce after Friday's near 4% drop on USDA's yield number. Soybean meal gained 2.2% to $368.70 while beans slipped to $12.83. Monday's quiet bounce doesn't undo Friday's loss on unpriced corn.

     CFTC COT: Managed Money
    Loading…
    
        
    Rank: net as a share of open interest, against every week since 2010. Bar: where the net sits in its 52-week range. COT is the CFTC’s weekly Commitments of Traders report, positions as of Tuesday. Managed money means hedge funds and commodity trading advisers. Net futures contracts held long vs short by that group in CFTC’s disaggregated report. The rank beside each market is the COT page’s one measure: the net as a share of open interest, ranked against every week since 2010; crowded means the top or bottom tenth of that record on the side the position is on. The bar is a second, shorter yardstick: where the net sits between its 52-week low and high in contracts, not adjusted for the size of the market. The badges name any market that is crowded on the rank, grains and live cattle separately. Neither is a headcount of who’s long or short, and one grain can run the other way. “Since” compares the front-month close on the report’s Tuesday with today’s, so a contract roll in between is inside the number. Interpretation of public CFTC data, not a trading signal.

    Frequently Asked Questions
    Cash bids near you
    
    
    
      
    What are corn futures prices today?
    AGSIST shows CBOT corn futures from Yahoo Finance, delayed about 15 minutes and refreshed every 15 minutes. You get the front month and the next December, each labeled with its contract month (for example Dec ’26), with net change, percent change, and 52-week range. Prices are in dollars per bushel. Corn futures
    
      
    How do I find cash grain bids near me?
    Enter any US ZIP code on AGSIST’s Cash Bids page at agsist.com/cash-bids to see posted corn, soybean, and wheat elevator prices from grain elevators within 50 miles of that ZIP by default. You can widen the search up to 250 miles. Basis is shown alongside cash price for each location. Cash bids near you
    
      
    Is AGSIST free?
    Yes. You don't need an account or a subscription. The spray advisory, urea risk monitor, break-even and grain bin calculators, cash bids and the AGSIST Daily briefing are free for non-commercial use.
    
      
    How often are grain prices updated on AGSIST?
    Grain futures prices on AGSIST are updated about every 15 minutes while futures markets trade, from Sunday evening to Friday afternoon, Central time. The AGSIST Daily briefing is published every morning, weekends included, with overnight market analysis.
    
      
    What is urea volatilization risk?
    Urea volatilization is the loss of nitrogen fertilizer as ammonia gas when urea is surface-applied without incorporation. Risk is highest when soil temperatures exceed 50°F, humidity is high, and no rain is forecast within 2–4 days. The AGSIST urea risk monitor scores your local conditions right now from 0–100. Urea loss risk
    
      
    What is grain basis and why does it matter?
    Grain basis is the difference between your local cash elevator price and the futures contract it is quoted against. A basis of −30 cents means the elevator pays 30 cents below futures. A stronger (less negative) basis means local buyers want grain. The cash bids page shows each elevator’s posted basis next to its cash price.
    
      
    When is the WASDE report released?
    The USDA WASDE (World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates) report is released monthly, typically around the 10th of each month at 12:00 PM Eastern Time. The AGSIST USDA calendar lists every report date and how much each one tends to move the market. USDA report calendar
    
      
    What is the corn-to-soybean price ratio and how do farmers use it?
    The corn-to-soybean price ratio is November soybean futures divided by December corn futures. A higher ratio leans toward soybeans and a lower one toward corn, but where the line falls depends on your own yields, input costs, and basis, so run break-even math on your own numbers. Both contract prices update every 15 minutes on AGSIST’s corn and soybean futures pages. Break-even calculator
