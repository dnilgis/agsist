Custom farm rates by state: the going rate for hired field work

Every state custom rate survey I could find, lined up. Each job shows the middle rate across the states, how far apart they run, and each state's own number, whether you're hiring the work or doing it. Rates are survey averages; your local custom operator may charge more or less.

My sheet

How I figure the going rate

For each job I take each state survey's own average, as the survey printed it, and show the middle one. Half the states sit at or above it, half at or below. Under it you get the lowest and highest of the state averages and how many surveys priced the job. Tap a job and every state's number is there with its year, how many answered, and the lowest and highest answer that survey printed.

Each state counts once, however many people answered. I don't inflate older surveys or adjust anything. A survey of rates from before 2023 is shown with its year but kept out of the middle. A job needs 3 surveys before I give a middle; with fewer, each number stands on its own. Surveys word jobs their own way (combine only, or combine, cart and haul), so I only line up jobs that read the same, and each state's wording is under its number.

Beside some jobs I also show what it costs to do the work yourself, from the University of Illinois machinery cost estimates (August 2025): New machinery held 10 years (combine 7), diesel at $3.00 a gallon, labor at $22.00 an hour, overhead (depreciation, interest, insurance, housing, repairs); no profit. Field operations are sized for a 1,400 acre grain farm; the combine is 470 hp on 2,500 acres; bales assume 1.3 tons an acre. Those are costs to own and run the machine, not rates anyone charged, so they never go in the middle.

9 surveys, 2024 to 2026 rates. Read the data: blend.json (the going rates), index.json and one file per state beside it.

  • North Dakota, 2024: 2024 North Dakota Custom Rates, Part I Early Season Operations (August 2024, marked draft) and Part II Late Season Operations (December 2024), North Dakota State University Extension (late season survey run by USDA NASS). The survey.

Questions

What does custom combining cost per acre?

Survey averages for combining corn, per acre: Iowa $46.05 (2026 survey, 119 answers); Indiana $36.97 (2025 survey, 53 answers); Kansas $41.87 (2026 survey, 82 answers); Maryland and Delaware $40.03 (2025 survey, answers not printed); Missouri $47.56 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 9 answers); North Dakota $43.87 (2024 survey, 38 answers); Nebraska $49.29 (2026 survey, 42 answers); Ohio $34.61 (2024 survey, 60 answers); Pennsylvania $38.60 (2022 survey, answers not printed); Wisconsin $38.47 (2025 survey, 34 answers). Soybeans: Iowa $43.05 (2026 survey, 94 answers); Indiana $35.94 (2025 survey, 54 answers); Kansas $38.67 (2026 survey, 79 answers); Maryland and Delaware $39.63 (2025 survey, answers not printed); Missouri $42.33 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 9 answers); North Dakota $36.02 (2024 survey, 61 answers); Nebraska $50.87 (2026 survey, 38 answers); Ohio $33.53 (2024 survey, 58 answers); Oklahoma $27.11 (2021 survey, 9 answers); Pennsylvania $38.70 (2022 survey, answers not printed); Wisconsin $38.45 (2025 survey, 29 answers). Some surveys price combining alone and some include the grain cart and hauling, so check the wording in each state's table. Rates are survey averages; your local custom operator may charge more or less.

What is the custom rate for planting corn?

Survey averages for planting row crops without fertilizer, per acre: Iowa $26.50 (2026 survey, 69 answers); Indiana $22.97 (2025 survey, 31 answers); Kansas $22.64 (2026 survey, 48 answers); Maryland and Delaware $27.23 (2025 survey, answers not printed); Missouri $24.65 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 23 answers); North Dakota $21.02 (2024 survey, 28 answers); Nebraska $29.19 (2026 survey, 8 answers); Ohio $23.51 (2024 survey, 44 answers); Pennsylvania $28.10 (2022 survey, answers not printed); Wisconsin $23.22 (2025 survey, 9 answers). Planting with fertilizer and no-till planting run a little higher in most surveys; each state's table has its lines.

What does custom spraying cost?

Ground rig spraying, per acre, chemical not included: Iowa $9.35 (2026 survey, 121 answers); Indiana $8.39 (2025 survey, 40 answers); Kansas $8.57 (2026 survey, 196 answers); Maryland and Delaware $14.01 (2025 survey, answers not printed); Missouri $9.42 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 20 answers); North Dakota $9.23 (2024 survey, 111 answers); Nebraska $9.85 (2026 survey, 29 answers); Ohio $9.89 (2024 survey, 97 answers); Oklahoma $6.48 (2021 survey, 56 answers); Pennsylvania $15.60 (2022 survey, answers not printed); Wisconsin $11.01 (2025 survey, 29 answers).

Who sets these custom rates?

Nobody sets them. Each number is an average of what farmers and custom operators told a university or state survey they charged or paid. AGSIST copies the survey's own numbers and links the survey; it does not set, adjust or update them between surveys. Your local custom operator may charge more or less.

Why do the same jobs differ between states?

The surveys were taken in different years, ask about the job in different words (combine only, or combine, cart and haul), and hear from different people. Some print the lowest and highest answer, Iowa leaves out the single lowest and highest, Purdue prints a one standard deviation band and Oklahoma prints no range. Compare the wording before you compare the numbers.

What does custom grain hauling cost per bushel?

Kansas's 2026 survey prices hauling from the field to the farm or the nearest elevator. Corn averaged 23.9 cents a bushel, plus 5.5 cents a bushel a mile past 16 miles. Soybeans averaged 24.2 cents a bushel, plus 5.6 cents a bushel a mile past 18 miles.

Do custom combiners charge extra for a big corn crop?

Some do. In Kansas's 2026 survey, harvesters who charge a base rate averaged $35.27 an acre for corn, plus $0.279 a bushel on yield over 69 bushels an acre. Those who charge one flat rate with no yield charge averaged $41.87 an acre.
