Custom farm rates by state: the going rate for hired field work

Every state custom rate survey I could find, lined up. Each job shows the middle rate across the states, how far apart they run, and each state's own number, for hiring the work or doing it. Rates are survey averages; your local custom operator may charge more or less.

My sheet

How I figure the going rate

For each job I take each state survey's own average, as the survey printed it, and show the middle one. Half the states sit at or above it, half at or below. Under it you get the lowest and highest of the state averages and how many surveys priced the job. Tap a job and every state's number is there with its year, how many answered, and the lowest and highest answer that survey printed.

Each state counts once, however many people answered. I don't inflate older surveys or adjust anything. A survey of rates from before 2023 is shown with its year but kept out of the middle. A job needs 3 surveys before I give a middle; with fewer, each number stands on its own. Surveys word jobs their own way (combine only, or combine, cart and haul), so I only line up jobs that read the same, and each state's wording is under its number.

Beside some jobs I also show what it costs to do the work yourself, from the University of Illinois machinery cost estimates (August 2025): New machinery held 10 years (combine 7), diesel at $3.00 a gallon, labor at $22.00 an hour, overhead (depreciation, interest, insurance, housing, repairs); no profit. Field operations are sized for a 1,400 acre grain farm; the combine is 470 hp on 2,500 acres; bales assume 1.3 tons an acre. Those are costs to own and run the machine, not rates anyone charged, so they never go in the middle.

Questions

What does custom combining cost per acre?

Custom combining corn runs about $41.87 an acre, the middle of 9 state surveys (2024 to 2026 rates). Surveys run from $36.97 in Indiana to $49.29 in Nebraska. Soybeans run about $38.67, from $35.54 in Ohio to $50.87 in Nebraska. Survey averages for combining corn, per acre: Indiana $36.97 (2025 survey, 53 answers); Iowa $46.05 (2026 survey, 119 answers); Kansas $41.87 (2026 survey, 82 answers); Maryland and Delaware $40.03 (2025 survey, answers not printed); Missouri $47.56 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 9 answers); Nebraska $49.29 (2026 survey, 42 answers); North Dakota $43.87 (2024 survey, 38 answers); Ohio $37.35 (2026 survey, 81 answers); Pennsylvania $38.60 (2022 survey, answers not printed); Wisconsin $38.47 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 34 answers). Soybeans: Indiana $35.94 (2025 survey, 54 answers); Iowa $43.05 (2026 survey, 94 answers); Kansas $38.67 (2026 survey, 79 answers); Maryland and Delaware $39.63 (2025 survey, answers not printed); Missouri $42.33 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 9 answers); Nebraska $50.87 (2026 survey, 38 answers); North Dakota $36.02 (2024 survey, 61 answers); Ohio $35.54 (2026 survey, 85 answers); Oklahoma $27.11 (2021 survey, 9 answers); Pennsylvania $38.70 (2022 survey, answers not printed); Wisconsin $38.45 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 29 answers). Some surveys price combining alone and some include the grain cart and hauling, so check the wording in each state's table. Rates are survey averages; your local custom operator may charge more or less.

What is the custom rate for planting corn?

Planting row crops without fertilizer runs about $24.65 an acre, the middle of 9 state surveys (2024 to 2026 rates). Surveys run from $21.02 in North Dakota to $29.19 in Nebraska. Survey averages for planting row crops without fertilizer, per acre: Indiana $22.97 (2025 survey, 31 answers); Iowa $26.50 (2026 survey, 69 answers); Kansas $22.64 (2026 survey, 48 answers); Maryland and Delaware $27.23 (2025 survey, answers not printed); Missouri $24.65 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 23 answers); Nebraska $29.19 (2026 survey, 8 answers); North Dakota $21.02 (2024 survey, 28 answers); Ohio $25.20 (2026 survey, 54 answers); Pennsylvania $28.10 (2022 survey, answers not printed); Wisconsin $23.22 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 9 answers). Planting with fertilizer and no-till planting run a little higher in most surveys; each state's table has its lines.

What does custom spraying cost?

Spraying with a self-propelled or ground rig runs about $9.42 an acre, the middle of 9 state surveys (2024 to 2026 rates). Surveys run from $8.39 in Indiana to $14.01 in Maryland and Delaware. Ground rig spraying, per acre, chemical not included: Indiana $8.39 (2025 survey, 40 answers); Iowa $9.35 (2026 survey, 121 answers); Kansas $8.57 (2026 survey, 196 answers); Maryland and Delaware $14.01 (2025 survey, answers not printed); Missouri $9.42 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 20 answers); Nebraska $9.85 (2026 survey, 29 answers); North Dakota $9.23 (2024 survey, 111 answers); Ohio $10.63 (2026 survey, 105 answers); Oklahoma $6.48 (2021 survey, 56 answers); Pennsylvania $15.60 (2022 survey, answers not printed); Wisconsin $11.01 (2025 rates, 2026 survey, 29 answers).

Who sets these custom rates?

Nobody sets them. Each number is an average of what farmers and custom operators told a university or state survey they charged or paid. AGSIST copies the survey's own numbers and names the survey; it does not set, adjust or update them between surveys. Your local custom operator may charge more or less.

Why do the same jobs differ between states?

The surveys were taken in different years, ask about the job in different words (combine only, or combine, cart and haul), and hear from different people. Some print the lowest and highest answer, Iowa leaves out the single lowest and highest, Purdue prints a one standard deviation band and Oklahoma prints no range. Compare the wording before you compare the numbers.

What does custom grain hauling cost per bushel?

Survey averages for hauling grain by truck, and how far each survey's rate carries it: Iowa 2026 $0.20 a bushel (25 mi one way, to market; 97 answers); Indiana 2025 $0.188 a bushel (average 23.2 mi one way, to market; 47 answers); Kansas 2026 $0.239 a bushel (field to storage or elevator, up to 16 mi; 117 answers); Missouri 2025 rates $0.26 a bushel (bin to market, over 20 mi; 6 answers); North Dakota 2024 $0.222 a bushel (base charge, average base 47.62 mi; 27 answers); Nebraska 2026 $0.16 a bushel (flat rate to storage or elevator, miles not stated; 22 answers); Ohio 2026 $0.19 a bushel (farm to market, average 23.4 mi one way; 69 answers); Wisconsin 2025 rates $0.18 a bushel (miles not stated; 14 answers). The middle of the 8 current surveys is $0.195 a bushel, but the surveys measure different distances, so set the miles beside the number before you compare. Older, not in the middle: Pennsylvania 2022 $0.28 (base charge, miles not stated).

What does custom round baling cost per bale?

Survey averages for baling round bales, per bale: Iowa 2026 $13.15 (11 answers, "Hay baling, large round bales without wrap"); Indiana 2025 $19.58 (13 answers, "Baling large round bales (5 feet wide)"); Kansas 2026 $17.50 (4 answers, "Baling large round bales under 1,500 pounds, without net"); Maryland and Delaware 2025 $15.01 (answers not printed, "Bailing-large Round"); Missouri 2025 rates $17.20 (5 answers, "Round bale with twine"); North Dakota 2024 $12.99 (21 answers, "Baling round bales, 1,500 lbs or less (wire or twine included)"); Ohio 2026 $11.98 (23 answers, "Large round bales, baled and left in field"). The middle of the 7 current surveys is $15.01 a bale. Bale size, twine or net wrap differ by survey, so read each state's wording. Rates are survey averages; your local custom operator may charge more or less.

Do custom combiners charge extra for a big corn crop?

Some do. In Kansas's 2026 survey, harvesters with a base rate averaged $35.27 an acre for corn (80 answers). The bushel charge on top averaged $0.279 a bushel for yield over 69 bushels an acre (65 answers). Those with one flat rate and no yield charge averaged $41.87 an acre (82 answers).
