Soybean and corn
yield calculator

Corn and soybean bushels per acre from your own field counts: ear counts for corn, pod counts for soybeans.

When to count, and how accurate it is

Count corn from the milk stage (R3) on and soybeans at R6 to R7, the same ear-count and kernel-count math crop tour scouts run in August. Sample at least 5 locations per field, 10 ears or plants each. The calculator averages up to 5 sites.

Method: Yield Component Method (ears × kernel rows × kernels per row ÷ 85, for a 1/1000-acre sample; 85,000 kernels per bushel is what Purdue’s Bob Nielsen suggests for today’s hybrids, where 90,000 was the original figure). Accuracy: corn estimates run about ±20 bu/ac, the figure Purdue, Iowa State, Ohio State and Penn State Extension all give for this method. Soybean estimates are looser still; Michigan State puts them at ±25% or worse before R6. Not a substitute for yield monitor or combine data.

Yield (bu/ac) = (ears × kernel rows × kernels/row) ÷ 85
Ear count × 1000 = ears/acre (1/1000 acre row method). The 85 stands for 85,000 kernels in a 56-lb bushel. The method started with 90,000; kernels have gotten bigger as hybrids improved, and Purdue’s Bob Nielsen now suggests 80,000 to 85,000. The yield range shows what changes if a bushel holds 65,000 to 95,000 kernels. That range is not the field error. Valid from the milk (R3) stage on. Test weight is not part of the formula. With several sites, each site’s yield is worked out and the field number is their plain average. Worked example: 32 ears × 16 rows × 35 kernels ÷ 85 = 211 bu/ac.

Method: ears times kernel rows times kernels per row, divided by 85 (or the kernels per bushel you pick, in thousands), for a 1/1000-acre sample; the range sweeps 65,000 to 95,000 kernels per bushel; field error is about ±20 bu/ac · Sources: Purdue (Nielsen, “Estimating Corn Grain Yield Prior to Harvest”), Iowa State, Ohio State and Penn State Extension · Reviewed by Sigurd Lindquist, Certified Crop Adviser (CCA)

Field sampling
• Sample at least 5 locations per field, at least 200 feet apart and away from field edges.
• Avoid sampling spots you noticed from the road; select locations before walking the field.
• Count ears in the designated 1/1000-acre row length for your row width (the calculator shows it under the ear count box).
• Count kernel rows and kernels/row on 10 representative ears per location, not the biggest or smallest.
• Test weight does not change this estimate. Weigh it at harvest, because low test weight docks grain value.
• Average multiple locations: field variability of 30–50 bu/ac between zones is common.

Common questions

How do I estimate corn yield before harvest?
The ear count method: count ears in 1/1000 acre row length, then count kernel rows and kernels per row on 10 representative ears. Formula: ears × kernel rows × kernels per row / 85, for a 1/1000-acre sample. The 85 is 85,000 kernels per bushel: Purdue's Bob Nielsen suggests 80,000 to 85,000 for today's hybrids, and 90,000 was the method's original figure. Kernel weight varies, so the estimator also shows a range across 65,000–95,000 kernels per bushel. Count at least 5 sites and the estimator averages them. Enter these counts in the AGSIST Yield Estimator for a field estimate.
How accurate is the pre-harvest yield estimate?
The yield component method is accurate to roughly plus or minus 20 bushels per acre, a figure commonly cited in extension guides. Accuracy improves if you sample at least 5 locations and 10 ears per location, and estimate at the milk (R3) stage or later. Because kernel weight varies, the estimator also shows a range built from 65,000 to 95,000 kernels per bushel.
How do I estimate soybean yield before harvest?
Count plants per acre, then count pods per plant and seeds per pod on 10 representative plants. Formula: plants/ac × pods/plant × seeds/pod / (seeds/lb × 60). The AGSIST estimator calculates this automatically.
