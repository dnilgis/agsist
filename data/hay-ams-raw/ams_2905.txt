Compared to last report: Trade activity Moderate. Demand Good to Very Good all markets of new crop hay.  Premium small squares of alfalfa 
sold 25-75 cents/bale higher. Corn Silage harvest is mostly done in northern Colorado with southern Colorado delayed from last week's rain. 
Southeast Colorado received between 2-4" of rain last week, stretching from Pueblo to Lamar.  According to the U.S. Drought Monitor's High 
Plains Summary for October 6, 2026, widespread heavy precipitation abetted robust improvement across eastern Colorado, central and 
western Kansas, and parts of southern Nebraska. Numerous locations reported 3 to locally over 6 inches of rain, which was sufficient to 
support 2-category improvements in portions of southeastern Colorado and the western half of Kansas. Precipitation amounts declined 
abruptly north of a line from central Colorado through northeastern Nebraska, and only a few tenths of an inch at best was observed beyond 
this delineation. Still, improvements were introduced in parts of the central and northern Dakotas where earlier precipitation may have been 
more beneficial than initially assessed. The only areas of deterioration noted were in northeastern Colorado and part of north-central 
Wyoming, which consistently have been north and/or west of the heaviest precipitation. Elsewhere, dryness and drought intensities did not 
markedly change from last week.  For Drought Classifications, 32.94% of the state falls in Extreme-Exceptional drought (D3-D4) categories, and 
67.06% of the state falls into the Abnormally Dry-Severe Drought (D0-D2) categories.  The next available report will be Thursday, October 22, 
2026.
Please Note: The above volumes (tonnage, acres, and bales) listed on this USDA LPGMN report are for confirmed trades only, it does not 
include estimated volume (tonnage) for bids or offers to the trade.
