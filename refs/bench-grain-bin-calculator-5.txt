What is a grain bin?

Grain bins or grain silo towers are vast galvanized metal tins used to store harvested grains such as soybeans, corn, and wheat. It protects your months of hard work and harvests from rodents, weather, and other destructive elements.

Most metal grain bins come in circular or cylindrical shapes, but different shapes (e.g., square and rectangular flat storage) are readily available for farm producers interested in custom designs. The round shapes are popular because they are easier to clean compared to shapes with corners.

Another common feature of grain bin designs is a piled or conical top and, sometimes, a hopper bottom. These top-fills allow the grain bin to be quickly loaded with grains through a central hatch, while the hopper bottom allows the grain bin's content to be easily emptied thanks to gravity.

The structure also ensures proper ventilation and movement of air current through the metal grain bins. Flat bottom rectangular grain bins are also common. They are easier and cheaper to construct with a concrete foundation that makes them suited for holding grains indefinitely.

Just like shapes, the sizes also vary if you're considering grain bins for sale. Some metal grain bins can hold 1,000 bushels (about 25.4 metric tons) of grain, while others can hold up to 1 million bushels.

FAQs

How do you calculate grain bin capacity?

Depending on the shape of the grain bin, you calculate the grain bin capacity by first calculating its volume in cubic feet and converting it to bushels using a conversion factor of 0.7786 bushels per cubic foot.

How to calculate bushels in a grain bin?

To calculate the number of bushels in a grain bin, multiply the volume by the conversion constant of 0.7786 bushels per cubic foot. You can use this tool as a grain bin bushel capacity calculator to find the number of bushels in a grain bin.

How do you calculate grain bin capacity of corn?

You can calculate the grain bin capacity for a grain bin storing the corn by using the formula:

π × Radius of grain bin2 × Height of corn in grain bin × 0.7786

Flat bottom vs round large grain silos — which is a better?

The main difference between the round and flat bottom grain bins is the construction cost. Flat bottom grain bins are easier and cheaper to construct than the large round grain silos with hopper bottoms. Suppose you will be accessing your grain storage often, or you're holding grain for the short term. In that case, a round grain bin is preferred because it is easier to unload the grains, but if you are storing grains for the long term, the flat bottom grain bins are cheaper. However, you will need a piece of equipment such as an auger transporter or conveyor to unload grains.
