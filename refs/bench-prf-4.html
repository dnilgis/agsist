HOW DOES PRF INSURANCE WORK?

PRF (Pasture, Rangeland, Forage) insurance coverage is based on the direct correlation between below-average rainfall and decreased forage production. Here's a snapshot of how it works:

Insure your grazing or haying ground for up to 90% of average rainfall.

Receive indemnity payments when rainfall is lower than average in your area.

No premium is due up-front - indemnities go toward your premium first until the balance is satisfied. Additional payments go straight to you.

Your land is insured in two-month intervals throughout the year.

PRF insurance covers grazing or haying acres, whether owned or leased.

PRF FAQs

Answers to your PRF questions.

When do you get PRF insurance indemnities?

Your PRF insurance indemnities will hit your mailbox when: 

‍

  • Your PRF insurance premium is paid in full.
  • Your insured grid(s) receive less rainfall than your chosen coverage level (70%-90% of the long-term average) during one of your insured intervals. 

‍Remember: PRF insurance is self-funding. Indemnities are credited to your premium balance first. Once the balance is paid in full, any additional indemnities are issued directly to you.

How does the USDA subsidize PRF insurance?

The government subsidy on PRF insurance depends on your chosen coverage level.

  • 90% coverage has a 51% subsidy
  • 85% to 80% coverage has a 55% subsidy
  • 75% to 70%  coverage has a 59% subsidy

Your coverage level refers to the percentage of normal rainfall that your PRF policy insures.

‍

When is the PRF insurance deadline?

The deadline to sign an application for a  PRF insurance policy is December 1 of the year prior to your policy year.

How is PRF insurance different from FSA Disaster Programs?

The biggest difference between PRF insurance and the FSA programs is that PRF insurance is offered as an insurance product, in which the producer is responsible for a premium. 

The FSA disaster programs are aid programs, in which there is no input from the producer. These programs require the producer to meet certain criteria and fill out the necessary paperwork. After doing so, the producer may or may not receive the needed funds.

It is worth noting that the funds from most FSA offices are often issued after the need arises. Indemnities from PRF insurance are often available about 60-90 days after the interval ends.

How does PRF insurance work?

PRF insurance works by measuring precipitation in your specific grid using NOAA data. You choose the two-month periods (called intervals) and coverage levels (between 70%-90%). If precipitation falls below your selected coverage level in that interval, your policy triggers an indemnity. There's no claims process. You only owe a premium if the indemnities don't cover your balance by September 30.

How does PRF insurance pay out?

If your insured grid receives less precipitation than your selected coverage level during a covered interval, you receive an indemnity. That indemnity first covers your policy premium. Once your premium is fully paid, any remaining indemnity is paid directly to you. Payments are determined automatically based on NOAA rainfall data—no paperwork or claims required.

Is PRF insurance rainfall insurance?

Pasture, Rangeland, and Forage (PRF) insurance is often informally referred to as rainfall insurance or drought insurance because ranchers utilize the coverage to protect their profits from the impact of below-average rainfall.

How to qualify for PRF insurance

PRF insurance is built for the livestock producer, not the landowner. If you own livestock, you can qualify for PRF insurance, whether your insured land is owned or leased.

Hay ground can also qualify for PRF insurance coverage if it is used to harvest forage for livestock – as long as it is not enrolled in a different crop insurance program.

‍

When can I collect PRF Insurance indemnities?

Your indemnities first go toward your premium. Once your premium is satisfied, any future indemnities come straight to you.

How do you create the best PRF insurance policy for me?

How do I qualify for PRF insurance?

If you have cattle, you probably qualify for PRF insurance. For grazing acres, beginning in 2027 you need an insurable interest in enough livestock to equal at least 25% of an average stocking rate for the insured acres, and you must keep stocking records for three years.

Can I participate in FSA programs and PRF insurance at the same time?

Yes, PRF insurance can be used along with FSA assistance programs.

How are PRF insurance agents paid?

Agents are paid a commission based on the premium of your policy.

What is a GIS Grid for PRF insurance?

An area of 0.25 degrees latitude by 0.25 degrees longitude (roughly 17 miles on a side) used to help identify coverage for PRF purposes. This is what will be considered your “local area” and used for measuring precipitation.

Where do PRF indemnity payments come from?

The check that you’ll receive for your indemnity payments come from your insurance provider. However, the money itself comes from the USDA’s subsidy of PRF and is administered by the USDA’s Risk Management Agency (RMA).

What kind of land can I insure with PRF insurance?

PRF insurance can be applied to any acreage that you run on, whether owned or leased. Acreage on agency-managed land isn’t insurable if the agency hasn’t authorized use for the crop year.

How much does PRF insurance cost?

That is based on your county values assigned by the USDA. It can vary.

What conditions characterize sustainable grazing conditions?

Sustainable grazing conditions are conditions where both livestock and wildlife can feed without detriment to habitat and soils. These are conditions where average long term forage volatility is reduced through quality management practices.
