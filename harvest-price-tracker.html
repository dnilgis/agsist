When does your price get set?

Your revenue guarantee is built from two averages — a projected price before you plant, and a harvest price at the end. RMA decides when each one is taken, and it is not the same month everywhere.

Corn and soybean harvest prices are set in October.

Across most of the country. Eight southern states discover in August instead — pick your state and this page will only show you yours.

What your guarantee is worth

Revenue Protection starts at APH yield × coverage level × projected price, and rises to the harvest price if that average finishes higher. It never recalculates down.

Fifteen years of projected vs. harvest

The question every Revenue Protection decision quietly asks: how often does the harvest price finish below the projected price? For corn, 10 of the last 15 crop years (2011–2025). For soybeans, 10 of 15. That history is why the price side of RP exists.

A discovery price is a plain average of daily settlements — about 19 trading days in February, 22 in October — so early in a window one limit move swings it hard, and by mid-month the same move barely nudges it. That is why every in-window figure on this page is labelled a running estimate and carries its day count.

Key figures at a glance

  • The 2026 projected price is $4.62 for corn and $11.09 for soybeans (February average of December corn / November soybean CBOT settlements). Per-state projected prices differ — Georgia's 2026 corn projected price is $4.42.
  • Corn’s harvest price has finished below its projected price in 10 of the last 15 crop years (2011–2025); soybeans’ in 10 of 15.
  • The biggest single-year harvest rally was 2012 corn: $5.68 projected → $7.50 harvest, lifting standard Revenue Protection guarantees (policies without the harvest-price exclusion) by 32% in one October.
  • The steepest percentage fall was 2014 corn: $4.62 → $3.49 (−24%), the kind of year the price side of RP is bought for.

Common questions

How is the projected price set?

It's the average daily settlement of the December corn (or November soybean) futures contract across every trading day in February, rounded to the nearest cent. That number, times your coverage level, times your APH yield, is your minimum per-acre revenue guarantee — under Revenue Protection it rises to the harvest price if October averages higher.

How is the harvest price set?

The same contract, averaged across every trading day in October. Revenue Protection uses the higher of the projected or harvest price, so an October rally raises your guarantee; a decline does not lower it.

How often does the harvest price come in below the projected price?

Across 2011–2025, corn’s harvest price finished below its projected price in 10 of 15 crop years, and soybeans’ in 10 of 15. A lower harvest price never lowers a Revenue Protection guarantee — the policy uses the higher of the two — but it is central to how price-loss indemnities are figured. The full year-by-year table is on this page; verify official figures at prices.rma.usda.gov and talk to your own agent about what it means for your policy.

Why do other crops show different months?

Each crop — and sometimes each region within a crop — has its own contract and discovery window under RMA's Commodity Exchange Price Provisions. Winter wheat keys off the July contract of harvest year — Kansas City for hard red winter states, Chicago for soft red — discovered in a late-summer window before planting; spring wheat keys off September Minneapolis futures. Exact windows vary by state; those tracks are added here once verified.

What about cotton and peanuts?

Both are here, and pick your state to see them. Cotton discovers against the ICE December contract in 17 states — a projected price of $0.6900 /lb set January 15 to February 14, and a harvest window that is September, October or November depending on the state. Peanuts run on the same calendar in 13 states, priced separately by type: Runners $0.2455 /lb, Virginia $0.2848, Spanish $0.3191. The difference is that peanuts have no exchange and no contract month behind them — RMA sets that price without a futures board, so there is a window but nothing to watch trade during it.

Are these the official RMA prices?

No. These are RMA's own published figures, mirrored from their price discovery service. While a window is open, the figure is RMA's running average to date, not a final price, and it is labelled that way. The official price is RMA's alone — verify at prices.rma.usda.gov and talk to your agent.
