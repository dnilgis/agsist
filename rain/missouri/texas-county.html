The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 4 miles north of Licking (station ID 234919), 17 miles from the middle of Texas County. It reported 279 of the 282 days from Jan 1 to Oct 9; the 3 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 279 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 3 stations inside Texas County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Texas County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

Drier so far. The weather station 4 miles north of Licking had 30.24 inches from Jan 1 to Oct 9, 82% of the usual 36.94 inches for the same days.

What was the wettest month in Texas County in 2026?

April, with 7.58 inches at the weather station 4 miles north of Licking, counting Jan 1 through Oct 9.

What was the biggest rain in Texas County in 2026?

3.64 inches on Mar 5, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Oct 9. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Texas County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 9, the weather station 4 miles north of Licking had 43.86 inches in 2025 and 30.24 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 3 days). On the 279 days both years reported, 2026 had 13.62 inches less than 2025.
