How much rain has Daviess County, Missouri, had in 2026?

32.41 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 1.1 miles west-northwest of Gallatin, Daviess County, through Oct 18. That is 2.13 inches more than a usual year to date (107% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 1.1 miles west-northwest of Gallatin had its biggest one-day reading on Sep 16, at 2.76 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 19 days, from Jan 26 to Feb 13, with not even a trace in the gauge. On the days both years reported, this year is 3.46 inches more than last year. That puts it 2.14 inches more than a usual year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 1.1 miles west-northwest of Gallatin (gauge ID US1MOFSA061), 3 miles from the middle of Daviess County. It reported 276 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 15 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 276 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Daviess County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
