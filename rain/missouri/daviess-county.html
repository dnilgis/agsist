2026 so far in Daviess County

  • Biggest one-day reading: 2.76 inches on Sep 16.
  • Longest dry stretch: 19 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Jan 26 to Feb 13.
  • 2025 by the same date: 29.24 inches at the same gauge (it missed 4 days). On the 284 days both years reported, 2026 had 2.98 inches more than 2025.
  • On 2 reports the observer read the gauge after more than one day. That rain counts on the day it was read, so it is in the totals but not split by day.
  • Across the county: about 43.56 inches, 121% of usual, Jan 1 to Nov 8, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over Daviess County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 16 points above this gauge: rain falls unevenly, and one gauge can catch or miss a storm.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 1.1 miles west-northwest of Gallatin (gauge ID US1MOFSA061), 3 miles from the middle of Daviess County. It reported 288 of the 306 days from Jan 1 to Nov 2; the 18 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 288 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Daviess County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
