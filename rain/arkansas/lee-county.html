The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 2 miles south of Marianna (station ID 034638), 4 miles from the middle of Lee County. It reported 270 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 16 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 270 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Lee County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Lee County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

Drier so far. The weather station 2 miles south of Marianna had 27.28 inches from Jan 1 to Oct 13, 73% of the usual 37.20 inches for the same days.

What was the biggest rain in Lee County in 2026?

2.17 inches on Feb 15, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Oct 13. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Lee County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 13, the weather station 2 miles south of Marianna had 51.11 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 7 days) and 27.28 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 16 days). On the 265 days both years reported, 2026 had 16.64 inches less than 2025.
