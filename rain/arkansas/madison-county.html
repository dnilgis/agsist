How much rain has Madison County, Arkansas, had in 2026?

46.85 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station 2 miles south of Kingston, Madison County, through Oct 18. That is 6.04 inches more than a usual year to date (115% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station 2 miles south of Kingston had a lot of its year early. January was the wettest month at 11.41 inches, and Jan 25 alone brought 6.00 inches, the biggest single day. The longest dry stretch ran 15 days, Aug 9 to Aug 23, with not even a trace in the gauge. On the days both years reported, this year is 5.61 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 2 miles south of Kingston (station ID 033909), 12 miles from the middle of Madison County. It reported every one of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 291 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Madison County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
