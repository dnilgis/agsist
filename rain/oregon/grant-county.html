How much rain has Grant County, Oregon, had in 2026?

5.31 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station 35 miles west-northwest of John Day, Grant County, through Oct 10. That is 1.93 inches less than a usual year to date (73% of usual).

Through Oct 10, the weather station 35 miles west-northwest of John Day has gone 47 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, from Jul 17 to Sep 1. September came right after and was the wettest month at 1.56 inches. The biggest single day was 0.46 inches on Jun 26. That leaves the year 0.93 inches less than last year at this point, and 1.93 inches less than a usual year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 35 miles west-northwest of John Day (station ID 04125), 32 miles from the middle of Grant County. It reported every one of the 283 days from Jan 1 to Oct 10. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 283 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 8 stations inside Grant County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
