The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 6.3 miles south of Monmouth (gauge ID US1ORPK0031), 13 miles from the middle of Polk County. It reported 280 of the 282 days from Jan 1 to Oct 9; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 280 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 5 stations inside Polk County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Polk County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

What was the wettest month in Polk County in 2026?

February, with 4.96 inches at the volunteer rain gauge 6.3 miles south of Monmouth, counting Jan 1 through Oct 9.

What was the biggest rain in Polk County in 2026?

1.50 inches on Feb 24, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Oct 9. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Polk County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 9, the volunteer rain gauge 6.3 miles south of Monmouth had 20.44 inches in 2025 and 18.33 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 2 days). On the 280 days both years reported, 2026 had 2.11 inches less than 2025.
