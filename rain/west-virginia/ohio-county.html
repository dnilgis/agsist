How much rain has Ohio County, West Virginia, had in 2026?

39.48 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in Pike Island, Ohio County, through Oct 18. That is 8.01 inches more than a usual year to date (125% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station in Pike Island had August as the wettest month at 10.77 inches, and the biggest single day came Aug 11 with 2.71 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 11 days, Jan 27 to Feb 6, with nothing in the gauge. On the days both years reported, this year is 6.49 inches more than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Pike Island (station ID 467018), 6 miles from the middle of Ohio County. It reported 272 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 19 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 272 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 2 stations inside Ohio County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
