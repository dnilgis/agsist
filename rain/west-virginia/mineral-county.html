How much rain has Mineral County, West Virginia, had in 2026?

34.79 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 averaged over Mineral County, Jan 1 to Nov 26, 98% of normal. That is a county-wide estimate (the PRISM 4 km weather grid averaged over the county), not one gauge, against NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal.

LatelyestimateLast 24 hours 1.94 in, 7 days 1.95 in. NOAA radar county average to 2 am Eastern, Nov 27.

One weather station’s gauge, the Keyser station (NOAA daily record): 33.52 inches, Jan 1 to Nov 25, at the weather station 2 miles south-southwest of Keyser. That is 3.53 inches less than a normal year to date (90% of normal). It is not a county-wide figure.

The station

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 2 miles south-southwest of Keyser, 3 miles from the middle of Mineral County. It reported 320 of the 329 days from Jan 1 to Nov 25; the 9 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught, snow melted down to water.

Is Mineral County wetter or drier than normal in 2026?

What was the wettest month in Mineral County in 2026?

July, with 5.13 inches at the weather station 2 miles south-southwest of Keyser, counting Jan 1 through Nov 25.

What was the biggest rain in Mineral County in 2026?

1.83 inches on Oct 11, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Nov 25. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Mineral County?

From Jan 1 to Nov 25, the weather station 2 miles south-southwest of Keyser had 39.60 inches in 2025 and 33.52 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 9 days). On the 320 days both years reported, 2026 had 6.06 inches less than 2025.

Sources and terms

Daily NOAA records through RCC-ACIS, read Nov 27, 2026; updated daily. NOAA’s daily numbers come in a few days late and are sometimes corrected.

Normal is NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily average for this station, added up over the same 320 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Mineral County that reported nearly every day in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
