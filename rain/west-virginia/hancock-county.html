How much rain has Hancock County, West Virginia, had in 2026?

Through Oct 12, the weather station in New Cumberland had 40.15 inches, 8.03 inches more than a usual year. The biggest single day was Mar 27 at 2.08 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 10 days, Sep 23 to Oct 2, with nothing in the gauge. On the days both years reported, this year is 14.15 inches more than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in New Cumberland (station ID 466442), 3 miles from the middle of Hancock County. It reported 272 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 19 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 272 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 3 stations inside Hancock County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
