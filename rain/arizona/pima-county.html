2026 so far in Pima County

  • Wettest month: September, 6.10 inches.
  • Biggest single day: 1.59 inches on Sep 30.
  • Longest dry stretch: 55 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Mar 11 to May 4.
  • On record: 3rd wettest by Nov 1 of 32 years on record here (1983 on); the last wetter year was 1984, the last drier one 2024. Years with too many missed days are not counted.
  • Across the county: about 9.47 inches, 97% of usual, Jan 1 to Nov 1, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over Pima County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 55 points below this station: rain falls unevenly, and one station can catch or miss a storm.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 17 miles northwest of Tucson (station ID 028795), 36 miles from the middle of Pima County. It reported every one of the 305 days from Jan 1 to Nov 1. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 305 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 102 stations inside Pima County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
