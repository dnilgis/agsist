How much rain has Lawrence County, South Dakota, had in 2026?

17.32 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in Lead, Lawrence County, through Oct 13. That is 9.43 inches less than a usual year to date (65% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station in Lead has caught 10.97 inches less than it had by this day last year. June was the wettest month at 3.21 inches. The biggest single day was May 22, with 1.68 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 12 days, Aug 1 to Aug 12, with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Lead (station ID 394834), 2 miles from the middle of Lawrence County. It reported every one of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 286 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 7 stations inside Lawrence County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
