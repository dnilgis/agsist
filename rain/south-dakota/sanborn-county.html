How much rain has Sanborn County, South Dakota, had in 2026?

18.32 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile west-southwest of Woonsocket, Sanborn County, through Oct 13. This gauge has no NOAA normal, so there is no usual year to compare it with.

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge west-southwest of Woonsocket had its wettest month in August at 4.42 inches, and its biggest one-day reading came Aug 13 with 2.14 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 15 days, Feb 5 to Feb 19, with not even a trace. On the days both years reported, that is 1.70 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile west-southwest of Woonsocket (gauge ID US1SDSB0003), 10 miles from the middle of Sanborn County. It reported every one of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

This gauge has no NOAA normal, so this page gives no usual year or percent for it.

Why this gauge: 3 stations inside Sanborn County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
