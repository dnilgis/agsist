  • 2025 by the same date: 25.43 inches at the same station (it missed 3 days). On the 294 days both years reported, 2026 had 1.31 inches less than 2025.
  • On record: 35th wettest by Oct 24 of 99 years on record here (1902 on); the last wetter year was 2025, the last drier one 2023. Years with too many missed days are not counted.
  • Across the county: about 21.84 inches, 87% of usual, Jan 1 to Oct 28, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over Turner County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 16 points below this station: rain falls unevenly, and one station can catch or miss a storm.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Marion (station ID 395228), 9 miles from the middle of Turner County. It reported every one of the 297 days from Jan 1 to Oct 24. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 297 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Turner County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
