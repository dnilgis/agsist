How much rain has Scioto County, Ohio, had in 2026?

36.13 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in Portsmouth-Sciotoville, Scioto County, through Oct 18. That is 0.68 inches more than a usual year to date (102% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station in Portsmouth-Sciotoville has caught 35.81 inches, 0.84 inches more than a usual year. May was the wettest month at 7.86 inches, and the biggest single day was 2.90 inches on Jul 19. The gauge then went 13 days in a row without a drop from Aug 24 to Sep 5. On the days both years reported, this year is 2.42 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Portsmouth-Sciotoville (station ID 336781), 6 miles from the middle of Scioto County. It reported 290 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 290 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Scioto County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
