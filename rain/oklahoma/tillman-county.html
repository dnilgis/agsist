13.01 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 6.0 miles south-southwest of Frederick, Tillman County, through Oct 3. That is 8.09 inches less than a usual year to date (62% of usual).

Through Oct 3, the volunteer rain gauge 6.0 miles south-southwest of Frederick has 13.01 inches, 8.09 inches less than a usual year. June was the wettest month at 4.10 inches, and the biggest single day was 1.84 inches on Jun 6. Then the gauge went 35 days in a row with nothing in it, not even a trace, from Jul 19 to Aug 22. The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 6.0 miles south-southwest of Frederick (gauge ID US1OKTN0002), 8 miles from the middle of Tillman County. It reported 267 of the 276 days from Jan 1 to Oct 3; the 9 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 267 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 2 stations inside Tillman County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
