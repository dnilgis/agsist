How much rain has Adair County, Oklahoma, had in 2026?

33.76 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north of Bunch, Adair County, through Oct 17. That is 3.69 inches less than a usual year to date (90% of usual).

Through Oct 12, the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north of Bunch has caught 33.04 inches, 3.64 inches less than a usual year. June was the wettest month with 8.95 inches, and the biggest one-day reading was 2.69 inches on Apr 4. The gauge then went 18 days in a row with nothing in it, from Jul 31 to Aug 17. On the days both years reported, 2026 is running 6.30 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north of Bunch (gauge ID US1OKAD0002), 15 miles from the middle of Adair County. It reported 271 of the 290 days from Jan 1 to Oct 17; the 19 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 271 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Adair County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
