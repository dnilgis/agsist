The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north of Bunch (gauge ID US1OKAD0002), 15 miles from the middle of Adair County. It reported 266 of the 285 days from Jan 1 to Oct 12; the 19 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace counts as zero. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 266 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Adair County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Adair County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

What was the biggest rain in Adair County in 2026?

2.69 inches on Apr 4, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Oct 12. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Adair County?
