How much rain has McPherson County, Nebraska, had in 2026?

17.25 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 11.3 miles east-northeast of Tryon, McPherson County, through Oct 13. That is 5.16 inches less than a usual year to date (77% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 11.3 miles east-northeast of Tryon had its wettest month in July with 4.28 inches. The biggest single day was May 31 at 1.82 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 17 days, Mar 16 to Apr 1, with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace. On the days both years reported, this year sits 5.60 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 11.3 miles east-northeast of Tryon (gauge ID US1NEMP0001), 16 miles from the middle of McPherson County. It reported 281 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 5 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 281 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside McPherson County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
