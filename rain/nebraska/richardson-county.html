How much rain has Richardson County, Nebraska, had in 2026?

35.27 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station 2 miles southwest of Shubert, Richardson County, through Oct 18. That is 4.76 inches more than a usual year to date (116% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station 2 miles southwest of Shubert had September as its wettest month at 9.65 inches, and the biggest single day came Sep 14 with 2.83 inches. Early on it went 19 days with nothing in the gauge, from Jan 26 to Feb 13. Even so, the year is 3.20 inches behind last year at this point.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 2 miles southwest of Shubert (station ID 257800), 6 miles from the middle of Richardson County. It reported every one of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 291 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 10 stations inside Richardson County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
