How much rain has Washington County, Nebraska, had in 2026?

30.73 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Blair, Washington County, through Oct 18. This gauge has no NOAA normal, so there is no usual year to compare it with.

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Blair has caught 29.63 inches. September was the wettest month at 7.65 inches, and the biggest single day was 2.14 inches on Oct 1. On the days both years reported, that is 10.17 inches less than last year. The longest dry stretch ran 14 days, Feb 6 to Feb 19, with not even a trace.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Blair (gauge ID US1NEWS0004), 4 miles from the middle of Washington County. It reported every one of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

This gauge has no NOAA normal, so this page gives no usual year or percent for it.

Why this gauge: 3 stations inside Washington County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
