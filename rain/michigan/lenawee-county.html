The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 2 miles north-northeast of Adrian (station ID 200032), 3 miles from the middle of Lenawee County. It reported 286 of the 287 days from Jan 1 to Oct 14; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 286 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 2 stations inside Lenawee County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Lenawee County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Lenawee County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 14, the weather station 2 miles north-northeast of Adrian had 26.69 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 3 days) and 30.35 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 1 day). On the 283 days both years reported, 2026 had 3.46 inches more than 2025.
