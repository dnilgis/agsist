How much rain has Kenedy County, Texas, had in 2026?

20.69 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station 4 miles southeast of Armstrong, Kenedy County, through Oct 18. That is 2.48 inches less than a usual year to date (89% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station 4 miles southeast of Armstrong shows 1.94 inches less than a usual year. The gauge went 54 days in a row with nothing in it, from Jul 18 to Sep 9. October has been the wettest month at 6.38 inches, with the biggest single day 3.46 inches on Oct 4. On the days both years reported, this year has 2.18 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 4 miles southeast of Armstrong (station ID 410345), 6 miles from the middle of Kenedy County. It reported 287 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 4 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 287 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Kenedy County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
