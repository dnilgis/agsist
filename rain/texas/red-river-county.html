Then it went 24 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, from Aug 19 to Sep 11. On the days both years reported, 2026 sits 2.91 inches less than 2025.

  • Longest dry stretch: 24 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Aug 19 to Sep 11.

  • On 2 reports the observer read the gauge after more than one day. That rain counts on the day it was read, so it is in the totals but not split by day.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 5 miles north-northwest of Avery (station ID 410443), 14 miles from the middle of Red River County. It reported 281 of the 283 days from Jan 1 to Oct 10; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 281 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 2 stations inside Red River County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
