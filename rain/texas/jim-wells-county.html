How much rain has Jim Wells County, Texas, had in 2026?

30.69 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station 4 miles south-southwest of Mathis, Jim Wells County, through Oct 18. That is 8.03 inches more than a usual year to date (135% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station 4 miles south-southwest of Mathis had 30.69 inches, 8.50 more than a usual year. May was the wettest month at 9.96 inches. Then the gauge stayed empty for 46 days in a row from Aug 4 to Sep 18, and on Oct 2 it caught 6.02 inches, the biggest single day. On the days both years reported, that is 10.37 inches more than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 4 miles south-southwest of Mathis (station ID 415661), 25 miles from the middle of Jim Wells County. It reported 289 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 289 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 3 stations inside Jim Wells County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
