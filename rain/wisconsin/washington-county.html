How much rain has Washington County, Wisconsin, had in 2026?

36.23 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in West Bend Public Works, Washington County, through Oct 18. That is 5.57 inches more than a usual year to date (118% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station in West Bend Public Works had its wettest month in April, with 8.06 inches. The biggest single day came Jul 9 with 2.86 inches. Then the gauge sat empty for 17 days in a row, Jul 10 to Jul 26, not even a trace. The year is running 1.26 inches more than 2025 did by the same day.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in West Bend Public Works (station ID 479052), 3 miles from the middle of Washington County. It reported every one of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 291 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 12 stations inside Washington County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
