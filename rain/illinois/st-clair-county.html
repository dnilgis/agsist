How much rain has St. Clair County, Illinois, had in 2026?

36.72 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 1.5 miles south-southeast of Mascoutah, St. Clair County, through Oct 18. That is 0.29 inches less than a usual year to date (99% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 1.5 miles south-southeast of Mascoutah caught its most in April, 6.23 inches. The biggest single day was May 19 with 2.05 inches. From Aug 26 to Sep 9 it went 15 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace. On the days both years reported, that is 2.67 inches more than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 1.5 miles south-southeast of Mascoutah (gauge ID US1ILSC0026), 7 miles from the middle of St. Clair County. It reported every one of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 291 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 10 stations inside St. Clair County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
