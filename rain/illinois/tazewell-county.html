How much rain has Tazewell County, Illinois, had in 2026?

39.45 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 1 mile south of Morton, Tazewell County, through Oct 13. That is 7.81 inches more than a usual year to date (125% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 1 mile south of Morton had June as its wettest month at 10.08 inches, and the biggest single day came Jun 11 with 2.72 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 10 days, Feb 3 to Feb 12, with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace. On the days both years reported, this year is 16.50 inches more than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 1 mile south of Morton (gauge ID US1ILTZ0001), 7 miles from the middle of Tazewell County. It reported every one of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 286 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 10 stations inside Tazewell County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
