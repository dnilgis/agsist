How much rain has Hancock County, Illinois, had in 2026?

33.82 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in Augusta, Hancock County, through Oct 13. That is about the same as a usual year to date (100% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station in Augusta has caught about what it does in a usual year. June did most of the work at 9.31 inches, and the biggest single day was Jun 22 with 2.71 inches. Then it shut off for 20 days in a row, from Aug 21 to Sep 9, with not even a trace in the gauge. That still puts it 7.48 inches more than last year at this point.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Augusta (station ID 110330), 16 miles from the middle of Hancock County. It reported every one of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 286 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 6 stations inside Hancock County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
