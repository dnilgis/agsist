How much rain has Caroline County, Maryland, had in 2026?

34.45 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 5.8 miles west-southwest of Denton, Caroline County, through Oct 18. That is 2.49 inches less than a usual year to date (93% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 5.8 miles west-southwest of Denton had July as its wettest month at 9.60 inches. The biggest single day was Jul 22 with 3.40 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 20 days, Jan 27 to Feb 15, with nothing in the gauge. On the days both years reported, this year is 0.82 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 5.8 miles west-southwest of Denton (gauge ID US1MDCL0010), 5 miles from the middle of Caroline County. It reported 282 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 9 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 282 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Caroline County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
