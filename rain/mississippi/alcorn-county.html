The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 5 miles west-southwest of Corinth (station ID 221962), 3 miles from the middle of Alcorn County. It reported 278 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 8 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace counts as zero. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 278 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 3 stations inside Alcorn County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Alcorn County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

Drier so far. The weather station 5 miles west-southwest of Corinth had 34.86 inches from Jan 1 to Oct 13, 79% of the usual 44.35 inches for the same days.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Alcorn County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 13, the weather station 5 miles west-southwest of Corinth had 43.16 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 3 days) and 34.86 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 8 days). On the 275 days both years reported, 2026 had 8.46 inches less than 2025.
