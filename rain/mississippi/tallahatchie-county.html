How much rain has Tallahatchie County, Mississippi, had in 2026?

30.91 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 3.5 miles southwest of Charleston, Tallahatchie County, through Oct 13. That is 13.27 inches less than a usual year to date (70% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 3.5 miles southwest of Charleston had 30.91 inches, 13.27 inches less than a usual year. January was the wettest month at 5.59 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 18 days, Sep 14 to Oct 1, with not even a trace in the gauge. On the days both years reported, this year is 4.64 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 3.5 miles southwest of Charleston (gauge ID US1MSTL0001), 5 miles from the middle of Tallahatchie County. It reported 281 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 5 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 281 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Tallahatchie County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Tallahatchie County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

What was the wettest month in Tallahatchie County in 2026?

January, with 5.59 inches at the volunteer rain gauge 3.5 miles southwest of Charleston, counting Jan 1 through Oct 13.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Tallahatchie County?
