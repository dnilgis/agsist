How much rain has Monroe County, Georgia, had in 2026?

36.27 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 8.3 miles south of Forsyth, Monroe County, through Oct 11. That is 3.60 inches less than a usual year to date (91% of usual).

Through Oct 11, the volunteer rain gauge 8.3 miles south of Forsyth had June as its wettest month at 6.00 inches, and the biggest single day came Jun 19 with 2.74 inches. From Apr 7 to Apr 25 it went 19 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace. On the days both years reported, this year sits 4.17 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 8.3 miles south of Forsyth (gauge ID US1GAMN0010), 7 miles from the middle of Monroe County. It reported 281 of the 284 days from Jan 1 to Oct 11; the 3 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 281 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Monroe County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
