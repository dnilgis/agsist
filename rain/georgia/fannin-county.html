2026 so far in Fannin County

  • Wettest month: August, 11.86 inches.
  • Biggest one-day reading: 6.13 inches on Nov 5.
  • Longest dry stretch: 14 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Oct 18 to Oct 31.
  • 2025 by the same date: 53.46 inches at the same gauge. 2026 had 8.38 inches more than 2025.
  • On 5 reports the observer read the gauge after more than one day. That rain counts on the day it was read, so it is in the totals but not split by day.
  • Across the county: about 53.01 inches, 100% of usual, Jan 1 to Nov 10, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over Fannin County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 15 points below this gauge: rain falls unevenly, and one gauge can catch or miss a storm.

The station

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 9.4 miles west-northwest of Blue Ridge, 10 miles from the middle of Fannin County. It reported every one of the 314 days from Jan 1 to Nov 10. Rain here means everything the gauge caught, snow melted down to water.

Sources and terms

Daily NOAA records through RCC-ACIS, read Nov 11, 2026; updated daily. NOAA’s daily numbers come in a few days late and are sometimes corrected. Volunteer gauge data: CoCoRaHS.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 314 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 4 stations inside Fannin County reported nearly every day in 2026, and this is the one used. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
