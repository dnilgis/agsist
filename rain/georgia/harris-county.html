How much rain has Harris County, Georgia, had in 2026?

40.02 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 6.9 miles south of Waverly Hall, Harris County, through Oct 10. That is 0.35 inches more than a usual year to date (101% of usual).

Through Oct 10, the gauge south of Waverly Hall sits about where a usual year would, 0.35 inches more. June was the wettest month at 7.61 inches, and the biggest rain came May 7 with 3.98 inches. Before that, the gauge went 19 days in a row with nothing in it, Apr 7 to Apr 25. On the days both years reported, this year is 2.35 inches more than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 6.9 miles south of Waverly Hall (gauge ID US1GAHR0007), 14 miles from the middle of Harris County. It reported 270 of the 283 days from Jan 1 to Oct 10; the 13 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 270 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Harris County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
