35.54 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 6.8 miles east-northeast of Macon, Jones County, through Oct 18. That is 4.85 inches less than a usual year to date (88% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 6.8 miles east-northeast of Macon has caught 35.52 inches, 4.35 inches less than a usual year. July was the wettest month at 5.76 inches, and the biggest one-day reading was 2.80 inches on Aug 7. The gauge went 22 days in a row with nothing in it, from Apr 7 to Apr 28. The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 6.8 miles east-northeast of Macon (gauge ID US1GAJN0006), 10 miles from the middle of Jones County. It reported 289 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 289 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Jones County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
