The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 6.8 miles east-northeast of Macon (gauge ID US1GAJN0006), 10 miles from the middle of Jones County. It reported 284 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 284 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Jones County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Jones County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

Drier so far. The volunteer rain gauge 6.8 miles east-northeast of Macon had 35.52 inches from Jan 1 to Oct 13, 89% of the usual 39.87 inches for the same days.

What was the wettest month in Jones County in 2026?

July, with 5.76 inches at the volunteer rain gauge 6.8 miles east-northeast of Macon, counting Jan 1 through Oct 13.

What was the biggest rain in Jones County in 2026?

2.80 inches on Aug 7, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Oct 13. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Jones County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 6.8 miles east-northeast of Macon had 42.91 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 3 days) and 35.52 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 2 days). On the 281 days both years reported, 2026 had 7.15 inches less than 2025.
