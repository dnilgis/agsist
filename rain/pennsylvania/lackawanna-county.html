How much rain has Lackawanna County, Pennsylvania, had in 2026?

35.97 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile northeast of Richmondale, Lackawanna County, through Oct 13. This gauge has no NOAA normal, so there is no usual year to compare it with.

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile northeast of Richmondale had its wettest month in July, with 5.79 inches. The biggest single day came Jul 6 at 2.00 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 7 days, Oct 4 to Oct 10, without even a trace in the gauge. On the days both years reported, this year is 3.31 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile northeast of Richmondale (gauge ID US1PALC0027), 16 miles from the middle of Lackawanna County. It reported 285 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

This gauge has no NOAA normal, so this page gives no usual year or percent for it.

Why this gauge: 2 stations inside Lackawanna County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
