How much rain has Armstrong County, Pennsylvania, had in 2026?

49.17 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 3.1 miles north-northwest of Shelocta, Armstrong County, through Oct 13. That is 12.98 inches more than a usual year to date (136% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 3.1 miles north-northwest of Shelocta had 12.98 inches more than a usual year. August did the most of it with 8.54 inches, and Aug 3 alone put 2.23 inches in the gauge. The longest dry stretch ran 9 days, May 29 to Jun 6. Last year at this point the gauge sat 12.92 inches behind this year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 3.1 miles north-northwest of Shelocta (gauge ID US1PAAR0008), 11 miles from the middle of Armstrong County. It reported every one of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 286 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 6 stations inside Armstrong County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
