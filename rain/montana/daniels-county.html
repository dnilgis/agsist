How much rain has Daniels County, Montana, had in 2026?

9.09 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station 4 miles northwest of Scobey, Daniels County, through Oct 12. That is 2.31 inches less than a usual year to date (80% of usual).

Through Oct 12, the weather station 4 miles northwest of Scobey had June as its wettest month at 4.55 inches. The gauge then sat empty for 29 days in a row, from Jul 15 to Aug 12. The biggest single day came later, 3.30 inches on Sep 7. That leaves 2026 at 2.00 inches more than 2025 by the same day.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 4 miles northwest of Scobey (station ID 247425), 5 miles from the middle of Daniels County. It reported 257 of the 285 days from Jan 1 to Oct 12; the 28 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 257 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 2 stations inside Daniels County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
