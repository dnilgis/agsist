The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Cut Bank (gauge ID US1MTGR0006), 31 miles from the middle of Glacier County. It reported 284 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 7 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 284 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 2 stations inside Glacier County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Glacier County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

Wetter so far. The volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Cut Bank had 14.89 inches from Jan 1 to Oct 18, 115% of the usual 12.98 inches for the same days.

What was the wettest month in Glacier County in 2026?

What was the biggest rain in Glacier County in 2026?

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Glacier County?
