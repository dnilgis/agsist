How much rain has Granite County, Montana, had in 2026?

14.68 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station less than a mile south-southwest of Dummond, Granite County, through Oct 18. That is 5.09 inches more than a usual year to date (153% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station less than a mile south-southwest of Dummond has caught 14.68 inches, 5.23 more than a usual year. September was the wettest month at 3.05 inches, and the biggest single day was Mar 14 with 0.96. It went 21 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, from Jan 8 to Jan 28. On the days both years reported, this year is 5.04 inches more than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station less than a mile south-southwest of Dummond (station ID 24139), 23 miles from the middle of Granite County. It reported 276 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 15 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 276 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 6 stations inside Granite County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
