2026 so far in Utah County

  • Wettest month: April, 2.90 inches.
  • Biggest single day: 1.21 inches on Apr 1.
  • Longest dry stretch: 25 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Mar 7 to Mar 31.
  • 2025 by the same date: 15.85 inches at the same station (it missed 1 day). On the 300 days both years reported, 2026 had 1.88 inches less than 2025.
  • On record: 55th wettest by Oct 28 of 95 years on record here (1928 on); the last wetter year was 2025, the last drier one 2022. Years with too many missed days are not counted.
  • Across the county: about 15.53 inches, 101% of usual, Jan 1 to Oct 28, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over Utah County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 15 points above this station: rain falls unevenly, and one station can catch or miss a storm.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Spanish Fork Power House (station ID 428119), 4 miles from the middle of Utah County. It reported every one of the 301 days from Jan 1 to Oct 28. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 301 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 14 stations inside Utah County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
