2026 so far in Oliver County

  • Wettest month: June, 2.41 inches.
  • Biggest single day: 1.30 inches on May 31.
  • Longest dry stretch: 32 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Jan 1 to Feb 1.
  • 2025 by the same date: 19.65 inches at the same station (it missed 5 days). On the 280 days both years reported, 2026 had 10.98 inches less than 2025.
  • On record: 57th wettest by Oct 26 of 58 years on record here (1943 on); the last wetter year was 2025, the last drier one 1988. Years with too many missed days are not counted.
  • Across the county: about 12.81 inches, 76% of usual, Jan 1 to Nov 3, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over Oliver County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 24 points above this station: rain falls unevenly, and one station can catch or miss a storm.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 4 miles southeast of Center (station ID 321456), 7 miles from the middle of Oliver County. It reported 285 of the 299 days from Jan 1 to Oct 26; the 14 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 285 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Oliver County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
