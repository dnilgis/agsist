The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Liberal (station ID 144695), 12 miles from the middle of Seward County. It reported 284 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 284 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 2 stations inside Seward County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Seward County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

Drier so far. The weather station in Liberal had 14.52 inches from Jan 1 to Oct 13, 80% of the usual 18.26 inches for the same days.

What was the wettest month in Seward County in 2026?

June, with 4.15 inches at the weather station in Liberal, counting Jan 1 through Oct 13.

What was the biggest rain in Seward County in 2026?

1.35 inches on Jun 4, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Oct 13. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Seward County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 13, the weather station in Liberal had 22.20 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 6 days) and 14.52 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 2 days). On the 278 days both years reported, 2026 had 8.31 inches less than 2025.
