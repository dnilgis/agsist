How much rain has Meade County, Kansas, had in 2026?

12.98 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile west-northwest of Meade, Meade County, through Oct 18. That is 7.27 inches less than a usual year to date (64% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile west-northwest of Meade had 12.74 inches, 7.14 inches less than a usual year. June was the wettest month at 4.85 inches, and the biggest single day was 1.08 inches on Jun 22. The gauge went 26 days in a row with nothing, not even a trace, from Mar 7 to Apr 1. Last year by Oct 13 it had 23.90 inches, so this year is 11.16 inches less.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile west-northwest of Meade (gauge ID US1KSME0010), 4 miles from the middle of Meade County. It reported every one of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 291 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 4 stations inside Meade County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
