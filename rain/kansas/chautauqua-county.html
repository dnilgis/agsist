Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 2.8 miles south of Peru had June as its wettest month at 7.50 inches. The biggest single day was Apr 2 with 3.45 inches. From Aug 24 to Sep 9 it went 17 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace. On the days both years reported, that is 14.93 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 2.8 miles south of Peru (gauge ID US1KSCQ0003), 11 miles from the middle of Chautauqua County. It reported 288 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 3 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

This gauge has no NOAA normal, so this page gives no usual year or percent for it.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Chautauqua County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
