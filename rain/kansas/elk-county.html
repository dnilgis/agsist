How much rain has Elk County, Kansas, had in 2026?

28.02 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station 1 mile north of Grenola, Elk County, through Oct 13. That is 5.73 inches less than a usual year to date (83% of usual).

Through Oct 3, the weather station 1 mile north of Grenola had its wettest month in June with 8.69 inches, and its biggest one-day reading was 3.52 inches on Jun 26. After that it went 20 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, from Aug 22 to Sep 10. On the days both years reported, this year is 2.98 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 1 mile north of Grenola (station ID 143248), 13 miles from the middle of Elk County. It reported 284 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 284 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 2 stations inside Elk County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
