The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the CoCoRaHS volunteer gauge 3.6 miles south-southeast of Aurora (gauge ID US1INOH0001), 6 miles from the middle of Ohio County. It reported 266 of the 280 days from Jan 1 to Oct 7; the 14 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace counts as zero. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 266 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. CoCoRaHS began in 1998, so no gauge has 30 years of its own, and NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 2 stations inside Ohio County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. The pick goes to one with NOAA normals first, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
