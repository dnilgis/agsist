How much rain has Cheatham County, Tennessee, had in 2026?

32.61 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 6.3 miles east-northeast of Ashland City, Cheatham County, through Oct 11. This gauge has no NOAA normal, so there is no usual year to compare it with.

Through Oct 11, the volunteer rain gauge 6.3 miles east-northeast of Ashland City caught 8.34 inches in July, its wettest month. The biggest single day was Jul 22 at 2.32 inches. Then it went 14 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, Aug 28 to Sep 10. On the days both years reported, that is 13.11 inches less than 2025 had.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 6.3 miles east-northeast of Ashland City (gauge ID US1TNCH0032), 8 miles from the middle of Cheatham County. It reported 283 of the 284 days from Jan 1 to Oct 11; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

This gauge has no NOAA normal, so this page gives no usual year or percent for it.

Why this gauge: 4 stations inside Cheatham County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
