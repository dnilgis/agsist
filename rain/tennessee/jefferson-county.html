The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the CoCoRaHS volunteer gauge 2.0 miles northeast of White Pine (gauge ID US1TNJF0004), 11 miles from the middle of Jefferson County. It reported 268 of the 277 days from Jan 1 to Oct 4; the 9 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace counts as zero. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 268 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. CoCoRaHS began in 1998, so no gauge has 30 years of its own, and NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Jefferson County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
