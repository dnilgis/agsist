The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles east-southeast of Atoka (gauge ID US1TNTP0003), 5 miles from the middle of Tipton County. It reported 274 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 12 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 274 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Tipton County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Tipton County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

Drier so far. The volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles east-southeast of Atoka had 31.52 inches from Jan 1 to Oct 13, 76% of the usual 41.61 inches for the same days.

What was the wettest month in Tipton County in 2026?

June, with 6.52 inches at the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles east-southeast of Atoka, counting Jan 1 through Oct 13.

What was the biggest rain in Tipton County in 2026?

2.70 inches on Jun 23, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Oct 13. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Tipton County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles east-southeast of Atoka had 50.57 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 7 days) and 31.52 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 12 days). On the 267 days both years reported, 2026 had 19.53 inches less than 2025.
