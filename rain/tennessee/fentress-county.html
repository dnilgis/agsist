38.18 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 12.9 miles south of Jamestown, Fentress County, through Oct 18. That is 5.75 inches less than a usual year to date (87% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 12.9 miles south of Jamestown has been running 5.22 inches less than a usual year. June was the wettest month at 6.95 inches, and the biggest one-day reading came Jun 28 with 3.00 inches. From Aug 24 to Sep 5 the gauge went 13 days in a row with nothing in it, not even a trace. On the days both years reported, this year is 11.48 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 12.9 miles south of Jamestown (gauge ID US1TNFN0005), 9 miles from the middle of Fentress County. It reported 288 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 3 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 288 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Fentress County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
