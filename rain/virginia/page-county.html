How much rain has Page County, Virginia, had in 2026?

35.46 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 1.4 miles north of Shenandoah, Page County, through Oct 13. That is 1.41 inches more than a usual year to date (104% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 1.4 miles north of Shenandoah had its wettest month in August with 7.16 inches, and the biggest single day came Aug 23 with 3.13 inches. Early on it went 19 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, from Jan 28 to Feb 15. On the days both years reported, this year has 5.68 inches more than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 1.4 miles north of Shenandoah (gauge ID US1VAPG0001), 11 miles from the middle of Page County. It reported 281 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the 5 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 281 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 2 stations inside Page County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
