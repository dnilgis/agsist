How much rain has Rappahannock County, Virginia, had in 2026?

34.60 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in Sperryville, Rappahannock County, through Oct 18. That is 2.33 inches less than a usual year to date (94% of usual).

Through Oct 8, the weather station in Sperryville had September as its wettest month at 4.64 inches. The biggest one-day reading came Jul 29 with 1.77 inches. The longest dry stretch ran 9 days, Jan 1 to Jan 9, with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace. That leaves the station 4.60 inches less than a usual year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Sperryville (station ID 447985), 4 miles from the middle of Rappahannock County. It reported 268 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 23 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 268 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Rappahannock County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
