2026 so far in Lincoln County

  • Wettest month: October, 1.82 inches.
  • Biggest one-day reading: 0.62 inches on Oct 11.
  • Longest dry stretch: 77 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Apr 28 to Jul 13.
  • 2025 by the same date: 2.35 inches at the same station (it missed 1 day). On the 299 days both years reported, 2026 had 1.57 inches more than 2025.
  • On 3 reports the observer read the gauge after more than one day. That rain counts on the day it was read, so it is in the totals but not split by day.
  • Across the county: about 6.75 inches, 77% of usual, Jan 1 to Oct 27, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over Lincoln County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 15 points above this station: rain falls unevenly, and one station can catch or miss a storm.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Rachel (station ID 266514), 48 miles from the middle of Lincoln County. It reported every one of the 300 days from Jan 1 to Oct 27. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 300 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 2 stations inside Lincoln County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
