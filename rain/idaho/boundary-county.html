The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 2.0 miles east-southeast of Bonners Ferry (gauge ID US1IDBD0010), 10 miles from the middle of Boundary County. It reported 279 of the 298 days from Jan 1 to Oct 25; the 19 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

This gauge has no NOAA normal, so this page gives no usual year or percent for it.

Why this gauge: 4 stations inside Boundary County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
