How much rain has Bingham County, Idaho, had in 2026?

5.40 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in Aberdeen, Bingham County, through Oct 14. That is 2.02 inches less than a usual year to date (73% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station in Aberdeen had October as its wettest month at 1.31 inches, and 1.15 inches of that came on Oct 12, the biggest single day. Earlier on, the gauge went 34 days in a row with nothing in it, from Jan 6 to Feb 8. On the days both years reported, this year sits 0.62 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Aberdeen (station ID 100010), 28 miles from the middle of Bingham County. It reported 286 of the 287 days from Jan 1 to Oct 14; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 286 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 3 stations inside Bingham County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
