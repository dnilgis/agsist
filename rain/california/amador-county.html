How much rain has Amador County, California, had in 2026?

24.69 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles north of Volcano, Amador County, through Oct 6. That is 4.81 inches less than a usual year to date (84% of usual).

Through Oct 6, the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles north of Volcano had its wettest month in February with 8.38 inches, and the biggest single day came Feb 18 at 2.62 inches. Then it went 39 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, from Jul 20 to Aug 27. On the days both years reported, this year is 0.70 inches more than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles north of Volcano (gauge ID US1CAAM0009), 3 miles from the middle of Amador County. It reported 270 of the 279 days from Jan 1 to Oct 6; the 9 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 270 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 3 stations inside Amador County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
