The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station at Camarillo Airport (station ID 23136), 17 miles from the middle of Ventura County. It reported every one of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 291 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 9 stations inside Ventura County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

How much rain has Ventura County, California, had in 2026?

Is Ventura County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

Drier so far. The weather station at Camarillo Airport had 6.19 inches from Jan 1 to Oct 18, 68% of the usual 9.16 inches for the same days.

What was the wettest month in Ventura County in 2026?

February, with 3.52 inches at the weather station at Camarillo Airport, counting Jan 1 through Oct 18.

What was the biggest rain in Ventura County in 2026?

1.34 inches on Feb 16, 2026, the most the gauge caught in one day this year through Oct 18.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Ventura County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 18, the weather station at Camarillo Airport had 5.62 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 1 day) and 6.19 inches in 2026. On the 290 days both years reported, 2026 had 0.57 inches more than 2025.
