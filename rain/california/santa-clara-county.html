The station

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 1.4 miles southwest of Morgan Hill, 8 miles from the middle of Santa Clara County. It reported every one of the 323 days from Jan 1 to Nov 19. Rain here means everything the gauge caught, snow melted down to water.

Questions

How much rain has Santa Clara County, California, had in 2026?

16.46 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 averaged over Santa Clara County, Jan 1 to Nov 19, 92% of normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km weather grid, against NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal. The Morgan Hill gauge had 17.56 inches, Jan 1 to Nov 19 (88% of normal).

Is Santa Clara County wetter or drier than normal in 2026?

Drier so far. Averaged over Santa Clara County, about 16.46 inches fell from Jan 1 to Nov 19, 92% of the normal 17.86 inches for the same days.

What was the wettest month in Santa Clara County in 2026?

February, with 5.37 inches at the volunteer rain gauge 1.4 miles southwest of Morgan Hill, counting Jan 1 through Nov 19.

What was the biggest rain in Santa Clara County in 2026?

3.22 inches on Nov 19, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Nov 19. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Santa Clara County?

From Jan 1 to Nov 19, the volunteer rain gauge 1.4 miles southwest of Morgan Hill had 20.11 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 3 days) and 17.56 inches in 2026. On the 320 days both years reported, 2026 had 4.60 inches less than 2025.

How many growing degree days has Santa Clara County had in 2026?

3,422 corn GDDs (base 50°F, cap 86°F) May 1 to Nov 19 at a weather station 22 miles from the rain gauge, 104% of its 1991-2020 average for the same days (21 years).

Has Santa Clara County had a fall freeze yet in 2026?

No, not through Nov 19: no day at 32°F or colder since Jul 1 at a weather station 22 miles from the rain gauge.

Sources and terms

Daily NOAA records through RCC-ACIS, read Nov 20, 2026; updated daily. NOAA’s daily numbers come in a few days late and are sometimes corrected. Volunteer gauge data: CoCoRaHS.

Normal is NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily average for this gauge, added up over the same 323 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 12 stations inside Santa Clara County reported nearly every day in 2026, and this is the one used. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
