How much rain has San Juan County, Washington, had in 2026?

20.61 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Orcas in San Juan County, through Nov 9. That is 1.18 inches more than a usual year to date (106% of usual). Averaged over the whole county, about 18.98 inches, 86% of usual.

2026 so far in San Juan County

  • Wettest month: March, 4.86 inches.
  • Biggest single day: 1.39 inches on Jan 12.
  • Longest dry stretch: 19 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Aug 4 to Aug 22.
  • 2025 by the same date: 19.14 inches at the same gauge. 2026 had 1.47 inches more than 2025.
  • Across the county: about 18.98 inches, 86% of usual, Jan 1 to Nov 9, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over San Juan County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 20 points below this gauge: rain falls unevenly, and one gauge can catch or miss a storm.

The station

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Orcas, 3 miles from the middle of San Juan County. It reported every one of the 313 days from Jan 1 to Nov 9. Rain here means everything the gauge caught, snow melted down to water.

Sources and terms

Daily NOAA records through RCC-ACIS, read Nov 10, 2026; updated daily. NOAA’s daily numbers come in a few days late and are sometimes corrected. Volunteer gauge data: CoCoRaHS.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 313 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 14 stations inside San Juan County reported nearly every day in 2026, and this is the one used. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
