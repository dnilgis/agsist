How much rain has Marion County, Iowa, had in 2026?

46.19 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 averaged over Marion County, Jan 1 to Nov 17, 134% of normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km weather grid, against NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal.

Pella station: 44.41 inches, Jan 1 to Nov 15, at the weather station 1 mile south of Pella. That is 10.51 inches more than a normal year to date (131% of normal).

2026 so far in Marion County

  • Wettest month: September, 9.23 inches.
  • Biggest single day: 3.42 inches on Sep 9.
  • Longest dry stretch: 11 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Oct 18 to Oct 28.
  • 2025 by the same date: 30.27 inches at the same station (it missed 3 days). On the 314 days both years reported, 2026 had 14.10 inches more than 2025.
  • On record: 7th wettest by Nov 15 of 80 years on record here (1899 on); the last wetter year was 2014, the last drier one 2025. Years with too many missed days are not counted.

The station

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 1 mile south of Pella, 10 miles from the middle of Marion County. It reported 317 of the 319 days from Jan 1 to Nov 15; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught, snow melted down to water.

Sources and terms

Daily NOAA records through RCC-ACIS, read Nov 18, 2026; updated daily. NOAA’s daily numbers come in a few days late and are sometimes corrected.

Normal is NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily average for this station, added up over the same 317 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Marion County that reported nearly every day in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
