How much rain has Kossuth County, Iowa, had in 2026?

26.43 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile northwest of Burt, Kossuth County, through Oct 16. That is 3.18 inches less than a usual year to date (89% of usual).

Through Oct 9, the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile northwest of Burt had its wettest month in September at 6.12 inches, and its biggest single day came Sep 21 with 1.68 inches. Earlier on, the gauge went 29 days in a row with nothing in it, not even a trace, from Feb 2 to Mar 2. On the days both years reported, this year sits 10.16 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile northwest of Burt (gauge ID US1IAKS0005), 1 mile from the middle of Kossuth County. It reported 282 of the 289 days from Jan 1 to Oct 16; the 7 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 282 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 2 stations inside Kossuth County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
