The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 5.0 miles southeast of Ragley (gauge ID US1LABG0002), 17 miles from the middle of Beauregard Parish. It reported 285 of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 285 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside Beauregard Parish that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
