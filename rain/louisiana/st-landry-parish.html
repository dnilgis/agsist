2026 so far in St. Landry Parish

  • Wettest month: May, 13.86 inches.
  • Biggest one-day reading: 4.06 inches on Mar 8.
  • Longest dry stretch: 16 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Sep 14 to Sep 29.
  • 2025 by the same date: 58.07 inches at the same gauge. On the 281 days both years reported, 2026 had 3.26 inches less than 2025.
  • On 2 reports the observer read the gauge after more than one day. That rain counts on the day it was read, so it is in the totals but not split by day.
  • Across the county: about 48.42 inches, 92% of usual, Jan 1 to Nov 8, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over St. Landry Parish (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal).

The station

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 2.7 miles east of Grand Coteau, 12 miles from the middle of St. Landry Parish. It reported 281 of the 312 days from Jan 1 to Nov 8; the 31 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught, snow melted down to water.

Sources and terms

Daily NOAA records through RCC-ACIS, read Nov 10, 2026; updated daily. NOAA’s daily numbers come in a few days late and are sometimes corrected. Volunteer gauge data: CoCoRaHS.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 281 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: it is the only one inside St. Landry Parish that reported nearly every day in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
