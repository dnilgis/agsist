How much rain has Hoke County, North Carolina, had in 2026?

34.43 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 3.8 miles northwest of Red Springs, Hoke County, through Oct 18. This gauge has no NOAA normal, so there is no usual year to compare it with.

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 3.8 miles northwest of Red Springs had its wettest month in August at 8.33 inches, and its biggest single day came Aug 28 with 4.67 inches. Back in spring it went 19 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, from Apr 7 to Apr 25. On the days both years reported, this year is 1.73 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 3.8 miles northwest of Red Springs (gauge ID US1NCHK0014), 12 miles from the middle of Hoke County. It reported 290 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

This gauge has no NOAA normal, so this page gives no usual year or percent for it.

Why this gauge: 2 stations inside Hoke County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
