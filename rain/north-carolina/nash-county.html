How much rain has Nash County, North Carolina, had in 2026?

31.19 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles west of Nashville, Nash County, through Oct 12. That is 9.44 inches less than a usual year to date (77% of usual).

Through Oct 12, the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles west of Nashville has caught most of its year in July, with 8.69 inches in that wettest month. The biggest single day came Jul 13 at 2.52 inches. Before that, the gauge sat empty for 15 days in a row from May 28 to Jun 11. On the days both years reported, that is 5.80 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 3.2 miles west of Nashville (gauge ID US1NCNS0002), 2 miles from the middle of Nash County. It reported 284 of the 285 days from Jan 1 to Oct 12; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 284 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 6 stations inside Nash County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
