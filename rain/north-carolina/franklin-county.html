How much rain has Franklin County, North Carolina, had in 2026?

30.97 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in Louisburg, Franklin County, through Oct 18. That is 8.17 inches less than a usual year to date (79% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station in Louisburg had July as its wettest month at 13.08 inches, with the biggest single day on Jul 13 at 2.83 inches. Before that, it went 19 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, from Apr 7 to Apr 25. On the days both years reported, this year is 11.86 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Louisburg (station ID 315123), 2 miles from the middle of Franklin County. It reported 285 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the 6 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 285 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 4 stations inside Franklin County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
