How much rain has Sumter County, Florida, had in 2026?

39.05 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 4.3 miles south-southwest of Lady Lake in Sumter County, through Nov 3. That is 6.07 inches less than a usual year to date (87% of usual).

Averaged over the whole county, about 48.70 inches, 104% of usual, Jan 1 to Nov 9.

2026 so far in Sumter County

  • Wettest month: July, 9.71 inches.
  • Biggest one-day reading: 3.59 inches on Jul 5.
  • Longest dry stretch: 23 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, Apr 10 to May 2.
  • 2025 by the same date: 28.45 inches at the same gauge (it missed 1 day). On the 304 days both years reported, 2026 had 10.60 inches more than 2025.
  • On 1 report the observer read the gauge after more than one day. That rain counts on the day it was read, so it is in the totals but not split by day.
  • Across the county: about 48.70 inches, 104% of usual, Jan 1 to Nov 9, from the PRISM 4 km grid averaged over Sumter County (usual: NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal). That is 17 points above this gauge: rain falls unevenly, and one gauge can catch or miss a storm.

The station

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 4.3 miles south-southwest of Lady Lake, 14 miles from the middle of Sumter County. It reported 305 of the 307 days from Jan 1 to Nov 3; the 2 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught, snow melted down to water.

Sources and terms

Daily NOAA records through RCC-ACIS, read Nov 10, 2026; updated daily. NOAA’s daily numbers come in a few days late and are sometimes corrected. Volunteer gauge data: CoCoRaHS.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 305 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 5 stations inside Sumter County reported nearly every day in 2026, and this is the one used. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
