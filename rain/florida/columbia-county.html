The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 7.9 miles south-southwest of Lake City (gauge ID US1FLCB0012), 10 miles from the middle of Columbia County. It reported 272 of the 284 days from Jan 1 to Oct 11; the 12 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 272 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 7 stations inside Columbia County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Columbia County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

What was the wettest month in Columbia County in 2026?

August, with 9.28 inches at the volunteer rain gauge 7.9 miles south-southwest of Lake City, counting Jan 1 through Oct 11.

What was the biggest rain in Columbia County in 2026?

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Columbia County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 11, the volunteer rain gauge 7.9 miles south-southwest of Lake City had 36.77 inches in 2025 and 39.89 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 12 days). On the 272 days both years reported, 2026 had 3.58 inches more than 2025.
