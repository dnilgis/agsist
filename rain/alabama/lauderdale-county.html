The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in Lexington (station ID 010260), 16 miles from the middle of Lauderdale County. It reported every one of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace counts as zero. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 286 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 3 stations inside Lauderdale County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Lauderdale County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

What was the wettest month in Lauderdale County in 2026?

June, with 7.83 inches at the weather station in Lexington, counting Jan 1 through Oct 13.

What was the biggest rain in Lauderdale County in 2026?

4.77 inches on Jun 8, 2026, the biggest one-day reading this year through Oct 13. A reading that covers more than one day is not counted as a one-day rain.

How does 2026 compare with 2025 in Lauderdale County?

From Jan 1 to Oct 13, the weather station in Lexington had 55.42 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 1 day) and 43.35 inches in 2026. On the 285 days both years reported, 2026 had 12.07 inches less than 2025.
