How much rain has Fairbanks North Star Borough, Alaska, had in 2026?

11.58 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station in North Pole, Fairbanks North Star Borough, through Oct 13. That is 0.32 inches more than a usual year to date (103% of usual).

From Mar 15 to Apr 9 it went 26 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace. That still left it 0.74 inches more than last year by Oct 13.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station in North Pole (station ID 506581), 23 miles from the middle of Fairbanks North Star Borough. It reported every one of the 286 days from Jan 1 to Oct 13. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 286 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 17 stations inside Fairbanks North Star Borough reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
