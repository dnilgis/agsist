How much rain has Lee County, Kentucky, had in 2026?

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 4 miles north of Beattyville (station ID 150483), 2 miles from the middle of Lee County. It reported 277 of the 285 days from Jan 1 to Oct 12; the 8 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 277 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: it is the only one inside Lee County that reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.

Is Lee County wetter or drier than usual in 2026?

From Jan 1 to Oct 12, the weather station 4 miles north of Beattyville had 51.44 inches in 2025 (2025 missed 12 days) and 35.42 inches in 2026 (2026 missed 8 days). On the 265 days both years reported, 2026 had 15.43 inches less than 2025.
