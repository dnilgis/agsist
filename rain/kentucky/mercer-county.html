How much rain has Mercer County, Kentucky, had in 2026?

42.36 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the volunteer rain gauge 10.8 miles northwest of Harrodsburg, Mercer County, through Oct 18. That is 1.54 inches more than a usual year to date (104% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the volunteer rain gauge 10.8 miles northwest of Harrodsburg put a lot of its year into June, with 12.36 inches that month. The biggest single day came Jun 28 at 3.41 inches. Then it went 19 days in a row with nothing in the gauge, not even a trace, from Aug 22 to Sep 9. On the days both years reported, that is 17.58 inches less than 2025.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge 10.8 miles northwest of Harrodsburg (gauge ID US1KYME0008), 8 miles from the middle of Mercer County. It reported 290 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this gauge, added up over the same 290 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 2 stations inside Mercer County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes next, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far. Then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
