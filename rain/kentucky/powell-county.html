How much rain has Powell County, Kentucky, had in 2026?

35.31 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 at the weather station 2 miles west of Stanton, Powell County, through Oct 18. That is 8.69 inches less than a usual year to date (80% of usual).

Through Oct 13, the weather station 2 miles west of Stanton has 34.41 inches, 8.97 inches less than a usual year. June was the wettest month at 7.11 inches, and the biggest single day came back on Jan 25 with 1.62 inches. The gauge then went 10 days in a row with nothing, not even a trace, from Sep 23 to Oct 2. On the days both years reported, that is 15.73 inches less than last year.

The station, and what usual means

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the station 2 miles west of Stanton (station ID 157652), 5 miles from the middle of Powell County. It reported 290 of the 291 days from Jan 1 to Oct 18; the one day with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught: rain, and snow melted down to water. A trace, too little to measure, adds nothing to the totals, but it ends a dry stretch: something fell that day. It is read once a day, usually in the morning, and each reading is filed under the day it was read, so an evening storm can show up on the next day.

Usual means NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily normals for this station, added up over the same 290 days the station reported, so a missing day counts on neither side.

Why this station: 2 stations inside Powell County reported at least 90% of the days in 2026. Mountain snow sites, and stations far above the rest in the county, come last, since their rain is not what the farms below get. Where snow is normal, a station that keeps a full record of both rain and snowfall comes first, over a nearer one whose snowfall record has gaps, if it is no more than 10 miles farther or twice as far, even when only the nearer one has NOAA normals. Then the pick goes to one with NOAA normals, then the most days reported, then the one nearest the middle of the county. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
