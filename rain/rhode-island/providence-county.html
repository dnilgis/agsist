How much rain has Providence County, Rhode Island, had in 2026?

44.07 inches of rain have fallen so far in 2026 averaged over Providence County, Jan 1 to Nov 18, 101% of normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km weather grid, against NOAA’s 1991-2020 gridded normal.

Greenville gauge: 41.73 inches, Jan 1 to Nov 14, at the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Greenville. That is 2.11 inches less than a normal year to date (95% of normal).

The station

The numbers come from NOAA’s daily record for the volunteer rain gauge less than a mile north-northwest of Greenville, 2 miles from the middle of Providence County. It reported 313 of the 318 days from Jan 1 to Nov 14; the 5 days with no report count as unknown, not as dry. Rain here means everything the gauge caught, snow melted down to water.

Sources and terms

Daily NOAA records through RCC-ACIS, read Nov 19, 2026; updated daily. NOAA’s daily numbers come in a few days late and are sometimes corrected. Volunteer gauge data: CoCoRaHS.

Normal is NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 daily average for this gauge, added up over the same 313 days the gauge reported, so a missing day counts on neither side. The volunteer gauges started in 1998, so none has 30 years of its own; NOAA estimates the rest from long-record stations nearby.

Why this gauge: 19 stations inside Providence County reported nearly every day in 2026, and this is the one used. Your own rain gauge can read differently a few miles away.
