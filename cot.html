CFTC Commitments of Traders

Where the big speculative funds sit in grain, cattle, hogs and milk, and how crowded that is. Not a price forecast.

Data: CFTC Commitments of Traders, released Fridays for positions as of the Tuesday before.

Who the funds are, and how old these numbers are

These are the traders the CFTC calls managed money: registered commodity trading advisors, commodity pool operators and unregistered funds that trade futures for clients. They trade paper and never own a bushel. Crowded positioning tells you how much buying or selling could be forced if the market turns, not which way it turns. The test on the full record is below.

Positions are counted on Tuesday and published Friday afternoon (the Monday after, when a federal holiday falls Wednesday to Friday that week), so they are at least three days old when they land and older every day after. The figures below carry their own age. Does positioning predict price? We test our own rule, in public, one call per crop each week.

    How these are counted

    Futures: each group's net position in the futures-only report. Options: its net position in the futures-and-options combined report minus the futures-only one. CFTC counts options there as futures equivalents (adjusted by each option's delta), not as a count of option contracts.

    Rank: the share of the other weeks in the last 3 years when that position was lower, ties counted half; the number of weeks is printed with it. A week change is ranked by its size against every weekly change in those 3 years. Each week is ranked only against the weeks before it.

    Unusual: an options position in its top or bottom 5%, an options week change in its top 5%, or the share held by the largest 4 or 8 traders at a 3-year high or low (Legacy combined report). Not ranked: a market where options are under 5% of open interest.

    Source: CFTC Commitments of Traders: Disaggregated Futures-and-Options Combined, Disaggregated Futures-Only and Legacy Futures-and-Options Combined reports.

      What does this mean for farmers?

      What the report is, how to use it, what managed money means and where the position sits.

      What is the COT Report?

      The CFTC publishes Commitments of Traders usually on Friday at 3:30 pm Eastern (on the Monday after when a federal holiday falls on the Wednesday, Thursday or Friday of the report week; a Monday holiday does not move it), counting positions held as of the previous Tuesday. It splits the market into four reportable categories: managed money, producers and merchants, swap dealers and other reportables, plus a small-trader remainder. This page leads with managed money, which is the speculative money, and breaks out all five further down. Every contract has two sides, so the categories net to zero: when funds buy, somebody sold.

      How to use it

      As a measure of who is exposed. It does not call direction: on the whole record (the test is above), positioning and price move together in the week they are measured; the relationship with the next four to thirteen weeks is close to zero. What a crowded position does tell you is how much buying or selling could be forced if the market turns.

      What "managed money" means

      CTAs, commodity pools and hedge funds. They do not own grain, they trade paper, and their buying and selling moves the futures leg. It does not follow that it moves your basis. Basis is your local cash price minus futures, and it is set by what is happening at your elevator: bushels arriving, space, freight and demand. Fund flow and basis frequently move in opposite directions.

      Where the position sits

      The bar on each card covers the last year. The sections above rank the same position against the whole record we hold, measured as a share of all open contracts, which is the comparison that survives a market doubling in size. When the card and the section disagree they are answering different questions: one about this year, one about the whole record.
