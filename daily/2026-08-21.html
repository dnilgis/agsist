Tour scouts found a smaller bean crop than last year, corn held steady, and now the livestock market waits on Cattle on Feed.

THE TAKEAWAY

Tour confirmed a smaller bean crop; the market already knew. Cattle on Feed decides the afternoon.

Pro Farmer's tour wrapped Thursday with pod counts running below year-ago in key areas, and the bean board yawned: November beans at **$12.33**, up roughly 7 cents on the day but still 6 cents short of the $12.39 call level -- an overbought market that got good news and still couldn't close the gap. December corn at **$5.03** is the cleaner story, sitting comfortably above the $4.90 weekly-thread line ahead of Cattle on Feed at 2 PM CT.

  • WTI at $87.21 added 0.9%, on pace for a second straight weekly gain; Iranian crude cargoes to Chinese independent refiners have nearly dried up following reinstated U.S. sanctions, and China's oil imports are set to rise roughly 1.2 million barrels per day in Q4 as buyers hunt replacement supply.

Iranian supply squeeze is a cargo story now, not a Hormuz story. That makes the crude bid more durable.
THE MORE YOU KNOW
Phosphate from Morocco: what the first shipment in 5 years means for input costs
The first Moroccan phosphate shipment to the U.S. in five years just landed under temporary countervailing duty relief. Morocco holds roughly 70% of the world's known phosphate reserves. If the CVD relief holds, it opens a non-China, non-Russia supply lane for fertilizer heading into 2027 planning season, a meaningful optionality shift for input-cost risk.

DISEASE
Screwworm Case in Sonora Threatens Aug. 24 Port Reopening
A confirmed screwworm case in Sonora, Mexico puts the first phased reopening at Douglas, AZ in serious doubt. If the port date slips, the feeder supply tightness that has been priced into the rally all summer does not unwind on schedule, and break-even math for fall placements needs a second look.
LOGISTICS
Tyson Joslin Closure Leaves Thousands of Illinois Cattle Unplaced
The abrupt Tyson closure in Joslin, Illinois is leaving thousands of head without a processing home, adding a new capacity hole alongside the Cargill lockout. The industry was already over-capacity in processing but under-capacity in active kill; this deepens the packer-margin problem heading into fall runs.
