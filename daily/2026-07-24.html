WHEAT DROPS 3%; CATTLE BOUNCE INTO COF

Wheat gave back its July gains in a single session while cattle firmed ahead of this afternoon's Cattle on Feed report, and soybeans added to the week's gains on hot-and-dry forecasts into early August.

🧵 FRIDAY RESOLUTIONDoes the USDA Crop Progress report confirm the corn pollination stress the nearby futures are pricing, or does a clean crop condition reading reverse the front-month break?
⚡Overnight Surprise: Chicago Wheat DN 3.0%

Wheat fell 21 cents to $6.79, dropping sharply in what the Feedstuffs recap called straight profit-taking after a strong bull run, and that's exactly what it looks like: no new bearish headline, just a market that ran too far too fast and finally gave some back. Soybeans told the opposite story, tacking on nearly 5 cents to $12.47 on fund and technical buying with hot-and-dry forecasts threatening fill through early August, the critical window the 2026 planting season has been building toward since the wet May start. Meanwhile, cattle bounced to $227.07 ahead of this afternoon's 2:00 PM CT Cattle on Feed report, the monthly number that has the whole complex holding its breath; the market is positioning, not committing, and the report decides which direction that commitment runs.

🧠 THE MORE YOU KNOW
The meal/oil split is pricing something the headline crush margin isn't.
Today's bean complex closed with meal up 0.7% to $335.30 and oil down 1.2% to $71.87, a divergence inside a market that moved in the same direction on the headline. When meal leads and oil lags, the crush is being driven by protein demand, not biofuel demand; that matters because the two demand streams have completely different price ceilings and seasonality. Soybean oil at $71.87 is still at 76% of its 52-week range, historically well-supported, but the meal share of crush value is running above its seasonal average as livestock feed demand stays strong. The China purchase commitment, the $17 billion annual US ag purchase commitment through 2028 announced May 18, skews toward bean and meal exports specifically; if that flow is building as promised, the meal/oil split is the first place it shows up in price structure before it appears in export sales data.
