THE TAKEAWAY

Beans need export sales above 400K MT today to make this bounce mean something.

Soybeans closed at $11.54, up nearly a nickel from yesterday's lows, but the bounce is positioning ahead of today's 7:30 AM export sales print, not a verdict on what tour scouts found in the fields this week. The tour's pod counts are the reason beans can't sustain a rally without demand confirmation behind them. Today's export number either validates the bounce or kills it.

THE NUMBER
57%
EU natural gas storage fill rate, Aug 5
Europe's gas storage hit its lowest level since 2011 at 57% full heading into winter, per Gas Infrastructure Europe. That tightens global LNG demand and supports crude, which rose 1.8% today. Diesel is the ag input story; watch fall fieldwork costs.

Beans Bounce Before Export SalesMEDIUM CONVICTION
●DRIVERPro Farmer tour pod count pressure all week; 7:30 AM export sales print is today's catalyst.
↺Beans: yesterday called down toward $11.59; closed $11.54, call confirmed.
  • Nearby beans closed at $11.54, up nearly 5 cents, but November held just $11.76 with a 0.1% gain, which is a thin bounce after days of tour-driven pressure.
  • The Pro Farmer crop tour, ongoing since Monday, has been building a bear case in pod counts all week; the bounce today looks like short-covering ahead of the 7:30 AM export sales print, not a demand signal.
  • With China's $17 billion annual US ag purchase commitment (May 18 deal, through 2028) as the structural backstop, a confirmed sale above 400K MT would be the first real demand-side catalyst of the week.
Bounce is real but rented; export sales decide whether it has legs.
Corn Flat; Farm Bill Markup TodayLOW CONVICTION
●DRIVERSenate Ag Committee farm bill markup today; E15 provision confirmed in Aug 1 Senate text.
  • Nearby corn settled at $4.37, off half a cent, and December closed at $4.60 with the same fractional drift, four sessions of noise inside a tight band.
  • Heat is building across the eastern Corn Belt per today's Brownfield weather, but with pollination already closed clean, the crop is in dough and dent stage; heat stress now hurts yield at the margin, not catastrophically.
  • Senate Ag Committee markup is scheduled this morning on the 2026 Farm Bill, with year-round E15 in the text; that's a structural corn-demand tailwind that doesn't move the board today but matters for 2027 consumption math.
Corn is waiting on the Aug 12 WASDE; the farm bill is the slow-burn positive.
Cattle Firm; Feeders Hold Above $348MEDIUM CONVICTION
●DRIVERPlains cattle heat death losses (Drovers, 1d ago); Cargill lockout processing constraint ongoing.
  • Live cattle closed at $234.10, up 1.0%, and feeders held $348.77, up 0.8%, with the Cargill multi-plant lockout, ongoing since May 19 and still removing roughly 6,000 head of daily processing capacity, keeping the processing-constrained price floor intact.
  • Nighttime heat and no airflow killed thousands of cattle across the Plains this week per Drovers, a supply-side event that tightened the near-term fed supply picture and gave bulls a fresh narrative.
  • The screwworm-driven closure of Mexican feeder imports, with the first phased port reopening set for Aug 24 at Douglas, AZ, is still inside the feeder price; watch the feeder/live spread as that date approaches.
Cattle acting like supply is tight from three directions at once: lockout, border, heat.
Crude Firms; Diesel the Real Input StoryMEDIUM CONVICTION
●DRIVERUS diesel exports record high, domestic stockpiles falling (EIA via oilprice.com, 6h ago); Indian refiner West Africa buying (oilprice.com, 28m ago).
  • WTI crude closed at $76.37, up 1.8%, as Indian state refiners continued buying West African crude after Middle East supply disruptions, and the Iran-Hormuz cycle, a July re-escalation that has since cooled with the premium deflating again into August, is keeping supply chains rerouted.
  • US diesel exports hit a record high last week while domestic stockpiles slide, per EIA data reported today; that combination means fall fieldwork diesel costs stay elevated even as crude itself isn't running hard.
Crude up on rerouting demand; diesel tightness is the farmer's actual input problem.

POLICY
USDA Authorizes 60-Day Crop Insurance Premium Delay
Secretary Rollins announced at Minnesota Farmfest that USDA's Risk Management Agency is allowing approved insurers to grant producers up to 60 additional days on crop insurance premium payments. For operations with thin 2026 margins, that's a cashflow bridge worth knowing about before your next premium due date.
POLICY
Senate Farm Bill Markup Opens This Morning in Washington
Senate Ag Chairman Boozman called this a 'now or never' moment as his committee takes up markup today ahead of the Senate recess, with the Sep 30 extension deadline closing in. Year-round E15 and delayed SNAP cost-share are in the current text; a floor vote still needs 60 votes.
