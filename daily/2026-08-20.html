BEANS FIRM, CORN FLATMEDIUM CONVICTION
●DRIVERPro Farmer tour results running below year-ago; fund and technical buying lifted soybeans sharply Wednesday.
↺Beans: yesterday's 21-cent session consolidated, not reversed.
  • Soybeans added 1.5 cents to $12.25, quiet on the surface, but yesterday's 21.5-cent session showed the fund and technical buyers are still present; tour results running below last year gave them the excuse.
  • November beans at $12.40 keep carry working old-crop into new-crop, no spread warning yet.
  • Corn at $4.77 flat for the session; Dec at $5.02 hasn't moved, and with tour ear counts not yet fully in, the board is simply waiting.
Beans have momentum and a story; corn is in a holding pattern until tour wraps and exports print.
EXPORT SALES: 7:30 AM CTMEDIUM CONVICTION
●DRIVERUSDA Weekly Export Sales release, 7:30 AM CT today; China soybean buying active per grower association comments.
  • Weekly export sales print at 7:30 AM CT; beans under 300K MT would say the Wednesday rally was position-squaring, not demand-driven.
  • China is buying, per the Minnesota Soybean Growers Association and confirmed sales earlier this week, but the China purchase commitment (May 18 deal, through 2028) has shown a buy-the-rumor-sell-the-fact pattern that caps follow-through.
  • Corn export pace is the secondary read; a strong number could finally give the flat corn board a reason to move.
Beans above 300K MT confirms demand is real; below that, the chart is doing the work alone.
→ Old-crop corn: if export sales print above 500K MT on corn, that is your window to re-evaluate holds before harvest pressure builds.
CRUDE RUNS ON HORMUZ FRACTURESMEDIUM CONVICTION
●DRIVERIranian crude exports effectively halted per Rapidan Energy; supertanker VLCC prices hit record highs; Chinese refiners buying Iraqi crude.
  • WTI ran 2.8% to $86.86, the biggest single-session move in several weeks, driven by reports that Iranian crude exports are now effectively blocked and Chinese refiners are scrambling for Iraqi barrels.
  • Supertanker prices hit all-time highs above $130M; the Iran-Hormuz cycle, a pattern of escalation and premium deflation running since early spring, appears to be in a re-escalation leg with less optimism about a quick resolution.
  • Natural gas eased to $2.75, not a concern for fall fieldwork; diesel tightness remains the input-cost story to watch.
Crude at $86.86 is a diesel problem for fall fieldwork; watch crack spreads, not the headline WTI number.
CATTLE EASE, DAIRY STEADYLOW CONVICTION
●DRIVERCorn strength pressured feeder margins Wednesday; USDA suspended Section 338 tariffs on Canada for three days over dairy TRQ dispute.
↺Cattle: Cargill deal framing from yesterday, lockout still ongoing, no resolution update.
  • Live cattle slipped 0.9% to $222.97 and feeders dropped 1.0% to $336.40; Wednesday's news said feeders led lower on corn strength, which fits the math.
  • The Cargill multi-plant lockout, with roughly 6,000 head of daily processing capacity still removed, keeps the packer margin story front-and-center; the processing constraint, not cattle numbers, is still the binding factor.
  • Class III Milk barely moved at $16.65; a three-day pause on Section 338 tariffs against Canada's dairy TRQs adds a policy wrinkle, but no price signal yet.
Cattle are drifting, not breaking; the Aug 24 Mexican port reopening at Douglas, AZ is the next structural catalyst.
THE MORE YOU KNOW
WHY 300K MT IS THE BEAN DEMAND LINE
USDA counts export sales in metric tons. Soybeans need roughly 300K MT per week to stay on pace for the USDA's current 1.97 billion bushel export forecast. Miss that line today and the math starts to argue with the chart -- which already ran 21.5 cents Wednesday.
