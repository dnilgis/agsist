FUNDS LIQUIDATE INTO JUNE, CATTLE DIVERGE

Grains shed 2% Friday as memorial money exits markets before summer doldrums.

THE TAKEAWAY

Funds booking summer profits, not fundamentals driving grain weakness.

Corn closed $4.47, down 9 cents Friday and 2% for the week, as managed money booked profits ahead of the long weekend. The funds liquidated 18,000 contracts net across grain complexes Friday, classic memorial rotation out of summer-slow positions. Next week's planting data will decide if this is profit-taking or something deeper.

Grains Close Worst MayMEDIUM CONVICTION
Corn finished $4.47, down 9 cents Friday and the worst May close since 2020. Memorial liquidation drove the action, not fundamentals, as managed money shed 18,000 contracts across the grain complex. December corn at $4.75 says the market's pricing normal weather through summer. The University of Nebraska wheat tour adjustments due to drought damage didn't translate to corn premium, confirming this was fund flow, not crop concern. Next week's USDA planting progress will test whether the $4.40 low holds.
Fund liquidation, not crop worry, drove worst May finish in six years.
Cattle Firm Into GrillingMEDIUM CONVICTION
Live cattle closed $239.05, down less than 1% despite broad commodity selling Friday. Grilling season demand is offsetting the fund rotation hitting other ags, with cutout values holding firm through the week. Feeders at $348.42 dropped harder, down 1.3%, but that's positioning for cheaper corn, not demand worry. Heat stress concerns from veterinarians remind producers that summer cattle management matters more than summer price direction. The divergence between cattle resilience and grain weakness confirms different fundamental stories.
Cattle holding firm as grilling demand trumps fund liquidation hitting grains.
Energy Extends Iran EaseLOW CONVICTION
WTI crude closed $87.36, down nearly 1% Friday as Iran tensions continued easing from March's Strait of Hormuz disruption. The energy shock Kansas Fed President Schmid warned might not be transitory is looking increasingly transitory. Natural gas at $3.29 reflects seasonal demand patterns more than geopolitical premium. Fertilizer prices remain the energy story farmers care about, with FTC probe confirming what producers already know: costs aren't sustainable. The energy-agriculture margin squeeze persists even as headline crude retreats.
Iran premium fading but fertilizer costs still squeezing farm margins.
THE MORE YOU KNOW
Memorial liquidation: the calendar's most reliable trade
18,000 grain contracts liquidated Friday fits the memorial pattern perfectly. Funds book profits ahead of summer doldrums when weather matters more than position size. The trade works because June through August are statistically the quietest months for grain volatility, making large positions expensive to carry. Memorial liquidation usually marks the low until August weather scares or September harvest reality arrives.
