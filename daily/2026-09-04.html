GRAINS EASE; BEANS HOLD BETTERMEDIUM CONVICTION
  • December corn slipped 3.5 cents to $5.37, four sessions in a row lower; harvest pressure is doing what harvest pressure does, and there is no fresh export sales number to interrupt it.
  • November beans gave back a nickel to $13.10, still the strongest leg in the complex; the May 18 China purchase commitment (roughly $17 billion through 2028) has kept floor bids intact, but Friday offered no fresh buyer.
  • Soybean meal held flat while soybean oil dropped 3.3%, widening the meal/oil spread -- protein demand is the engine, fat is the passenger.
Corn is pricing harvest. Beans are pricing demand. They are not the same story and should not trade that way into the WASDE.
CATTLE HOLD; BEEF EXPORTS SUPPORT FLOORLOW CONVICTION
●DRIVERBeef export value +6% in July (USDA); Trump executive orders on cattle market access signed Friday.
  • Live cattle slipped to $213.15, off 0.5%, with the Cargill multi-plant lockout (ongoing since May 19, still removing roughly 6,000 head of daily processing capacity) keeping packer margins as the binding constraint, not cattle supply.
  • Beef export value rose 6% in July year-over-year per USDA trade data, a number that argues the floor here is real even as cattle sit at the bottom 16% of their 52-week range.
  • Trump signed new executive orders Friday aimed at supporting ranchers and expanding market access; separately, a House bill was introduced to nullify beef import authority, two political signals that the cattle producer has Washington's attention, for what that's worth at settlement.
Export data says the demand floor is real. The processing constraint is still the ceiling.

THE MORE YOU KNOW
The Diesel Crack Spread: What Farmers Actually Pay
Diesel rack prices track refining margins more than crude. With crack spreads at record levels heading into harvest, the per-gallon cost at the co-op is running well above what the WTI price alone implies. A $91 crude number is not a $91 farm input story right now.

POLICY
USDA Piloting Satellites and Tech for Crop Yield Estimates
USDA Secretary Rollins confirmed Tuesday the agency is launching a pilot to replace survey-based acreage and yield estimates with satellite and remote-sensing data. For producers, this means future WASDE numbers could carry different error characteristics than the farmer-survey model built over decades.
DISEASE
Screwworm Port Reopening: CBP Now Active in Response
US Customs and Border Protection confirmed Friday it is facilitating sterile-fly movement and training personnel ahead of the phased reopening at Douglas, AZ set for August 24. The screwworm-driven closure of Mexican feeder cattle imports remains the structural story under the historic feeder rally; the Aug 24 date is the next real tell.
