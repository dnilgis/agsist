What leaves the field when you bale stalks, chop silage or sell hay

Sigurd Lindquist, CCA, Chetek, Wisconsin

A dry ton of corn stalks carries off4.6 lb P₂O₅ + 32 lb K₂O
Cost to put it backloading a dry ton

Every ton of stalks, straw, silage or hay you haul off takes phosphate and potash with it. Here is how much, by crop, and what it costs to buy it back at fertilizer prices Kansas co-ops posted through the latest read. Potash is most of the bill.

A2809 is the high end for stalk potash. Iowa State PM 1688 and Purdue put a dry ton at 20 to 21 lb of K₂O. Across the three guides a dry ton costs loading to put back. The dollars use A2809 unless you switch the source.

Pounds from UW Extension A2809, Table 4.2. Stalks, straw and hay are per ton of dry matter. Nitrogen and sulfur are left out: A2809 gives no N or S removal per ton, and we print only what we have read in a published table.

Where these prices come from and the math

Every price comes from the one list behind fertilizer prices: the middle cash price farm co-ops post on their own websites (Kansas co-ops, and Kansas and Wisconsin co-ops for urea), read each weekday, each with its co-ops and date. Nothing here is typed in by hand. Phosphate is MAP (DAP if no co-op posts MAP), after the nitrogen in it is credited at the urea price: the same pick as the fertilizer prices page and Fast Facts, so the two never disagree. Potash is potash. Nitrogen is only shown for that credit.

Your dealer’s price will differ, and Kansas is not everywhere. Put your own pounds and prices through the same math.

What is not on this page yet

  • Nitrogen and sulfur. A2809 gives no N or S removal per ton. They go on once a published table (Iowa State PM 1688, the Tri-State guide or Penn State’s Agronomy Guide) is saved and read. Even then, N in stalks and straw is mostly tied up in the residue, so it will be shown on its own line, not in the replacement total.
  • Earlage and high-moisture corn. Not in A2809. Not shown until a source gives them.
  • Other sources for straw and hay. Corn stalks now show Iowa State PM 1688 (as quoted by Iowa State ICM, 4.8 lb P₂O₅ and 18 lb K₂O a ton at 15% moisture) and Purdue (3.6 and 20 a dry ton) beside A2809. For straw and hay A2809 is still the only table read; the Tri-State guide (Bulletin 974) goes beside it once a copy is saved.
  • Potash washes out of stalks. Iowa State notes that rain after harvest leaches K from leaves and stalks, so bales made after a wet fall test lower than the plant at maturity. That is one reason A2809’s figure runs high for baled stalks.

Reading it right

  • This is replacement cost, not a price for your bales. What a bale sells for depends on your market. This shows the fertilizer side of the trade so you know what you are giving away.
  • Weigh some bales. A2809 counts by the dry ton. Bale weight and moisture move the per-bale number more than anything else here.
  • Soil test level matters. On ground testing high in K, the potash leaving may not need to go back right away. On ground at or below optimum it does.
  • Stalks are more than P and K. Residue also holds soil, feeds organic matter and catches snow. Those are real but are not priced here.

Questions

How much P and K is in a ton of corn stalks?

UW Extension A2809 puts corn stover at 4.6 lb of P₂O₅ and 32 lb of K₂O per ton of dry matter (Table 4.2). A ton of bales at 15% moisture is 0.85 dry ton, so about 3.9 lb of P₂O₅ and 27 lb of K₂O. At 3 dry tons an acre that is about 14 lb of P₂O₅ and 96 lb of K₂O leaving each acre. Potash is most of the dollar value.

What is the fertilizer value of a bale of corn stalks?

Multiply the dry tons in the bale by 4.6 lb of P₂O₅ and 32 lb of K₂O, then by the price of a pound of each. A 1,000 lb bale at 15% moisture is 850 lb of dry matter, 0.425 dry ton: about 2 lb of P₂O₅ and 13.6 lb of K₂O. The calculator on this page prices it at the co-op prices posted through the date shown above. Weigh a few of your own bales; weight and moisture move the answer more than anything else.

Why is nitrogen not counted?

The source for these pounds, UW Extension A2809, gives P₂O₅ and K₂O removal for every crop but no N or S removal per ton. We do not fill in a number we have not read in a published table. Nitrogen in stalks and straw is also mostly tied up in residue and is not there for next year's crop the way a pound of fertilizer N is, so P and K are the honest replacement cost.

How much P and K does corn silage take off?

A2809 lists corn silage at 3.6 lb of P₂O₅ and 8.3 lb of K₂O per ton at 65% moisture. A 20 ton crop takes about 72 lb of P₂O₅ and 166 lb of K₂O an acre, more than twice the potash that 180 bushels of grain take (52 lb), because the stalks and leaves leave too.

How much do soybeans, wheat and hay remove?

Per A2809 Table 4.2: soybeans 0.8 lb P₂O₅ and 1.4 lb K₂O a bushel; wheat 0.5 and 0.35 a bushel, wheat straw 6 and 28 per dry ton; oat straw 9.4 and 47; alfalfa 13 and 60 per dry ton; grass hay 15 and 55 per dry ton.

Where do the fertilizer prices come from?

From the same list as the fertilizer prices page: the middle cash price that farm co-ops post on their own websites (Kansas co-ops, and Kansas and Wisconsin co-ops for urea), read each weekday, with the co-ops and the date on every price. Phosphate is MAP (DAP if no co-op posts MAP) with its nitrogen credited at the urea price, potash is potash. That is the same pick the fertilizer prices page, Fast Facts and the manure calculator use, so every page shows one phosphate price. Your dealer's price will differ; the math is shown so you can redo it.
