What this can’t tell you. These are county means from a voluntary USDA survey. Rents vary widely inside a county, driven by soil, drainage, field size and how badly a neighbor wants the ground. Year-over-year stats above compare only counties published in both years, so a county dropping out of the survey can’t fake a trend. Treat any county number as the start of a conversation, not a rate card.

Questions

What is the average cash rent per acre in North Dakota for 2026?

The median USDA NASS county cash rent for non-irrigated cropland in North Dakota is $64.50 per acre across the 53 counties with a published 2026 rate. Rents vary widely by county: highest Cass ($163), Richland ($163), Traill ($161); lowest Mckenzie ($29.50), Golden Valley ($35.50), Burke ($37). The county mean is a survey reference point, not a rate card.

How much did North Dakota cash rent change from 2025 to 2026?

Comparing the same 53 counties published in both years, the median non-irrigated cropland rent moved +2.4% year over year.

Why is my North Dakota county not listed?

USDA NASS publishes a county rate only where enough Cash Rents Survey responses came back (and the county has at least 20,000 acres of cropland plus pasture). If your county is missing, NASS did not publish a rate for it, and this page never estimates one.

When is county cash rent data released?

USDA NASS releases county cash rent estimates each August for the current crop year. This page rebuilds automatically when new data lands.
