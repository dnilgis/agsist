Questions

What is the average cash rent per acre in Oregon for 2026?

The middle Oregon county pays $210 per acre for irrigated cropland: half of the 23 counties with a published 2026 USDA NASS rate are above it and half below. NASS also puts out its own statewide average, a separate figure that is not shown here. Rents vary widely by county: highest Hood River ($759), Umatilla ($478), Clackamas ($358); lowest Harney ($78), Wallowa ($109), Baker ($118). For non-irrigated cropland the middle county paid $99.50 (15 counties). The county mean is a survey reference point, not a rate card.

How much does pasture rent for per acre in Oregon?

The median USDA NASS county cash rent for permanent pasture in Oregon is $21 per acre across the 25 counties with a published 2026 pasture rate. Highest Tillamook ($128), Yamhill ($65.50), Clackamas ($65); lowest Wheeler ($4), Wallowa ($5.50), Wasco ($6.90). Each county's pasture rate is in its own column in the county table below.

How much did Oregon cash rent change from 2025 to 2026?

Comparing the same 20 counties published in both years, the median irrigated cropland rent went from $201.50 to $198.50, -1.5% year over year. That is the change in the state median, not in a typical county: each county's own rent moved its own way.

Why is my Oregon county not listed?

USDA NASS publishes a county rate only where enough Cash Rents Survey responses came back (and the county has at least 20,000 acres of cropland plus pasture). If your county is missing, NASS did not publish a rate for it.

When is county cash rent data released?

USDA NASS releases county cash rent estimates each August for the current crop year. This page rebuilds automatically when new data lands.
