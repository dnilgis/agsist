Montana rent jumped when corn went over $5 in 2010 to 2012. In 2021 to 2023 it rose only 3.3% a year.

In the 18 counties published both years, the median went from $20.50 in 2009 to $24.25 in 2013, up 18%. In the 37 published in 2020 and 2024, it went from $25 to $28.50, up 14%. With corn back under $5: up 13% from 2013 to 2020 (23 counties); up 5% from 2024 to 2026 (36 counties).

Questions

What is the average cash rent per acre in Montana for 2026?

The middle Montana county pays $30.50 per acre for non-irrigated cropland: half of the 40 counties with a published 2026 USDA NASS rate are above it and half below. NASS also puts out its own statewide average, a separate figure that is not shown here. Rents vary widely by county: highest Cascade ($45.50), Pondera ($44), Flathead ($42.50); lowest Beaverhead ($18.50), Stillwater ($20), Wheatland ($21.50). For irrigated cropland the middle county paid $97.50 (29 counties). The county mean is a survey reference point, not a rate card.

How much does pasture rent for per acre in Montana?

The median USDA NASS county cash rent for permanent pasture in Montana is $9.80 per acre across the 48 counties with a published 2026 pasture rate. Highest Ravalli ($29), Lake ($22), Pondera ($16.50); lowest Treasure ($4.40), Sanders ($4.70), Musselshell ($5.60). Each county's pasture rate is in its own column in the county table below.

How much does farmland rent for per acre per month in Montana?

Cash rent is quoted per acre per year, not per month. Divided by 12, the middle Montana county's 2026 non-irrigated cropland rent of $30.50 a year is about $2.54 an acre a month. Pasture: $9.80 a year, about $0.82 a month.

How much did Montana cash rent change from 2025 to 2026?

Comparing the 38 counties published in both years, the median non-irrigated cropland rent went from $29.75 to $30.50, +2.5% year over year. That is the change in the state median, not in a typical county: each county's own rent moved its own way.

Why does my Montana county say not published?

USDA NASS publishes a county rate only where enough Cash Rents Survey responses came back (and the county has at least 20,000 acres of cropland plus pasture). A county marked not published had no 2026 rate of any kind from NASS. Some counties NASS reports only inside a combined district, and those show as not published too. A rent more than three survey years old shows n/a with the last year it was published.

When is county cash rent data released?

USDA NASS releases county cash rent estimates in August of each survey year for that crop year. There was no survey in 2015 or 2018.

Sources and terms

Source: USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026 county estimates, and NASS county yield estimates. Data as of 2026-10-06.

These are the 2026 county rents, the newest NASS has put out (released Aug 21, 2026). NASS puts county rents out in late August of each survey year (it ran no survey in 2015 or 2018); the 2027 date is not on its calendar yet.

What this can’t tell you. These are county means from a voluntary USDA survey. Rents vary widely inside a county, driven by soil, drainage, field size and how badly a neighbor wants the ground. Year-over-year stats compare only counties published in both years.
