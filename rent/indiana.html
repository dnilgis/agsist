Two more ways to figure a fair rent

Share of gross. Your bushels times a price, and the share of that the rent takes. The share starts at the county’s own at this price.

Default: Indiana’s 2025 average price received for corn (USDA NASS, preliminary).

Return on land value. What the land is worth times the return an owner wants on it.

Cost-based. What is left of your gross after your own costs and pay for your work. That needs your budget, so this page doesn’t guess one.

Questions

What is the average cash rent per acre in Indiana for 2026?

The middle Indiana county pays $217 per acre for non-irrigated cropland: half of the 89 counties with a published 2026 USDA NASS rate are above it and half below. NASS also puts out its own statewide average, a separate figure that is not shown here. Rents vary widely by county: highest Warren ($303), Benton ($300), Clinton ($298); lowest Perry ($127), Switzerland ($134), Brown ($134). For irrigated cropland the middle county paid $275.50 (14 counties). The county mean is a survey reference point, not a rate card.

How much does pasture rent for per acre in Indiana?

The median USDA NASS county cash rent for permanent pasture in Indiana is $56 per acre across the 34 counties with a published 2026 pasture rate. Highest LaGrange ($124), Henry ($92.50), Gibson ($88); lowest Crawford ($28), Owen ($30), Parke ($35). Each county's pasture rate is in its own column in the county table below.

How much does farmland rent for per acre per month in Indiana?

Cash rent is quoted per acre per year, not per month. Divided by 12, the middle Indiana county's 2026 non-irrigated cropland rent of $217 a year is about $18.08 an acre a month. Pasture: $56 a year, about $4.67 a month.

How much did Indiana cash rent change from 2025 to 2026?

Comparing the 88 counties published in both years, the median non-irrigated cropland rent went from $213.50 to $217, +1.6% year over year. That is the change in the state median, not in a typical county: each county's own rent moved its own way.

Why does my Indiana county say not published?

USDA NASS publishes a county rate only where enough Cash Rents Survey responses came back (and the county has at least 20,000 acres of cropland plus pasture). A county marked not published had no 2026 rate of any kind from NASS. Some counties NASS reports only inside a combined district, and those show as not published too. A rent more than three survey years old shows n/a with the last year it was published.

When is county cash rent data released?

USDA NASS releases county cash rent estimates in August of each survey year for that crop year. There was no survey in 2015 or 2018.

When must I give notice to terminate an Indiana farm lease?

Year-to-year tenancy: written notice at least 3 months before the end of the lease year (roughly end of November for a Feb-end term). Miss it and the lease typically continues another year on the same terms. The AGSIST cash farm lease page has the details and a printable lease.

Sources and terms

Source: USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026 county estimates, and NASS county yield estimates. Data as of 2026-10-06.

These are the 2026 county rents, the newest NASS has put out (released Aug 21, 2026). NASS puts county rents out in late August of each survey year (it ran no survey in 2015 or 2018); the 2027 date is not on its calendar yet.

What this can’t tell you. These are county means from a voluntary USDA survey. Rents vary widely inside a county, driven by soil, drainage, field size and how badly a neighbor wants the ground. Year-over-year stats compare only counties published in both years.
