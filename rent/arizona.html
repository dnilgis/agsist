Questions

What is the average cash rent per acre in Arizona for 2026?

The middle Arizona county pays $271 per acre for irrigated cropland: half of the 4 counties with a published 2026 USDA NASS rate are above it and half below. NASS also puts out its own statewide average, a separate figure that is not shown here. Rents vary widely by county: highest Yuma ($747), La Paz ($276), Maricopa ($266). With under five published counties, a highest/lowest split would overstate the sample. The county mean is a survey reference point, not a rate card.

How much does pasture rent for per acre in Arizona?

The median USDA NASS county cash rent for permanent pasture in Arizona is $3.10 per acre across the 5 counties with a published 2026 pasture rate. Highest Pinal ($4.10), Apache ($3.70), Santa Cruz ($3.10); lowest Navajo ($1), Yavapai ($2.40), Santa Cruz ($3.10). Each county's pasture rate is in its own column in the county table below.

How much does farmland rent for per acre per month in Arizona?

Cash rent is quoted per acre per year, not per month. Divided by 12, the middle Arizona county's 2026 irrigated cropland rent of $271 a year is about $22.58 an acre a month. Pasture: $3.10 a year, about $0.26 a month.

How much did Arizona cash rent change from 2025 to 2026?

Too few counties were published in both years for a clean year-over-year figure.

Why does my Arizona county say not published?

USDA NASS publishes a county rate only where enough Cash Rents Survey responses came back (and the county has at least 20,000 acres of cropland plus pasture). A county marked not published had no 2026 rate of any kind from NASS; 1 Arizona counties are in the table that way. Some counties NASS reports only inside a combined district, and those show as not published too. A rent more than three survey years old shows n/a with the last year it was published.

When is county cash rent data released?

USDA NASS releases county cash rent estimates in August of each survey year for that crop year. There was no survey in 2015 or 2018.

Sources and terms

Source: USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026 county estimates, and NASS county yield estimates. Data as of 2026-10-06.

These are the 2026 county rents, the newest NASS has put out (released Aug 21, 2026). NASS puts county rents out in late August of each survey year (it ran no survey in 2015 or 2018); the 2027 date is not on its calendar yet.

What this can’t tell you. These are county means from a voluntary USDA survey. Rents vary widely inside a county, driven by soil, drainage, field size and how badly a neighbor wants the ground. Year-over-year stats compare only counties published in both years.
