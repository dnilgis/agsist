What this can’t tell you. These are county means from a voluntary USDA survey. Rents vary widely inside a county, driven by soil, drainage, field size and how badly a neighbor wants the ground. Year-over-year stats above compare only counties published in both years, so a county dropping out of the survey can’t fake a trend. Questions

What is the average cash rent per acre in Ohio for 2026?

The median USDA NASS county cash rent for non-irrigated cropland in Ohio is $167.50 per acre across the 76 counties with a published 2026 rate. Rents vary widely by county: highest Shelby ($256), Hardin ($250), Clark ($247); lowest Belmont ($26.50), Jefferson ($45.50), Noble ($47.50). The county mean is a survey reference point, not a rate card.

How much did Ohio cash rent change from 2025 to 2026?

Comparing the same 72 counties published in both years, the median non-irrigated cropland rent moved -0.3% year over year.

Why is my Ohio county not listed?

USDA NASS publishes a county rate only where enough Cash Rents Survey responses came back (and the county has at least 20,000 acres of cropland plus pasture). If your county is missing, NASS did not publish a rate for it, and this page never estimates one.

When is county cash rent data released?

USDA NASS releases county cash rent estimates each August for the current crop year. This page rebuilds automatically when new data lands.
