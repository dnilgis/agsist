Nightly results

Scouts run two routes at once, Monday through Thursday, and states report at the nightly meetings. Corn is a sampled yield in bushels per acre. Soybeans are pod counts in a three-foot square — more pods means more beans, but pods do not convert cleanly into bushels, so treat them as a direction, not a yield.

Across the same 11 years, the tour's soybean production number missed the final by 0.13 billion bushels on average, against 0.12 billion for USDA's August forecast. On beans it is close to a coin flip between them.

How to use this. The tour is the only wide, boots-in-the-field count of this crop, and in a year where the trade doubts USDA it moves the board. But it samples fields along fixed routes in seven states, which is not the same thing as measuring the country — and the record above says it has not been more accurate than USDA's August number. Read the nightly state figures for where the crop is better or worse than expected, and be careful about trading the national estimate as though it settles the argument.

What counts as the final. Final yield here means the figure USDA published the following January in its annual summary. USDA revises a few of those later by a tenth to half a bushel; this page uses the January number for every year so the comparison stays consistent.

Our own number is on the record too. The AGSIST nowcast above was locked Aug 11 — the day before the August WASDE — and published on the analyst board before the tour rolled. We also filed two directional calls that report: corn revising up, beans revising up. USDA revised both down. Both are graded misses, kept on the record. It stays where it is whether it ends up looking smart or not.
