Jefferson County, West Virginia

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $7,118 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $10.02 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

55.1% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 33.56 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 25, 90% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 200 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,099 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $7,118 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $55 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. USDA cash rent fell 26.7% from 2016 to 2026. 40% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.2% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 6% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 152.9 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 2,912 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $7.76 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.81, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 46% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.1 days a year and damaging wind on 5, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 70.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 6.7%.

Jefferson County is a small corn county where heat and drought is the main thing that costs farmers money. Heat and drought went from 58 percent of crop insurance indemnities in 1989-1999 to 77 percent in 2020-2025, and the loss ratio over the last ten closed crop years is 0.81. Non-irrigated cash rent is $55 per acre in 2026, down 26.7 percent from 2016. The July nightly low averaged 65.6 F over 2017-2026, against a 1991-2020 normal of 64.1 F, and the county has seen 35 weeks of severe drought or worse since 2021.

Quick answers for Jefferson County

What is cash rent in Jefferson County, West Virginia?

The county average is $55 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Jefferson County, West Virginia?

Land and buildings were worth $7,118 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.
