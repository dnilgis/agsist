Summers County, West Virginia

Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 39.22 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 26, 103% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 187 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,779 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $2,702 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $23.50 an acre (USDA, 2023). We read recorded sales in Florida, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.47 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 31, 2026). 5% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. 24% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.8% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 0.1% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 94.9 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 225 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $9.66 per base acre, PLC $2.38.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (94.98 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 25, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $0.00 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $20.99 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. PRF grazing county base value: $30.70 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

0.2% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.5 days a year and damaging wind on 2.2, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 43.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1992 to 2003; too much rain caused 42.8%.

Cropland cash rent in Summers County stood at $24 an acre in 2023, up 56.7% from $15 in 2013. The census value of land and buildings was $2,702 an acre in 2022, up 18.9% from $2,272 in 2017, or 3.5% a year. That puts the county under the state median of $3,215 and 14th lowest of 47 counties in West Virginia. Pasture rent ran $16 an acre in 2026, and 24% of farmland was rented in 2022.

Quick answers for Summers County

What is cash rent in Summers County, West Virginia?

The county average is $24 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2023. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Summers County, West Virginia?

Land and buildings were worth $2,702 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.
