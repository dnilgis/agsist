Lee County, South Carolina

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,655 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $8.29 per base acre for corn.

Lee County vs South Carolina: $52 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 84% of the other 37 South Carolina counties with a 2026 rent (7th highest of 38).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 31.63 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 75% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 242 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,817 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $2,655 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $52 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.62 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 9, 2026). 42% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 2% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 25.3% from 2016 to 2026. 48% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 6% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 4% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 15.6 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 60 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 4,311 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $8.29 per base acre, PLC $2.61.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (103.86 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $0.00 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $22.95 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (30.15 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $7.41 per base acre, PLC $8.86. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (49.05 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $49.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 588 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $11.15 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.80, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 42% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.6 days a year and damaging wind on 3.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 35.4% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 29.1%.
