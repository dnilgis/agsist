Smith County, Mississippi

Severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $3,551 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $10.00 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost-free season: 241 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,923 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $3,551 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $31 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 19.2% from 2016 to 2026. 19.2% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 4.5% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 2.4% of the cropland, CRP left out. 204.3 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 80 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $10.00 per base acre, PLC $1.04.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (41.5 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (28.6 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: close, either one, but expected payments are not printed, as wheat has too little price history of its own. PRF grazing county base value: $41.40 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.2 days a year and damaging wind on 6, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 67.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1992 to 2025; too much rain caused 25.7%.

Smith County cropland rent stands at $31 an acre for 2026, up 19.2% from $26 in 2016. That is 16th lowest of 71 counties in Mississippi, well under the state median of $67. The census value of land and buildings ran $3,551 an acre in 2022, up 23.6% from $2,873 in 2017, and 30th highest of 82. 19% of farmland was rented in 2022.
