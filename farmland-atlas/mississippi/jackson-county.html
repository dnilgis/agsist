Jackson County, Mississippi

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $5,657 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

98.8% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 53.88 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 25, 90% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost: most years have no spring freeze after February; the first fall freeze comes around Nov 29 in the median year. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,157 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $5,657 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.55 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 7, 2026). 32% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. 19% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 4% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 5% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 271.6 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 1,197 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

PRF grazing county base value: $39.20 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $11.36 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.91, 2016 to 2025. Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.1 days a year and damaging wind on 2, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 23% of crop insurance claims paid from 1990 to 2025; too much rain caused 23.7%.

The census value of land and buildings in Jackson County was $5,657 an acre in 2022. That ranks 2nd highest of 82 in Mississippi, well over the state median of $3,313. It was up 12.9% from $5,012 in 2017, 2.5% a year. The last cash rent on the books is $37 an acre from 2010, and 19% of the farmland was rented in 2022.

Quick answers for Jackson County

What is farmland worth in Jackson County, Mississippi?

Land and buildings were worth $5,657 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.
