Clay County, Kentucky

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. South East subregion (27 counties, 10 agent surveys), UK survey 2025: cropland $3,100 an acre; hay $2,800, pasture $2,400, woodland $1,600. ARC-CO is expected to pay $9.69 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 38.25 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 18, 84% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 195 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,171 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

3% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.6 days a year and damaging wind on 3.6, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 18.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1995 to 2025; too much rain caused 61.3%.

Cash rent in Clay County runs $51 an acre for 2025, down 5.6% from $54 in 2013. The census value of land and buildings came in at $2,956 an acre in 2022, up 63.0% from $1,814 in 2017. That still sits under the state median of $4,449 and ranks 14th lowest of 114 counties in Kentucky. 25% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.
