Ohio County, Kentucky

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Mid West subregion (15 counties, 11 agent surveys), UK survey 2025: cropland $8,500 an acre; hay $6,100, pasture $5,400, woodland $4,400. ARC-CO is expected to pay $13.01 per base acre for corn.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $5.09 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.66, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 56% of the median yield. 0.7% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). What stands out in Ohio County is the water, and it is wet water more than dry. Excess moisture and flood account for 68 percent of the crop insurance indemnities paid from 1989 through 2025, and the county has paid out more than it took in premium in 6 of the 19 closed crop years. Heat and drought claims went from 51 percent of indemnities in 1989-1999 to 15 percent in 2020-2025. Only 1 percent of harvested cropland is irrigated, and the county's non-irrigated cash rent was $168 per acre in 2024, up 35.5 percent from 2014. The median corn yield is 180 bushels per acre, and the worst year, 2012, was 101.4.

Quick answers for Ohio County

What is cash rent in Ohio County, Kentucky?

The county average is $168 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2024. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Ohio County, Kentucky?

Land and buildings were worth $3,919 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.

What is the corn yield in Ohio County, Kentucky?

The county's 14-yr trend (projected 2026) is 198.8 bushels an acre (all practices), fit to 14 years of USDA NASS county yields. A trend line is a projection from past years, not a forecast of this year's crop.
