Kenton County, Kentucky

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. North Central subregion (15 counties, 7 agent surveys), UK survey 2025: cropland $8,700 an acre; hay $6,600, pasture $6,100, woodland $5,200. ARC-CO is expected to pay $9.63 per base acre for corn.

Kenton County vs Kentucky: $46 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 12% of the other 85 Kentucky counties with a 2026 rent (11th lowest of 86).

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

0.5% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.8 days a year and damaging wind on 2.9, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 34.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 56.8%.

Cropland rent in Kenton County runs $46 an acre for 2026, the 11th lowest of 86 in Kentucky against a state median of $125. The census value of land and buildings went the other way, at $7,531 an acre in 2022, up 34.6% from $5,597 in 2017 and 8th highest of 114. Only 12% of the farmland was rented in 2022, and the corn trend sits at 174.9 bu.
