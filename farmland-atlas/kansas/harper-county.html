Harper County, Kansas

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Non-irrigated cropland sold for an average $2,379 an acre in 2025, by the Kansas Land Values book. ARC-CO is expected to pay $6.75 per base acre for corn.

Harper County vs Kansas: $47 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 24% of the other 101 Kansas counties with a 2026 rent (tied for 25th lowest of 102).

Heat and drought is the story in Harper County. It was 46 percent of crop insurance indemnities in 1989-1999, 16 percent in 2000-2009, and 83 percent in 2020-2025. Only 1 percent of harvested cropland is irrigated, so most of the ground depends on rain. The median corn yield for 2008-2023 was 82.8 bushels per acre, but 2017 was 48.1. Non-irrigated cash rent is $47 per acre in 2026, up 16 percent from 2016. The census value of land and buildings was $1,751 per acre in 2022.

Quick answers for Harper County

What is cash rent in Harper County, Kansas?

The county average is $47 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Harper County, Kansas?

Land and buildings were worth $1,751 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.

What is the corn yield in Harper County, Kansas?

The county's 2008-2023 median is 82.8 bushels an acre (all practices), from USDA NASS county yields, a county figure and not any one field.
