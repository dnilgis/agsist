Clark County, Kansas

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Non-irrigated cropland sold for an average $2,159 an acre in 2025, by the Kansas Land Values book.

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

96% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 20.06 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 18, 92% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 197 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,463 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $16.85 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.89, 2016 to 2025. Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.9 days a year and damaging wind on 1.7, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 81.5% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 2.1%.

USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026

Cropland in Clark County rents for $38 an acre in 2026, tied for 9th lowest of 102 in Kansas and under the state median of $58. That rent is up 21.0% from $31 in 2016. The census value of land and buildings was $1,288 an acre in 2022, 2nd lowest of 104 in the state, up 8.5% from $1,187 in 2017. 61% of farmland here was rented in 2022.
