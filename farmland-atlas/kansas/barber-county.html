Barber County, Kansas

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Non-irrigated cropland sold for an average $2,650 an acre in 2025, by the Kansas Land Values book.

Barber County vs Kansas: $43 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 18% of the other 101 Kansas counties with a 2026 rent (19th lowest of 102).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

77.5% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 22.33 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 81% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 204 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,525 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $17.11 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.98, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 75% of the median yield. 6.3% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.5 days a year and damaging wind on 3.9, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 74% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 6.4%.
