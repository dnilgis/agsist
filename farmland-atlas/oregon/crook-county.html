Crook County, Oregon

Extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $1,808 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Text itFacebook

Weather

Drought now: extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 8.24 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 69% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 112 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 1,999 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $1,808 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.14 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 10, 2026). None of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland outright, 2% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. 23% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 1% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 57.5 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 117.5 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 11,061 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $11.80 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 12 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

92.3% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.3 days a year and damaging wind on 0.1, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 12.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 3.1%.

The census value of land and buildings in Crook County was $1,808 an acre in 2022, up 89.3% from $955 in 2017. That still sits 7th lowest of 36 in Oregon, against a state median of $4,773.
