Custer County is a heavily irrigated county, with 58 percent of harvested cropland under pivots and 93 percent of that water coming from groundwater. Irrigated ground rents for $240 per acre against $100 for dryland, and the county's wells have a median depth of 315 feet. Heat and drought was the largest cause of crop insurance indemnities, at 46 percent of the $192.1 million paid over 1989-2025. Its share went from 11 percent in 1989-1999 to 65 percent in 2000-2009, and was 41 percent in 2020-2025. Dryland rent is up 30.9 percent since 2016, and the county has 2,323 irrigation wells with no decommission date on record.
