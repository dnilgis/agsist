Two-fifths of Harlan County's cropland is irrigated, and that dependence comes with risk. Heat and drought have driven crop insurance claims hard. They went from 19 percent of indemnities in 1989-1999 to 61 percent in 2000-2009 and stayed high through 2010-2019 at 65 percent. Corn yields have climbed 1.7 bushels per acre a year over 2008-2025, with a median of 175.4 bushels per acre, though the worst year was 2013 at 133.6. Non-irrigated cash rent is $117 per acre in 2026, up 41.8 percent from 2016. Most irrigation water comes from groundwater, 93 percent, and 1,150 irrigation wells have no decommission date on record.
