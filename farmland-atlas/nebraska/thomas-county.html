Thomas County, Nebraska

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. North district (16 counties), UNL survey 2026: dryland without irrigation potential $1,915 an acre; high grade $2,445, low grade $1,380. ARC-CO is expected to pay $13.57 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

38.4% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 23.33 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 25, 99% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 156 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,696 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.6 days a year and damaging wind on 1.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 13.3% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2020; too much rain caused 5.7%.

The census value of land and buildings in Thomas County was $1,065 an acre in 2022. That is up 36.5% from $780 in 2017, or 6.4% a year, and up from $503 in 2012. It sits 3rd lowest of 93 in Nebraska, well under the state median of $4,499. In 2022, 27% of the farmland was rented.

Quick answers for Thomas County

What is farmland worth in Thomas County, Nebraska?

Land and buildings were worth $1,065 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.
