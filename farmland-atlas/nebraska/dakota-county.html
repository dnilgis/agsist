Dakota County, Nebraska

Rain is at 83% of normal. Land and buildings run $7,081 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $13.41 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 23.3 inches, 83% of normal (28.01 inches) through Nov 17, at Emerson. Frost-free season: 169 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,888 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $7,081 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $264 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent fell 2.6% from 2016 to 2026. 72.6% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.5% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 0.9% of the cropland, CRP left out. 35.7 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 1,052 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

County median corn basis: 39 cents under Dec '26 futures, 1 elevator in the county.

Best corn cash bid within 30 miles on Nov 18: $4.14 at Plymouth Energy-Merrill, 27 miles. 9 elevators we read sit within 30 miles. The newest bids load further down. Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $13.41 per base acre, PLC $4.01.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (159.6 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $58.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 1,851 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $6.67 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 0.82 over the same years. The worst corn year since 2008 made 50% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.6 days a year and damaging wind on 1, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 64.6% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 9.5%.

Dakota County is mostly watered from the ground. Irrigated ground rents for $322 per acre, against $264 for dryland, and 99 percent of the irrigation water comes from groundwater. Heat and drought has been the biggest cause of crop insurance claims since 1989, and its share went from 20 percent in 1989-1999 to 67 percent in 2020-2025. Weeks in severe drought or worse have been frequent here, with 42 in 2022 alone. Land is valued at $7,081 per acre, but that figure is the operators' own estimate, not a sale price.
