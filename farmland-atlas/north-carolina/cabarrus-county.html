Cabarrus County, North Carolina

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $9,055 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $7.90 per base acre for corn.

Cabarrus County vs North Carolina: $70 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 38% of the other 85 North Carolina counties with a 2026 rent (33rd lowest of 86).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 35.21 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 86% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 220 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,581 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $9,055 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $70 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.67 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 10, 2026). 42% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 8% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 25% from 2016 to 2026. 35% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.2% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 1% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 157 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 549 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $9.20 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.05, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 47% of the median yield. 5.2% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.3 days a year and damaging wind on 5.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 55.4% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 31.1%.

Cabarrus is a county where heat and drought drive most of the crop insurance claims. Heat and drought was 91 percent of indemnities in 1989-1999 and 55 percent in 2020-2025, and the loss ratio over the ten closed years 2016-2025 is 1.05, so insurers have paid out more than they took in premium. Only 5 percent of harvested cropland is irrigated. The median corn yield for 2008-2024 is 125.2 bushels per acre, but 2015 fell to 58.4. Non-irrigated cash rent is $70 per acre in 2026, up 25 percent since 2016. For a landowner, that rent is set against land the census puts at $9,055 per acre.
