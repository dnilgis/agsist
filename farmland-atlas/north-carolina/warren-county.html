Warren County, North Carolina

Extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $4,721 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $5.84 per base acre for corn.

Warren County vs North Carolina: $47.50 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 16% of the other 85 North Carolina counties with a 2026 rent (15th lowest of 86).

Weather

Drought now: extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 25.43 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 60% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 216 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,482 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $4,721 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $47.50 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.67 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 31, 2026). 51% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. USDA cash rent rose 3.3% from 2016 to 2026. 34% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 27% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 107.1 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 11,941 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $5.84 per base acre, PLC $1.53.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (60.95 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $0.00 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $13.47 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (22.53 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $8.05 per base acre, PLC $7.25. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (40.14 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $55.90 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $17.61 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.25, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 23% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.4 days a year and damaging wind on 3.8, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 59.7% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 17.1%.

Warren County's crop insurance record leans hard on heat and drought. Those causes made up 60 percent of the $28.3 million in indemnities paid over the closed crop years 1989-2025, and their share went from 58 percent in 1989-1999 to 69 percent in 2020-2025. The loss ratio is 1.32 for the full period and 1.25 over the last ten closed crop years, so claims have run above premiums here. Non-irrigated cash rent is $48 per acre for 2026, and the median county corn yield for 2009-2023 was 87.2 bushels per acre, with 2010 the worst year at 20.
