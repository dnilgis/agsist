Stokes County, North Carolina

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $4,842 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $7.88 per base acre for corn.

Stokes County vs North Carolina: $49.50 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 20% of the other 85 North Carolina counties with a 2026 rent (18th lowest of 86).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

72.3% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 31.71 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 74% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 211 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,312 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $4,842 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $49.50 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.61 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 31, 2026). 18% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 0.9% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 5.3% from 2016 to 2026. 21% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.7% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 7% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 136.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 34.5 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). No foreign-held acres were filed under AFIDA for 2024; none reported can also mean none filed.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $7.88 per base acre, PLC $1.70.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (67.7 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $0.00 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $14.96 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (21.55 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $8.35 per base acre, PLC $6.80. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (37.68 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $55.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 19 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $29.93 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.73, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 61% of the median yield. 3.8% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.4 days a year and damaging wind on 7, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 55.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 20.4%.

Quick answers for Stokes County

What is cash rent in Stokes County, North Carolina?

The county average is $50 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Stokes County, North Carolina?

Land and buildings were worth $4,842 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.

What is the corn yield in Stokes County, North Carolina?

The county's 14-yr trend (projected 2026) is 139.5 bushels an acre (all practices), fit to 13 years of USDA NASS county yields. A trend line is a projection from past years, not a forecast of this year's crop.
