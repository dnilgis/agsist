Frederick County, Maryland

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $11,000 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $9.55 per base acre for corn.

Frederick County vs Maryland: $123 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 60% of the other 20 Maryland counties with a 2026 rent (9th highest of 21).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

99.3% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 35.14 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 87% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 205 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,088 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $11,000 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $123 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.59 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 13, 2026). 27% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. USDA cash rent rose 17.1% from 2016 to 2026. 46% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 5% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 24% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 187.1 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 8,549 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $9.55 per base acre, PLC $2.64.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (105.11 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $80.48 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $23.23 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (33.39 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.49 per base acre, PLC $9.28. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (51.4 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $88.30 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 262 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $10.37 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.64, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 48% of the median yield. 0.7% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.4 days a year and damaging wind on 9.2, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 80.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 12.6%.

Frederick County's ground is mostly dryland, and the weather risk that shows up in the claims is heat and drought. Heat and drought made up 94 percent of indemnities in 1989-1999 and 90 percent in 2020-2025, and the county has paid out more than its premiums in 5 of 24 closed crop years. Summer nights are also warming: the July average low has reached 66.2 F over 2017-2026, against a 1991-2020 normal of 64.9 F. Cash rent for non-irrigated ground is $123 per acre in 2026, up 17.1 percent since 2016, against a census land value of $11,000 per acre.
