Yakima County, Washington

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,782 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $16.24 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

4.6% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 4.59 inches, 70% of normal (6.57 inches) through Nov 17, at Selah, 2 mi NE. Frost-free season: 160 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 1,946 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $2,782 an acre in the 2022 census.

We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. 13.7% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.6% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 5.6% of the cropland, CRP left out. 132.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 160 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 90,736 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $16.24 per base acre, PLC $4.09.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (163.1 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $24.20 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $6.40 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 1.26 over the same years. The worst corn year since 2008 made 93% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.1 days a year and damaging wind on 0.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 23.3% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 2.4%.

Cash rent in Yakima County was $150 an acre in 2021, up 170.3% from $56 in 2011. The census value of land and buildings came to $2,782 an acre in 2022, barely up 1.0% from $2,755 in 2017. That is 10th lowest of 34 in Washington, well under the state median of $5,067. Irrigated rent ran $231 an acre in 2026, 4th lowest of 14 in the state.
