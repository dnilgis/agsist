Jefferson County, Tennessee

Rain is at 94% of normal. Land and buildings run $6,648 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $7.82 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 38.94 inches, 94% of normal (41.54 inches) through Nov 14, at White Pine, 2 mi NE. Frost-free season: 211 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,426 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $6,648 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $58 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Cash rent rose 30.7% from 2016 to 2026. 26% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 1% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 4% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 279.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 4,516 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

PRF grazing county base value: $47.60 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $18.73 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.96, 2016 to 2025. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.8 days a year and damaging wind on 4.2, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 62.5% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 21.4%.

Non-irrigated cash rent, 2026: the highest of 16 surveys since 2009; up 30.7% from $44 in 2016.

USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026

Cropland rent in Jefferson County runs $58 an acre for 2026, up 30.7% from $44 in 2016. That sits under the state median of $65 and ranks 35th lowest of 88. The census value of land and buildings came in at $6,648 an acre in 2022, up 29.1% from $5,149 in 2017, and ranks 16th highest of 93 against a state median of $5,131. 25% of farmland here was rented in 2022.
