Haywood County, Tennessee

Severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $3,625 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $12.02 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 34.67 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 19, 73% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 220 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,717 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $3,625 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $152 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.68 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 27, 2026). 25% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 11% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 21.6% from 2016 to 2026. 63% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.9% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 6% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). Cattle: NASS withholds this county's count (D). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 66 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 2,309 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Best corn cash bid within 30 miles on Nov 22: $4.60 at Jackson, 27 miles.

1 elevator we read sits within 30 miles. The newest bids load further down. Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $12.02 per base acre, PLC $3.17.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (126.45 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $92.39 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $27.95 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (30.78 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.03 per base acre, PLC $10.53. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (58.35 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $56.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 559 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $4.81 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.68, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 70% of the median yield. 15.4% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.6 days a year and damaging wind on 1.6, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 38.9% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 47.1%.
