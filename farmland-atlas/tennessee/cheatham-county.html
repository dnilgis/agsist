Cheatham County, Tennessee

Extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $6,673 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $10.76 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost-free season: 213 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,531 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $6,673 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $94 an acre (USDA, 2024). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. 28% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 1.1% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 7.3% of the cropland, CRP left out. 129.3 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 440 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $10.76 per base acre, PLC $2.22.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (88.5 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (32.4 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: leans ARC-CO, but expected payments are not printed, as wheat has too little price history of its own. PRF grazing county base value: $56.60 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $8.40 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 1.13 over the same years. The worst corn year since 2008 made 38% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.9 days a year and damaging wind on 3.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 44% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 44.3%.

Cheatham County's notable thing is the water, and it cuts both ways. Flood and excess moisture is the largest cause of crop insurance claims here, at 44 percent, and the county's loss ratio of 1.13 over the 2016-2025 crop years means claims have paid out more than the premiums brought in. Heat and drought claims went from 27 percent of indemnities in 1989-1999 to 57 percent in 2020-2025, and the July nightly low has averaged 69.8 F over 2017-2026 against a 1991-2020 normal of 68 F. Non-irrigated cash rent was $94 per acre in 2024, up 14 percent from 2014, and land and buildings were valued at $6,673 per acre in 2022. Not much CRP ground remains, and there is no wind or solar on the books yet.
