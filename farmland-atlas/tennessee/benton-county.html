Benton County, Tennessee

Severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $3,196 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $9.74 per base acre for corn.

Benton County vs Tennessee: $73 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 56% of the other 87 Tennessee counties with a 2026 rent (tied for 38th highest of 88).

Weather

Drought now: severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county.

96.8% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 40.39 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 85% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 213 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,572 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $3,196 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $73 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.62 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 26, 2026). 7% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 12% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 9% from 2016 to 2026. 19% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 4% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 7% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 60.6 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 3 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 529 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $9.74 per base acre, PLC $2.68.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (106.86 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $79.12 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $23.62 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (38.09 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $56.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 24 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $13.07 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.10, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 47% of the median yield. 1.6% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.9 days a year and damaging wind on 3.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 47.4% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 43.9%.

Quick answers for Benton County

What is cash rent in Benton County, Tennessee?

The county average is $73 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Benton County, Tennessee?

Land and buildings were worth $3,196 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.

What is the corn yield in Benton County, Tennessee?

The county's 14-yr trend (projected 2026) is 191.7 bushels an acre (all practices), fit to 9 years of USDA NASS county yields. A trend line is a projection from past years, not a forecast of this year's crop.
