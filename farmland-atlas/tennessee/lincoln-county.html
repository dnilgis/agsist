Lincoln County, Tennessee

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $5,222 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $11.83 per base acre for corn.

Lincoln County vs Tennessee: $155 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 92% of the other 87 Tennessee counties with a 2026 rent (8th highest of 88).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 41.82 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 81% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 214 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,495 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $5,222 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $155 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.74 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 28, 2026). 22% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 5% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 96.2% from 2016 to 2026. 33% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 7% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 4% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 254.9 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 223 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.83 per base acre, PLC $3.04.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (121.15 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $88.88 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $26.77 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (36.66 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $10.79 per base acre, PLC $9.40. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (52.09 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $56.30 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 187 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $4.51 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.47, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 45% of the median yield. 4.8% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.7 days a year and damaging wind on 3.1, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 65.4% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 16%.

The census value of land and buildings was $5,222 an acre in 2022, up 42.6% from $3,663 in 2017, and sits 46th highest of 93. Corn trends at 173.0 bu for 2026, close to the state median of 172.5 bu.
