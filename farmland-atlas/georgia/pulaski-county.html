Pulaski County, Georgia

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $3,436 an acre (2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

48% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 36.44 inches, 86% of normal (42.53 inches) through Nov 17, at Hawkinsville. Frost: the season runs past the March to November window in half the years or more, so no length is shown. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,006 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $3,436 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $142 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 57.8% from 2016 to 2026. 25.5% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 1.9% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 2.1% of the cropland, CRP left out. 12.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 1,496 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

PRF grazing county base value: $51.60 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 49 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $8.75 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 0.87 over the same years. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.3 days a year and damaging wind on 0.9, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 62.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 10.6%.

Cropland in Pulaski County rented for $142 an acre in 2026, the highest of 105 in Georgia and well over the state median of $58. That rent was $90 in 2016, up 57.8% over ten years. The census value of land and buildings came to $3,436 an acre in 2022, up 28.6% from $2,671 in 2017, and still 44th lowest of 135 against a state median of $4,057. 25% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.
