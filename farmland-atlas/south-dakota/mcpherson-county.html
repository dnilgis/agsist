Heat and drought is the story in McPherson County. It drove 51 percent of the crop insurance indemnities paid over 1989-2025, and its share went from 10 percent of claims in 1989-1999 to 61 percent in 2020-2025. The county has 2026 non-irrigated cash rent at $100 per acre, up 27.6 percent from 2016, and the census value of land and buildings is $2,389 per acre. Only 1 percent of harvested cropland is irrigated, so the dry years land on the crop. The corn yield trend has been rising 2.5 bushels per acre a year, with a median of 117 bushels and a worst year at 70.2 in 2021.
