Midland County, Michigan

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $5,538 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $12.43 per base acre for corn.

Midland County vs Michigan: $179 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 93% of the other 70 Michigan counties with a 2026 rent (6th highest of 71).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 28.59 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 96% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 170 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,538 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $5,538 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $179 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.59 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 27, 2026). 11% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 34% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 43.2% from 2016 to 2026. 44% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 67% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 5% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 88.8 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 206.4 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 249 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $4.77 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.57, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 92% of the median yield. 1.1% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.8 days a year and damaging wind on 2.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 27.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 42%.

Midland County's heat and drought claims stand out. Their share of crop insurance indemnities went from 19 percent in 1989-1999 to 41 percent in 2020-2025, and the county has paid out more than premium in only 1 of 19 closed crop years. Flood and excess moisture is still the largest cause at 42 percent of the total. Only 1 percent of harvested cropland is irrigated, so most of the ground depends on rain. Non-irrigated cash rent is $179 per acre for 2026, up 43.2 percent from 2016.
