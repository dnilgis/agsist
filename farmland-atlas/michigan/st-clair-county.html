St. Clair County, Michigan

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $5,814 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $11.85 per base acre for corn.

St. Clair County vs Michigan: $94 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 46% of the other 70 Michigan counties with a 2026 rent (33rd lowest of 71).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

16.6% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 28.83 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 93% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 190 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,632 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $5,814 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $94 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.63 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 31, 2026). 0.6% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 65% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 30.6% from 2016 to 2026. 40% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 37% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 1% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 48.6 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 95.6 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 11,649 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

County median corn basis: 40 cents under Dec '26 futures, 1 elevator in the county.

Best corn cash bid within 30 miles on Nov 24: $3.95 at Vhgc Inc, 21 miles. 2 elevators we read sit within 30 miles. The newest bids load further down. County median soybean basis: 90 cents under Jan '27 futures, 1 elevator.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.85 per base acre, PLC $3.06.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (122.04 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $9.55 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $26.97 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (34.52 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.08 per base acre, PLC $10.08. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (55.82 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $81.50 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 23 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $10.51 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.03, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 81% of the median yield. 0.4% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.8 days a year and damaging wind on 4.6, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 16.6% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 65%.
