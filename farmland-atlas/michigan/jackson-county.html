Jackson County, Michigan

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $4,952 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $11.48 per base acre for corn.

Jackson County vs Michigan: $97 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 47% of the other 70 Michigan counties with a 2026 rent (34th lowest of 71).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 35.85 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 25, 110% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 170 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,553 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $4,952 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $97 an acre (USDA, 2026). Fewer than 5 recorded farm sales here in the last two years, so no sale median. Soils: NCCPI 0.50 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 27, 2026). 28% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 11% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 12.8% from 2016 to 2026. 39% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 11% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 5% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 66.7 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 125 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 2,175 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.48 per base acre, PLC $2.89.

PRF grazing county base value: $82.30 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $4.82 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.52, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 56% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.4 days a year and damaging wind on 2.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 49.1% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 16.2%.

Jackson County's most telling number is the crop insurance picture. Indemnities have paid out $57.9 million on closed crop years since 1989, and the loss ratio is 0.7, with 4 of 22 crop years paying more than premium. Heat and drought was the largest cause at 49 percent of that, and its share went from 42 percent in 1989-1999 to 53 percent in 2020-2025. Non-irrigated cash rent for 2026 is $97 per acre, up 12.8 percent since 2016, and the corn yield trend over 2008-2025 is up 3.2 bushels per acre a year.

Quick answers for Jackson County

What is cash rent in Jackson County, Michigan?

The county average is $97 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Jackson County, Michigan?

Land and buildings were worth $4,952 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.

What is the corn yield in Jackson County, Michigan?

The county's 14-yr trend (projected 2026) is 177.4 bushels an acre (all practices), fit to 14 years of USDA NASS county yields. A trend line is a projection from past years, not a forecast of this year's crop.
