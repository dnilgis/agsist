Saginaw County, Michigan

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $5,683 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $12.12 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 29.44 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 96% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 172 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,603 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $5,683 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $159 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.60 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 28, 2026). 11% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 60% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 4.6% from 2016 to 2026. 45% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 75% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 8% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 20.5 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 120 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 7,342 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $4.38 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.63, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 87% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.6 days a year and damaging wind on 3.8, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 22.3% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 48.8%.

Saginaw County's notable number is the claims record: excess moisture and flood account for 49 percent of the $94.0 million paid out since 1989, and the county's crop insurance loss ratio is 0.57 over those closed years, with 5 of 31 crop years paying out more than premium. Heat and drought went from 16 percent of indemnities in 1989-1999 to 28 percent in 2020-2025, so that share has grown, though it has not moved steadily. The rent is $159 per acre for non-irrigated ground, up 4.6 percent since 2016. Groundwater supplies 94 percent of irrigation water, and the July nightly low has warmed to 61.9 F in 2017-2026.
