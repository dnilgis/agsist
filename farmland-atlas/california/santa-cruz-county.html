Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 29.11 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 113% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost: most years bring no killing frost from March through November, so no season length is shown. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,119 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $38,401 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.52 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 15, 2026). None of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland outright, 11% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. 61% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 3% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 8% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 21.3 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 200 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $1.39 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.30, 2016 to 2025. 95.1% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.1 days a year and damaging wind on 0.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 24.7% of crop insurance claims paid from 1995 to 2025; too much rain caused 30.1%.

Santa Cruz County is mostly irrigated ground, 95 percent of harvested cropland in the 2022 census, and the water comes mostly from wells, 71 percent in 2015. Irrigated cash rent was $2,220 per acre in 2025, against $75 non-irrigated back in 2014. The census put land and buildings at $38,401 per acre in 2022, and that is what operators think the ground is worth, not a sale price. Heat and drought claims were 0 percent of indemnities in 1989-1999 and 51 percent in 2010-2019, so that is the pattern to watch. Excess moisture and flood is the largest cause of claims across 1995-2025, and the loss ratio over those closed years is 0.31.
