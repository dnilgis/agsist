Sutter County, California

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $14,191 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $15.08 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost: most years bring no killing frost from March through November, so no season length is shown. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,353 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $14,191 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $37.50 an acre (USDA, 2023). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.40 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 10, 2026). None of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland outright, 49% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. 47% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 3% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 2% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 13.6 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 100 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 15,986 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $15.08 per base acre, PLC $3.96.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (157.61 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $120.31 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $34.83 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $12.74 per base acre, PLC $14.45. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (80.02 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $27.40 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $11.98 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.74, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 67% of the median yield. 96.1% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.1 days a year and damaging wind on 0.1, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 16.4% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 31.4%.

Sutter County is an irrigated county first. Irrigation covers 96 percent of harvested cropland, and 73 percent of the water comes from groundwater. The irrigated cash rent was $420 per acre in 2026, against $38 per acre for non-irrigated ground in 2023. Irrigated rent was 12.11 times the non-irrigated rent in 2023, up from 4.00 in 2010. Crop insurance has paid out more than it took in: the loss ratio is 1.42 over closed crop years 1989-2025, and 1.74 over 2016-2025. Irrigation failure is the largest cause of claims, at 34 percent. Heat and drought went from 13 percent of claims in 1989-1999 to 10 percent in 2020-2025.
