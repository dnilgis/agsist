How this works

Each quarter, ag bankers in the district say whether each item was higher, lower or the same as a year earlier. The index is the percent saying higher, minus the percent saying lower, plus 100. Over 100 means more said higher; under 100, more said lower; 100 is an even split.

The numbers come from the Kansas City Fed's table of every district survey. Chicago and Dallas quarters are checked against those banks' own files; a quarter where the two disagree is left out rather than guessed, and shows as a gap in the chart, as does a quarter not reported. Left out where the two files disagree: Chicago: 2010 Q2, 2022 Q2, 2025 Q3; Dallas: 2010 Q1, 2010 Q2, 2010 Q3, 2010 Q4, 2011 Q1, 2012 Q3, 2012 Q4, 2013 Q3, 2015 Q3, 2015 Q4, 2016 Q4, 2017 Q1, 2019 Q3, 2019 Q4, 2020 Q1.

Fed districts do not follow state lines: a split state shows each district that reports. Richmond and San Francisco have no reading in the table after 2021 Q4 and 2020 Q2, so they are not shown. Read 2026-10-28.
