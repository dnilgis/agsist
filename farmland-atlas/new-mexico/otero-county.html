Otero County, New Mexico

Rain is at 127% of normal. Land and buildings run $754 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

27.5% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 11.24 inches, 127% of normal (8.86 inches) through Nov 16, at White Sands Nat'l Mon.. Frost-free season: 205 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,253 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $754 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. 38% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 1% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 4% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 23.1 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 105 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 25,726 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

PRF grazing county base value: $8.40 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $8.11 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 2.19, 2016 to 2025. 93.8% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.7 days a year and damaging wind on 2.5, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 5.4% of crop insurance claims paid from 1990 to 2025; too much rain caused 0.6%.

The census value of land and buildings in Otero County runs $754 an acre for 2022. That is up 71.8% from $439 in 2017, or 11.4% a year. It sits a bit under the state median of $784 and ranks 12th lowest of 30 in New Mexico. 38% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.
