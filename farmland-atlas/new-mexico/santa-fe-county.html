Santa Fe County, New Mexico

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $1,245 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $12.15 per base acre for corn.

Text itFacebook

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0.4% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 15.74 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 115% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 164 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,466 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $1,245 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. 11% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.1% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 2% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 16.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 5 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 14 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $12.15 per base acre, PLC $2.99.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (119.21 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $88.43 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $26.35 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $10.10 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

63.1% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.8 days a year and damaging wind on 1.5, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Claims by cause: too few claims here to split them.

Santa Fe County irrigated land rents for $41 an acre in 2025. The census value of land and buildings came in at $1,245 an acre in 2022, up 41.3% from $881 in 2017, or 7.2% a year. That sits well above the state median of $784 and ranks 5th highest of 30 in New Mexico. Only 11% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.
