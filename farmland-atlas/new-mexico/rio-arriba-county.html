Rio Arriba County, New Mexico

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $1,689 an acre (2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost-free season: 139 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,128 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $1,689 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $14 an acre (USDA, 2025). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. 14.5% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 3.1% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 3% of the cropland, CRP left out. 13.3 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). No foreign-held acres were filed under AFIDA for 2024; none reported can also mean none filed.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

Wheat, 2026: close, either one, but expected payments are not printed, as wheat has too little price history of its own. PRF grazing county base value: $10.10 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

88.6% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.7 days a year and damaging wind on 0.5, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 19.3% of crop insurance claims paid from 1990 to 2020; too much rain caused 2.5%.

Cropland rent in Rio Arriba County runs $14 an acre for 2025, down 28.2% from $20 in 2013. Irrigated ground rents for $44 an acre in 2024. The census value of land and buildings came to $1,689 an acre in 2022, up 96.9% from $858 in 2017, and that ranks 3rd highest of 30 in the state against a state median of $784. Only 14% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.
