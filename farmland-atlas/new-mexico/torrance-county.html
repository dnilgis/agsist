Torrance County, New Mexico

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $784 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

71.6% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 14.60 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 110% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 164 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,602 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $784 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.22 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 8, 2026). 11% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. 33% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 0.5% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 19.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 1,089 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 164,252 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included. The 2023 filings showed 68,799 acres, so read the change with care.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $6.33 per base acre, PLC $5.09. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (28.2 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. Figures of Nov 24, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $11.50 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

65.7% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.4 days a year and damaging wind on 1.8, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 2.3% of crop insurance claims paid from 1990 to 2014; too much rain caused 3.3%.

Irrigated rent in Torrance County was $102 an acre in 2024. The census value of land and buildings came to $784 an acre in 2022, up 64.4% from $477 in 2017, or 10.4% a year. Back in 2012 it was $364. That 2022 value ranks 15th highest of 30 in New Mexico, and about 33% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.

Quick answers for Torrance County

What is cash rent in Torrance County, New Mexico?

The county average is $102 an acre for irrigated cropland in 2024. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Torrance County, New Mexico?

Land and buildings were worth $784 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.
