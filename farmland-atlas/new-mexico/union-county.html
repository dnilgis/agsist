Union County, New Mexico

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $852 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 16.24 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 104% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 171 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,748 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $852 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.38 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 1, 2026). None of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland outright, 19% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. 37% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 6% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 31.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 190 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 277,133 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $11.50 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $19.03 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.75, 2016 to 2025. Reported hail an inch or larger on 4.4 days a year and damaging wind on 3.6, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 36.3% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 4.1%.
