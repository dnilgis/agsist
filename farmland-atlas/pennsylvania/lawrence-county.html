Lawrence County's biggest story is water, and it's too much of it. Excess moisture and flood is the largest cause of crop insurance claims here at 49 percent, and the loss ratio over the ten closed crop years 2016-2025 is 0.65, which is well above what farmers paid in premiums. Heat and drought has been a smaller part of the claims: its share went from 54 percent in 1989-1999 to 19 percent in 2010-2019, then rose to 48 percent in 2020-2025. Cash rent for non-irrigated ground is $54 per acre, up 28.6 percent since 2016. Corn yields have a small upward trend at 0.9 bushels per acre a year, with a median of 144.1 bushels and a worst year at 114.9 in 2015.
