Lawrence County's biggest story is water, and it's too much of it. Excess moisture and flood is the largest cause of crop insurance claims here at 49 percent, and the loss ratio over the ten closed crop years 2016-2025 is 0.65, which is well above what farmers paid in premiums. Heat and drought has been a smaller part of the claims: its share went from 54 percent in 1989-1999 to 19 percent in 2010-2019, then rose to 48 percent in 2020-2025. Cash rent for non-irrigated ground is $54 per acre, up 28.6 percent since 2016. Corn yields have a small upward trend at 0.9 bushels per acre a year, with a median of 144.1 bushels and a worst year at 114.9 in 2015.

Quick answers for Lawrence County

What is cash rent in Lawrence County, Pennsylvania?

The county average is $54 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Lawrence County, Pennsylvania?

Land and buildings were worth $5,532 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.

What is the corn yield in Lawrence County, Pennsylvania?

The county's 14-yr trend (projected 2026) is 155.7 bushels an acre (all practices), fit to 12 years of USDA NASS county yields. A trend line is a projection from past years, not a forecast of this year's crop.
