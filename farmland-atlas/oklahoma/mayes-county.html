Mayes County, Oklahoma

Severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $3,529 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $5.39 per base acre for corn.

Mayes County vs Oklahoma: $31 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 52% of the other 73 Oklahoma counties with a 2026 rent (tied for 35th highest of 74).

Weather

Drought now: severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 39.42 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 94% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 213 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,706 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $3,529 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $31 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.68 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 8, 2026). 49% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. USDA cash rent rose 17% from 2016 to 2026. 25% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 1% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 259.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 808.4 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 1,930 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $5.39 per base acre, PLC $1.79.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (71.23 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $0.00 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $15.74 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (17.88 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $6.04 per base acre, PLC $6.59. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (36.51 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $37.40 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $18.84 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.96, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 29% of the median yield. 0.2% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.1 days a year and damaging wind on 4.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 50.7% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 42.5%.
