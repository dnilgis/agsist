Payne County, Oklahoma

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $3,264 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $5.34 per base acre for corn.

Payne County vs Oklahoma: $31 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 52% of the other 73 Oklahoma counties with a 2026 rent (tied for 35th highest of 74).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 33.03 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 95% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 216 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,732 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $3,264 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $31 an acre (USDA, 2026). Fewer than 5 recorded farm sales here in the last two years, so no sale median. Soils: NCCPI 0.50 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 28, 2026). 44% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. USDA cash rent rose 59% from 2016 to 2026. 38% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 7% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 182.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 398 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $31.60 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $24.21 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.98, 2016 to 2025. 4.3% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.6 days a year and damaging wind on 3.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 65.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 21.5%.

Cash rent in Payne County runs $31 an acre for 2026, up 59.0% from $20 in 2016. That sits right at the state median of $31 and is tied for 35th highest of 74. The census value of land and buildings was $3,264 an acre in 2022, up 34.8% from $2,421 in 2017, which ranks 12th highest of 77 against a state median of $2,515. 38% of farmland here was rented in 2022.

Quick answers for Payne County

What is cash rent in Payne County, Oklahoma?

The county average is $31 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Payne County, Oklahoma?

Land and buildings were worth $3,264 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.
