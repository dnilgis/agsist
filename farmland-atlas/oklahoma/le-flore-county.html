Le Flore County, Oklahoma

Extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,896 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $6.50 per base acre for corn.

Le Flore County vs Oklahoma: $22 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 18% of the other 73 Oklahoma counties with a 2026 rent (tied for 14th lowest of 74).

Weather

Drought now: extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost-free season: 223 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,706 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $2,896 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $22 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.64 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 28, 2026). 22% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. USDA cash rent rose 15.8% from 2016 to 2026. 23% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains under 0.1% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 0.8% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 194.1 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 200 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 39,605 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included. The 2023 filings showed 16,797 acres, so read the change with care.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $6.50 per base acre, PLC $2.19.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (87.14 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $39.30 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $19.26 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (11.86 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $5.59 per base acre, PLC $6.55. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (36.26 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $34.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $16.13 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.93, 2016 to 2025. 2.2% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 3 days a year and damaging wind on 5.6, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 33.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 60.2%.
