Choctaw County, Oklahoma

Extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,605 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $8.10 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost-free season: 233 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,871 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $2,605 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $33 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. 22.5% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 0.9% of the cropland, CRP left out. 224.9 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 85 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 4,224 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $8.10 per base acre, PLC $2.47.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (98.6 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Soybeans ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $34.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $25.30 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 1.40 over the same years. The worst corn year since 2008 made 46% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.3 days a year and damaging wind on 2.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 54.4% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 30%.

Choctaw County's crop insurance record is the thing to look at first. Indemnities have paid out more than the premiums in the closed years, with a loss ratio of 1.28 across 1989-2025 and 1.4 over the last ten closed crop years. Heat and drought made up 54 percent of those claims, and their share went from 50 percent in 1989-1999 to 57 percent in 2020-2025. Corn yields have trended up 4.5 bushels per acre a year, but the worst year, 2009, was 62 bushels per acre against a median of 136.2. Non-irrigated cash rent is $33 per acre, and the census puts land and buildings at $2,605 per acre in 2022, up 46.8 percent since 2017.
