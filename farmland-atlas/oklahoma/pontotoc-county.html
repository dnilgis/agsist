Pontotoc County, Oklahoma

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,534 an acre (2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

48.5% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 39.94 inches, 102% of normal (39.07 inches) through Nov 16, at Ada, 0.3 mi NNW. Frost-free season: 227 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,806 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $2,534 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $18 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 20.7% from 2016 to 2026. 20.1% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.5% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 3.4% of the cropland, CRP left out. 131.6 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 147.5 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 2,140 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

PRF grazing county base value: $32.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

Reported hail an inch or larger on 3.6 days a year and damaging wind on 3.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 41.1% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2022; too much rain caused 16.9%.

Stands out against its own record

$18

Non-irrigated cash rent, 2026: the highest of 13 surveys since 2009; up 20.7% from $15 in 2016.

USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026

Cropland rent in Pontotoc County runs $18 an acre for 2026, tied for 4th lowest of 74 in Oklahoma and well under the state median of $31. That rent is up 20.7% from $15 in 2016. Pasture rents for $14 an acre in 2026. The census value of land and buildings came to $2,534 an acre in 2022, up 16.9% from $2,168 in 2017, or 3.2% a year, and sits close to the state median of $2,515.
