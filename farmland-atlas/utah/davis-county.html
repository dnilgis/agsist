Davis County, Utah

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $14,480 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $12.57 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

37.7% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost-free season: 178 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,620 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $14,480 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. 12% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 13% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). 64.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 2 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $12.57 per base acre, PLC $3.02.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (120.5 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $7.40 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

85.6% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.4 days a year and damaging wind on 2.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 27.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 22.2%.

Irrigated cropland in Davis County rents for $153 an acre in 2026, which ranks 3rd highest of 24 in Utah against a state median of $107. Cash rent was $42 an acre in 2021, up 80.4% from $23 in 2009. The census value of land and buildings came to $14,480 an acre in 2022, up 55.3% from $9,325 in 2017, and sits 3rd highest of 28 where the state median is $3,900.
