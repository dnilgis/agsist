Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 14.71 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 137% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 180 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,905 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $4,475 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.27 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey dates Sep 10, 2026 to Sep 11, 2026). None of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland outright, 1% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. 31% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 3% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 71.6 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 95 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 1,280 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

PRF grazing county base value: $7.40 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

99.7% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.1 days a year and damaging wind on 0.6, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live.

The census value of land and buildings in Kane County sits at $4,475 an acre for 2022, up 146.7% from $1,814 in 2017. That puts it 11th highest of 28 in Utah, above the state median of $3,900. Irrigated rent runs $107 an acre in 2026, right at the state median and 12th highest of 24, while pasture goes for $3. 31% of the farmland here is rented.
