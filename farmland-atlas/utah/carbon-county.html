Carbon County, Utah

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,617 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $11.29 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

74.5% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 10.91 inches, 131% of normal (8.3 inches) through Nov 17, at Wellington, 3 mi E. Frost-free season: 134 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,156 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $2,617 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. 43% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 0.8% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 48.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). No foreign-held acres were filed under AFIDA for 2024; none reported can also mean none filed.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.29 per base acre, PLC $2.44.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (97.1 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $3.60 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

99.5% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.4 days a year and damaging wind on 0.5, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Claims by cause: too few claims here to split them.

Irrigated cash rent in Carbon County runs $89 an acre for 2026. That is 4th lowest of 24 in Utah, against a state median of $107. The census value of land and buildings sat at $1,238 an acre in 2017 and reached $2,617 in 2022, up 111.4%, yet it still ranks 7th lowest of 28 under a state median of $3,900. In 2022, 43% of the farmland here was rented.
