Conecuh County, Alabama

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,458 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $7.84 per base acre for corn.

Conecuh County vs Alabama: $27.50 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 17% of the other 58 Alabama counties with a 2026 rent (11th lowest of 59).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

10.2% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 49.22 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 25, 92% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 249 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,967 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $2,458 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $27.50 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.71 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 10, 2026). 31% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. USDA cash rent fell 24.7% from 2016 to 2026. 26% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.1% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 1% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 121.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 116,614 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

PRF grazing county base value: $50.00 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $14.37 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.84, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 47% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.4 days a year and damaging wind on 1.9, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 49.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 24%.

Conecuh County's notable number is heat and drought. They made up 52 percent of crop insurance indemnities in 2020-2025, up from 46 percent in 1989-1999, and the county's largest cause over the full 1989-2025 record at 50 percent. The 2011 drought was the worst year, with 45 weeks of severe drought or worse. Non-irrigated rent is $28 per acre in 2026, down 24.7 percent from 2016. The county's corn yield trend has been up 4.9 bushels per acre a year over 2009-2025, with a median of 116.2 bushels.

Quick answers for Conecuh County

What is cash rent in Conecuh County, Alabama?

The county average is $28 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Conecuh County, Alabama?

Land and buildings were worth $2,458 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.

What is the corn yield in Conecuh County, Alabama?

The county's 14-yr trend (projected 2026) is 148.6 bushels an acre (all practices), fit to 10 years of USDA NASS county yields. A trend line is a projection from past years, not a forecast of this year's crop.
