Hill County, Montana

Rain is at 103% of normal. Land and buildings run $1,066 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $11.39 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: 10.99 inches, 103% of normal (10.7 inches) through Nov 17, at Simpson 6 N - Wildhorse. Frost-free season: 132 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,057 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $1,066 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $30.50 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. USDA cash rent rose 7% from 2016 to 2026. 46% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 0.8% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 8 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 20,575 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.39 per base acre, PLC $1.85.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (73.7 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $11.30 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 2,263 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $17.60 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.92, 2016 to 2025. 0.5% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.3 days a year and damaging wind on 3.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 59.9% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 2.2%.
