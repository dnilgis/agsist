Powder River County, Montana

Severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $957 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $10.69 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 10.46 inches, 83% of normal (12.63 inches) through Nov 17, at Moorhead, 9 mi NE. Frost-free season: 143 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,378 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $957 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $18 an acre (USDA, 2025). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. 24% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 0.4% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 46.7 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 8,181 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $10.69 per base acre, PLC $0.99.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (39.4 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $8.10 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $21.17 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.96, 2016 to 2025. 11.9% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 3.6 days a year and damaging wind on 3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 49% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 0.5%.

Cropland in Powder River County rents for $18 an acre in 2025, and pasture goes for $7 an acre in 2026. The census value of land and buildings was $957 an acre in 2022, up 73.7% from $551 in 2017, which works out to 11.7% a year. That sits under the state median of $1,237 and ranks 8th lowest of 53. Only 24% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.
