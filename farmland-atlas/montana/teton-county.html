Teton County, Montana

Rain is at 120% of normal. Land and buildings run $1,825 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $11.39 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 13.79 inches, 120% of normal (11.46 inches) through Nov 17, at Choteau. Frost-free season: 129 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 1,701 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $1,825 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $42 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 18.3% from 2016 to 2026. 41% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 0.2% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 0.5% of the cropland, CRP left out. 46.8 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 3,083 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.39 per base acre, PLC $2.89.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (115 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $11.30 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 15,367 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $11.92 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 0.98 over the same years. 26.1% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.1 days a year and damaging wind on 1.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 54.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 3.6%.

Cropland rent in Teton County was $42 an acre in 2026, up 18.3% from $36 in 2016 and 6th highest of 40 in Montana against a state median of $31. The census value of land and buildings was $1,825 an acre in 2022, up 53.9% from $1,186 in 2017. That ranks 14th highest of 53. Renters worked 41% of the county's farmland in 2022.
