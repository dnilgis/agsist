Surry County, Virginia

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,949 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $11.00 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

85.5% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost-free season: 232 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,592 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $2,949 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $80 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 22.1% from 2016 to 2026. 47.1% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 26.4% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 54.9% of the cropland, CRP left out. 21.9 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 4,204 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $11.00 per base acre, PLC $2.83.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (112.7 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $76.50 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $3.53 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 0.32 over the same years. The worst corn year since 2008 made 34% of the median yield. 6% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.1 days a year and damaging wind on 2, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 67.8% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 19.1%.

Heat and drought is the main story in Surry County. It made up 89 percent of crop insurance indemnities in 1989-1999 and 67 percent in 2020-2025, and the county paid out more than premium in just 1 of 17 closed crop years. The corn yield trend is up 5.3 bushels per acre a year, with a median of 143.7 bushels, but 2010 was 49. Only 6 percent of harvested cropland is irrigated, so most of the ground depends on rain. The county has 390.3 MW of operating solar and no wind. Non-irrigated rent is $80 per acre, up 22.1 percent since 2016.
