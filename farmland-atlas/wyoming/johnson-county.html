Johnson County, Wyoming

Extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $1,165 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $7.34 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 6.21 inches, 54% of normal (11.45 inches) through Nov 17, at Kaycee. Frost-free season: 139 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,021 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $1,165 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $9 an acre (USDA, 2024). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. 40.8% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 2.3% of the cropland, CRP left out. 29 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). No foreign-held acres were filed under AFIDA for 2024; none reported can also mean none filed.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $7.34 per base acre, PLC $1.90.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (75.9 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $8.40 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

80% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.4 days a year and damaging wind on 2.2, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 3.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2021; too much rain caused 0%.

The census value of land and buildings in Johnson County was $1,165 an acre in 2022, up 98.1% from $588 in 2017. That puts it 11th lowest of 22 in Wyoming, a bit under the state median of $1,248. Irrigated cropland rented for $78 an acre in 2026, tied for 7th lowest of 15 and right at the state median. Pasture went for $5 an acre in 2026, and 41% of the farmland was rented in 2022.
