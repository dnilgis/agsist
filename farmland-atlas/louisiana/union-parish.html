Union Parish, Louisiana

Exceptional drought (D4) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $3,928 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: exceptional drought (D4) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 32.67 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 18, 66% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 253 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,966 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $3,928 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $26.50 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. USDA cash rent fell 34.6% from 2016 to 2026. 18% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 5% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 260.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 1,081 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

PRF grazing county base value: $61.60 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

0.3% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.7 days a year and damaging wind on 5.9, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 15.5% of crop insurance claims paid from 2019 to 2025; too much rain caused 42.4%.

Cash rent in Union Parish runs $27 an acre for 2026, 14th lowest of 46 in Louisiana and well under the state median of $56. That rent is down 34.6% from $41 in 2016. The census value of land and buildings went the other way, up 29.0% to $3,928 an acre in 2022 from $3,044 in 2017, which ranks 23rd highest of 63. Only 18% of farmland here was rented in 2022.
