Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

95.7% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 12.35 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 81% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 143 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,240 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $12.77 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.81, 2016 to 2025. Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.6 days a year and damaging wind on 3.1, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 54.1% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 22.4%.

Heat and drought is the story in McKenzie County. It caused 54 percent of the crop insurance indemnities paid from 1989 to 2025, and its share went from 24 percent in 1989-1999 to 79 percent in 2020-2025. The county has had 207 weeks of severe drought or worse since 2000, and 2021 alone had 52 of them. Irrigation is barely used here, with only 3 percent of water from groundwater, so irrigated cash rent of $185 per acre rests on very little ground. Non-irrigated rent is $30 per acre, down 1.7 percent since 2016.
