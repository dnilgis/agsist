Burleigh County, North Dakota

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $1,991 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $7.85 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 12.37 inches, 68% of normal (18.2 inches) through Nov 17, at Bismarck Municipal Airport. Frost-free season: 145 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,283 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $1,991 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $72 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 38.5% from 2016 to 2026. 54.4% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 1% of the cropland, CRP left out. 87.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 1,160 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $7.85 per base acre, PLC $2.26.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (89.9 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $18.90 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: 144 acres under contract end in fiscal year 2027 (USDA FSA).

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $13.18 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 0.77 over the same years. The worst corn year since 2008 made 54% of the median yield. Reported hail an inch or larger on 3.3 days a year and damaging wind on 4.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 53.9% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 20%.

Heat and drought is the story in Burleigh County. Its share of crop insurance indemnities went from 21 percent in 1989-1999 to 73 percent in 2020-2025, and the 2021 season alone had 36 weeks of severe drought or worse. Corn is still doing fine on average, with a median yield of 99.8 bushels per acre over 2009-2025, though the worst year, 2021, was 54. Non-irrigated cash rent for 2026 is $72 per acre, up 38.5 percent since 2016. Wind is the other big presence here, with 201.4 MW operating and nothing solar yet. CRP covers 7.8 percent of the cropland base.
