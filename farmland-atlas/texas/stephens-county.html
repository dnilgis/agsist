Stephens County, Texas

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,831 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $5.13 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

68.2% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 27.97 inches, 103% of normal (27.17 inches) through Nov 16, at Breckenridge. Frost-free season: 243 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,993 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $2,831 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $11 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Cash rent rose 25% from 2016 to 2026. 28% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 1% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 47.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). No foreign-held acres were filed under AFIDA for 2024; none reported can also mean none filed.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $5.13 per base acre, PLC $0.75.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (30 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Wheat, 2026: close, either one, but expected payments are not printed, as wheat has too little price history of its own. PRF grazing county base value: $27.60 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.6 days a year and damaging wind on 1.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 80.7% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 7.5%.
