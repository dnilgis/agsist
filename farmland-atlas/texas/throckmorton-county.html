Throckmorton County, Texas

Severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,232 an acre (2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain: no station here reported nearly every day this year, so we show no rain figure. Frost-free season: 244 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,007 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $2,232 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $24 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 44.1% from 2016 to 2026. 55.2% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drainage: NASS withholds this county's figure (D). Cover crops go on 0.7% of the cropland, CRP left out. 77.1 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 287.6 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 65,235 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

Wheat, 2026: close, either one, but expected payments are not printed, as wheat has too little price history of its own. PRF grazing county base value: $27.60 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $38.24 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 1.03 over the same years. Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.3 days a year and damaging wind on 1.8, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 66.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 6.2%.

USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026

Cropland rent in Throckmorton County runs $25 an acre in 2026, up 44.1% from $17 in 2016. That sits just under the Texas median of $27 and ranks tied for 78th lowest of 190. The census value of land and buildings came in at $2,232 an acre in 2022, up 58.5% from $1,408 in 2017. 55% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.
