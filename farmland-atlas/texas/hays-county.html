Hays County, Texas

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $11,177 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $6.80 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

52.7% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 33.63 inches, 99% of normal (34.13 inches) through Nov 17, at Wimberley, 3.6 mi ENE. Frost: most years bring no killing frost from March through November, so no season length is shown. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,195 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $11,177 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $26 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent fell 11.7% from 2016 to 2026. 34.6% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 3.1% of the cropland, CRP left out. 53.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 3,508 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $6.80 per base acre, PLC $1.71.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (68.3 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 16, 2026. Wheat, 2026: close, either one, but expected payments are not printed, as wheat has too little price history of its own. PRF grazing county base value: $27.20 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $19.97 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 1.08 over the same years. The worst corn year since 2008 made 15% of the median yield. 6.8% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.1 days a year and damaging wind on 1.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 83.1% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 10.3%.
