King County, Texas

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $1,130 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 17.17 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 19, 78% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 243 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,897 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $1,130 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $35 an acre (USDA, 2025). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.49 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 3, 2026). 19% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. 16% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. The 2022 census reports no cover crops here. 32.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). No foreign-held acres were filed under AFIDA for 2024; none reported can also mean none filed.

Grain

Grain: we do not read elevator bids in this state yet.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $23.90 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $65.71 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.63, 2016 to 2025. 46.8% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.1 days a year and damaging wind on 1.6, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 75.2% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 4.4%.

Cash rent in King County runs $35 an acre for 2025, up 125.8% from $16 in 2014. That is 2nd highest of 16 in Texas, against a state median of $24. The census value of land and buildings was $1,130 an acre in 2022, up 63.1% from $693 in 2017, and still 10th lowest of 246 in the state. Only 16% of farmland here was rented in 2022.
