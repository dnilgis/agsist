Lubbock County, Texas

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $2,673 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

17.5% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 15.87 inches, 92% of normal (17.29 inches) through Nov 17, at Lubbock International Airport. Frost-free season: 220 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,612 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $2,673 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $42 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Cash rent rose 44.8% from 2016 to 2026. 55% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 15% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 81.6 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 924 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

1 elevator we read sits within 30 miles. The newest bids load further down. Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $34.95 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.33, 2016 to 2025. 64.8% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 4.9 days a year and damaging wind on 7, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 60.3% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 4.2%.

$42

Non-irrigated cash rent, 2026: the highest of 16 surveys since 2009; up 44.8% from $29 in 2016.

USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026

Cropland rent in Lubbock County runs $42 an acre for 2026, up 44.8% from $29 in 2016. That sits well above the $27 state median and ranks tied for 33rd highest of 190. Irrigated ground rents for $76 an acre in 2026, under the $99 state median. The census value of land and buildings was $2,673 an acre in 2022, up 57.1% from $1,702 in 2017, and 55% of the farmland here was rented in 2022.
