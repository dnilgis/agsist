Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 30.87 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 25, 97% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost: most years bring no killing frost from March through November, so no season length is shown. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,401 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $3,741 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $29 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.44 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 3, 2026). 51% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 6% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 70.6% from 2016 to 2026. 30% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 6% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 0.7% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 105.9 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 356.1 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 7,493 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included. The 2023 filings showed 3,136 acres, so read the change with care.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $20.12 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.17, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 26% of the median yield. 12.8% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.9 days a year and damaging wind on 0.8, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 77.1% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 5.2%.

Goliad County is mostly dryland, and the water question runs through most of what the numbers say. Only 13 percent of harvested cropland is irrigated, and all of that water comes from groundwater. Crop insurance has paid out $14.3 million on closed crop years since 1989, with heat and drought behind 77 percent of it. That share was 43 percent in 1989-1999 and 93 percent in 2010-2019, and it was 80 percent in 2020-2025. Non-irrigated rent is $29 per acre, up 70.6 percent since 2016, and the land and buildings are valued at $3,741 per acre by operators in 2022.

Quick answers for Goliad County

What is cash rent in Goliad County, Texas?

The county average is $29 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Goliad County, Texas?

Land and buildings were worth $3,741 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.

What is the corn yield in Goliad County, Texas?

The county's 14-yr trend (projected 2026) is 65.8 bushels an acre (all practices), fit to 6 years of USDA NASS county yields. A trend line is a projection from past years, not a forecast of this year's crop.
