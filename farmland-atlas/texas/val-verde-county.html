Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 25.97 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 145% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost: most years have no spring freeze after February; the first fall freeze comes around Nov 28 in the median year. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,290 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $1,000 an acre in the 2022 census.

We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.35 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 3, 2026). None of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland outright. 44% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 1% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 8.1 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 276 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 141,205 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Risk

The census value of land and buildings in Val Verde County was $1,000 an acre in 2022, down 0.8% from $1,008 in 2017. Back in 2012 it was $570. That 2022 figure ranks 2nd lowest of 246 in Texas, well under the state median of $2,782. In 2022, 44% of the farmland here was rented.
