Robertson County, Texas

Severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $3,500 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $9.10 per base acre for corn.

Robertson County vs Texas: $11 an acre cash rent in 2025, higher than 7% of the other 15 Texas counties with a 2025 rent (2nd lowest of 16).

Weather

Drought now: severe drought (D2) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 24, 2026. Rain this year: about 29.26 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 25, 80% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost: most years have no spring freeze after February; the first fall freeze comes around Nov 25 in the median year. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,163 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $3,500 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $11 an acre (USDA, 2025). We read recorded sales in Florida, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.49 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 3, 2026). 26% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland. 43% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 4% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 209.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 43,835 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $9.10 per base acre, PLC $2.25.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (89.59 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 25, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $0.00 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $19.80 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $5.60 per base acre, PLC $5.34. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (29.59 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $31.30 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $8.67 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.73, 2016 to 2025. 17.6% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.4 days a year and damaging wind on 0.8, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 46% of crop insurance claims paid from 1990 to 2025; too much rain caused 24.3%.

Quick answers for Robertson County

What is cash rent in Robertson County, Texas?

The county average is $11 an acre for non-irrigated cropland in 2025 and $98 an acre for irrigated cropland in 2026. It is the USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, a county average and not the rent on any one farm.

What is farmland worth in Robertson County, Texas?

Land and buildings were worth $3,500 an acre in the 2022 Census of Agriculture, the operators' own estimate. It is not an appraisal, and a sale on a given farm can come in well above or below it.
