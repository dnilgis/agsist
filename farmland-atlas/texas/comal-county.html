Comal County, Texas

Rain is at 98% of normal. Land and buildings run $5,507 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 33.6 inches, 98% of normal (34.23 inches) through Nov 17, at Canyon Lake, 2.5 mi W. Frost: most years bring no killing frost from March through November, so no season length is shown. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,203 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $5,507 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $12 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Cash rent rose 13.6% from 2016 to 2026. 35% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 3% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 65.3 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 5,811 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $4.32 per base acre, PLC $3.30. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (18.3 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $27.20 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Risk: no crop insurance record for this county.

8.9% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.7 days a year and damaging wind on 1.8, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 87.6% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 6.2%.

Cash rent in Comal County ran $13 an acre in 2026, up 13.6% from $11 in 2016. That is 7th lowest of 190 in Texas, where the state median is $27. The census value of land and buildings tells a different story at $5,507 an acre in 2022, up 61.3% from $3,415 in 2017 and 27th highest of 246.
