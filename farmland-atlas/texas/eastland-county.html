Eastland County, Texas

Rain is at 92% of normal. Land and buildings run $3,026 an acre (2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: no drought level covers half the county.

35.4% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 25.18 inches, 92% of normal (27.38 inches) through Nov 16, at Cisco, 4.1 mi N. Frost-free season: 243 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 3,964 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $3,026 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $20 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 70.8% from 2016 to 2026. 37.8% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 2.9% of the cropland, CRP left out. 72.2 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 4,787 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

Wheat, 2026: close, either one, but expected payments are not printed, as wheat has too little price history of its own. PRF grazing county base value: $27.60 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $33.00 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Loss ratio 1.06 over the same years. 9.1% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 2.4 days a year and damaging wind on 1.3, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 73.7% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 9.3%.

USDA NASS Cash Rents Survey, 2026

Cash rent in Eastland County runs $21 an acre for 2026, tied for 48th lowest of 190 in Texas against a state median of $27. That rent is up 70.8% from $12 in 2016. The census value of land and buildings came in at $3,026 an acre in 2022, up 54.1% from $1,964 in 2017, or 9.0% a year.
