Hopkins County, Texas

Extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $4,732 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census).

Weather

Drought now: extreme drought (D3) covers at least half the county.

100% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: 30.1 inches, 75% of normal (40.29 inches) through Nov 18, at Sulphur Springs. Frost-free season: 264 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,009 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $4,732 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $30.50 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. USDA cash rent rose 15.1% from 2016 to 2026. 29% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 4% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 349.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 250 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 16,584 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn ARC-CO: no expected payment for this county.

Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $6.09 per base acre, PLC $5.40. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (29.9 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. Figures of Nov 16, 2026. PRF grazing county base value: $34.60 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $24.70 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.11, 2016 to 2025. 5.2% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 1.3 days a year and damaging wind on 2, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 19.9% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 65.3%.

Cropland rent in Hopkins County runs $31 an acre for 2026, up 15.1% from $27 in 2016. That rent is tied for 73rd highest of 190 Texas counties, against a state median of $27. The census value of land and buildings came to $4,732 an acre in 2022, up 62.2% from $2,918 in 2017 and 45th highest of 246. Pasture rents for $20 an acre in 2026, and 29% of farmland was rented in 2022.
