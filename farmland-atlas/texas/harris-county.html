Harris County, Texas

Moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $9,000 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $9.60 per base acre for corn.

Harris County vs Texas: $34 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 69% of the other 189 Texas counties with a 2026 rent (tied for 57th highest of 190).

Weather

Drought now: moderate drought (D1) covers at least half the county.

97.7% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 47.20 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 23, 97% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost: most years bring no killing frost from March through November, so no season length is shown. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 4,411 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $9,000 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $34 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.45 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Sep 3, 2026). 22% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 3% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent rose 74.4% from 2016 to 2026. 20% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). The 2022 census reports no tile-drained cropland here. Cover crops go on 0.8% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 96.4 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). Solar and wind proposed but not built: 2.4 megawatts of nameplate capacity (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 10,205 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Grain

Grain: no elevator we read within 30 miles.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $9.60 per base acre, PLC $2.30.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (91.63 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $4.11 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $20.25 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Wheat, 2026: ARC-CO expected $4.85 per base acre, PLC $4.23. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (23.41 bushels) and past years' price swings around $6.20 wheat (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Price range from how far USDA's November wheat price missed the final season-average price, 2015 to 2025 (11 years); for 2015 to 2018 USDA printed a range, and we use its middle. PRF grazing county base value: $27.40 an acre, crop year 2027. CRP: FSA lists no contract expirations for this county.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $6.15 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 0.33, 2016 to 2025. 38.6% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 3.4 days a year and damaging wind on 6.4, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 46.5% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 37.6%.

Cropland rent in Harris County runs $34 an acre in 2026, up 74.4% from $20 in 2016 and tied for 57th highest of 190 in Texas. The census value of land and buildings was $9,000 an acre in 2022, 5th highest of 246 and well over the state median of $2,782. That value climbed only 4.2% from $8,635 in 2017, 0.8% a year. Renters work 20% of the farmland here.
