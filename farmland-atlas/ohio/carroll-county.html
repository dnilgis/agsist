Carroll County, Ohio

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $5,992 an acre (operators' own estimate, 2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $8.60 per base acre for corn.

Carroll County vs Ohio: $64.50 an acre cash rent in 2026, higher than 13% of the other 75 Ohio counties with a 2026 rent (11th lowest of 76).

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 17, 2026. Rain this year: about 42.66 inches averaged over the county, Jan 1 to Nov 24, 112% of NOAA's 1991-2020 gridded normal. That is an estimate from the PRISM 4 km grid, the figure the county rain page leads with. Frost-free season: 178 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,766 in the median year, 1996 to 2025 (NOAA).

Land

Land and buildings: $5,992 an acre in the 2022 census.

OSU's survey covers western Ohio's northwest and southwest regions only, so no figure for this county. Western Ohio Cropland Values and Cash Rents 2025-26, Ohio State University Extension, survey of January to April 2026. Cash rent, non-irrigated: $64.50 an acre (USDA, 2026). We read recorded sales in Florida, Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, Vermont and Wisconsin so far, not in this state. Soils: NCCPI 0.56 acre-weighted across the county's rated farmland (USDA SSURGO, survey Aug 31, 2026). 10% of the county's mapped acres are prime farmland, 6% more are prime farmland if drained or otherwise improved. USDA cash rent fell 1.5% from 2016 to 2026. 31% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 6% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 11% of the cropland, CRP left out (2022 census). 126 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 40 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Soybeans: no elevator we read in the county posts a bid.

Program

Corn, 2026: ARC-CO expected $8.60 per base acre, PLC $2.60.

This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (103.61 bushels) and past years' price swings around $4.60 corn (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Figures of Nov 24, 2026. Corn, 2025 crop: ARC-CO $65.31 per base acre (FSA posted rate), PLC $22.90 (from FSA's posted prices), at the county-average PLC yield; for 2025 the farm gets the higher one, before the sequestration cut. Soybeans, 2026: ARC-CO expected $0.00 per base acre, PLC $0.00. This assumes a normal crop, the county-average PLC yield (32.51 bushels) and past years' price swings around $12.10 soybeans (USDA WASDE, Nov 10, 2026). Wheat ARC-CO or PLC: it depends on your PLC yield. PRF grazing county base value: $61.20 an acre, crop year 2027. No CRP contracts here end in fiscal year 2027.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $15.49 per $100 of coverage a year, 2016 to 2025 (the last ten closed crop years, USDA RMA).

Loss ratio 1.15, 2016 to 2025. The worst corn year since 2008 made 66% of the median yield. 0.1% of harvested cropland is irrigated (2022 census). Reported hail an inch or larger on 0.8 days a year and damaging wind on 4.2, 2010 to 2025. Reports lean to places where people live. Heat and drought caused 58.6% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 29%.
