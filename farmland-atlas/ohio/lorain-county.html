Lorain County, Ohio

Abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county. Land and buildings run $8,788 an acre (2022 census). ARC-CO is expected to pay $13.19 per base acre for corn.

Weather

Drought now: abnormally dry (D0) covers at least half the county.

0% of the county is in drought (D1 or worse) on the U.S. Drought Monitor map of Nov 10, 2026. Rain this year: 36.33 inches, 100% of normal (36.19 inches) through Nov 17, at Elyria, 0.4 mi SE. Frost-free season: 199 days in the median year, 1996 to 2025. Corn growing degree days, May through September: 2,791 in the median year.

Land

Land and buildings: $8,788 an acre in the 2022 census.

Cash rent, non-irrigated: $137 an acre (USDA, 2026). We do not read recorded sales in this state yet. Cash rent rose 45% from 2016 to 2026. 43.2% of the land in farms is rented from others (2022 census). Tile drains 47.7% of the cropland (2022 census). Cover crops go on 7.2% of the cropland, CRP left out. 50.3 cattle and calves per 1,000 acres of land in farms (2022 census). No solar or wind capacity is proposed here (EIA, 2025). Foreign-held land: 20 acres filed with USDA under AFIDA for 2024, forest included.

Risk

Crop insurance claims: $5.52 per $100 of coverage a year, the last ten closed years.

Heat and drought caused 13.4% of crop insurance claims paid from 1989 to 2025; too much rain caused 69.4%.

Lorain County is a dryland county where the water risk shows up as too much rain, not too little. Excess moisture and flood account for 69 percent of the $52.7 million in indemnities paid on closed crop years since 1989, and only 1 percent of harvested cropland is irrigated. Heat and drought claims went from 32 percent of indemnities in 1989-1999 to 20 percent in 2020-2025, and that latest share is the one to watch. The county has paid out less than it took in premium over the last ten closed crop years, with a loss ratio of 0.52. Non-irrigated cash rent is $137 per acre, up 45 percent since 2016.
