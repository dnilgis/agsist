    Corn N Rates by Soil and Price Ratio (A2809 Table 6.1)

    Total lb N/ac, starter included. The big number is the rate with the most return to N (MRTN, the maximum return to nitrogen); under it, every rate within $1/ac of that return. N:corn ratio = $ per lb of N ÷ $ per bu of corn. The column for the prices in the box at the top is marked.

    Which end of the range (A2809):
    Upper end: more than half the soil covered by residue, or all N from manure.
    Low end: over 10% organic matter, or N left from last year. Or take the high end less the N a spring soil nitrate test (PPNT) finds.
    High end: any soil under 2% organic matter.
    Middle to low: corn after small grains, and sands with 2 to 9.9% organic matter.

    How it works: A2809 Table 5.1 gives tons of 60–69 NI lime from your water pH and buffer pH (the Sikora buffer pH, the lime-need number on a Wisconsin soil report). Your lime = that × 65 ÷ NI (use the middle of the grade, 85 for 80–89). Lime only when water pH is more than 0.2 below target. Minimum 2 ton of 60–69 lime (1.5 of 80–89); never over 12 ton. NI is not the same as CCE: use the NI on the lime label or ask the quarry. Example: buffer 6.5, water 6.0, target 6.8 is 195 − 28.4 × 6.5 + 0.144 × 6.0 = 11.3 ton of 60–69 lime, or 8.5 ton of 80–89. Cost uses the price you type; lime prices swing with haul distance. Source: UW Extension A2809, Chapter 5. Other states use other buffers and formulas; use your state’s guide outside Wisconsin.

    Waterhemp management priority across the Corn Belt: Waterhemp has confirmed resistance to seven site-of-action groups: 2, 4, 5, 9, 14, 15 and 27. Groups 2 and 9 are widespread; resistance to 4, 15 and 27 is in some populations, and fields with five- and six-way resistant waterhemp are on record. That makes it the highest resistance-management priority in most corn and soybean regions. Use 3+ effective MOA programs with a soil residual, and do not rely on post-only programs.

    Common questions

    How much nitrogen should I apply to corn?
    It depends on your soil, the last crop and the N:corn price ratio: the price of a pound of N divided by the price of a bushel of corn. UW Extension A2809 (Mar 2026 web edition), from Wisconsin trials, gives corn after soybeans on high-yield loamy soil 140 lb N at a 0.05 ratio, 120 at 0.10, 105 at 0.15 and 90 at 0.20. Corn after corn on the same soil gets 190, 165, 150 and 135. Subtract manure and legume credits. The N box on the Reference tables tab works out the ratio from posted N and corn prices. Outside Wisconsin, use your state's MRTN rates.
    What is my manure worth in fertilizer value?
    Multiply the total N, P2O5 and K2O in each ton or 1,000 gallons by what those nutrients cost as urea, MAP and potash. That is the value of all of it. A crop can use 80% of the P2O5 and K2O and 25 to 65% of the N the first year, by UW Extension A2809. The Manure tab has the pounds by manure type and a calculator at dated posted prices. The manure value page at agsist.com/manure-value prices every manure type, an acre and for a whole field.
    What is the economic threshold for soybean aphid?
    250 aphids per plant on average, aphids on more than 80% of plants, and the population rising. That threshold runs through R5. University of Minnesota Extension sets no threshold for R6.
    How do I calculate growing degree units for corn?
    GDU (base 50°F) = [(daily max temp, capped at 86°F) + (daily min temp, floor at 50°F)] / 2 - 50. Accumulate daily from planting.
    When is it worth spraying Japanese beetles on corn silks?
    Iowa State says spray only when all three are true: three or more beetles per ear, silks clipped to less than an inch, and pollination not done yet. Feeding is worse at the field edge, so look across the whole field before you make the call.
    When do I start and stop scouting beans for aphids?
    Minnesota Extension says start in mid to late June and keep going until R6.5, when pods and leaves begin to yellow, whatever the calendar says. Once a field has aphids, check it every week, because they build fast.
