Commodity loan: what a CCC marketing loan costs to hold grain

A marketing assistance loan from FSA puts cash in hand at harvest on grain you keep. You borrow your county's loan rate on each bushel for up to nine months and pay interest at the CCC rate. This page figures that interest per bushel, and sets it next to the carry you would earn by storing.

Carry: what a later delivery bid pays over today's. Sell now or store? figures it from your elevator's bids; bring that number here.

How the interest adds up

interest a bushel a month = loan rate x interest rate / 12 corn at $2.42 and 5%: 2.42 x 0.05 / 12 = 1.01 cents soybeans at $6.82 and 5%: 6.82 x 0.05 / 12 = 2.84 cents

The 5% is only an example to show the arithmetic; it is not the current CCC rate. The page uses FSA's posted rate once it has read it, or the one you type.

Questions

What is a CCC marketing loan?

A loan from USDA's Commodity Credit Corporation, made through your county FSA office, with your grain as the collateral. For the 2026 crop the national loan rate is $2.42 a bushel for corn and $6.82 for soybeans, and each county has its own rate near those. The loan runs up to nine months.

What does the loan cost a bushel?

The loan rate times the CCC interest rate, divided by 12, for each month. At an example rate of 5%, corn at $2.42 costs about 1 cent a bushel a month (2.42 x 0.05 / 12), 9.1 cents for nine months. Soybeans at $6.82 cost about 2.8 cents a month.

When does it pay to take the loan and store?

When the carry, what a later delivery pays over today's bid, is more than the interest plus what storing costs you. Sell now or store? figures the carry from your elevator's bids; put that number in the carry box here.

Is it cheaper than my operating line?

Put your operating line's rate in the calculator and it shows both on the same dollars for the same months. The CCC rate is set by FSA each month; your lender's rate is whatever your note says.

Where do I get the loan?

At your county FSA office. This page does not check eligibility; the office decides whether you and the grain qualify. Corn and soybean loans for the 2026 crop are available through May 31, 2027.

Where do the county loan rates come from?

FSA posts a loan rate for every county each crop year. The page reads FSA's files on a schedule. Until the first read it uses the national rate and says so. Your county office has the official number.

Will the loan rate stay the same next year or change?

The national rates are written into the law for each of the 2026 through 2031 crop years: $2.42 a bushel for corn and $6.82 for soybeans. FSA says the One Big Beautiful Bill Act extended the loan program through 2031, and the 2026 rates went up from 2025. Your county rate is set on its own, near the national one.

Can I get a loan on wheat or oats too, or just corn and beans?

Yes. FSA makes these loans on wheat, oats, barley and other crops. The national rates are $3.72 a bushel for wheat and $2.20 for oats (7 U.S.C. 9032). Loans on the 2026 wheat, oats and barley crops can be taken out through March 31, 2027, two months before the May 31 cutoff for corn and soybeans. This calculator does corn and soybeans only, but the interest math is the same.
