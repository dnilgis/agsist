Harvest Fuel Relief 2026: Rates, Sign-Up and What USDA Has Said

Last checked Nov 13, 2026.

As of Nov 13, 2026, USDA has not published payment rates, sign-up dates or a Federal Register notice for Harvest Fuel Relief. Here is everything that is confirmed, what is coming, and what nobody has answered yet.

How we checked on Nov 13: we could not open usda.gov or the FSA program page from our servers. We read USDA’s own words in the copy Nebraska CropWatch posted on Oct 20. We searched the Federal Register and read the outlets named below, the newest dated Nov 11. None reports rates, dates or a notice.

Every fact below carries one label. USDA says means we read it at USDA or in a USDA-authored copy. Reported by means we read it at that outlet’s page, on the date shown. A fact we could not open at its source is not on this page.

What USDA has confirmed

These come from USDA’s Oct 19, 2026 announcement. We read it in the USDA-authored copy on Nebraska CropWatch, posted Oct 20. USDA’s own program page is here; we link it and take no dated claim from it.

USDA calls the farm-bill program crops “Title I” commodities and the rest “non-Title I.” Here we say program crops and other crops.

  • USDA says Oct 19, 2026
    $8 billion in one-time payments for the 2026 harvest season, to give relief during temporary market disruptions and higher input costs.
  • USDA says Oct 19, 2026
    FSA runs it. The money comes through the Commodity Credit Corporation under its Charter Act.
  • USDA says Oct 19, 2026
    Same style as Farmer Bridge Assistance. One uniform formula covers a portion of modeled losses in the 2026 crop year. It is not farm by farm.
  • USDA says Oct 19, 2026
    Three inputs. FSA-reported planted acres, Economic Research Service cost of production estimates, and WASDE yields and prices.
  • USDA says Oct 19, 2026
    Rates for the program crops come in November. The payment method for other eligible commodities will be announced at a later date.
  • USDA says Oct 19, 2026
    No crop insurance linkage. USDA still urges producers to use the risk tools in the Working Families Tax Cuts Act.
  • USDA says Oct 19, 2026
    Other crops. Specialty crop growers, fishermen and others outside the program crops are encouraged to send fuel cost data by email. USDA will consider it when it sets rates.
  • USDA says Oct 19, 2026
    Login.gov. Eligible producers are encouraged to create an account before the application period is announced. Pre-filled applications will be online for accounts linked to USDA.

What USDA officials added in interviews

  • Reported by Agweek, Oct 23, 2026
    USDA Undersecretary Richard Fordyce said payments tie to 2026 acreage, with a different rate for each crop. Agweek reported that the program will not cover hauling after the crop leaves the field, or livestock movement.
  • Reported by Agweek, Oct 23, 2026
    Fordyce said it will run like Farmer Bridge Assistance, with pre-filled applications that already carry acres and rates by crop. Agweek adds that the first tranche goes to the program crops and the second to specialty crops.
  • Reported by Hoosier Ag Today, Oct 27, 2026
    Fordyce said USDA will go in “a couple of phases.” Program crops come first because the data are readier. Specialty crops, other crops and fishermen follow.
  • Reported by Brownfield, Oct 26, 2026
    Fordyce said rates for the program crops should be final within a week. He gave no timeline for other crops, only that it would not be “a great long time.” As of Nov 13 we found no rates.

Which crops are Title I

Reported by Agweek, Oct 23, 2026
Agweek lists the farm-bill program crops this way. Grains: barley, corn, oats, grain sorghum and wheat. Rice, cotton and peanuts: long grain rice, medium and short grain rice, seed cotton and peanuts.

Oilseeds: soybeans, canola, crambe, flaxseed, mustard seed, rapeseed, safflower, sesame seed and sunflower seed. Pulses: dry peas, lentils, and large and small chickpeas. USDA has not published a crop list for this program, so treat the list as a guide.

How to get ready now

1. Link a Login.gov account to USDA

USDA says Oct 19, 2026
USDA encourages it before the application period is announced, so the pre-filled application can find you. Accounts are made at login.gov.

Reported by Hoosier Ag Today, Oct 27, 2026
Fordyce urged producers to log in now and check that the profile is linked.

2. Check your acreage on file with FSA

USDA says Oct 19, 2026
Planted acres reported to FSA are one input to the formula.

Reported by Ketel Thorstenson CPA, Nov 9, 2026
An error in reported acres could affect your payment.

We suggest asking your county FSA office to confirm your 2026 acreage report. Ask whether any cutoff date applies. We have seen a claim of one and could not confirm it at USDA, so we do not state it.

3. Not a Title I grower? Send fuel data to HarvestFuel@usda.gov

USDA says Oct 19, 2026
USDA encourages specialty crop growers, fishermen and other growers outside the program crops to email fuel cost data. USDA will consider it when it sets rates. The address is HarvestFuel@usda.gov.

That email is data, not an application.

Reported by Massachusetts Division of Marine Fisheries, Nov 2, 2026
The advisory says USDA monitors the address for information only and has no form for it yet. It says fishermen must resubmit through the official application process once it opens.

Reported by Baker Tilly, Nov 6, 2026
Useful records are gallons purchased, price per gallon and total fuel expense, with prior-year figures for context. USDA has not set a format. The Massachusetts advisory lists what fishermen could send, and says supporting documents may be needed later.

Questions nobody has answered yet

For each one we say what has been reported. None of these has a USDA answer as of Nov 13.

Landlords and tenants: who gets the payment?

Reported by The Bullvine, Oct 31, 2026
Producer-share rules are still pending. The earlier Farmer Bridge program used the share each applicant reported, but USDA has not said HFR will. Ask your landlord now what the lease says about government payments.

Silage and hay

Reported by The Bullvine, Oct 31, 2026
USDA’s release does not say whether corn silage acres count. Farmer Bridge counted them.

Reported by Brownfield, Oct 26, 2026
Alfalfa and hay are not among the program crops.

Reported by Michigan Farm News, Nov 11, 2026
The American Dairy Coalition asked USDA to credit repeated hay cuttings, counted as acres times cuttings.

Dairy

Reported by Michigan Farm News, Nov 11, 2026
The coalition asked for a dairy rate per hundredweight of milk marketed, with the per-acre method kept for program crops. It cites 2018 and 2019 Market Facilitation Program dairy payments as precedent. USDA has not answered.

Livestock

Reported by Agweek, Oct 23, 2026
The program will not cover livestock movement.

Reported by DTN, Oct 30, 2026
The U.S. Cattlemen’s Association criticized the lack of cattle hauling costs. Senator Chuck Grassley said he would support extending help to livestock.

Payment limits and income tests

Reported by Baker Tilly, Nov 6, 2026
USDA has not addressed payment limits or other producer-level restrictions.

Reported by Ketel Thorstenson CPA, Nov 9, 2026
Income restrictions and entity rules are also open.

Reported by Agweek, Oct 29, 2026
Columnist Kent Thiesse noted there was no word on the 5.7% sequestration cut that applied to the latest ARC and PLC payments.

Proration: could rates be cut to fit $8 billion?

We found no published answer. USDA says rates cover a portion of modeled losses, but it has not said how that portion is set or whether sign-up could change it.

Automatic or do you apply?

USDA says Oct 19, 2026
Pre-filled applications will be online. Whether you must sign and send one is not stated.

Reported by Agweek, Oct 29, 2026
One columnist expects FSA to calculate each payment automatically from planted acres. USDA has not said so.

How much CCC money is left?

Reported by Hoosier Ag Today, Oct 27, 2026
Lawmakers asked whether the Commodity Credit Corporation is near its spending ceiling. Fordyce said USDA always keeps a buffer and is comfortable with what is left. No figure was given.

How the money is figured

What has been published

USDA says Oct 19, 2026
The payment covers a portion of modeled 2026 crop-year losses, as one national figure. Rates are set by commodity, from three inputs: FSA planted acres, ERS cost of production estimates, and WASDE yields and prices.

Reported by Agweek, Oct 23, 2026
Fordyce said payments tie to 2026 acreage, with a different rate for each crop.

Reported by Ketel Thorstenson CPA, Nov 9, 2026
It is not a reimbursement of the diesel you bought.

What is missing

  • The rate for each crop, and which crops are in.
  • What share of modeled losses is covered, and whether that share can change.
  • Which acres count, and how late or prevented-planted acres are treated.
  • Payment limits, income tests, entity rules and any sequestration cut.
  • The method for specialty crops, other crops, dairy and livestock.

Without the rates, any dollar figure for your farm would be a guess. We show none. We will update this page when USDA posts the rates.

Questions

What is Harvest Fuel Relief?

USDA says it will make $8 billion available in one-time payments for the 2026 harvest season. The Farm Service Agency runs it, and the Commodity Credit Corporation pays. It uses one uniform formula that covers part of modeled 2026 crop-year losses. It does not repay each farm's fuel bills. USDA announced it on Oct 19, 2026.

Has USDA published Harvest Fuel Relief payment rates?

No. As of Nov 13, 2026, we found no rates. USDA said on Oct 19 that rates for the program crops, such as corn, soybeans and wheat, would be released in November. It said the method for other eligible commodities would come at a later date.

When can farmers apply for Harvest Fuel Relief?

USDA has not announced an application period. Hoosier Ag Today and Illinois Corn reported that pre-filled applications would open in January 2027. That is a reported date, not a confirmed FSA date.

How do farmers get ready to apply?

USDA says to create a Login.gov account and link it to USDA before the application period is announced. Pre-filled applications will be online for linked accounts. Also check that the 2026 acreage on file with your county FSA office is correct.

Is crop insurance required for Harvest Fuel Relief?

No. USDA says there is no crop insurance linkage. It strongly urges producers to use the risk management tools in the Working Families Tax Cuts Act.

Is the payment based on fuel receipts?

No receipts are part of the announced formula. USDA says the payment uses FSA-reported planted acres, Economic Research Service cost of production estimates, and WASDE yields and prices. Reported by Agweek on Oct 23: USDA's Richard Fordyce said payments tie to 2026 acreage, with a different rate for each crop.

What should specialty crop growers and fishermen do?

USDA encourages specialty crop growers, fishermen and other producers outside the program crops to email fuel cost data to HarvestFuel@usda.gov. That email is data for USDA to consider when it sets rates. It is not an application. USDA has not announced the payment method for these crops.

Does Harvest Fuel Relief cover dairy and livestock?

Not yet confirmed. Reported by Agweek on Oct 23: the program will not cover livestock movement. Dairy groups have asked USDA for a rate based on hundredweights of milk and for credit for repeated hay cuttings. USDA has not answered.

Is there a payment limit or an income test?

USDA has not said. Baker Tilly reported on Nov 6 that USDA had not addressed payment limits or other producer-level restrictions. Ask your tax adviser how a payment could affect your limits.

How much will a farm get per acre?

Nobody knows yet, and we show no estimate. USDA has not released the rates. Some economists have floated scenarios, which we list on this page with their sources, but they are not USDA rates.
