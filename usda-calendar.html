About These Reports

WASDE

World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates. Monthly global crop and livestock supply/demand balance. Released around the 10th of each month at 12:00 PM ET.

HIGH· Moves on surprises

Prospective Plantings

Farmer survey of intended spring planting acres for corn, soybeans, and wheat. Released late March. It shapes the market's acreage view for the year ahead.

EXTREME· Major price mover

Acreage

Planted and harvested acreage for major crops from the June survey, out the same day as the June 1 Grain Stocks. A surprise here can move the market as much as any report all year.

EXTREME· Can move limit on a surprise

Crop Production (Aug)

The August report has USDA's first survey-based corn and soybean yields of the season, replacing the trend-based numbers in earlier reports.

EXTREME· First survey yields

Grain Stocks (Sep 1)

The September 1 count is the most watched of the four quarterly grain stocks reports. It pins down the old-crop carryout that the new marketing year starts from.

EXTREME· Critical carry-out signal

Grain Stocks (Dec/Mar/Jun)

Quarterly inventory of corn, soybeans, wheat, sorghum, and oats on farms and in commercial facilities. December, March, and June reports track mid-marketing-year pace.

HIGH· Quarterly carry-out signal

Crop Progress

Weekly condition ratings (Excellent/Good/Fair/Poor/VP) and planting or harvest progress by state. Released Mondays at 4:00 PM ET (Tuesday after a Monday holiday) from the first Monday in April through the Monday after Thanksgiving (Nov 30 in 2026). The numbers by state land on our crop ratings and harvest progress page.

LOW-MOD· Summer G/E ratings move prices most

Export Sales

Weekly net sales of corn, soybeans, and wheat to foreign buyers. Released Thursdays at 8:30 AM ET by USDA FAS. Large China purchases can move futures at release.

MODERATE· China sales = high impact

How forecasters are scored. Public pre-report forecasts measured against the USDA actual: avg error is the mean absolute % miss, beat-trade is the share landing closer than the consensus, bias is the mean signed % error. Errors are pooled only inside a metric group (yield with yield, ending stocks and production with each other) because a 2% miss on a 180 bu yield and a 2% miss on a 310 million bushel carryout are not the same miss; the site’s own in-line bands (0.5% for yields, 2% for stocks) have graded every report since Oct 10, 2026. A forecaster needs 3+ scored calls in one group to be listed above the line, and this page prints no finishing order until at least two of them have 10+ each. It tracks the most public forecasters, not necessarily the best. It is a record of who called what, not advice or an endorsement.

Methodology

What “expected” means, what gets measured, how a report is scored and where the actual comes from.

What "expected" means. The estimate is the pre-report trade survey: the average of the figures grain analysts and trade firms submit in the days before a USDA report, the same numbers the wire services aggregate and the market trades into ahead of the release. The range is the spread of those guesses. It is what the market has already assumed, before USDA says a word.

What gets measured. The headline supply figures that move price: U.S. ending stocks (carryout) for corn, soybeans, and wheat, plus production and yield in the months USDA reports them, plus the quarterly Grain Stocks report, a different measure: a survey estimate of the bushels held on farms and in elevators and processing plants on the first of the quarter, built from NASS's surveys of farm operators and off-farm storage, rather than a balance-sheet projection. These are the numbers traders watch first, because a surprise there ripples through the whole balance sheet.

How a report is scored. Each figure is graded on how far it landed from the trade average. Within 0.5% for a yield, 1% for production and 2% for stocks is in line: two per cent of a 183 bu/acre yield is 3.7 bushels, which would score almost any print as unremarkable, while two per cent of a 2.1 billion bushel carryout is about 42 million and is a fair band. Past the band, a supply figure below the trade average is bullish (less supply than expected) and above it is bearish (more supply than expected). The survey's low and high are printed beside the grade as context ("inside the range, near the top", "outside the trade range") and do not change it. A figure with no trade estimate on record is not scored at all and says so. This rule took over on Oct 10, 2026, and every report on this page was regraded with it. From Oct 3 to Oct 9 any print inside the trade range was graded in line however far from the average; October’s corn ending stocks landed 10.3% over the average and read in line on a day December corn fell 20½¢. A trade estimate only counts if its source is dated before the report. One whose date could not be established is kept and marked "source date unknown". The bullish and bearish surprise thresholds on the report card are the edges of the trade range: a print past either one is outside the surveyed range of analyst estimates. The page does not yet hold enough graded reports to say how price responds when one is.

Where the actual comes from, and when. The corn and soybean yields this board grades are NASS's, published in the Crop Production report at the same hour as the WASDE. A watcher asks NASS for the release month's forecast by name every five minutes inside the release window and fills the figure in the moment it exists. Nothing is written before the report is public, and a figure the source does not publish is left blank with the reason rather than estimated. Ending stocks and the rest of the balance sheet are printed only in the WASDE itself and are still filled in by hand. Grain Stocks prints on its own separate release day and is filled in by hand from that report, not from the WASDE.

Sources. Report dates from the USDA WASDE and NASS release schedules; trade estimates from the published pre-report surveys, linked on each figure; actuals from USDA NASS (yields and Grain Stocks) and the WASDE itself (ending stocks, typed in by hand). Fund positioning from the CFTC Commitments of Traders, dated on the card. This is a map of expectations, not a trade recommendation or financial advice.

The rule

Written down before the first live call, and the backtest that came with it.

Written down on 2026-10-03, and backtested once that day, before the first live call, as rule set bot-v1.1. The backtest came out no better than a coin flip and is published as such. On 2026-10-05, after that day’s close and before the first live call was written, the timing was corrected so live calls match what was backtested (one call per report, the release date for holiday weeks, shutdown weeks skipped); the direction rule did not change. Any later change gets a new version number and a new record of its own.

  1. Crowding first. Take managed money's net position (CFTC) as a share of open interest, from that week's report. Rank it against the 156 reports before it, about three years. This is the bot’s own rule, not the site’s measure of crowded: the COT page ranks the same share against every week since 2010 and calls funds crowded only in the top or bottom tenth of that whole record (90th/10th percentile, on the side the position is on), and it makes no forecast from it; its own tests find no timing signal. Under the bot’s rule, at or above the 90th percentile of the three years: call down. At or below the 10th: call up.
  2. Otherwise, trend. If the roll-repaired front-month index (Yahoo’s continuous futures with the jump at each contract change removed) is higher than 13 weeks ago, call up; lower, call down; exactly flat, no call.
  3. Grade. An up call is right when the close at the exit (the entry close of the report 4 weeks later) is above the entry close. A down call is right when it is below. A close equal to the entry is a miss.

There is no seasonal signal. The seasonal figures the site has are built from all years at once, so using them would grade 2013 with knowledge from 2026.

Live prices
Daily closes of the named contract (for example Dec ’26 corn), from Yahoo Finance daily bars. The call is made after the close, before the 7 PM Central evening reopen, from that day’s reading; its entry price is then replaced once, on the next run, by the day’s bar as Yahoo restates it, and the log notes any change. A call is graded on the run after its exit day, from the restated exit bar. Yahoo’s bars are not the exchange’s settlement file. The Oct 5, 2026 calls were first read at 8:34 PM, after the reopen; each note says how the entry was set. Grains are quoted in cents per bushel; the page shows them in dollars. The contract is the first listed month still trading on the grading day, so no live call spans a contract roll.
Entry and exit days
Entry: the close of the first business day after CFTC published the report that is not a federal holiday. Federal holidays are skipped even when CME trades (Columbus Day, Veterans Day), and a day CME is closed moves the entry to its next session. Exit: the entry close of the report four weeks later. Both are fixed when the call is made. Release dates: scripts/cot_calendar.py. CFTC publishes on the Friday of the report week at 3:30 PM Eastern, after grains settle; a federal holiday delays it. Reports held back by a government shutdown (2013, 2018–19, 2025) are skipped, because their real release dates are not all known.
Positioning
CFTC Disaggregated Futures-Only Commitments of Traders, managed money long minus short, divided by open interest. A report counts from the close of the first non-holiday business day after CFTC published it (it lands at 3:30 PM Eastern, after grains settle; a federal holiday delays it).
Backtest prices
The roll-repaired front-month index in data/cot-deep.json: Yahoo's continuous series with the return across each contract change dropped. It is an index, not a price. One call per weekly report, same entry and exit days as live. Decisions whose grading day falls after 2026-10-02 are left out, so the backtest never overlaps the live record. The continuous series follows Yahoo's own roll, which can differ by a few days from the exchange calendar the repair uses.
Overlap, counted every way
Weekly calls on a four-week window overlap, and the four crops on one report are one bet on one month of markets. So the record is counted every way: all calls, tested with a Newey–West test that allows for the overlap; and four disjoint groups, by report week counted mod 4, in which no two calls on the same crop overlap; the four crops on one report share one window. The page prints the range across the four groups. Picking one group would let the luckiest stand in for all.
Coin-flip odds
The chance that pure guessing does at least as well, from the Newey–West test on all calls. Five records are tested at once (all crops together and each crop), so the odds are adjusted with the Benjamini–Hochberg method before any record is called a finding.
Baselines
How often the price fell, and rose, over the same windows: what a bot that always said down, or always said up, would have scored.
Minimum before a rate is shown
At least 40 graded calls and 30 non-overlapping four-week windows in time (crops on the same report share one window). Below that the page says “Not enough calls yet”.
Raw files
/data/predictions.json (calls, record, rule) and /data/predictions-backtest.json (every backtest call). Code: scripts/prediction_bot.py; its selftests carry hand-worked answers.

Common questions

When is the next USDA crop report?
USDA's next crop reports, WASDE and Crop Production, come out Tuesday, Nov 10, 2026, at 12:00 p.m. ET (11 a.m. CT). Crop Progress comes out Mondays at 4:00 p.m. ET through Nov 30, 2026.
What time do USDA reports come out?
The big crop reports come out at 12:00 p.m. ET (11 a.m. CT): WASDE, Crop Production, Grain Stocks, Prospective Plantings and Acreage. Cattle on Feed, Hogs and Pigs, Milk Production and Cold Storage come out at 3:00 p.m. ET (2 p.m. CT), the time NASS uses for most of its other reports. Crop Progress is out Mondays at 4:00 p.m. ET (3 p.m. CT) in season, and Export Sales Thursdays at 8:30 a.m. ET. Source: the NASS 2026 release calendar and USDA's WASDE schedule.
When is the WASDE report released?
The USDA WASDE report is released monthly, typically around the 10th of each month at 12:00 PM Eastern Time (11:00 AM Central). It can move corn and soybean futures sharply at release.
When is the USDA Export Sales report released?
The USDA Export Sales report usually comes out Thursday at 8:30 AM Eastern Time from the USDA Foreign Agricultural Service. A federal holiday earlier in the week can push it to Friday. It covers weekly net export sales of corn, soybeans, and wheat.
When is the Prospective Plantings report?
The USDA Prospective Plantings report is typically released in late March, usually the last business day of the month. It surveys farmers on intended corn, soybean, and wheat planting acres and is one of the most market-moving reports of the year.
When is Crop Progress released?
USDA Crop Progress is released every Monday at 4:00 PM Eastern Time (Tuesday when Monday is a federal holiday), from the first Monday in April through the Monday after Thanksgiving (Nov 30 in 2026, per the NASS calendar). It provides weekly condition ratings and planting and harvest progress percentages by state.
What happens if I miss the Dec. 11 ARC/PLC deadline for 2026?
FSA says a farm with no 2026 election in by Dec. 11, 2026 keeps its 2025 election for its crops, and the farm gets no payments for the 2026 program year.
What does "priced in" mean?
Markets move on surprises. If the trade already expects a bearish number, that expectation is built into today's price: "priced in." Price reacts to the gap between the report and what was expected, which is why the expectation matters as much as the report.
Where do the expectations come from?
The estimate range is the pre-report trade survey, typed in from the published survey and left blank with the reason when none has been filed. Positioning is the weekly CFTC Commitments of Traders, dated on the card, because a position is only a fact about the Tuesday it was taken. Implied odds appear when a prediction market is quoting the report and the card says so when none is. Together they show what the market has already assumed.
What's a "surprise threshold"?
The trade average plus or minus its band. Since Oct 10, 2026 every figure is graded on its distance from the pre-report trade average: within 0.5% for a yield, 1% for production and 2% for stocks it is in line, and past that it is a surprise. For a supply number like stocks, production or yield, below the band is bullish and above it is bearish. So a print can sit inside the analysts' range and still be a surprise: the October corn yield, 181.2 against a 177.8 average, was inside the 173.2 to 182.1 range and graded bearish. The range is printed beside the grade as context. This page does not yet hold enough graded reports to say how price responds when a print lands past a threshold.
Is this a trade recommendation?
No. It's a map of expectations, not advice. It tells you where the bar is set so you can judge a report's reaction for yourself.
