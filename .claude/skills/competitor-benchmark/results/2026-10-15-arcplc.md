## Where we win
- **A pre-computed answer for every county, crop and practice, with no download or login.** There are about 2,970 corn county/practice rows, each with a verdict, the expected dollars for each program, and the count of past years each one won. farmdoc, AFPC and K-State all make the farmer bring inputs and run it. Nobody else has a page for "Story County ARC or PLC" that ranks.
- **Both years on one screen:** 2026 (Dec 11) and 2027 (Nov 2 to Mar 15). We show 2027 ERP estimates with the math shown ($4.34 / $10.62 / $6.35), and the 2027 call uses Dec '27 futures. Most competitors still talk only about 2026.
- **Honest verdict grades.** Pick / Leans / Close / Neither pays use a stated statistical rule, and a back-test checks our ARC-CO rates against all 92,577 FSA rows to the cent. The page says plainly when there is "no answer yet". The counter sheet for the FSA office is unique.
- **Phone-first and fast.** Static HTML, no Excel, and a one-point check plus a price-by-yield grid.

## Where competitors beat us
- **AFPC:** it simulates price and county yield together and shows **probability charts** of each program's payments. It also handles the farm's own irrigated share. Our 11 past-year scenarios give a count ("0 of 11"), not a distribution. For 2026 we hold the county yield at benchmark, so a drought-hit county's yield risk is missing until the farmer types a number.
- **farmdoc and AFPC model ARC-IC.** Farm CPA Report ("ARC-IC might be the best call you make this fall") is pushing it this season. We only say "this tool does not model it".
- **MSU's Oct 9 article answers faster.** It says it in one paragraph: corn ARC-CO triggers at about $4.53 vs PLC $4.42, PLC wins only below about $3.62, and soybeans most likely pay nothing at $12.00. It also gives yield-shortfall triggers by crop (corn 3.7%, soybeans 8.7%). On our main page, a phone shows only pickers and the deadline table on the first screen. The Quick answer sits on screen two, below an email box, and starts with 2027 ERPs instead of "ARC-CO for most corn".
- **FSA wins on authority.** It's where you enroll (Login.gov online election), and it has the official deadline notice. We link to it but don't say you can elect online.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **Put the verdict first (S).** On the main page, move the Quick answer above the deadline table and the reminder box. Start it with: "For 2026 most corn leans ARC-CO (2,521 of 2,967 county rows); soybeans: neither pays at USDA's $12.00 in 2,478 counties; wheat: close." On county pages, put the corn and soybean cards directly under the H1, before the picker. Put the county name in the H1 ("Story County: ARC or PLC 2026").
2. **Add a "what yield drop makes ARC pay" line to each county card (S).** Use this county's benchmark and USDA's price, for example: "ARC-CO pays on corn here if the county yield comes in under 215 bu at $4.70 (90% of 223.82 bu x $5.03, divided by $4.70)." It's the number MSU and K-State lead with. It comes from the data we already have and doesn't forecast anything.
3. **Add a 2026 county yield nudge (M).** Where NASS or RMA county or state 2026 yield estimates exist (the Oct crop report has state yields), offer them as a labeled, optional "state yield vs trend" input. The 2026 call shouldn't silently assume a normal crop in a dry county. Hold the call to "Leans" when the yield is a state proxy. AFPC's main edge is yield risk.
4. **Add a simple ARC-IC section (M).** Explain when ARC-IC beats ARC-CO (your yields differ from the county's, whole-farm 65% rule, benchmark = your Olympic yield x the same benchmark price). Give a one-input check: "your 5-year farm yield vs county benchmark". Link to AFPC's ARC-IC spreadsheet. This closes the one gap a CCA would be asked about this fall.
5. **Render each verdict once in the HTML (S-M).** County pages repeat each crop's verdict three times for the Lower/Same/Higher toggle (visible in raw text and to crawlers/LLMs). Render "Same" in the HTML and build Lower/Higher from the state data file. That cuts the biggest county page (McLean ND, 95 KB) roughly in half, keeps it inside the 150 KB rule with room to spare, and stops search snippets reading "ARC-CO Leans ARC-CO ... ARC-CO Leans". Also add one line: "You can elect online at fsa.usda.gov/arc-plc with Login.gov, or at your county office."

All five respect the standing rules: no agent routing, no consulting, derived numbers labeled, no em dashes, and smaller pages.

**Best on the internet today? Close:** for "ARC or PLC in my county" we're the only free page that gives the answer without inputs, and the most transparent one. AFPC still beats us on yield risk and probability, ARC-IC is missing, and the answer sits a screen too low on phones.
