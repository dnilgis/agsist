## Where we win
- **Coverage.** It works at any US point.
  - The MRCC list (fetched Oct 16) shows measured inversion networks live in seven listings (IA, IN, KS, MI, MO, ND/MN, SD), with IL and KY "coming soon". Each one covers its own stations.
  - Nobody else gives an hourly, product-aware read for a field anywhere else.
- **Label logic.** No station tool in the set models a label. We check, by product:
  - 2026 OTT dicamba heat rules: the 95 F block and the 85-94 F half-acres cap.
  - The Minnesota and Illinois 85 F rules and cutoff dates.
  - The sunrise/sunset window.
  - Enlist, contact and glyphosate wind limits.
- **The math is on the page:** tank mix with partial last tank, calibration both ways, mix order and nozzles. All checked above.
- **The card says why and when.** "Watch: gusts to 18 mph, over the 15 mph limit. Next good window: 5PM." No competitor in the set phrases a verdict like that.

## Where they beat us, and how
- **Measured inversion.**
  - SDSU: "It is determined from the vertical temperature difference (either 3 and 10 ft or 6 and 33 ft)." Its stations update every 5 minutes.
  - NDAWN: it "sends a push notification to the user" when an inversion forms.
  - Ours is a rule (10 m wind under 5 mph, hour, cloud cover).
  - **No links out:** the page has no link to any mesonet (grep finds no "mesonet" or "NDAWN"). That holds even in the states where a measured reading exists.
- **Records.** SDSU's "Record by Email" sends any station's conditions from the last 7 days. "The South Dakota Department of Agriculture accepts SD Mesonet data for recordkeeping purposes." We offer nothing for the record the dicamba label requires.
- **Boom wind is stability-aware there.** SDSU says 3 ft wind "can range from 28% to 85% of a standard height wind depending on conditions". It derives it with EPA methods. We use one fixed 0.748 (2 m, neutral air) for every hour.
- **Focus.** The station tools are one screen. Ours runs 15,467 px on a phone and 228 KB.

## Bugs found
| # | Finding | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **OTT dicamba outside MN/IL is never stopped by a label date.** On Oct 16 in Iowa, dicamba reads "Marginal" with "Next good window: 9AM-10AM". The page text says "Apply only through R1 ... or the label's cutoff date, whichever comes first", but it never names that date and the rating never checks it. Fix: quote the 2026 label cutoff from the label itself and block after it, as the MN/IL rules already do. Verify the date at the label before shipping. | Low-medium (it tells a farmer an illegal window is "good" late in the season) |
| S2 | **The boom factor is one number.** 0.748 is the 2 m neutral-air ratio. SDSU gives 28-85% for 3 ft depending on conditions, and Sprayers101 gives about 0.67 at 1 m. In a stable evening the true ratio is lower, so the page tends to overstate boom wind in the hours that matter most for inversion. Fix: say on the card that it is a midpoint, and quote the range. | Low |
| S3 | The page grew from 219 KB / 12,800 px to 228 KB / 15,467 px on a phone since Oct 15. | Low (clarity, speed) |

## The 5 changes, ranked
1. **Link the measured network for the field's state (S).**
   - Use the geolocated state. Where the MRCC list shows a live network (IA, IN, KS, MI, MO, ND/MN, SD, plus Oklahoma's DRA), show one line under the inversion row: "Measured inversion near you: <network> (opens their station page)."
   - It is a plain link, with no data copied. It turns our weakest cell into a pointer to the best source.
   - Farmers helped: many. Gap: big. Honesty risk: low.
2. **Apply and quote the dicamba label cutoff date (S).**
   - Read the 2026 Engenia/Stryax/Tavium label dates and block after them, with the label quoted.
   - Farmers helped: some. Gap: medium. Honesty risk: medium until the label is read at the source.
3. **Read live station data where it is open (M).**
   - Where a network serves data the browser can read (CORS), show the nearest station's measured inversion and 3 ft wind with its time and distance, beside our forecast.
   - Load it only when the location is in that network.
   - Farmers helped: many. Gap: big. Honesty risk: low if each reading is labeled with station and time.
4. **Split the page (M).**
   - Keep the verdict, hourly strip, product picker and calculators.
   - Load the dicamba rules, nozzle table, tips and calendar on demand from one data file. That gets the phone page well under 10,000 px and the HTML under 150 KB.
   - Farmers helped: many. Gap: medium. Honesty risk: none.
5. **A copyable spray-record line (S).**
   - One tap copies the hour's forecast wind, gust, direction, temp, RH and delta T, with the location and time.
   - The copy is labeled "forecast at 10 m, not an on-site reading; the label requires your own measurement".
   - Farmers helped: some. Gap: medium. Honesty risk: medium (it must never pass as a measured record).
