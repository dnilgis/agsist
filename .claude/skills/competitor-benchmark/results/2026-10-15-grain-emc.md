# Head to head: agsist.com/grain-emc (new page)

Checked Oct 15, 2026, while building the page. I rendered grain-emc.html at 390x844 and 1280x800, light and dark, with a saved location, with none, and through the ZIP box. The Open-Meteo forecast and the ZIP table were mocked because the sandbox cannot reach them. There was no sideways scroll and no page errors. The page is 37 KB, and the math and UI scripts together are about 27 KB.

**What I could not see:** the sandbox proxy blocks every competitor host, and WebFetch could not resolve any domain. Every competitor cell below comes from search-result text and is marked *.

## Numbers checked by hand
- **Corn, 50 F and 60% (D245.6 Modified Chung-Pfost, A 374.34, B 0.18662, C 31.696):**
  - T = 10 C. -ln 0.6 = 0.5108; x 41.696 / 374.34 = 0.05690; -ln = 2.8665; / 0.18662 = 15.36% dry basis.
  - That is **13.3% wet basis**. Minnesota and Kentucky Extension tables both say 13.8%*.
- **Wheat, 60 F (ASAE Modified Henderson, K 2.3007e-5, N 2.2857, C 55.815):**
  - At 20, 40, 60 and 80% RH the page gives 7.90, 10.97, 13.73 and 16.91%.
  - Minnesota Extension's table gives 7.9, 11.0, 13.7 and 16.9%*. That is within 0.05 at all four, which is how I confirmed the constants.
- **Corn against 18 published cells (Minnesota and Kentucky, 40 to 70 F, 50 to 70% RH):**
  - The equation reads 0.2 to 0.9 point drier at every cell but one (Minnesota at 60 F and 50% RH, where it is 0.15 wetter).
  - Arkansas reads wetter still: 15.7% at 60 F and 70%, against our 14.2%*.
  - This is why an hour counts as "dries" only when its EMC is at least 1 point under the target. The tests pin that the margin covers every Minnesota and Kentucky cell.
- **Fan heat:** 50 F at 80%, warmed 5 F: es(10 C) = 12.260 hPa, es(12.78 C) = 14.734 hPa, so 80 x 12.260 / 14.734 = **66.6%**. Matches.
- **Cooling:** 15 / 0.2 cfm/bu = **75 fan hours** (Cornell Cooperative Extension rule*). Matches.

## Where we win
- **The answer on the first phone screen:** "Fans dry it / Close call / Fans wet it", the EMC now against your target, and the best run of drying hours ("Zach's window"), with no app to install.
- **Hour by hour for 72 hours, from a ZIP or the phone's location, remembered.** Every hour is a colored bar you can tap. Clemson works from a 5-day forecast*, but no free tool I found shows the hour strip with a named best window.
- **An answer you can check with your own gauge:** the highest humidity that still dries at today's temperature, plus a table every 10 F.
- **Fan heat done with the math shown**, starting at 0 rather than an invented number.
- **Honest about the spread.** The page prints its corn number next to Minnesota's, Kentucky's and Arkansas's, and uses a 1-point margin because they disagree. No competitor I found shows that.
- **Cooling for storage:** cooling hours ahead against your grain temperature, and fan hours per front from your cfm per bushel, with the extension winter targets.
- **Works offline for a typed reading**, and gives a one-line answer to copy or print.

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **Soybeans and other grains** (Clemson*, the INTA/ISU app*). I left soybeans off because I could not read the D245.6 soybean constants at a source. That is the biggest gap. Fix: get the D245.6 table (an ASABE copy or the Osborn et al. 1989 paper) and check it against a published soybean table.
- **Choice of model** (Clemson*). Ours uses one equation per grain, and that is deliberate. Showing the extension-table spread covers the same honesty need with less clutter.
- **Grain temperature and moisture after aeration** (INTA/ISU app*). We count cooling hours, but we do not predict the final grain state.
- **In-bin sensing** (controllers*). Hardware reads the plenum and the grain directly. A free page cannot, but our manual box takes a plenum reading.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **Soybeans (M):** verify the D245.6 soybean constants at a primary source, then add them with the same table tests.
2. **More grains (M):** sorghum (Kentucky's table cites D245.6 Modified Chung-Pfost*), barley and oats, each only after its constants are verified.
3. **Grain moisture now (S):** an optional "my grain is at 18%" input. Air between that and the target still dries wet grain, and the page could say so.
4. **Final grain state after a cooling front (M):** only from a cited model, the way the INTA/ISU app does it*.
5. **Overnight dew-point note (S):** show the forecast dew point so a reader sees when night air will saturate.

Verdict: **yes**. This is the only free web page I found that answers "fans on or off right now" for your location on the first screen. It falls short on grain coverage (no soybeans yet), which is a gap in verified sources rather than in code.
