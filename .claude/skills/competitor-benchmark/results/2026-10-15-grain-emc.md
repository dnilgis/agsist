# Head to head: agsist.com/grain-emc (rebuilt after the audit panel)

Checked Oct 15, 2026, after the rebuild. I rendered the page at 390x844 and 1280x800, light and dark, in all three jobs (drying, cooling, holding), at 8 pm with a foggy night ahead. Open-Meteo was mocked, and the service worker was blocked. There was no sideways scroll and no page errors. Each EMC chart prints to exactly one landscape Letter page.

**What I could not see:** the sandbox blocks every competitor host. Every competitor cell below comes from search-result text and is marked *.

## Numbers checked by hand
- **Corn at 50 F and 60%:**
  - Chung-Pfost gives 15.360% dry basis, which is 13.3% wet.
  - Henderson (K 8.6541e-5) gives 16.085% dry basis, which is 13.9% wet.
  - The page shows "13.3 to 13.9%". Minnesota and Kentucky both list 13.8%*.
- **Wheat at 60 F:** 7.90, 10.97, 13.73 and 16.91% at 20, 40, 60 and 80% RH. Minnesota lists 7.9, 11.0, 13.7 and 16.9%*.
- **"Dries to target" against 19 Minnesota and Kentucky table cells:** for every target from 10 to 20%, a dry call never lands on a cell whose table value is at or over the target.
  - Arkansas runs wetter than both corn fits (15.7% at 60 F and 70%, against our 14.2 to 15.1%). The page prints that gap.
- **Corn humidity limits:**
  - Corn holds 15.5% at 65 to 71% RH at 40 F, 69 to 74% at 50 F, and 72 to 77% at 60 F, depending on the fit.
  - It dries toward 15.5% under about 64%, 68% and 71% RH at the same temperatures.
- **Fan heat:** 50 F at 80% plus 5 F gives 55 F and 66.6%.
- **Cooling front:** 15 / 0.1 cfm per bushel = 150 h, inside the 100 to 200 h range NDSU, Iowa State and Nebraska give.

## Where we win
- **The job comes first.** No competitor I found separates drying wet grain, cooling and holding dry grain. The tables, Clemson, Arkansas, OPI and the Multi-Grain calculator all compare air EMC to a single moisture.
  - Drying compares the air to the grain's own moisture and follows NDSU's run-continuously advice, with its rain, fog and snow exceptions.
  - Cooling goes by the average outside temperature against the grain temperature.
  - Holding uses the EMC-versus-target test.
- **A plain headline for tonight:** for example, "Now dries until about 10 pm. Then wets until about 9 am tomorrow." Times are in the farm's own time zone.
- **Both corn fits on every number, with the spread against Minnesota, Kentucky and Arkansas printed.** Wet calls use the wetter fit.
- **Printable corn and wheat EMC charts** on one Letter page each, in grayscale for a black-and-white printer, with a QR code back to the page. They are also crawlable HTML.
- **Fan heat** with a sourced typical range and the math shown, plus a count of how many hours 3 F of fan heat would change.

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **Soybeans and more grains** (Clemson*, Arkansas*, the INTA/ISU app*, Multi-Grain*). Soybeans stay off until their D245 constants are read at a source.
- **Final grain temperature and moisture after aeration** (INTA/ISU app*).
- **In-bin sensing** (controllers*).

## Five changes, ranked
1. **Soybeans (M):** read the D245 Modified Halsey soybean constants at a source, then test them against the Arkansas and NDSU tables, which disagree by about 0.7 point.
2. **Sorghum, barley and oats (M):** add each only once its constants are verified.
3. **Verify the corn constant sets at a primary copy (S, needs library access):** Thompson et al. 1968 or Brooker et al. 1992 for Henderson, and D245.6 for Chung-Pfost.
4. **Dew point in the drying view (S),** so a reader sees when night air will saturate.
5. **Grain state after a cooling front (M),** only from a cited model.

Verdict: **yes for corn and wheat.** It is the free web tool I found that best answers "should my fans run right now" for the job the farmer is actually doing. Clemson and the Iowa State app still cover more grains.
