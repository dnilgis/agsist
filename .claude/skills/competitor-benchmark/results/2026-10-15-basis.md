# Head to head: agsist.com/basis (plus /basis-map and /basis/<state>)

Checked Oct 15, 2026, from the read-only checkout. I rendered basis.html at 390x844 and 1280x800. No sideways scroll, no JS errors (one blocked analytics call). The page loads data/basis-states.json (network snapshot of Oct 14), data/transport/basis.json and data/transport/journey.json (USDA AgTransport, generated Oct 10).

**The other URLs:** basis-map.html and all 29 basis/<state>.html files are now noindex redirect stubs. basis-map goes to /basis#by-state, and each state stub goes to /cash-bids/<state>/#basis (canonical set, none in a sitemap). The state basis content is therefore scored in the cash-bids report, not here. One side effect: farmers who search "basis map" land on a page with no map.

**What I could not see:** I could not open any competitor host (DNS refusal from the sandbox). Competitor cells are from search-result text and are marked *.

## Where we win
- **Every state at once, free, with n.** Nobody else I found shows a middle basis for 22 states from elevator boards read directly, with the elevator count, week-over-week change and outliers named. Purdue covers the Eastern Corn Belt, IFB and CARD cover Iowa, and K-State covers Kansas.
- **Against its own normal.** USDA regions against their own same-week 5-year normal, with n years, is a better question than "is basis weak". Only Purdue does something like it (2- and 3-year averages), and only monthly, for one region.
- **It explains the why.** Freight (barge vs normal), the farm-to-Gulf spread and storage sit on the same page, with a source line naming the dataset IDs.
- **It caught a bad USDA week and said so.** I saw no competitor that flags a data break in plain words.

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **A map (IFB, K-State, Purdue, the retired CARD maps).** "Basis map" is how many farmers search, and our basis-map URL now redirects to a table.
- **Your state against your state's normal.** The headline Iowa number (-40¢, network) and Iowa's normal (USDA -31¢ for this week) sit in two different boxes. The farmer has to put them together.
- **History chart (Purdue, IFB).** Both show basis over time. We have the USDA history in basis.json (hist arrays) and network change points since June, but the page shows only bars for this week.
- **Readability.** The intro and the USDA summary are dense ("6 of 10 regions more than 10¢ under normal, 2 more than 10¢ over..."). IFB's map is read in two seconds.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **Put the state's normal right next to the state's number (S).** In "Your state today", add one line where USDA tracks the state: "USDA's Iowa normal for this week: -31¢ (5-yr). You are about 9¢ weaker than normal." Mark it as USDA's regional series, not the network. The data is already loaded on the page.
2. **Bring back a map, on demand (M).** Draw a state choropleth of the network medians from data/us-states.geo.json, built only when "Show map" is tapped, so the page stays small. Then point basis-map at it (#map) instead of the table.
3. **A season line per state (M).** Behind a "Show since June" button, draw the USDA region's weekly basis (hist) with its 5-year band. For network states, also draw the median since June from data/basis-sparklines. Nothing bulky goes in the HTML.
4. **Make "normal" robust and say so (S).** Show the 5-year median, or the min-max band, beside the mean so one drought fall cannot set the yardstick. Kansas corn (+7¢ "normal") is the live example. Also date the Gulf box ("bids of Oct 2; Illinois passed the jump check").
5. **One plain sentence on top (S).** Replace the two-line intro with something like: "Iowa corn basis today is about -40¢ under Dec, 9¢ weaker than normal for mid-October, and 6 of 10 USDA regions are weaker than normal." Keep the rest below.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Yes, for a free national view.** No free page I found covers this many states from elevator boards, with counts, against a dated USDA normal and the freight behind it. It loses to Iowa Farm Bureau and Purdue on maps and history inside their own states. Changes 1 to 3 close that gap.
