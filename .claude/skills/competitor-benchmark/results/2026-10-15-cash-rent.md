## Where we win
- **All 47 states, every county, from 2008 to 2026, in one sortable table per state**, published about six weeks after NASS released the data. The table has irrigated and pasture columns, YoY on matched counties, and an honest "a county dropping out can't fake a trend" note. University surveys cover one state each. NASS Quick Stats makes you build the query yourself.
- **It links to the land record.** Each county opens its Atlas page, with rent as a share of gross, the rent share calculator, yield trend and insurance record. It also links the state's lease-termination law (Iowa 562.6, Nebraska case law, SDCL 43-32-22.1) and a printable lease. No competitor ties those together.
- **The numbers are clean.** Every figure I checked matches the source file and outside reporting.

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **Rent by land quality (ISU, ISPFMRA, Purdue).** This is the question a landlord and tenant actually argue about. ISU gives each county's rent for high, medium and low quality thirds and rent per CSR2 point. ISPFMRA gives $227 (fair) to $384 (excellent). A county mean of $266 does not tell a tenant on 90 CSR2 ground what to pay.
- **Next year (farmdoc).** farmdoc reads the 2026 numbers and says what 2027 rents are likely to do (the ISPFMRA midyear survey points to -$3 to -$5). Rent talks in Sept to Nov are about 2027. Our page has no outlook line.
- **A fair-rent method (ISU AgDM "Computing a cropland cash rental rate", UMN).** They show the methods: share of gross, return on land value, cost of production. We have share of gross on the Atlas but not on the state page, where people land.
- **Second survey for the same state (ISU, UNL, UMN).** Iowa readers see ISU's $270 everywhere. Our page never mentions it or explains why it differs from NASS.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **Add "rent for your ground, not the county average" (M).** The skill's rule is not to copy a competitor's data. So build our own number instead of reprinting ISU's or ISPFMRA's tables. Take the county rent per bushel of county trend yield, which the Atlas already computes, and scale it by the reader's own yield (APH or a typical yield). The line would read: "At 220 bu ground in Story County, the county's rent per bushel says about $X." Label it derived and give its date. Link ISU, ISPFMRA or UNL as the quality-class surveys for that state. This runs client-side from the state JSON already on the Atlas, so there is no page-size cost.
2. **"What 2027 rent may do" line on each state page (S).** Cite farmdoc / ISPFMRA, ISU or UNL where they publish an outlook. Elsewhere, compute rent as a share of the 2027 county trend yield at Dec '27 futures. Label it derived and give the date.
3. **Answer the "average" FAQ with the state average first (S).** Put NASS's state figure first, then the median county, and say why they differ. On Iowa, also name ISU's $270 and why it differs (different survey, different respondents).
4. **Put the three fair-rent methods on the state page (S).** Show share of gross, return on land value and cost-based for the median county, each one line with its arithmetic. Link to the Atlas calculator for your own county.
5. **Show the release date (S).** Add "NASS released these on <date>; next release late August 2027" to the hero. Readers will then trust that $266 is the newest number and know when to come back.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Yes for "cash rent in <county>, any state".** No one else gives every county in 47 states, current, with history and lease law. But for Iowa and Illinois, ISU and ISPFMRA answer the harder question better: what *this quality* of ground rents for, and where 2027 is heading.
