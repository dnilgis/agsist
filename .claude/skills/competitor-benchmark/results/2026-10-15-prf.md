## Where we win
- **It does what RMA's tool does, then explains it.** It prices every 2027 grid, interval and coverage level, and the page says it matched RMA "14 of 14 policies to the dollar" (Oct 14). It adds 75 years of payouts on the new NCEI record, the middle year, the longest dry run, a CSV, and the 8 neighbor grids. RMA's DST makes you find the grid in one tab, read indexes in another and price in a third. ProAg puts its quoting behind the myProAg login. FMH has no calculator.
- **It is current on 2027 when almost nobody else is.** It covers the NCEI switch (record now starts 1951), the Jan-Feb 25% cap, and the 25% stocking-rate rule with RMA's "full premium is owed" quote, all with dates. Every extension piece I found was from the 2024 or 2026 crop year or older. OSU still says 1948 and a 60% interval cap. Texas Farm Bureau and AgriLife posts are about the 2026 Dec 1 deadline.
- **County and state pages that nobody else has.** /prf/texas/anderson-county answers "PRF in Anderson County" on the first screen with:
  - all 11 grid IDs
  - the county base values ($34.60 grazing, $224 hay)
  - the rate per $100 for each pair of months, with the range across grids
  - 2021 to 2026 index values
  - a driest-year table for each grid

  Search results only turned up 2013 RMA slide decks with county numbers.
- **It covers what the policy does not do.** It quotes Basic Provisions section 13, cites basis-risk research (Yu 2019, Keller and Saitone 2022), notes that the native sod rule is not modeled, and leaves the admin fee out on purpose. No insurer page says this.
- **Trust.** It has a byline, discloses that the author owns an agency, sells nothing, captures no leads and routes no one to an agent. It also states the national payback ($0.87 per $1 of full premium, 9 in 10 between $0.78 and $0.89), the opposite of a vendor's "paid 17 of 20 years" pitch.

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **Being found (RMA, insurers, extension).** None of my searches surfaced agsist ("PRF insurance calculator", "pasture rangeland forage insurance Texas 2027", "...Oklahoma rates county"). Searching "agsist.com PRF" returned platelet-rich fibrin pages. The first result for "PRF insurance calculator" is an everycalculators.com form where you type in the rate yourself. Our content wins, but the ranking pages win the reader.
- **Official standing (RMA DST).** RMA's grid locator is the one producers must use to sign up. Our page links it, but RMA's tool is the record an agent will quote from.
- **One clear point on month choice (farmdoc daily, AgriLife, K-State).** They say one thing: insure the months your forage needs rain. Our page says that too, and also quotes RMA's handbook that winter months are "not an effective use." But the "What the numbers would pick" card for the Barron County example says Jan-Feb 20%, Sep-Oct 10%, Nov-Dec 70%, with a "Use this setup" button. Three lines later it says "Picking months this way would not have beaten an even spread here." A careful reader sees the page arguing with itself. farmdoc never does.
- **Short (fact sheets, ProAg).** The calculator page is about 18,000 px tall on a phone with the example open. Extension sheets get a beginner to "I get it" in one screen of reading.
- **A dollar figure per acre in the county (Redd Summit, as search describes it).** It offers "instant quotes by county." Our county pages give the rate per $100 and the protection per acre, but never multiply them into what an acre costs.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **Put a dollar answer on every county page (M).** Add one labeled example line under the stat tiles, for 100 acres of grazing at 90% coverage, productivity factor 100. Use the same months as the main page's example (Apr-May and Jun-Jul, half each) and say it is not a recommendation. Show: "you pay $X ($Y an acre), USDA pays $Z, would have paid in N of 75 years, $A in an average year" on the middle grid. The baked text is about 0.4 KB per page, so about 1.3 MB across 3,070 pages, well inside the 150 KB cap (the largest county page is about 22 KB). This answers "how much does PRF cost per acre in <county>", the query the county pages should rank for.
2. **Make the pick card agree with the page (S).**
   - When the local backtest says the pick would not have beaten an even spread here, lead with that line and drop the "Use this setup" button.
   - When most of the pick sits in the grid's two driest intervals or Nov to Feb, add the page's own sentence: "That is why winter and fall months look good on a payback table. It is not a sign they cover the grass you grow."
   - Nothing new is claimed. This removes the one place a skeptic can say the page contradicts itself, and it keeps the "no best months" rule.
3. **Get found for 2027 searches (S-M).** The state and county titles say "2027 Grids and Rates", which is good. Add an H2 and FAQ entry, "What changed in PRF for 2027", to /prf/how-prf-works and every state page (3 bullets: NCEI, Jan-Feb 25% cap, stocking rule), with dateModified bumped. Give prf.html a dateModified, since it has none in its JSON-LD; the county pages have one. Check Search Console for /prf impressions. Right now the people asking "PRF changes 2027" get ProAg's and RMA's bulletins, and we have the clearer answer.
4. **Shorten the calculator page on phones (S).** Fold "How payouts compare with the premium" (the 11-row national table) and "New for 2027" into closed `<details>`, or move them to /prf/how-prf-works, leaving one sentence and a link each. Consider moving the "For businesses" box below step 1, so the search box sits fully in the first phone screen. Today the box takes about 60 px above the tool. Placement is Sig's call; the slot itself stays.
5. **Fix two dead ends (S).**
   - A shared link `?g=30454` with no county opens on "pick the county" with no numbers at all. Show each county's figures side by side, or open on the county holding most of the grid and say so.
   - When the place lookup fails, the sticky bar still shows the Wisconsin example ("100 ac · you pay $150...") to someone who typed a Texas town. Hide it, or label it "Example, Barron County WI".
   - Smaller: add a one-line note where irrigated hay is valued below non-irrigated (Anderson County TX, $168 vs $224) saying it is USDA's own figure. Otherwise a reader will assume we made a mistake.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Yes on substance.** No free page, RMA's included, puts 2027 prices, 75 years of payouts, county values and the policy's limits for every grid in one readable place. It is not yet the page farmers land on, and the pick card's mixed message on winter months is the one weak spot an expert would catch.
