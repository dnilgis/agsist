## Where we win
- **It is still the only saved competitor that scores your forecast.** Every saved copy is a static article or a PDF. N-LAT says outright that it is not for in-season decisions.
- **Price math tied to a dated local price.** Example: "$623/ton, Kansas co-ops, middle of 4, posted Oct 6, 2026 ... about 12 to 18 lb of N". No saved competitor puts a dollar figure on NBPT.
- **Honest about cold ground.** The page caps the score but says it is "a design choice, not zero loss". Montana's Soil Scoop also lists frozen soil as high risk.

## Where they beat us, and how
- **Surface moisture, the factor three saved sources call the biggest.**
  - Montana Soil Scoop lists high-risk conditions and says risk rises with their number, "with soil moisture likely being the most important".
  - EB0209 Table 2 puts "Dry soil" in the lower-risk column.
  - FS Cooperatives leads with "If it stays dry for weeks: Urea is fine sitting there. It won't volatilize without moisture."
  - Our score uses air humidity (25%) and treats a dry forecast as a rain term of 70, "extended exposure". On a dry surface our page says the opposite of all three sources.
- **The first-screen answer (FS Cooperatives).** Three bullets answer "dry soil", "light shower (< 0.25")" and "≥ 0.5" rain" in one glance. Ours needs a ZIP first, then a gauge.
- **How long loss lasts (Montana EB0209).** The saved PDF says "Most volatilization from urea typically occurs during a two- to three-week period after application" and NBPT "can inhibit urea volatilization for two to 10 weeks". Our hero says "10–30% of its nitrogen within about 72 hours", and a card says "Most loss in the first few days". Manitoba's copy cites losses measured over 5 and 7 days, not 72 hours.
- **Soil texture and buffering (Montana, Manitoba).** Both list sandy, low-CEC, low organic matter and high-lime soils as higher risk. We ask only pH and tillage, though AGSIST's Field Scout already reads SSURGO.
- **Checking loss after the fact (Manitoba).** It explains Nitrogen Rich Strips: "an applicator width of 150 percent the recommended N rate ... compare crop growth". We say nothing about what to do after a bad window.

## Bugs found
- **Medium, advice contradicts its own rain line.**
  - Case: 0.60 in forecast tomorrow.
  - The verdict reads "High N-loss risk. Do not apply without NBPT stabilizer. Look for a cooler or wetter window", followed by "The forecast has 0.60 in tomorrow; if it comes, that is enough to move urea into the soil."
  - Montana's saved copy treats ">½-inch in single event" within 1 to 2 days as incorporation.
  - The cause: an incorporating rain moves only the 20% rain term. Spreading the day before half an inch is the textbook window, and the page tells the farmer to wait.
- **Medium, unsourced timing claim.**
  - The hero says "within about 72 hours", and the science card says "Most loss in the first few days".
  - Montana EB0209 (saved) says two to three weeks.
  - A farmer could read three dry days as "safe".
- **Low to medium, the headline and the strip disagree on a light shower.**
  - Case: under 1/4 inch at a 70% chance.
  - The headline treats it as lower risk (rain term 45, down from 70 when dry).
  - The strip and the page text call it "the worst case" (88).
- **Low, the residue card is static.** It shows "HIGH (no-till)" even when the picker says "Conventional (bare, tilled)".
- **Low, internal inconsistencies:**
  - The text says no-till loses "two to three times more N", but the multiplier is 1.5x.
  - The humidity factor shows "EXTREME" next to the text "Moderate humidity".
  - Still open from Oct 15: at 40-49°F the gauge says Moderate, while the box says "Cool: Manageable Risk" in the low style.
- **Check item, citation.** The page cites "Montana State field trials (23 trials, fall to spring) ... average 16% and up to 44%". The saved Montana Soil Scoop says "up to 44% ... with an average loss of 18%". The page should name which Montana publication gives 16%.

## Five changes, ranked
1. **Put surface moisture in the score (S to M).**
   - Use Open-Meteo `soil_moisture_0_to_1cm` (or the hourly dew point against temperature for dew), plus a "surface dry / damp / wet" picker that overrides it.
   - A dry surface with no rain forecast should read lower, not higher.
   - Weight moisture as the primary wetness term, and drop air RH to a minor one.
   - Why: three saved sources, two of them university, say moisture is the biggest factor, and our dry-forecast logic points the wrong way.
   - Honesty risk: medium. It is still a rule of thumb, so keep the "not a validated model" label and name the soil-moisture source.
2. **Make an incorporating rain change the advice (S).**
   - When 0.5 in or more is forecast within 48 hours, lead with "Spread before the rain: 0.60 in forecast tomorrow (chance X%). If it misses, you are exposed; NBPT covers that."
   - Do not show "look for a wetter window".
   - Why: today the page talks a farmer out of the best window.
   - Honesty risk: low, if the chance is shown with the amount.
3. **Fix the timing claims (S).**
   - Replace "within about 72 hours" and "Most loss in the first few days" with what the sources say: loss runs over about two to three weeks, faster when warm.
   - Cite EB0209 and Manitoba's 5-day and 7-day figures.
   - Name the publication behind 16%, or use the Soil Scoop's 18%.
   - Why: accuracy beats every score.
   - Honesty risk: none.
4. **Answer first, without a ZIP (S).**
   - Above the gauge, add three plain lines from the page's own rules: dry surface and no rain; a light shower under 1/4 in; 1/2 in or more within two days.
   - Keep the live score below for the farmer's own forecast.
   - Why: FS Cooperatives answers the question in one screen. We answer only after a ZIP.
   - Honesty risk: low.
5. **Make the cards follow the inputs, and add soil texture (S).**
   - Drive the residue and pH cards from the pickers. Add a "sandy / loam / clay" picker labeled with Montana's CEC reasoning.
   - Line the no-till text up with the multiplier, or say why they differ.
   - Show the applied multipliers under the score.
   - Why: the internal contradictions cost trust.
   - Honesty risk: low.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Close.** It is still the only free tool in the saved set that scores your forecast, and its arithmetic matched in every case I ran. But on a dry surface its logic points the opposite way from Montana State and the co-op article. It also tells a farmer to wait when half an inch of rain is coming tomorrow. Changes 1 and 2 would make it a yes.

**Change since Oct 15:** 40 to **40** (no change). The state-label and rain-amount fixes are real, but they are offset by what the saved sources exposed: the dry-surface logic, the 72-hour claim and the rain-tomorrow verdict. The Oct 15 verdict was "yes", built on competitor snippets. Reading the saved Montana and FS copies moves it to "close".
