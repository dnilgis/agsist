Notes on the copies:
- **Tradingster:** one table per contract with long, short and spread for all five categories, the week change, % of open interest and the trader count. It covers every CFTC market, and a "COT Signals and COT Analysis ... free preview" is new.
- **CFTC:**
  - The source of record. It has Disaggregated, Legacy, Supplemental and TFF reports, futures-only and futures-and-options combined, the long format with top-4 and top-8 trader concentration, and an API back to 2006.
  - New this week: "4603 OATS - CHICAGO BOARD OF TRADE".
- **DTN:**
  - Puts the position against its record in contracts ("record net long of 429,189 contracts set in October of 2010") and against a 2020 analog.
  - Adds Commodity Index Traders from the Supplemental report ("456,201 contracts net long as of Jan. 21").
  - Says futures and options combined is "the more commonly followed".

## Where we win
- **First screen for a grain farmer.** "The Read" now leads with corn and soybeans every week, with net, week change and percentile since 2010. The biggest mover (lean hogs) comes second.
- **Crowding against each market's own record as a share of open interest,** so 2010 and 2026 compare. DTN compares raw contracts, and its "record net long of 429,189" is not the record share.
- **It tests itself.** The coin-flip result is counted in spells, not weeks. No copy publishes its own null result.
- **Who took the other side,** average fund size, and same week of the year on one card. Tradingster has the raw categories but no reading of them.
- **Weight.** The analog depth now loads on demand, and the HTML is 67 KB.

## Where they beat us, and how
- **Futures and options combined (CFTC, DTN).** CFTC publishes both, and DTN calls the combined report the one most followed. We show futures only, so a farmer comparing with a DTN column will see a different number.
- **Index traders (DTN, CFTC Supplemental).** DTN reads Commodity Index Trader positions for corn and the ag complex. We have no index-trader line. Our "swap dealers" row is the nearest, and the page says index money sits there.
- **Every market (Tradingster, CFTC).** Both cover every reported contract, including oats, added this week. We cover 11.

## Bugs found
- **No math errors.**
- **Low (wording):** the corn detail opens "Funds hold an unusually large net long in Corn". The same card and The Read call the 81st percentile "high side but not extreme" and "not crowded". Use one phrase.
- **Open from Oct 15:**
  - Lean hogs' price is still withheld (prices.json stale_keys: hogs), and Minneapolis wheat has no price feed.
  - Both are still left off the scatter and the "what followed" tests. The page says so.

## Five changes, ranked
1. **Futures and options combined view.**
   - What: a toggle on the board and cards for the CFTC disaggregated futures-and-options combined report, with the view named on every number.
   - Why: it is what DTN and most columns quote, and a farmer seeing two numbers for "funds in corn" should see why.
   - Effort: M. Honesty risk: low. Run the percentiles and tests separately on each series, and never mix them.
2. **Fix the hogs and Minneapolis wheat price series.**
   - What: a verified roll rule for lean hogs and a price feed for Minneapolis wheat, so all 11 markets get the scatter and the "what followed" tests.
   - Why: hogs are this week's biggest story on the page, and they are the one market without a price test.
   - Effort: M. Honesty risk: medium. Withhold again if the roll check fails.
3. **Index trader line for the grains.**
   - What: from the CFTC Supplemental report, add Commodity Index Trader net and its percentile for the ag markets it covers, on demand under each card.
   - Why: index money is a large, slow long in corn, wheat and cattle, and DTN treats it as a separate story.
   - Effort: M. Honesty risk: low. It covers combined futures and options only, so label it that way.
4. **Full-record chart on demand.**
   - What: a "since 2010" button on each card that draws the net as a share of open interest from data in cot-deep.json. Split that file per market, since it is 1.3 MB.
   - Why: we chart 52 weeks, while the percentile uses 875. The reader should see the record the ranking uses.
   - Effort: S. Honesty risk: none.
5. **One crowding phrase everywhere.**
   - What: drive "unusually large", "high side", "crowded" and "not crowded" from one function of the percentile. Reuse it on /cattle-futures-prices, which now gives a different read of the same cattle position.
   - Why: the same number gets three descriptions across two pages.
   - Effort: S. Honesty risk: none.

## Verdict
**Yes.** It is the best free COT page I could read. It leads with corn and soybeans, ranks each position against its own record by share of open interest, tests itself and says "coin flip" when that is the answer, and every number I checked matches the CFTC fields Tradingster shows. The gaps are the combined report, index traders, and the two missing price series.
