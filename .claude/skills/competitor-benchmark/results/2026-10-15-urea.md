# Head to head: agsist.com/urea

Checked Oct 15, 2026, from the read-only checkout. I rendered urea.html at 390x844 and 1280x800. No sideways scroll, no JS errors, about 470 KB loaded, and the page is 128 KB. I mocked the Open-Meteo forecast and geocoder, because the sandbox cannot reach them, and checked the score by hand.

**What I could not see:** I could not open any competitor host. Competitor cells are from search-result text and are marked *.

## Numbers checked by hand
- **Current score, 72°F, 60% RH, 8 mph, 20% rain chance over 48 h:**
  - Factor scores: temp 72, humidity 75, wind 60, rain 70.
  - Weighted: 0.35 x 72 + 0.25 x 75 + 0.20 x 60 + 0.20 x 70 = 69.95, so **70**, "High Risk". Matches.
- **Same weather, no-till and pH over 7.5:**
  - 70 x 1.55 x 1.5 = 163, capped at **100**, "Extreme". Matches.
  - The two multipliers compound to 2.3x, so almost any warm day reads 100. That is a design call, but worth knowing.
- **45°F, same humidity, wind and rain:**
  - Raw score: 5.25 + 18.75 + 12 + 14 = 50.
  - Capped at 40 below 50°F, so **40**, "Moderate". Matches.
  - The recommendation box beside it says "Cool: Manageable Risk" in the low style, so the gauge and the box disagree in color and label (low).
- **5-day strip:**
  - 75/55°F, no rain: 50 x 0.55 + 70 x 0.45 = 59, High. Matches.
  - Same day with 0.10 in of rain: 50 x 0.55 + 88 x 0.45 = 67, High. Matches.
  - A day with 0.60 in: 8, Low. Matches.
  - 40/30°F: 34, capped at 22, Low. Matches.
- **Price line:**
  - $590/ton urea / 920 lb N = $0.641/lb N. Matches "about $0.64".
  - NBPT pays at $8-12/ac / $0.55-0.70 per lb N = 11.4 to 21.8 lb. Matches "11 to 22".
- **BUG (low, trust): state abbreviations are wrong.**
  - With a ZIP, the label is built as `admin1.substring(0,2).toUpperCase()`, so a ZIP in Ames shows "Ames, IO".
  - Minnesota, Missouri and Mississippi all show "MI"; Montana shows "MO"; Kansas shows "KA"; Texas and Tennessee show "TE".
  - Seen in the render.
- **Model honesty:** The page labels the score "an AGSIST rule of thumb with our own bands, not a validated model", which is right. Air RH stands in for surface moisture. Montana State* says surface soil moisture is the biggest single factor: dry soil kept loss under 20%, moist soil reached 44%.

## Where we win
- **The only free tool I found that turns your forecast into a urea loss score and a 5-day strip,** with the half-inch incorporation rule built in.
- **Honest about the cold.** Frozen ground is capped but not called safe, citing Montana's 16% average and 44% maximum.
- **No product push.** Koch and Corteva sell the stabilizer. This page names NBPT and does the break-even math with a price-per-lb-N range.

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **Soil moisture (Montana State).** Their trials say a dry or moist surface matters more than air humidity*. We do not ask, and do not use a modeled surface soil moisture, which Open-Meteo has.
- **Field-level models (FieldView/WinField).** Paid tools use field soil data and records*. Our pH and tillage are typed by hand.
- **Rain amount in the current score.** The score uses only the chance of rain, even when the strip shows 0.6 in tomorrow.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **Add modeled surface soil moisture (S).** Use Open-Meteo `soil_moisture_0_to_1cm`, plus a "surface dry / damp / wet" picker that overrides it. Weight it as the primary moisture term and replace RH. Label the source.
2. **Use forecast rain amounts in the headline (S).** When the next 48 hours forecast at least 0.5 in in one day, say "incorporating rain forecast for Sat (0.6 in), if it comes", rather than a probability-only rain term.
3. **Fix the state label (S).** Map admin1 to USPS codes, or read the state code from the geocoder.
4. **Make the gauge and box agree (S).** Below 50°F, tie the recommendation box's style to the capped level.
5. **Show the multipliers (S).** Under the score, print "x1.5 no-till, x1.55 pH>7.5" so a 100 is explained, not a surprise.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Yes, for a free public tool.** I found no free competitor that scores urea loss from your forecast, and the arithmetic is right in every case I ran. It is honest that the score is a rule of thumb. Soil moisture is the gap that would most improve it.
