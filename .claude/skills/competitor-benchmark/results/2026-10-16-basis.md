## Where we win
- **Every state at once, from elevator boards, with n.**
  - 22 states with a middle basis, the week's change and named outliers, plus a map, a 10-region USDA table against each region's own same-week 5-year normal, barge freight and the farm-to-Gulf spread on one page.
  - Purdue covers 5 Eastern Corn Belt states by crop reporting district, monthly. K-State maps the Plains weekly. CARD has been retired since 2011.
- **Your state's number beside its normal.** It is now one line, which none of the read competitors does for a farmer's own state on demand.
- **Data breaks are caught and named.** basis.json keeps a "held" list of USDA jumps it rejected, and the page says so in words when it holds a week.

## Where they beat us, and how
- **A deviation map.** K-State: "The basis deviation maps show the difference between the current basis and the three-year historical average basis for the same week and location." Its maps cover soybeans, corn, wheat and sorghum, from 500-1,000 locations. Our map colors absolute basis only, so a state that is always wide looks the same as one that is unusually wide this week.
- **History and seasonal outlook.**
  - Purdue's update charts each district's basis over time.
  - It reads the seasonal pattern: "Historical trends for both corn and soybeans indicate a weekly decline in basis across the region over the next month."
  - It names the plants: "Iowa is also the only state where the average spot price at ethanol plants is below the September futures price."
  - We have the 2026 USDA weekly history in basis.json and draw none of it.
- **Sorghum.** K-State maps grain sorghum basis. We have none.
- **Readability.** Purdue's update says, in three sentences at the top, where basis is strongest, weakest and heading. Our USDA summary paragraph is dense, and today it is stale (bug B1).
