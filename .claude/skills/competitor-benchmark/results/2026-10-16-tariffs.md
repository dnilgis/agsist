Competitors are scored only from the copies GitHub Actions saved on the references branch (fetched Oct 16). All six copies were readable. ## Numbers checked by hand
- **Stacked rates:** soybeans 3% + 10% = 13%, corn 1% + 10% = 11%, wheat 1% + 10% = 11%, pork 12% + 10% = 22%. All right. The saved S&P copy (Sep 24, 2026) gives the same 13% for soybeans, "including an additional 10% levy on top of the 3% most-favored-nation tariff".
- **Truce date:** Jan 10, 2027 on our page; the S&P copy quotes Bessent saying the Busan truce "scheduled to end Nov. 10" is "extended until Jan. 10". Matches.
- **Oct 15 loose ends:** fixed. The Jul 6 entry now says the Oct 1 deal had not come off by Sep 24 and Sep 28, and explains the fentanyl tariff problem. The Oct 30, 2025 entry now says the truce was set to run to Nov 10, 2026 and was later extended to Jan 10, 2027.
- **China purchase figure (bug, medium):** the Oct 14 entry says "China had bought about 13.7 MMT toward its 25 MMT commitment". It does not say whether that is calendar 2026 or the 2026/27 marketing year, or sales or shipments. The page's own source for the soybean row (S&P, Sep 24) says "So far in marketing year 2026-27 (September-August), China has committed to purchase 9.969 million mt", per USDA. Both can be true on different bases, but a reader sees 13.7 on one line and a cited source with 9.97, and the page does not say which counts toward the pledge. The saved RFD-TV copy makes the same point: the pledge could be measured "by sales, shipments, arrivals, calendar year, or marketing year".
- **Slightly behind (low):** our latest line is 8 to 10 cargoes (Reuters, Oct 14). The saved Farm Policy News copy (Oct 16) says state firms "have booked at least 20 soybean shipments, equivalent to around 1.3 million tons". Not wrong, two days old.

## Where we win
- **One dated table of today's rate, its parts and its source** for the crops a Midwest farmer sells. Every competitor is an article, a news list or a monthly PDF.
- **The timeline now closes its own loose ends.** Each 2026 event says whether it came true.
- **Byline added since Oct 15** ("By Sigurd Lindquist, Certified Crop Adviser (CCA), each rate links its source").
- **It ties the rate to the farmer's own basis** with a link on every row.

## Where they beat us, and how
- **NDSU: a dollar figure and a state figure.** The saved PDF estimates China's retaliation cut US ag exports to China by "$14.9 billion on an annualized basis" (soybeans about $6.8B), and names state exposure: "Iowa, California, and Illinois each show the largest absolute exposure at approximately $1.2 billion". We give rates, no dollars, no state.
- **S&P: the purchase number with its basis.** "So far in marketing year 2026-27 (September-August), China has committed to purchase 9.969 million mt", sourced to USDA. Ours gives 13.7 MMT with no basis.
- **RFD-TV: explains how the pledge is counted** (sales vs shipments vs arrivals; calendar vs marketing year) and that shipments lag sales by about six weeks. We do not.
- **Agri-Pulse: method note for a tracker** (weekly export sales plus daily flash sales, unknown destinations shown separately). We have no tracker.
- **Farm Policy News: two days fresher** on the China cargo count.

## Bugs
1. **Medium:** 13.7 MMT China purchase figure has no stated basis and sits beside a cited source giving 9.97 MMT for the marketing year (see above).
2. **Low:** cargo count is two days behind the newest public report.

## Five changes, ranked
1. **China purchase gauge from USDA export sales, basis stated (M).** "X of 25 MMT, USDA export sales to China, 2026/27 marketing year, through <date>; outstanding sales to unknown destinations shown apart." data/export-sales.json has no destination split today, so the fetch needs the by-country ESR call. Then rewrite the Aug 10, Sep 17 and Oct 14 lines against that one figure. Honesty risk: medium if the basis is not printed next to the number; low if it is.
2. **Fix the 13.7 MMT line now (S).** Say what it counts (Reuters' basis) and that USDA's marketing-year commitments were 9.97 MMT as of Sep 24. Honesty risk: low.
3. **Trade-aid row (S).** Farmer Bridge Assistance per-acre rates, cap, timing and any 2026 round, from USDA FSA's own page, dated. The rates came from search snippets on Oct 15 and are not in any saved copy, so verify at FSA first. Honesty risk: medium until verified.
4. **Shorten the banner (S).** One sentence and the date. On a phone it is about 11 lines before the table. Move Canada and Section 301 detail into row notes.
5. **"Last checked" next to "last changed" (S).** A daily check that writes the check date, so a quiet day reads as checked, not stale; update the cargo line within a day of a wire report. Honesty risk: low.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Close.** It is the highest-scoring page in this set and the only one-table, dated, sourced ag tariff tracker. But the one number farmers watch most, China's soybean purchases, is printed without a basis and conflicts on its face with the page's own cited source.

**Change since Oct 15:** 36 to 37 (+1). Byline and timeline fixes raised trust and accuracy; current dropped a point (Farm Policy News is two days fresher). Oct 15 competitor scores were from snippets; these are from saved copies, so competitor totals are not directly comparable.
