# Head to head: hail-map.html (agsist.com/hail-map), 2026-10-15

I rendered it locally at 390 x 844 and 1280 x 800 (shots in scratchpad/h2h/b4/). There was no sideways scroll and no JS errors.
- **Address lookup:** I tested it with a mocked geocoder for Des Moines (nominatim is blocked here, and the site service worker had to be blocked so the mock could answer). It works: 78 reports within 10 mi over 5 years, 25 hail days, largest 3.00" (Aug 19, 2022), and the NCEI 1955-2020 record (485 events), plus a year bar chart and a dated table.
- **What I could not see:** Live NWS warnings and the IEM 24h layer are blocked here. Every competitor site was blocked to WebFetch, so competitor facts come from search-result text and are marked that way.

Farmer and homeowner queries: "did it hail at my address", "hail map", "hail last night near me", "hail history by address", "crop hail map". Competitors:
- NOAA SPC daily storm reports
- NOAA NCEI Storm Events Database
- Iowa Environmental Mesonet LSR app
- Interactive Hail Maps / Hail Recon (paid)
- HailTrace (paid)
- GAF Interactive Hail Map (paid, $349-$649/yr per its snippet)
- HailFACTS by AnythingWeather ($40 PDF report, history from 2009)
- New since last check: StormAuditor (freemium address history, launched on Product Hunt) and Hail Yeah (iOS app, free tier with limited daily lookups). Both are unverified beyond listings.
- DTN Hail Swath (commercial radar polygons, ag use listed)

## Where we win
- **Free and complete for one address.** We give the address answer, the hail-day count, the largest stone and its distance, a dated table, CSV, print, the 1955-2020 NCEI record and radar swaths in one free page with no login. The paid tools charge for this and the free government tools make you assemble it.
- **Free hail alerts.** You set a pin and a radius and get one email the morning after radar-estimated hail. Only paid tools and NDAWN-style apps alert, and none of them are free for hail.
- **The most honest labeling we found.** "Color is report density, not hail risk" (9 of 10 densest cells are metros). MESH is labeled an estimate that runs large (NSSL). "2021 is in neither record." "Not a zero" markers. The radar check says plainly when a date is past its record.
- **Ag context the others lack.** It covers crop stage and hail loss (growing point below ground until about V6) and has state pages, county rankings and a storm log per day.

## Where competitors beat us, and how
- **Radar archive depth.** Interactive Hail Maps keeps MESH swaths for every storm date for "over four years" (Hail Recon listing), and the paid services generally go back to about 2011. HailFACTS goes back to 2009. Our swath archive covers 29 storm days back to Sep 15, 2026. For a claim from June, our radar check can't answer, and the page says so.
- **Recency of the ground record in the lookup.** The 30-day layer has reports through Oct 13/14, but the address lookup only counts reports through Oct 1 ("the 14 days since are not in these numbers yet"). IEM and SPC show yesterday's reports for any point.
- **National view.** At national zoom the 5-year heatmap is solid red from the Plains to the East Coast (1280 shot). The "hail alley" the text describes does not show. SPC's annual maps and NCEI's per-county plots read better at a glance.

## The 5 changes, ranked
1. **Deeper radar archive by point (L, but on budget).** Backfill MESH for April-September of 2022-2026 as one small per-state "storm days touching this cell" index (dates + max MESH band only, not polygons), loaded on demand. Then "Did it hail here on June 14, 2025?" gets an answer. That is the main thing farmers and adjusters pay for. It must fit the hail folder budget (data/hail is 24 MB now), so check scripts/check_site_budget.py first.
2. **Lookup includes the last 14 days (S).** Merge recent.json reports into runLookup with a "preliminary, not yet in the archive" tag, so the lookup is current to yesterday like IEM.
3. **Fix H1, H2, H3 (S).** Use one severe definition, filter non-states, and correct and date the paid-price range.
4. **Make the national map say what the text says (M).** Default the national view to hail days per county (or per 25 km cell) instead of raw report density, with report density as a toggle. Hail alley then shows, and the metro glow stops misleading.
5. **Crop-season view (M).** Add a "growing season only (May-Sep)" filter and, in the address result, "hail days during the corn/soy season, by year". That answers the farmer's real question (how often does my ground get hit while the crop is up?), which no free competitor does.

**Best on the internet today? Yes, for free use; close against paid tools.** No free page combines an address lookup, alerts, 70 years of record and honest labels like this one. The paid services still win on radar archive depth, and the lookup trails the page's own data by 14 days.
