## Where we win
- **Crowding against each market's own record, as a share of open interest, since 2010,** with n weeks and a clear crowded rule. Raw-contract charts (Barchart, CFTC) cannot compare 2010 with 2026.
- **It tests itself.** "Does this predict prices?" runs the record and says "coin flip" with the evidence counted in spells, not weeks. I found no free competitor that publishes its own null result.
- **Who took the other side**, plus a plain sentence a grain farmer can use: "a reason to look this week at unpriced bushels... not a reason to predict a move".

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **Charting any contract and any range (Barchart).** We show 52 weeks of nets, and the full record only through the percentile.
- **Futures and options combined (CFTC).** We use futures-only. Some analysts quote the combined report, and a farmer comparing with a DTN column may see a different number.
- **Weight and length.** 181 KB of HTML and a 13,000 px phone page. The first screen is good, but the analog tables are heavy to carry in every load.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **Lead with the farmer's crop (S).** "The Read" today leads with lean hogs, because the lead is picked by the biggest move. For the AGSIST audience, lead with corn and soybeans every week (one plain line each) and put the biggest mover second.
2. **Move the analog and "what followed" tables to data on demand (S).** Load them from cot-analysis.json when a market is opened. That roughly halves the HTML, per the CLAUDE.md rule on bulky depth.
3. **Fix the two missing price series (M).** Lean hogs are withheld for an unverified roll rule, and Minneapolis wheat has no price feed. Both are left off the scatter, as the page says.
4. **Futures+options toggle (M).** CFTC publishes the combined disaggregated report. Add it as a second view and label which one is shown.
5. **Longer chart on demand (S).** A "since 2010" button on each card that draws net as a share of open interest from cot-history.json.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Yes.** No free COT page I found ranks positioning against the full record by share of open interest, tests whether that predicts anything and says plainly when it does not, and puts the other side of the trade on the same card. Every number I checked matches the CFTC fields. The gaps are weight, the hogs and Minneapolis wheat price series, and leading with hogs for a grain audience.
