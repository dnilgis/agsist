I rendered it locally at 390 x 844 and 1280 x 800 (shots in scratchpad/h2h/b4/). There was no sideways scroll and no JS errors. The page is 219 KB and 12,800 px tall on a phone.
**What I could not see:** Open-Meteo is blocked here, so the live advisory never loaded. I fed the page a synthetic Open-Meteo forecast with geolocation set to Chetek, to see the live state and test the rating logic (spray-live-*.png). Every competitor site was blocked to WebFetch (DNS failure), so competitor facts come from search-result text and are marked that way.

Farmer queries: "can I spray today", "spray forecast", "spray conditions near me", "temperature inversion spraying", "delta T forecast", "tank mix calculator", "sprayer calibration calculator". General search for the first one returns mostly spam. The serious competitors are state networks:
- SDSU Mesonet Spray Tool: live and forecast 3 ft wind, inversion and delta T, updated every 5 min.
- NDSU NDAWN inversion network and app: measures 1 m vs 3 m air temperature and sends a push alert when an inversion forms.
- ND/MN Delta T forecast site: new for 2026 per UMN Crop News, June 2026. Gives 5-day delta T maps and an hourly table.
- Oklahoma Mesonet Drift Risk Advisor.
- WSU AgWeatherNet "When to Spray": 72-hour advisory across 360+ stations.
- Sprayers101 "Spraying Weather".
- DTN Ag Weather spray outlook: paid. I could not see it, so it is not scored.

## Where we win
- **Coverage.** It works anywhere in the US. Each state tool covers one state, or the region its stations sit in. For most Corn Belt farmers outside SD/ND/MN/OK we are the only free hourly spray read.
- **Product-specific label logic.** For 2026 OTT dicamba it checks the 95 F forecast high on the day of and the day after, the 85-94 F caution, and the 1 hr after sunrise to 2 hr before sunset window. Enlist and contact products have their own wind limits. None of the station tools model a label.
- **Mixing and calibration on the same page.** The tank mix calculator and sprayer calibration (I checked both by hand, below) plus the rainfast table, nozzle comparison and mix order. The station tools stop at the weather.
- **Honest data notes.** "Reading is at 10 m, at boom height (~3 ft) wind usually runs lower; confirm on-site." "The label means the NWS forecast; this page uses Open-Meteo." Missing values stay missing.

## Where competitors beat us, and how
- **Measured, not modeled.** SDSU, NDAWN and Oklahoma read actual 3 ft wind and actual temperature at two heights, so they see an inversion instead of guessing one. Our inversion read is a rule of thumb: 10 m wind under 5 mph plus hour of day plus cloud cover.
- **Alerts.** NDAWN pushes "inversion now" to the phone. We have nothing a farmer can leave running.
- **Delta T in F.** ND/MN and WSU give delta T in F (WSU ideal 3.6-14.4 F; ND efficacy caution above 14 F). We show C on a page where every other number is F.
- **Focus.** The station tools are one screen. Ours is 4,700 words, and on a phone the verdict card says "POOR, DO NOT SPRAY" without saying why. The why (gusts) sits a full screen lower.

## The 5 changes, ranked
1. **Fix S1 and S3 now (S).** Set the OTT dicamba rainfast to 4 h, add Minnesota's 85 F rule (geolocated state, or a state select) and quote the label's 50% sentence exactly. Wrong label numbers are the one thing that can hurt a farmer here.
2. **Recalibrate wind (S).** Estimate boom-height wind from 10 m (state the factor), rate gusts above the limit as Marginal "gusty" unless they are more than 5 mph over, and let the 10-15 band actually show. Write two worked hours into "Show the math".
3. **Verdict card says why and when (S).** For example: "Poor: gusts 16 mph (limit 15). Next good window 4-6 PM." Both numbers already exist on the page.
4. **Use real stations where they exist (M).** When the field is in SD, ND/MN, OK or another state with a mesonet, link (or read, if CORS allows) the nearest station's measured inversion and 3 ft wind: "Measured inversion at Brookings: none, 4:05 PM". That turns our weakest score into a win, because we would be the only page combining stations and label logic.
5. **Split the page (M).** Keep /spray to the verdict, hourly strip, product picker and calculators. Move the dicamba block, nozzle table, tips and calendar to one /spray-guide page loaded on demand. That cuts the phone page from 12,800 px and the file from 219 KB.

**Best on the internet today? Close.** It is the best free national spray page and the only one that applies 2026 dicamba label logic and does the tank math. But the dicamba rainfast row understates the label, Minnesota's 85 F rule is missing, and the gust rule makes it say "do not spray" far more often than the label requires. In their own states, the mesonet tools measure what we can only estimate.
