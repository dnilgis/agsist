## Where we win
- **Free and complete for one address.** It gives hail days and rate, the largest stone with date and distance, a dated table, preliminary reports to yesterday, the NCEI 1955-2020 record, a year chart, a 162-day radar check, CSV, print and free alerts. HailFACTS charges $40 for one address PDF. SPC gives one day's raw list.
- **Honest labels.** MESH is called an estimate that runs large, there are preliminary tags, the "radar record stops at" wording, "2021 is in neither record", and dashed no-data markers.
- **Ag context.** Crop stage and hail, state pages and storm-day pages.

## Where they beat us, and how
- **HailFACTS: depth and distance rings.** Its archive goes back to 2009, so a 2019 or 2023 storm gets a per-address size. It reports the maximum size at the point and at 1, 3 and 10 miles. Our radar archive starts May 5, 2026. Our ground record covers 2022-2026 plus NCEI to 2020, with 2021 missing.
- **SPC: open downloads per day.** CSV and KML for every day, filtered and raw. We offer CSV per lookup, not a per-day national file.
- **HailFACTS: it states a size "at the location".** We say "within 10 mi" and "~1 km cell", which is more honest but a weaker claim to an adjuster.

## Five changes, ranked
1. **Backfill the radar archive to 2022 as a compact point index (L, on budget).** Store dates and the maximum MESH band per ~1 km cell for April-September 2022-2025, per state, loaded on demand. That answers "did it hail here June 14, 2024" for the years farmers file claims. Check the hail folder budget in scripts/check_site_budget.py first (data/hail is 24 MB). Honesty risk: medium. Keep the estimate and "runs large" labels, and never print a size "at your address".
2. **Fix the phone lookup table (S).** Stack each report as date, size, distance and place on two lines below 480 px, so it reads without a swipe. Honesty risk: none.
3. **One radius everywhere (S).** Make the FAQ, structured data and table note say the radius actually used (default 10 mi, picker 5/10/25), and recount the county table at 1". Honesty risk: none. It removes wrong claims.
4. **Rings in the lookup result (S-M).** Add the largest report within 1, 3 and 10 mi, and the radar band in the cell and at 1 and 3 mi, from data already loaded. That matches the HailFACTS layout for free. Honesty risk: low, since ground reports are town-level and labeled so.
5. **Crop-season filter (M).** Add "May-Sep only" and "hail days while the crop was up, by year" to the address result. No free competitor answers how often ground gets hit in season. Honesty risk: low.

## Verdict
**Yes for free use, close against paid tools.** The lookup is now current to yesterday, and the radar check covers the whole 2026 season. All three hand checks matched. HailFACTS still wins on radar history before 2026 and on per-ring sizes, and the lookup table is hard to read on a phone.
