## Where we win
- **It has a named author with a credential and a reply address on every issue** ("Sigurd Lindquist, Certified Crop Adviser (CCA)... I read everything"), and no partner emails.
- **The harvest price clock, set against RMA's own posted figure, is something no newsletter I found carries.** It is the one number every insured farmer wants in October.
- **Prices are labeled honestly:** "Yahoo Finance, delayed, not CME settlements", with the time and "change vs Tue close".
- **It is short.** The lead, three sections and a watch list read in about two minutes on a phone.

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **Frequency (Barchart\*).** Three sends a day against our one. For a farmer selling into a midday rally, the morning note alone is stale by noon.
- **Analyst depth (Pro Farmer\*).** A staffed team with a marketing advice record. Our depth is a single writer plus data.
- **Local (Morning Ag Clips\*).** State editions. AGSIST Daily is national only. The homepage has ZIP bids, but the briefing does not use them.
- **Small copy mismatches.** Headline, teaser and section title sometimes disagree on the same fact (tops vs pushes toward $95). A careful reader notices.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **One consistency check across headline, teaser and section titles (S).** Fail the build when a price level in the teaser contradicts the lead, for example "toward $95" against a $95.36 lead. The generator already has a critic pass.
2. **Line up the RMA comparison by date (S).** Print RMA's figure for the same settle date as ours. When RMA lags a day, say "RMA through Oct 12: $4.96".
3. **A local line for subscribers with a ZIP (M).** Add "Your nearest bid: $X, basis Y, as of Z" from the bid feed, in the email and on the page when a ZIP is saved. No national newsletter does this.
4. **Exact percentages in the archive teasers (S).** Write "-4.1%", not "nearly 4%", from the same settle data.
5. **A short closing update after the close on report days (M, optional).** One paragraph posted to the page (not emailed) on WASDE and Crop Progress days. It narrows the frequency gap without more email.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Close.** For a free morning note with a named, credentialed author, honest price labels and the harvest price clock, I found nothing better. Paid services (Pro Farmer) have deeper analysis, and Barchart sends more often. No math errors. The weak spots are copy consistency and the lack of anything local.
