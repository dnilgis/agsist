## Where we win
- Free, no account, no ads in the bid list, no buyer behind it. Nobody else in the set is all four.
- Reads each elevator's own board and says so; every card has its own post time, a 72-hour cutoff, and "call to confirm". The ≈ headline price is labeled as re-marked on today's futures with the posted price beside it. That is more honest than any competitor I could see.
- Worth the Drive (net after round-trip miles) is free; FBN sells the same idea inside an account.
- Town pages answer "Bloomer corn price" with an actual price, phone number and nearby elevators. In search, "Bloomer WI corn price today" returned only NASS state averages from 2023 to 2025. Nobody else has this page.
- Hold-to carry net of storage and interest, week-over-week basis with n and range on state pages, and "elevators here we do not read yet" are all things I found nowhere else.

## Where competitors beat us
- **Coverage.** data/elevator-coverage.json: 1,923 boards read out of 8,404 known elevators (6,348 known, not read). Barchart, DTN and FBN each claim 4,000+ buying locations. Bloomer itself shows 1 read and 3 unread (River Country Co-op among them). Outside the network's areas a farmer simply gets fewer or no bids.
- **History.** Barchart lets you chart any bid or basis; Iowa Farm Bureau shows historical bid and basis charts. We have basis change points back to June in data/basis-sparklines/<ST>.json but the town pages do not show them.
- **Town page order on a phone.** The first card on Bloomer is the "Hold to Mar?" carry box. The price table starts below the fold. Elevator apps and Barchart lead with the price.
- **Polish bugs.** Desktop: when the futures label is long ("Nov '26 Soybeans") the delivery cell squeezes to one character per line ("Oc / t / 20 / 26", Wheaton Grain card). Basis is colored green or red with no key. The same CDR Farms bid ($4.15 corn, $12.07 beans, posted Oct 14 1:30 pm) reads "Nov 01, 2026" on the Bloomer page and "Oct 01, 2026" on the Wisconsin page, and data/bids.json has it at Oct 01.
- **Town pages miss structured data.** Only a BreadcrumbList; no FAQ or Dataset, and the meta description has no number in it. CLAUDE.md asks for the answer, key numbers, FAQ and structured data in every page.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **Read more boards where farmers are (L, dnilgis/bids).** Rank the 6,348 known-not-read elevators by how many town pages and ZIP searches have no read board within 25 mi, and build readers in that order (start with platforms that cover many locations at once). Print the honest count on /cash-bids: "We read 1,923 elevator boards directly." Coverage is the one score keeping this from a clear yes.
2. **Lead every town page with the answer (S, generator).** Order: H1, one sentence ("Corn $4.15, basis −65¢ under Dec, CDR Farms, posted Oct 14 1:30 pm"), price table, then the hold-to box. Put the same sentence in the meta description. Add a line comparing to the state: "7¢ weaker than the Wisconsin middle (−58¢, 37 elevators)". Data already exists in data/state-basis/WI.json.
3. **Basis history on demand (M).** On each town page and elevator card, a "Basis since June" button that loads data/basis-sparklines/<ST>.json and draws that elevator's line, labeled "change points only, since Jun 11". Nothing goes into the 1,512 HTML files; the data stays one file per state.
4. **Small FAQ + JSON-LD on town pages (S).** Three questions answered from that page's own data: what is corn paying in Bloomer today, what is the basis, how far is the next elevator. FAQPage plus Dataset JSON-LD. About 2 to 3 KB a page, so about 4.5 MB on the folder; still well under 60 MB.
5. **Fix the display bugs (S).** Give the delivery cell a min-width (or shorten the futures label to "Nov '26") so it never wraps per letter; add a one-line key for the basis colors; make town and state pages use one delivery label from one source so the same bid never shows two months; put the Worth the Drive winner as one line under the best-price cards ("Best after the drive on 1,000 bu: X, +$Y") instead of only in the collapsed panel at the bottom.

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Close.** Where the network reads boards, it is the best free answer anywhere: dated per elevator, honest, net of the drive, with town pages nobody else has. It falls short of a clear yes because it reads about 1,900 of 8,400 known elevators while Barchart, DTN and FBN claim 4,000+, and because it shows no basis history.
