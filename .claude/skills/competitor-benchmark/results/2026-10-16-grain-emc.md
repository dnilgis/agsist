## Bugs found
1. **Low/medium, sourcing: "the low fit lands on NDSU" is not true of NDSU's own published soybean table.**
   - What the page says:
     - The method text says the Modified Henderson fit reproduces NDSU engineer Ken Hellevang's soybean numbers (10.2% at 70 F and 60%, from Cornell-posted slides).
     - The "How sure" table says "The low fit lands on Minnesota and NDSU".
   - What NDSU's 2011 table says (32 to 80 F, 50 to 90% RH; fetched copy):
     - It lists 10.7% at 70 F/60%, which is the high fit, not 10.2%.
     - 23 of its 30 cells fall outside the page's two-fit range.
     - From 50 to 60% humidity it runs up to 0.7 point wetter than both fits. Examples:
       - 50 F/50%: NDSU 9.5, page 8.8 to 9.1
       - 32 F/50%: NDSU 10.0, page 9.3 to 9.5
     - At 80 to 90% humidity it runs drier than both. Example: 50 F/90%: NDSU 19.4, page 20.6 to 28.2.
   - Effect on the fan calls is small:
     - The one-point margin on "dries to target" covers the 0.7 point wet gap in every cell checked.
     - Wet calls use the high fit, which is above NDSU in damp air.
   - Still, the page tells farmers which extension source it matches, and that claim is partly wrong.
   - Fix: add NDSU's 2011 table as a column in "How sure", and drop "and NDSU" from the low-fit sentence.
2. Nothing else found. Corn, wheat and fan-heat numbers re-check the same as Oct 15.

## Where we win
- **We answer the farmer's question.** The answer card says "Fans wet it", gives the hours it holds, and gives the next window: "today 1 pm to 6 pm, 5 hours of drying air". It does this for drying, cooling or holding, at the farm's ZIP. The tables read (NDSU, Arkansas, PAMI) give a number for one air condition and leave the decision to the reader.
- **Both soybean camps shown and named.** The low fit matches Minnesota. The high fit matches every Kentucky and Arkansas cell. Arkansas's table (saved copy) equals our high fit in every cell checked.
- **Corn.** PAMI has no corn chart and the Arkansas soybean page has none. Clemson has corn only behind JS.
- **Printable grayscale charts with the math shown, and fan heat worked from vapor pressure.**

## Where they beat us
- **More grains.** PAMI charts barley, oats, peas, canola, lentils, flax and sunflower, each with its model named. We have corn, soybeans and wheat.
- **Model choice.** Clemson lets the reader pick a model or a composite average. We show two fits for corn and soybeans, one for wheat.
- **Celsius.** PAMI is in °C. We are °F only, which matters for Canadian readers searching "EMC chart".
- **Wheat classes.** PAMI charts hard red spring (Chung-Pfost), hard red winter and durum (Oswin) separately. We have one wheat curve, and we say soft red runs drier.
