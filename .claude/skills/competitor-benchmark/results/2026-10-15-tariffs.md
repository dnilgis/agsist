# Head to head: agsist.com/tariffs

Checked Oct 15, 2026, from the read-only checkout. I rendered tariffs.html at 390x844 and 1280x800. No sideways scroll and no JS errors (the analytics host is blocked here). About 380 KB loaded. The rates come from data/tariffs.json, dated Oct 14, 2026.

**What I could not see:** I could not open any competitor host (DNS refusal). Competitor cells and the policy checks below are from search-result text and are marked *. Most 2026 events on the page (the Sep 28 tariff-cut lists, the Section 338 actions, the Jul 24 Section 301 duties) I could not confirm from here either way.

## Numbers checked by hand
- **The stacked rates:**
  - Soybeans 3% MFN + 10% = 13%, corn 1% + 10% = 11%, wheat 1% + 10% = 11%, pork 12% + 10% = 22%. The arithmetic is right.
  - S&P Global (July 2026) gives the same 13% for soybeans and the same split.*
- **March 2025 tranche:** 15% on wheat, corn, cotton and chicken; 10% on soybeans, pork, beef and dairy. Matches.
- **Busan commitments:** 12 MMT late 2025, then 25 MMT a year 2026-2028. Matches the White House figures as reported.* Beijing has not confirmed them.*
- **EU ethanol:** €192/m³ undenatured and €102/m³ denatured. This matches €19.2 and €10.2 per hl.
- **Inconsistencies (low):**
  - The Jul 6, 2026 entry says the US "would end its 10% fentanyl tariff" in exchange for China dropping its 10%, for cargoes after Oct 1. But the Feb 20, 2026 entry says the Supreme Court voided the fentanyl tariffs. No later entry says whether the Oct 1 deal happened. Today's banner implies it did not. A reader cannot tell.
  - The Oct 30, 2025 entry says the truce was "later extended through Nov 10, 2026". The Busan suspension was for one year from Nov 10, 2025, so Nov 10, 2026 was the original end, not an extension.
  - The China pace looks inconsistent with outside figures. Aug 10 says "roughly a quarter of the way through" 25 MMT, and Sep 17 says "roughly halfway". S&P put confirmed 2026 US soybean sales to China at about 2.0 MMT through July 20.* The two may count different things (calendar year vs marketing year, sales vs shipments). The page should say which.
- **"China buys roughly 60% of globally traded soybeans":** right in general terms.

## Where we win
- **One table with today's rate, its parts and its date** for the crops a Midwest farmer sells. Each row has a source link. The academic sources are monthly PDFs or articles. They are not one table that stays current.
- **Updated the day after the news** (Oct 14 Reuters cargoes), with a timeline back to March 2025.
- **It ties tariffs to basis and links to the farmer's own cash bids.**

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **Dollar impact (NDSU).** NDSU estimates $14.9B of lost US ag exports to China from March 2025 to February 2026, $6.8B of it soybeans.* We give rates but no lost-sales figure.
- **China purchase pace with a number (Agri-Pulse).** They track it against the 25 MMT pledge.* Ours is "roughly a quarter" or "roughly halfway" with no figure or date basis. AGSIST already has data/export-sales.json.
- **Trade aid.** The $12B Farmer Bridge Assistance program ($44.36 corn, $30.88 soybeans, $39.35 wheat per acre on 2025 planted acres) is not on the page.* It is the tariff number most farmers actually received.
- **Trust signal.** There is no visible byline or "how I check rates" line. Farm Policy News and NDSU carry named authors.

## Five changes, ranked by impact
1. **A China purchase gauge from our own export-sales data (M).** Show "X of 25 MMT, USDA export sales through <date>", say whether it counts sales or shipments, and say calendar year or marketing year. Then the Aug and Sep timeline lines can cite the figure.
2. **Close the loose ends in the timeline (S).** Add one line on what happened to the Jul 6 "Oct 1" deal. Explain why the fentanyl tariff could still be traded after the February ruling. Fix "later extended through Nov 10, 2026".
3. **A trade-aid row (S).** List FBA rates per acre, the payment cap, the timing and the source, plus any 2026 round, dated and sourced.
4. **Shorten the banner (S).** One sentence on the rate, plus the date. Move the Canada detail into the table notes. On a phone it is now about 12 lines before the table.
5. **Byline and method line (S).** Write "Sigurd Lindquist, CCA. A rate changes here only when a government notice or two wire reports confirm it."

## Verdict
Best on the internet today? **Close.** It is the only maintained, dated, one-table ag tariff tracker I found, and the rate arithmetic is right. But it lacks the purchase pace with numbers, the dollar impact and the aid payments the university sources give. A few timeline lines contradict each other.
