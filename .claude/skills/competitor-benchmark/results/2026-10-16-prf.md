## Where we win
- **Every grid priced, every year counted, free.** RMA's program page links out to the locator, the DST and the history as separate tools. ProAg's quoting is for its agents. Redd Summit's analysis needs a form. We print the premium, USDA's share and 75 years of results, and both examples check out to the dollar.
- **County pages with numbers on the first screen.** Grid IDs, base values, the Jan-Feb cap, a priced example and the five-year index table. No readable competitor has a county page.
- **The limits, said plainly.** We quote section 13 ("possible for you to receive an indemnity payment without suffering a loss"), give the basis-risk research and name the native sod rule we do not model. Redd Summit's copy calls PRF "often informally referred to as rainfall insurance or drought insurance" and quotes a customer saying "When it doesn't rain we are still happy."
- **No agenda.** Redd Summit runs a quote form and a 3-step "contact a Redd Summit Agent" path, and says agents are paid on commission. ProAg ends in "find your local ProAg agent". We route no one.

## Where they beat us
- **PRF next to FSA disaster aid (Redd Summit).** It answers "How is PRF insurance different from FSA Disaster Programs?" and "Can I participate in FSA programs and PRF insurance at the same time? Yes". Neither prf.html, how-prf-works nor components/prf.js mentions the Livestock Forage Disaster Program. A rancher weighing PRF against LFP gets nothing from us.
- **In-season tracking (Redd Summit, claimed).** It offers "24/7 access to your PRF policy, with daily rain updates, forecasts, and historical data". That is behind its client login, so I did not verify it. Our county page shows 2026 only for the 6 intervals RMA has finalized, and nothing for the interval in progress.
- **One plain caution (ProAg).** "It is not designed to insure against ongoing or severe drought, as the coverage is based on precipitation expected during specific intervals only." Our FAQ covers drought, but the first screen does not.
- **Official standing (RMA).** RMA's page is where the 2027 data-source notice and the stocking-rate FAQ live. We cite them.

## Five changes, ranked
1. **Put a dated dollar line on the calculator's first screen (S).**
   - What: one line under the H1, for example "Example, grid 30454 Barron Co. WI: 100 ac grazing at 90% costs you $150 a year; would have paid in 44 of 75 years. Find yours below." The county pages already lead this way.
   - Why: the phone's first screen is a description, a sponsor box and a search field. The worked numbers are three screens down.
   - Honesty risk: low, if labeled as an example and not a recommendation.
2. **Answer "PRF or LFP, or both?" (S).**
   - What: one FAQ entry on prf.html and how-prf-works, from FSA's LFP rules and RMA:
     - LFP is FSA disaster aid, triggered by the Drought Monitor.
     - PRF is insurance on a grid index.
     - You can hold both.
     - Each pays on its own trigger and its own timing.
   - Why: it is the next question a rancher asks, and an agency page answers it.
   - Honesty risk: low, with FSA and RMA cited and no "which is better".
3. **Show the interval in progress (M).**
   - What: on county pages and the calculator, show "Sep-Oct 2026 so far, preliminary" from NOAA's published preliminary gridded data, clearly marked "not RMA's final index". Load it from one small data file per state, not baked into 3,070 pages.
   - Why: it is the reason ranchers pay an agency for a portal.
   - Honesty risk: medium. The preliminary figure can differ from RMA's final index, so show the gap from past intervals or do not ship.
4. **Add a beginning or veteran farmer option to the calculator (S).**
   - What: a checkbox that adds the extra subsidy points, from the RMA handbook. Today the page only says "Beginning and veteran farmers get a larger share".
   - Why: those readers see a premium that is too high.
   - Honesty risk: low, sourced.
5. **Tidy the two low issues (S).**
   - What: use one dateModified per page, and add "for that pair" to "the middle grid" in the county month table.
   - Honesty risk: none.
