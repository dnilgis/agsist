## Where we win
- **We answer the farmer's question, not the politician's.** "What it changes for a corn and soybean farm this year: not much," with $4.10 corn and $10.00 soybean reference prices on the first screen. No competitor I found opens with what it means for a farm. They open with votes.
- **The Oct 1 section is the best explainer anywhere.** It covers what did not stop (insurance, ARC/PLC, SNAP) and what did stop (the six unfunded programs, new CRP, dairy forward pricing and indemnity). It says the real date is Jan 1, 2027 and gives the order: dairy first, wheat Jun 1, corn Oct 1. The news stories each have one piece of that. CRS has all of it, but spread across a long report.
- **It is current and honest about its own record.** It was updated Oct 14, every section carries a date, and the update log admits two wrong calls (Aug 3 and Oct 6). Nobody else does that.
- **The money is shown, not just stated.** The $1.374T baseline is broken out by title, the CBO House score is split into 6-year and 11-year figures with the top line items, and the twelve titles are on one page. That is the CRS content, readable on a phone.
- **No agenda.** AFBF, NSAC and the Senate committee each argue for a side. We do not, and the byline and phone number are on the page.

## Where a competitor beats us, and how
- **CRS R48918 is more complete and more current on bill text.** Its Jul 29 update compares the House bill, the Senate bill and current law title by title. We still cite the April House-only version by its old title. The "Title by title" section also still says "Senate's June 23 draft" in several places.
- **Senate Ag Committee and congress.gov name the Senate bill: S. 5526.** It was reported and placed on the calendar Sep 24. Our page never prints "S. 5526", so anyone who searches the bill number never finds us.
- **NSAC is more honest about Senate conservation.** NSAC says the revised Senate bill takes money out of EQIP and CSP and moves it elsewhere in the title. Our Senate conservation cell lists only the gains (easement cost-share 50 to 60%, two new programs). That reads one-sided.
- **News reports give the House floor result.** Search results report that the Luna amendment stripped the pesticide-labeling preemption and liability shield. They also report that the House bill carries a Proposition 12 fix and dropped E15. Our House column says only "pesticide-labeling and E15 fights nearly derailed floor passage" and never says how they ended. Prop 12 is not on the page at all, and that is the biggest livestock item in the House bill.
- **DTN, Farm Progress and AgProud serve dairy and CRP farmers better.** They report that Dairy Margin Coverage keeps running through 2031 and that 2027 DMC enrollment is open. They explain what happens to new forward contracts signed after Oct 1, and they translate the permanent-law cliff into a number (parity support near $50/cwt). Our page has the CRS 252% figure but no DMC line and no cwt figure.
- **Everyone is shorter.** On a phone our page is 16,000 px. The "60-second read", "If you farm for a living", the Oct 1 section, the holdups list and the FAQ restate the status five times.

## The 5 changes that would make ours clearly the best (by impact)
1. **"What this means for my operation" picker (M).** Add one row of buttons near the top: Corn/soy, Wheat, Dairy, Cattle/hogs, CRP landowner, Specialty/organic. Each opens 3 or 4 sourced lines from a new `byOperation` block in data/farm-bill.json, rendered on click so it adds nothing to the page weight. Dairy: DMC runs through 2031, 2027 enrollment is open, forward pricing lapsed for contracts signed after Oct 1, and Jan 1 parity risk. CRP: contracts approved by Sep 30 are serviced, new and re-enrollment wait, with FSA's notice linked. Livestock: the Prop 12 fix is in the House bill and not in the Senate bill. This is the personal answer nobody gives.
2. **Fix the stale and one-sided cells (S).** Name S. 5526 in the H1 area, the compare header, the FAQ and the JSON-LD. Cite CRS R48918 as updated Jul 29 (House and Senate). Replace "Senate's June 23 draft" with "committee-passed bill" where the text applies. Add the EQIP/CSP shift to the Senate conservation cell, sourced to the bill or CRS rather than NSAC's adjectives. Say how the House pesticide and E15 fights ended, and add a Prop 12 row to the compare table.
3. **A dated "Next 90 days" strip (S).** Under the pills, put four dates in one line: Nov 3 election, Nov 9 Senate returns, Dec 11 funding runs out, Jan 1 permanent law (dairy). Next to it, one line on what each outcome means: a bill passes, a short extension (fixes the cliff only if it carries the suspension), or nothing. Right now a farmer has to read to the fourth section to get that.
4. **Cut the page by a third (M).** Keep the 60-second read, "If you farm for a living", Oct 1, the tracker and the FAQ in the HTML. Move "Title by title" and the CBO line-item table into data/farm-bill.json and render them behind one "Show all twelve titles" button. Sig's Oct 14 note already flags this. It drops the phone page from about 16,000 px to about 10,000 px and the file to roughly 60 KB.
5. **Give the cliff in milk-check terms (S).** Next to the 252% CRS figure, add the parity support price in $/cwt with its source and date, labeled CRS/USDA, and a link to the ARC/PLC and break-even tools as the "what you can do" line. Follow the honest-numbers rule: no number goes up until it is sourced.

None of these sell anything, route to an agent or add consulting, and all keep the 150 KB cap.

**Best on the internet today? Close.** For "what does the farm bill mean for my farm right now" no page I found beats it, and the Oct 1 / Jan 1 explainer is the best anywhere. But CRS is more complete on bill text, and dairy, livestock and CRP readers get specifics elsewhere that we leave out. Our page also misses the Senate bill number. Change 1 and change 2 would make it a clear yes.
