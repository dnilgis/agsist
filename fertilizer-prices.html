How these prices are read

Source. USDA Agricultural Marketing Service (AMS), Market News, production cost reports, read through USDA My Market News: Iowa (every two weeks), Illinois (every two weeks), Oklahoma (every two weeks), North Carolina (weekly), Inter-Mountain West, which covers Utah, Idaho, Colorado and Wyoming (monthly), and Pacific Northwest, which covers Oregon and Washington (every two weeks). The reports are free and public. Each one surveys fertilizer dealers and prints the low, high and average asking price per product, with its freight term (F.O.B. the dealer, or delivered).

What the table shows. The newest report on file, every fertilizer product USDA priced in it, in dollars per ton, as USDA printed it. A report date is the day USDA published it. Prices are asking prices, not quotes: call your dealer before you buy.

Changes. A change is this report’s average minus the average in the same report on an earlier date, for the same product, priced the same way. “Since” the last report means the report just before this one; a year ago means the report published closest to 52 weeks earlier, within 10 days. When that report did not list the product, priced it differently (another freight term or product name), or is not on file, no change is shown and the cell says why. When USDA’s own printed change for a product does not match the one worked out from the two reports, neither is shown.

Left out. A price USDA did not print, a zero, an average outside its own range, a price far outside the normal band for that product, a product listed twice in one report, and an average that moved more than 60% from the same product’s last report (taken as a misread). Farm diesel and propane from the same reports are on Fast Facts.

Your state. The page shows the report for the state it already knows (a place you saved on AGSIST or a state you picked). Where USDA has no report for that state, it shows the report whose state lies nearest, center to center. Nothing about you is sent to make that choice.

When it updates. The page reads USDA’s reports on Friday evening and again on Saturday and Monday. Charts show up to a year of each report.
