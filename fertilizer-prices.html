How these prices are read

Source. USDA Agricultural Marketing Service (AMS), Market News, production cost reports, read through USDA My Market News. The reports are free and public. Iowa, Illinois, Oklahoma and Pacific Northwest (Oregon and Washington) come out every two weeks. North Carolina is weekly. Inter-Mountain West (Utah, Idaho, Colorado and Wyoming) is monthly.

What the table shows. Each report surveys fertilizer dealers. For each product it prints a low, a high and one price for the whole survey, per ton, with the freight term. The table shows the newest report on file as USDA printed it. A report date is the day USDA published it. These are asking prices, not quotes. Call your dealer before you buy.

Changes. A change compares two reports from the same USDA survey, for the same product, priced the same way. The last report is the one just before. A year ago is the report published closest to 52 weeks earlier. No change is shown when that report left the product out, priced it another way, or is not on file. The cell says which. If USDA’s own printed change does not match the two reports, neither is shown. The year-ago column appears once a report from about a year earlier is on file.

Left out. A price USDA did not print, a zero, a price outside its own low and high, or far outside the normal band for that product. Also a product listed twice in one report, and a jump too big to be real. Farm diesel and propane from the same reports are on Fast Facts.

Your state. The page shows the report for a state it already knows: a place you saved on AGSIST, or a state you picked. Where USDA has no report for that state, it shows the nearest report. When every report is far away (more than about 450 miles, center to center), it picks none. Nothing about you is sent to make that choice.

Charts. Up to a year of each report, one line per product.
